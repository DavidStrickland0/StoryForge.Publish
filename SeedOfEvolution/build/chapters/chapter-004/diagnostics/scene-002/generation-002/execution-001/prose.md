"Read it," Thorne said. Her voice was flat, devoid of the urgency that Adrian’s pounding heart demanded. She pushed the paper across the polished surface of the desk. Adrian felt a cold knot form in his stomach. It was not fear, exactly. It was the same cold, hollow feeling he had when he checked his bank account and saw the overdraft fee. The light was still harsh, but it no longer felt like a pressure.

"Thank you," she said.

It was a strange thing to say. Thank you for signing away your right to refuse treatment. Thank you for allowing them to cut you open if you die on their floor. Thank you for keeping their data clean.

Adrian nodded back. He did not feel like speaking. He watched Thorne place the document into a folder, then into the filing cabinet beside her desk. The metal drawer slid shut with a sharp, metallic click. The sound was final, sealing the moment away.

"Dr. Thorne," he said.

She did not look up. "Yes?"

"If the side effects get worse," he said, his voice quiet, "what happens then?"

Thorne’s fingers stopped moving. She looked up at him, her pale eyes steady. "The protocol is designed to manage the side effect profile. If the symptoms become unmanageable, you will press the red button."

"And then?"

"And then the team will intervene."

Adrian stared at her. The words were clear, precise, and utterly empty of context. He did not know what *intervene* meant. He did not know what *unapproved therapeutic agents* were. He did not know what would happen to his body in the hands of people who had never known his name.

He closed his eyes, trying to slow his breathing, trying to bring his heart rate down.
