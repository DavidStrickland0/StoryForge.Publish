Dr. Thorne moved from the window. Her steps were silent, the soles of her shoes absorbing the sound against the linoleum floor. She did not smile. She did not offer a congratulations. She simply reached out, her fingers closing around the top edge of the document, and lifted it from the desk. She scanned the signature line, her pale eyes moving quickly over the ink, then back to the text above it.

"Good," she said. The word was flat, devoid of warmth. It was a confirmation of data, a checkmark in a ledger.

"Your final payment for the intake phase has been processed," she said. She held out the envelope. "It will hit your account within twenty-four hours. Check your banking app."

Adrian took the envelope. The paper felt thin and brittle in his trembling fingers. He didn’t open it. He didn’t need to. He slid the envelope into the pocket of his jeans, the fabric of his shirt clinging to his sweat-dampened back.

"Are you finished here?" Thorne asked.

Adrian nodded. He stood up, his knees popping audibly. "Your schedule is unchanged. Eight a.m. sharp, every morning. Bring your own water bottle. The specimen collection room is on the left, past the waiting area. Do not linger in the hallways. We have other participants coming in."

But he was not in pain. Pain was something he could report. Exhaustion was something he had to carry.

Thorne stopped typing. She turned her head slightly, her gaze sharp, cutting through the air between them. She didn’t call him a liar. She didn’t need to. The silence stretched, thin and taut, until Adrian felt the need to fill it.

"I’m managing," he added.

"Good," she said again. She turned back to her screen, the blue light reflecting off her glasses. The conversation was over.

He had signed the paper. He had made the choice. The money was coming. The trial was starting.

Adrian Vale walked down the street, the sun setting behind him, casting his shadow long and thin across the pavement. He was alone, he was tired, and he was exactly where he needed to be.
