The pharmacy wing was smaller than Adrian expected. It smelled of antiseptic and warm plastic, a sterile scent that seemed to cling to the back of his throat. The room was dominated by a long counter made of pale laminate, behind which shelves lined the walls, filled with identical white boxes and rows of vials. There was no waiting area, no magazine rack, no coffee machine. Just the counter, a single computer terminal, and a door at the far end marked *Dispensing*.

Adrian stood by the door, his hands still in his pockets, the weight of his keys and the new ID card pressing against his hip. He felt exposed. The previous room, the intake desk, had been a transaction. This felt like a procedure.

A man in a white coat was already standing behind the counter, organizing a tray of pill bottles. He didn’t look up immediately. He was focused on the small, precise task of lining up the caps, his movements efficient and devoid of wasted motion. He was older than Adrian, perhaps in his fifties, with thinning hair and a face that suggested he had been tired for a long time and was used to it.

"The new one?" the man asked, still not looking up. His voice was dry, practical.

"Yes," Adrian said. He realized he hadn’t introduced himself. "I’m Adrian. Adrian Vale."

The man stopped his work and turned. His eyes were sharp, assessing Adrian with a quick, clinical gaze that scanned him from head to toe before settling on his face. "Dr. Aris. Welcome to the program. You’re V-014, if I’m not mistaken."

"Yes."

"Good. Take a seat." Dr. Aris gestured to the two chairs on the other side of the counter. One was empty. The other was occupied by a man Adrian recognized from the waiting area earlier. It was Evans, the man with the receding hairline who had chosen the 8:00 AM slot. The man with the receding hairline who had chosen the 8:00 AM slot.

Evans was leaning back in the chair, his arms crossed over his chest. He looked relaxed, almost bored, which made Adrian feel even more tense. Evans caught Adrian’s eye and gave a small, acknowledging nod. It wasn’t friendly, exactly, but it wasn’t hostile. It was the nod of one person recognizing another who had made a specific, somewhat embarrassing choice.

Adrian sat in the empty chair. The plastic was cold against his jacket.

"First dose," Dr. Aris said. He reached for one of the white boxes on the counter and pulled out a small, amber-colored pill bottle. He shook it once, the sound of plastic clicking against plastic, and then unscrewed the cap. "One tablet, once a day. With a full glass of water. You have a two-hour window to take it. Miss the window, and the day is forfeit. You understand?"

"I understand," Adrian said. He knew this. David had explained it. But hearing it from Dr. Aris, in this quiet room, made it feel more real. More binding.

"Good." Dr. Aris poured a single pill into the palm of his hand. It was small, white, and unremarkable. He held it out to Adrian. "Take it now. I need to see you take it."

Adrian took the pill. It sat in his palm, light as a feather, yet it felt heavy with implication. He looked at Evans, who was watching the interaction with mild curiosity. Evans shrugged, a subtle movement that seemed to say, *This is part of it. Just do it.*

Adrian picked up the glass of water that Dr. Aris had placed on the counter. He brought the glass to his lips, tipped his head back, and swallowed the pill. It slid down his throat, smooth and cold. He drank the water, the taste neutral and clean.

"Good," Dr. Aris said. He made a note on a clipboard. "Any nausea? Dizziness?"

"No," Adrian said. He felt nothing. No rush, no burn, no strange tingling. Just the faint, lingering taste of water.

"Good. That’s all for today. You’ll receive the next dose tomorrow at the same time." Dr. Aris closed the bottle and placed it back on the shelf. "If you have any questions, you can ask David. He’s the intake coordinator. He knows the details better than I do."

Adrian stood up. His legs felt a little unsteady, perhaps from the cold, perhaps from the adrenaline of the last hour. He looked at Evans again.

"First day?" Evans asked. His voice was low, conversational.

"Yes."

"First pill?"

"Yes."

Evans nodded. "It’s weird, right? The first time, you just… do it. You don’t think about it. You just swallow and forget."

Adrian looked at him. "Do you forget it easily?"

Evans smiled, a small, wry expression that didn’t quite reach his eyes. "I’ve been doing this for three months. I don’t forget it. I just stop noticing it. It becomes part of the routine. Like brushing your teeth, except with more paperwork."

Adrian felt a small, unexpected relief. He had expected the other participants to be strangers, people who would avoid eye contact, people who were embarrassed by the same thing he was. But Evans seemed to have found a way to make it mundane. To make it just another part of his day.

"Does it get easier?" Adrian asked.

Evans leaned forward slightly, his elbows on his knees. "Easier? No. It doesn’t get easier. It just gets… normal. You stop thinking about the pill. You stop thinking about the cup. You just think about the money. And then, after a while, you stop thinking about the money too. You just think about the next thing you have to do."

Adrian looked down at his hands. They were trembling slightly. He clenched them into fists to stop it.

"I’m worried I’m going to fail," Adrian said. The words came out quieter than he intended. He didn’t want to sound weak. He didn’t want to sound like he was asking for pity. But the fear was still there, sitting in his stomach, a cold, heavy stone.

Evans looked at him for a moment. Then he reached into his own pocket and pulled out a small, folded piece of paper. It was a receipt, or maybe a note. He tore a small strip off the corner and held it out to Adrian.

"Here," Evans said. "Write this down. 8:00 AM. Room 104. Don’t be late. And if you can’t do it in thirty minutes, don’t panic. Just take a breath. It’s not the end of the world."

Adrian took the strip of paper. It was small, thin, and slightly damp from Evans’s hand. He folded it and put it in his pocket, next to his ID card.

"Thanks," Adrian said.

"Good luck, V-014," Evans said. He stood up, stretching his arms above his head. "See you tomorrow. Or maybe not. Depends on how much you like the taste of your own pride."

Adrian almost smiled. He almost smiled.

He walked to the door, his steps lighter than when he had entered. The air in the corridor was cooler, and the scent of medicine was fading. He felt different. Not better, exactly. But less alone. The weight in his pocket was still there, but it felt less like a secret and more like a tool. A key.

He was in. He had done it. The money was coming. He was a participant. The words didn’t feel right in his mouth, but they were true. He was part of the system now. The hunger was quieter. The fear was quieter.

He reached the end of the corridor, where the door to the clinical wing stood open. He stopped, taking a breath. The air from the door was cooler, carrying the faint scent of soap and metal.

He pushed the door open and stepped inside.
