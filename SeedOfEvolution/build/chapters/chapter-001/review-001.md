# Chapter Review

## Overall Assessment

The chapter effectively establishes Adrian's desperation and introduces the experimental medicine program, aligning with the active Short arc's first target. However, it contains significant continuity errors regarding the trial's frequency (daily vs. weekly) and a minor character knowledge inconsistency. The prose style is generally strong, though it occasionally relies on explanatory narration that could be trimmed.

## Findings

- [Canon] The chapter states the trial requires daily visits, contradicting the established canon which specifies daily medication but only daily specimen collection if traveling to the clinic, or generally daily duties. However, the specific dialogue in the chapter claims 'visit the clinic once a week', which contradicts the premise's 'visit the clinic every day' and the Short Arc target's 'daily routine'.
  Evidence: David said, '...visit the clinic once a week to provide a sample.' Later, Adrian thinks, 'He was expected to do it daily.' This internal contradiction and deviation from the premise's 'daily' requirement is a continuity error.
- [Character] Adrian's internal monologue claims he was 'expected to do it daily' immediately after David stated the requirement was 'once a week'. This is a logical inconsistency in the character's processing of the information just provided.
  Evidence: David: '...visit the clinic once a week...' Adrian: 'He was expected to do it daily.' The character contradicts the explicit information received in the same scene.
- [Style] The narrative frequently explains the emotional or physical significance of actions immediately after describing them, violating the rule against unnecessary explanation of meanings the reader can infer. This pattern dilutes the impact of the scene.
  Evidence: Examples: 'He gripped the edge of the desk... a strange sense of calm settle over him.' 'He felt a wave of relief so strong it nearly buckled his knees.' The prose often tells the reader how to feel rather than letting the action speak.
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter ends with Adrian waiting to see the terms, which aligns with the Chapter 1 target of 'gathering information' and 'tentative decision'. However, the transition from the financial aid office to the hospital is somewhat abrupt, lacking the 'exhausting all conventional options' beat fully established in the target's 'IntendedChange'.
  Evidence: Adrian rejects the warehouse job and immediately finds the hospital. The target suggests he should be 'exhausting all conventional options' before arriving at the hospital as the 'only viable path'. The rejection of the warehouse job is brief and may not sufficiently establish the 'exhaustion' of options.

## Engagement Findings

- The chapter maintains strong tension through Adrian's physical state and financial pressure. The interaction with David is well-paced, building the stakes of the decision.

## Revision Guidance

- Correct the trial frequency to match the premise (daily visits) or adjust the premise if weekly is intended, ensuring consistency between dialogue and internal thought.
- Remove redundant explanatory sentences that tell the reader the emotional impact of actions already clearly depicted.
- Expand the rejection of conventional options (e.g., the warehouse job) to better establish the 'exhaustion' of alternatives before the hospital introduction.
