The tablet screen glowed with a sterile, blue-white light, illuminating the fine print that David had scrolled into view. The font was small, a dense wall of text that seemed to blur at the edges where Adrian’s exhaustion pressed against his vision. He squinted, his hands gripping the edge of the reception desk to steady his trembling fingers. The wood was cold, a sharp contrast to the heat rising in his neck.

David watched him with the patient, detached curiosity of a man who had seen this particular expression a hundred times before. He did not speak. He did not offer a water bottle, which Adrian noticed with a flash of bitter resentment. The lobby was air-conditioned to a crisp, clinical chill that made his skin prickle, but his stomach was a different kind of cold, a hollow, gnawing void that had been empty since the granola bar two days ago.

"Page three," David said, his voice soft and professional. "The schedule and specimen collection protocols."

Adrian looked up, forcing his eyes to focus on the highlighted paragraph. *Daily dosing. Morning, upon waking. Specimen collection. Daily, within one to two hours of dosing. Method: Masturbatory stimulation to ejaculation. Collection vessel provided by facility or approved home kit. Submission to intake lab by 10:00 AM.*

The words sat on the screen, black and unyielding. *Masturbatory stimulation.* It was a clinical term, stripped of any human context, reduced to a mechanical function. Adrian felt a flush crawl up his chest, burning under his thin hoodie. He looked at David, expecting a smile, a joke, some acknowledgment of the absurdity of the situation. A nineteen-year-old, standing in a sterile lobby, being told that his primary contribution to medical science would be to jerk off into a plastic cup every morning before his first class.

David’s expression remained neutral. "Is the timing flexible? I have a nine o'clock lecture on Tuesdays and Thursdays."

David tapped a different section of the tablet. "The facility opens at 7:00 AM. You can collect the sample on-site, or you can use the home kit if you prefer. The kit includes a sealed, biohazard-rated container and a prepaid shipping label. It’s often more convenient for participants with early morning commitments."

Convenient. The word felt like a slap. Adrian looked back at the screen. *Masturbatory stimulation.* He read it again, the letters dancing slightly in the dim light. His pride, that fragile, stubborn thing that kept him standing when the rest of him wanted to collapse, felt like it was cracking under the weight of the practicality. He needed the money. The number David had mentioned earlier—$4,500 for the initial phase, plus the medical coverage—was the difference between staying in his apartment and sleeping in a park. It was the difference between keeping his student ID and being evicted.

"And if I miss a day?" Adrian asked, his voice sounding thinner than he intended. "If I’m late, or if I… can’t?"

David’s eyes flicked to his face, a quick, assessing glance. "Missed samples are logged as non-compliance. Three missed samples in a week results in a formal review of your participation status. Five, and you are removed from the trial. Your compensation is prorated accordingly."

No threats. No anger. Just a bureaucratic adjustment. Adrian swallowed, the movement dry and painful in his throat. He looked at the other participants in the waiting area, a scattered group of men sitting in the beige armchairs. Most were reading, checking their phones, or staring at the wall. They looked calm. Resigned. They did not look like men who were about to be told to perform an intimate act for a paycheck. Or perhaps they did, and they had simply stopped caring.

"Is it… private?" Adrian asked. The question came out small, almost shy.

"Completely," David said. "The collection rooms are soundproofed and monitored only for safety, not content. You are alone. No cameras record the act itself, only the timestamp of entry and exit for logging purposes. Your privacy is protected by law and by our institutional ethics board."

Adrian nodded, though the reassurance did little to ease the knot in his stomach. He looked back at the tablet. The compensation table was still visible on the bottom of the screen. *Weekly deposit. Direct to bank account. First payment issued upon completion of the first seven-day cycle.*

Seven days. He had to make it through the first week to see the money. If he failed, if he couldn’t do it, if the shame stopped him, he would be back to square one. And square one was a lease with a Friday deadline and a bank account that held less than forty dollars.

He looked at David. "Can I see the medical consent forms? The part about… side effects."

David nodded, swiping the screen. The text changed, scrolling past the logistical details into the medical disclosures. Adrian’s eyes scanned the list. *Mild nausea. Fatigue. Increased libido. Temporary dizziness. Rare instances of skin irritation at the injection site.* Wait, injection? He frowned, looking up.

"David, you said it was a daily medication. A pill?"

David paused, tilting his head slightly. "Ah. The protocol was updated last quarter. The oral formulation had inconsistent bioavailability. We’ve switched to a subcutaneous injection. It’s a small, fine-needle auto-injector. Painless, or close to it. You administer it once daily, usually in the abdomen."

Adrian blinked. The air in the lobby seemed to thin out, the recycled oxygen suddenly insufficient. An injection. Every day. In his belly. On top of the morning routine. On top of the sample collection.

"And the samples?" Adrian asked, his voice tight. "Still daily?"

"Yes," David said. "The injection stimulates the reproductive system. The sample allows us to track the cellular response in real-time. It’s essential for the safety data."

Adrian looked at the tablet, then at his own hands. They were pale, the veins blue and prominent under the skin. He thought of the needle. He thought of the cup. He thought of the money. He thought of the Friday deadline.

He felt a strange, cold clarity settle over him. The panic that had driven him into this lobby, the frantic, desperate energy of a man trying to hold back a tide with his bare hands, began to recede. In its place was a hollow, practical emptiness. He was not a person making a choice. He was a resource being allocated. His body was a machine that could be tuned, adjusted, and monetized. And right now, that machine was the only thing he had that was valuable enough to buy his survival.

"Show me the injection technique," Adrian said.

David nodded, satisfied by the shift in tone. He swiped the screen, bringing up a diagram of the abdomen with numbered injection sites. "We provide a training session before your first dose. I’ll have a nurse walk you through it. It’s straightforward."

Adrian studied the diagram. The lines were clean, medical, impersonal. He traced the path of the needle with his eyes. It was just a hole in the skin. Just a fluid entering a vein. Just a biological process being coaxed into overdrive.

"And the compensation," Adrian said, keeping his voice flat. "It covers the full tuition balance?"

"It covers the balance, plus a buffer for living expenses," David said. "You will not be short on rent or food for the duration of the trial."

Adrian looked at the number one more time. Four thousand five hundred dollars. It was enough to eat. It was enough to keep the lights on. It was enough to breathe.

He looked at the other men in the waiting area. One of them, a man in a gray flannel shirt, was checking his watch. He looked tired. He looked like he had been doing this for a while. He looked like he had decided that dignity was a luxury he could no longer afford.

Adrian felt a wave of nausea roll through his stomach, a physical rejection of the situation that was completely at odds with the logical part of his brain that was already calculating how to pay the electric bill. He pushed the nausea down, swallowing it along with the dry air.

"Okay," Adrian said.

David waited, his pen poised over a notepad.

"Show me the next step," Adrian said.

David’s lips curved into a thin, professional smile. It was the smile of a gatekeeper who had just watched a lock click open. He turned the tablet around, the screen now displaying a blank form titled *Participant Enrollment Agreement.*

"Let’s get you started," David said.
