The clinic’s exterior was a slab of beige concrete that seemed to absorb the last of the evening light. Adrian stood on the sidewalk for a moment, letting the cool air settle against his flushed skin. The headache behind his eyes had evolved from a dull throb into a sharp, vibrating pressure, as if his skull were a drum skin being tapped from the inside. His hands, tucked deep into the pockets of his jacket, were trembling. He could feel the tremor in his jaw, a fine, rhythmic shudder that made his teeth ache.

He checked the time. 6:42 PM. He had thirty-eight minutes until the window closed for late check-ins, a detail the intake coordinator had emphasized with a patience that felt almost predatory. He had to be calm. He had to be normal. He had to be a nineteen-year-old college student who worked nights at a diner and was here for a routine sample collection, nothing more.

Adrian stepped off the curb and crossed the street. The asphalt was dry, the city noise a low, constant hum of traffic and distant conversation. He moved with a deliberate, measured pace, his eyes fixed on the revolving doors ahead. Every step sent a jolt of static electricity through his nervous system, a side effect he had learned to manage by focusing on the sensation of his feet against the soles of his shoes. *Left, right. Left, right.* The rhythm was a anchor.

As he approached the entrance, the automatic doors slid open with a soft hiss. The air conditioning hit him like a wall of cold, dry air. It was too cold. The contrast between the humid evening outside and the sterile chill inside made his skin prickle, a heightened sensitivity that made every hair on his arms stand up. He shivered, a full-body convulsion that he suppressed by clenching his fists.

Inside, the intake office was a small, windowless room with white walls and a long, curved counter. The lighting was fluorescent, buzzing faintly at a frequency Adrian could almost hear. Behind the counter sat the receptionist. She had gray eyes and a face that seemed to be permanently set in an expression of polite neutrality. She was looking at her computer screen, her fingers moving rapidly over the keyboard.

Adrian approached the counter. He placed his hand on the surface, grounding himself. The laminate was cool and smooth.

"Name?" she asked. She didn't look up.

"Adrian Vale," he said. His voice came out steady. He was pleased with himself. He could hear the faint tremor in his chest, but his voice was solid.

The receptionist typed the name. A second later, she looked up. Her eyes swept over him, a quick, clinical assessment that felt invasive. She was looking for signs of distress. She was looking for a reason to flag him.

"Check-in time is 6:45," she said. "You’re on the late list. Nurse will be out in a moment."

"Thank you," Adrian said.

He stepped back from the counter. His legs felt heavy, as if he were wading through water. The fluorescent lights above the waiting area were too bright. The shadows in the corners of the room seemed to stretch and warp at the edges of his vision. He blinked, trying to clear the blur that was creeping in at the periphery.

He sat down on a plastic chair in the waiting area. The chair was hard and cold. He leaned forward, resting his elbows on his knees. He closed his eyes. He needed to focus. He needed to remember the words. *No side effects. No dizziness. No nausea.* Just a routine. Just a job.

The door to the waiting area opened. A nurse walked in. She was wearing a blue uniform that looked clean and sterile. Her hair was pulled back in a tight bun. She carried a clipboard.

"Vale?" she called out.

Adrian stood up. His knees buckled slightly. He caught himself on the armrest of the chair. He waited for the dizziness to subside. It didn’t. It lingered, a faint, rocking sensation, like being on a boat in rough seas.

"Ready to go?" the nurse asked. She was looking at him with a curious, appraising gaze.

"Yes," Adrian said. He smiled. It was a small, tight smile. "I’m ready."

The nurse nodded. She turned and walked toward the hallway. Adrian followed her. The hallway was long and narrow, lined with doors that had no numbers, only small, frosted windows. The floor was polished tile, reflecting the overhead lights. Adrian’s footsteps echoed softly.

They stopped at a door on the left. The nurse opened it.

"Room 4," she said. "Dr. Thorne will be with you shortly. Please, fill out the daily status form while you wait."

She handed him a clipboard. Adrian took it. His hands were shaking again. He could feel the tremor in his fingers as he held the pen. He looked at the form. The words on the page swam before his eyes. *How are you feeling today? Any pain? Any dizziness?*

He stared at the first question. *How are you feeling today?*

The answer was obvious. He was feeling terrible. He was feeling like his body was a machine that was slowly breaking down, gears slipping, oil leaking. But he couldn’t write that. He couldn’t admit that he was trembling. He couldn’t admit that his vision was blurry. He couldn’t admit that he was terrified.

Adrian looked at the nurse. She was watching him. Her eyes were sharp, intelligent. She was waiting.

"Is there a problem?" she asked.

"No," Adrian said. He shook his head. "No, I’m fine. Just a little tired."

The nurse’s gaze lingered on him for a second longer than necessary. Then she nodded.

"Okay," she said. "Take your time."

She left the room and closed the door behind her.

Adrian was alone.

He looked around the room. It was small and spartan. There was a desk, a chair, and a red emergency call button on the desktop. The walls were white. The air was still.

He sat down in the chair opposite the desk. He placed the clipboard on his lap. He picked up the pen. His hand was steady now, but only because he was holding it tightly, his knuckles white.

He looked at the first question again.

*How are you feeling today?*

Adrian wrote: *Fine.*

He moved to the next question.

*Any pain?*

He wrote: *No.*

*Any dizziness?*

He wrote: *No.*

*Any nausea?*

He wrote: *No.*

He filled out the rest of the form with the same mechanical precision. *No side effects. No changes in appetite. No changes in sleep.*

He set the clipboard on the desk. He looked at the red emergency button. It was bright, almost aggressive in its color. He didn’t touch it. He didn’t need it. He was fine.

The door opened.

Dr. Thorne walked in. She was tall, lean, with a dark, sleek bob haircut. Her eyes were pale, almost colorless. She carried a tablet.

"Hello, Adrian," she said. Her voice was calm, professional. "How are we feeling today?"

Adrian looked up. He forced a smile.

"Good," he said. "Thanks to you."

Dr. Thorne nodded. She looked at the form on the desk. She picked it up, scanned it, and set it back down.

"Good," she said. "Let’s get started."

She sat down at the desk. She opened her tablet.

"The protocol is the same as yesterday," she said. "Sample collection, then medication administration. Do you have any questions?"

Adrian shook his head.

"No," he said. "No questions."

Dr. Thorne nodded. She looked at him, her pale eyes steady and unblinking.

"Good," she said. "Then let’s begin."
