The walk to the clinic took twenty minutes, a distance that felt twice as long as usual. Adrian’s legs felt like they were filled with wet sand, each step a deliberate negotiation with gravity. The city lights smeared into long, jagged streaks as his vision blurred, the headache behind his eyes pulsing in time with his heartbeat. He kept his hands shoved deep into the pockets of his jacket, his fingers curled tight to hide the tremors that had started in his wrists and now seemed to have migrated into his elbows.

When he reached the building, the glass doors swished open with a soft hydraulic hiss. The lobby was brightly lit, the fluorescent lights humming with a frequency that made his teeth ache. The air conditioning blasted cold air against his face, and he flinched, rubbing his neck where the skin felt tight and raw.

The receptionist was already seated at the desk, her gray eyes scanning a screen as she typed. She looked up as Adrian approached, offering a polite, professional smile that didn’t quite reach her eyes.

"Good evening, Mr. Vale. You’re right on time."

"Good evening," Adrian said. His voice sounded thin in his own ears, stripped of its usual low resonance. He forced a smile, keeping his shoulders relaxed, his posture straight. He had to be careful. If she saw the shake in his hands, or the way his eyes darted around the room, she might ask questions. And questions meant forms. And forms meant delays. And delays meant the possibility that Dr. Thorne would notice.

"Check-in is a bit different today," she said, pulling a tablet from the desk. "We’re updating the participant database. Can you confirm your name and date of birth for me?"

"Adrian Vale. August twelfth, two thousand and four," he recited, the words coming out in a steady, mechanical rhythm. It was a script he had memorized, a shield against the fog that was starting to drift into the edges of his thoughts.

The receptionist typed the information, her fingers moving quickly over the glass surface. "And you’re here for your scheduled dosage and sample collection?"

"Yes," Adrian said.

"Perfect. Dr. Thorne is expecting you in Room 4. Nurse Miller is waiting to escort you."

A nurse, whom Adrian assumed was Miller, stood up from a nearby chair. She was a woman in her thirties with a kind face and a clipboard tucked under her arm. She walked around the desk and moved to stand beside Adrian, her eyes flicking over him with a quick, clinical assessment.

"Mr. Vale. How are you feeling today?"

Adrian’s stomach clenched. This was the moment. He could feel the tremors in his hands intensifying, a subtle vibration that threatened to betray him. He kept his hands in his pockets, his thumbs rubbing against the fabric of his jacket.

"Fine," he said. "Just a bit tired."

"Tired is common," the nurse said, her tone softening slightly. "The medication can be demanding. Did you eat a full meal before coming in?"

"I had a sandwich," Adrian lied. He hadn’t eaten anything since a stale piece of bread he’d found in his bag that morning. His stomach was a hollow, aching void, but admitting hunger might lead to an offer of food he couldn’t accept, or a comment on his pale complexion. He needed to be efficient. He needed to be compliant.

"Alright. Let’s get you to Dr. Thorne."

The nurse led him down a hallway lined with white walls and closed doors. The floor was polished linoleum, reflecting the overhead lights in a way that made Adrian’s eyes water. He focused on the small of the nurse’s back, on the way her clipboard moved with each step, anchoring himself to the physical reality of the world.

They stopped in front of a door marked *Room 4*. The nurse knocked, waited for a muffled response, and pushed the door open.

Dr. Thorne was sitting at her desk, reviewing a stack of papers. She looked up as they entered, her pale eyes sweeping over Adrian. She was tall, lean, and moved with a precise, athletic grace that made her seem almost out of place in the cluttered office. Her dark bob was cut sharp, framing her face in a way that emphasized her high cheekbones and the intensity of her gaze.

"Mr. Vale," she said. Her voice was calm, devoid of warmth or coldness. It was the voice of a machine, efficient and accurate. "Come in."

The nurse followed him into the room, closing the door behind them with a soft click. The room was small, dominated by a large desk and a examination table. The red emergency button on the desk was a bright, aggressive spot of color in the otherwise neutral palette of the room. Adrian’s eyes flicked to it, then away. He knew what it was for. He knew he wasn’t going to use it.

"Sit down," Dr. Thorne said, gesturing to the chair opposite her desk.

Adrian sat, keeping his hands in his lap, his fingers interlaced to keep them still. He could feel the heat rising in his face, a flush that he tried to ignore. The air in the room was cool, but he felt like he was sweating.

"Let’s begin the intake for today," Dr. Thorne said. She turned to the nurse. "Miller, please administer the daily dosage."

The nurse nodded and moved to a cabinet on the side of the room. She opened it, took out a small vial and a syringe, and turned to Adrian.

"Roll up your sleeve, please."

Adrian unbuttoned the cuff of his jacket and rolled up the sleeve of his shirt. His arm looked thin, the veins visible beneath the skin. He extended it onto the padded armrest, keeping his gaze fixed on a point on the wall behind Dr. Thorne. He couldn’t look at the needle. He couldn’t look at his own arm. He focused on the texture of the wallpaper, a plain, beige pattern that seemed to blur at the edges.

The nurse prepared the injection, her movements quick and practiced. Adrian felt the cold pinch of the alcohol wipe, followed by the sharp, stinging pain of the needle. He held his breath, his muscles tensing. The medication entered his bloodstream, a cold fire that spread out from the injection site.

"Done," the nurse said. She applied a small bandage to the site and discarded the syringe in a sharps container.

"Thank you," Adrian said. His voice was steady, even if his hands were shaking beneath the bandage.

Dr. Thorne picked up a pen and began to write on a form. "Any side effects since your last visit? Nausea, fatigue, changes in mood?"

Adrian took a deep breath. The headache was worse now, a tight band around his forehead. His vision was swimming, the room tilting slightly to the left. He could feel the tremors in his hands, a constant, low-level vibration that he had to fight to suppress.

"No," he said.

Dr. Thorne looked up at him, her pale eyes searching his face. She held his gaze for a second, then two. Adrian didn’t blink. He kept his expression neutral, his face a mask of calm compliance.

"Are you sure?" she asked. Her voice was flat, but there was a note of skepticism in it. "I’ve noticed your heart rate has been elevated in previous readings. And your skin sensitivity."

Adrian’s throat tightened. He could feel the truth pressing against the back of his teeth, desperate to get out. He wanted to tell her that he couldn’t see clearly. He wanted to tell her that his hands wouldn’t stop shaking. He wanted to tell her that he felt like he was falling apart, piece by piece, in a room that was too quiet and too bright.

But he couldn’t. If he told her, they would remove him. They would take away the money. He would lose his apartment. He would fail his classes. He would starve.

"No," he said again. "I’m fine."

Dr. Thorne studied him for a moment longer, then turned back to her form. She wrote something down, her pen scratching against the paper.

"Understood," she said. "Miller, please prepare the specimen collection kit."

The nurse moved to a counter on the side of the room and began to unpack a sterile kit. Adrian watched her, his mind drifting. He tried to focus on the task at hand, on the routine of the procedure, but his thoughts were scattered, jumping from one thing to another without any logical connection.

He thought about the library. He thought about the diner. He thought about the empty apartment waiting for him, with its peeling paint and its cold stove.

He thought about the money.

"Mr. Vale?"

Adrian blinked, snapping back to the present. Dr. Thorne was looking at him, her expression unreadable.

"I’m sorry," Adrian said. "I was thinking about something else."

Dr. Thorne’s eyes narrowed slightly. "Are you having difficulty concentrating?"

Adrian felt a cold spike of panic in his chest. He had to be careful. He had to be perfect.

"No," he said. "I’m fine. I’m ready."

Dr. Thorne nodded, though her expression remained skeptical. "Very well. Please proceed."
