The walk to the clinic was a negotiation with his own body.

Adrian moved through the streets with a deliberate, shuffling gait that masked the tremors seizing his calves. The evening air, which should have felt cool against his skin, registered as a series of abrasive textures against his exposed forearms. Every step sent a dull throb through his temples, a rhythmic pulse that matched the erratic hammering of his heart. He kept his eyes fixed on the sidewalk, tracing the cracks in the concrete to anchor his drifting focus. The city lights smeared into halos of orange and white, forcing him to blink repeatedly to clear the afterimages.

His hands shook as he reached for the clinic’s entry door. The metal handle was cold, and the sensation of the chill biting into his fingertips was almost painful. He wrapped his fingers around it, applying steady pressure until the shivering in his arms subsided enough to turn the lock. The mechanism clicked with a sound that seemed too loud in the quiet evening.

Inside, the lobby was warm and sterile. The fluorescent lights buzzed with a low hum that Adrian could feel in his teeth. The air smelled of antiseptic and old paper, a scent that had become as familiar and comforting to him as the smell of coffee at the diner. He straightened his jacket, smoothing down the front to hide the fact that his movements were jerky and uncoordinated.

The receptionist was seated behind the high desk, a stack of files neatly arranged in front of her. She looked up as he entered, her gray eyes scanning him with the practiced efficiency of someone who had seen a hundred tired, hungry young men walk through this door.

"Adrian Vale," she said. It was a statement, not a question. She did not wait for him to confirm his identity. Her pen moved across a clipboard, the scratch of the nib audible in the stillness. "You're late by four minutes. The nurse is expecting you in Room 4."

"I was stuck in traffic," Adrian said. The lie came easily, a smooth surface over the jagged reality of his exhaustion. He had walked here, his legs burning with a lactic acid buildup that made every stride feel like climbing a hill. But traffic was a reasonable excuse. Walking while shaking was not.

The receptionist raised an eyebrow, her gaze lingering on him for a fraction of a second longer than usual. Adrian held her stare, keeping his face neutral, his breathing shallow and controlled. He forced his shoulders to relax, unclenching the jaw that had been tight for the last hour.

"Room 4 is down the hall, left," she said, her tone remaining flat and professional. "Nurse Miller will be waiting."

Adrian nodded, his throat dry. He turned and walked toward the corridor. The hallway was narrow, the walls lined with posters about healthy lifestyles and clinical protocols that seemed grotesquely out of place in the context of his own deteriorating condition. He kept his pace steady, avoiding the windows that reflected his pale, haggard face.

He reached the door to Room 4 and pushed it open. The room was small, dominated by the examination table and the desk where Dr. Thorne usually sat. But tonight, the doctor was absent. Instead, a nurse stood by the counter, a clipboard in her hand. She was a woman of average height with dark hair pulled back in a severe bun. Her expression was unreadable, her eyes fixed on the door as if she had been expecting him to arrive at that exact second.

"Adrian," she said, her voice soft but firm. "I'm Nurse Miller. Dr. Thorne is running a bit late. We need to get your intake check done before she arrives."

Adrian stepped into the room, closing the door behind him. The latch clicked shut, sealing him in with the sterile air and the quiet hum of the ventilation system. He felt a spike of anxiety, a tightness in his chest that made it hard to breathe. He pressed his hands against his thighs, forcing them to stop shaking.

"I'm fine," he said, though the words felt hollow in his mouth. "Just a bit tired."

Nurse Miller nodded, uncapping a pen. "Let's start with the standard vitals. Blood pressure, heart rate, temperature. Can you roll up your sleeve?"

Adrian rolled up the left sleeve of his jacket, exposing the thin, pale skin of his forearm. The nurse took a moment to wrap the cuff around his arm, the rubber pressing against his skin with a gentle, constricting pressure. She began to inflate the cuff, the air hissing softly.

Adrian watched the digital display on the monitor. The numbers climbed, then stabilized. His heart rate was elevated, hovering in a range that indicated stress or physical exertion. He knew what she was seeing. He could feel the blood pounding in his ears, a loud, intrusive rhythm that threatened to drown out the ambient noise of the clinic.

"Your heart rate is a little high," Nurse Miller observed, her tone neutral. She marked something on her clipboard. "Have you been exercising today?"

Adrian hesitated. Lying was becoming difficult when the evidence of his condition was written in his own biology. "I was walking a lot," he said. It was the truth, if twisted. He had walked to the library, walked to the diner, and walked here. The distance was significant, and his body was reacting to the accumulated strain.

Nurse Miller studied him for a moment, her eyes searching his face for signs of deception. She found none, or chose to believe the plausible explanation he offered. She removed the cuff from his arm and checked his temperature with a digital thermometer. The device beeped softly, indicating a normal reading.

"Your temperature is fine," she said. "Now, I need to check your reflexes and neurological function. This is standard procedure for the trial, given the nature of the compound."

Adrian felt a cold knot form in his stomach. He knew what was coming. The compound was designed to stimulate reproductive cell production, but its interaction with his nervous system was unpredictable. The tremors in his hands were a clear sign that something was wrong, that the drug was affecting more than just his reproductive organs.

He extended his hand, palm up. Nurse Miller held a small reflex hammer and tapped his knee. His leg jerked, a strong, uncontrolled movement that made him wince. She repeated the test, tapping his bicep. The arm flinched, a reaction that was slightly exaggerated, his muscles tense and ready to react.

"Your reflexes are a bit heightened," she noted, her pen moving across the paper. "And I'm noticing some fine motor tremors. Is this something new?"

Adrian looked at his hand. It was steady now, but the memory of the shaking was still there, a ghost of movement that lingered in his muscles. He forced his fingers to relax, one by one, until his hand lay flat and still on the table.

"It's just stress," he said, his voice steady. "I've been working a lot."

Nurse Miller watched him for a long moment, her expression unreadable. She did not push the point, but she did not dismiss it either. She marked the observations on her clipboard, her handwriting neat and precise.

"Dr. Thorne should be here any minute," she said, standing up and gathering her equipment. "I'll leave you to rest. Try to keep your heart rate down."

Adrian nodded, his throat dry. He sat on the edge of the examination table, his hands clasped tightly together to hide the tremors that were beginning to creep back in. The room was quiet, the only sound the distant hum of the air conditioning and the faint ticking of a clock on the wall.

He closed his eyes, trying to steady his breathing. The headache was getting worse, a dull ache that pulsed behind his eyes. He could feel the medication coursing through his system, a warm, heavy presence that seemed to weigh down his limbs. He did not know if this was a side effect, a symptom of withdrawal, or the beginning of something more serious.

He did not have time to find out. He had to keep the payment flowing. He had to keep the trial running.

The door to Room 4 opened, and the sound of heels clicking on the tile floor announced Dr. Thorne's arrival.

Adrian opened his eyes, his face a mask of calm composure. He stood up, straightening his jacket, and turned to face the doctor.

"Dr. Thorne," he said, his voice clear and steady. "I'm ready for the next step."
