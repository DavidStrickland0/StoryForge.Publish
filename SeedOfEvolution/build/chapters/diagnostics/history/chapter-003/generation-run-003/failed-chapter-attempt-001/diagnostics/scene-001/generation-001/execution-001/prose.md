The walk to the clinic took twenty minutes, a duration that felt less like a transit time and more like a slow erosion. Adrian’s legs, already trembling from the diner shift, threatened to buckle with every few steps. The evening air, cool and damp, clung to his skin with an unpleasant intimacy; the fabric of his shirt felt too tight, the collar a rough band around his neck. He kept his eyes fixed on the pavement, counting his strides to keep the world from blurring into a smear of gray and amber light.

When he reached the clinic, the building stood quiet under the streetlights. It was a nondescript structure, beige brick and large glass windows that reflected the empty street rather than revealing the interior. The sign above the door read *Helix Bio-Research*, the letters clean and sterile. Adrian pushed through the glass door, the automatic sensor chiming with a polite, indifferent beep.

The intake office was bright, lit by fluorescent panels that hummed at a low frequency Adrian felt in his teeth. The air smelled of antiseptic and cold coffee. A receptionist sat behind a curved desk, her gray eyes scanning a computer screen. She looked up as he approached, her gaze flicking over his disheveled hair, his pale face, and the bag slung over his shoulder.

"Adrian Vale," she said. It wasn’t a question. Her voice was flat, practiced. "You’re checking in for the 8:00 AM slot?"

Adrian froze. He looked at the clock on the wall. It was 7:58 AM. He had misread the time on his watch, or perhaps his watch had stopped, or perhaps the last twenty minutes had slipped away in a fog of exhaustion. He had walked here in the dark, in the rain, and arrived before the scheduled start time.

"I... yes," he managed, his voice sounding thin in the quiet office. "I’m here for the 8:00 AM appointment."

The receptionist’s expression didn’t change. She didn’t seem surprised that he was early, or that he looked like he hadn’t slept, or that his hands were shaking slightly as he fumbled with the strap of his bag. She simply turned back to her screen and tapped a few keys.

"Please have a seat," she said, gesturing to the row of plastic chairs against the far wall. "Dr. Thorne will be with you shortly."

Adrian walked to the chairs. His knees ached. He sat down, lowering himself slowly to avoid the jolt that would surely send a spike of pain through his lower back. The chair was hard, the plastic cold against his skin through his thin trousers.

The waiting area was sparse. A magazine rack held three issues of a health journal, all from the previous month. A small potted plant sat on a side table, its leaves yellowing at the edges. The fluorescent lights buzzed. Adrian closed his eyes for a moment, trying to slow his heart rate, but the effort only made his vision swim. He opened his eyes and stared at the wall, where a framed poster displayed a diagram of the human reproductive system. The red and blue lines were stark, clinical, and utterly impersonal.

He thought about the emergency-intervention authorization. He had mentioned it to the receptionist during his first visit, vaguely, as part of the paperwork. He had assumed it was another form, another signature, another checkbox. But the receptionist had looked at him with a strange, unreadable expression when he’d asked about it. She had said, "That’s handled by Dr. Thorne directly. It’s a specific protocol."

He had signed the initial consent form, the one that waived his right to sue, the one that granted them access to his biological data, the one that bound him to daily visits. That form had been long, dense with legal jargon, and he had signed it with a steady hand, despite the tremor in his fingers. He had signed it because he needed the money. He had signed it because he had no other choice.

But this new document, the one he hadn’t seen yet, felt different. It felt heavier. He imagined it lying on Dr. Thorne’s desk, a single sheet of paper with a line at the bottom, waiting for his signature. He imagined the weight of that line. He imagined the consequences of signing it.

The door to the exam room at the end of the hallway opened. A nurse stepped out, pushing a cart with medical supplies. She glanced at Adrian, then looked away. Her eyes were quick, clinical, and already moving on to the next task. She disappeared into the hallway, leaving behind the faint smell of latex and disinfectant.

Adrian sat in the chair, his hands resting on his lap. He could feel the vibration of the fluorescent lights in his bones. He could feel the hunger in his stomach, a dull, persistent ache that had grown since he’d left the diner. He could feel the fatigue in his muscles, a heavy, leaden weight that made every movement feel like an effort.

He was waiting. He was waiting for Dr. Thorne. He was waiting for the paper. He was waiting for the end of his freedom.

The clock on the wall ticked.

The receptionist’s voice came from behind the desk, calm and distant. "Adrian? Dr. Thorne is ready for you."

He stood up. His legs felt unsteady, but he forced himself to walk. He crossed the room, the plastic chair scraping slightly against the floor as he moved past it. He paused at the door to the exam room, his hand hovering over the handle.

He took a breath.

He pushed the door open.

The room was small, with white walls and a single window that looked out onto a brick alley. A desk sat in the center, and behind it sat Dr. Aris Thorne. She was tall, lean, her dark bob cut neatly, her pale eyes fixed on a folder in front of her. She didn’t look up as he entered.

"Take a seat," she said.

Adrian sat in the chair across from her. He placed his bag on the floor beside him. He looked at the folder on her desk. He could see the edge of the paper, the crisp white corner peeking out from under the manila cover.

Dr. Thorne closed the folder. She leaned forward, her elbows resting on the desk. Her expression was neutral, professional. She looked at Adrian, then at the folder, then back at Adrian.

"Adrian," she said. "I have a document for you to review and sign."

She opened the folder and slid a single sheet of paper across the desk.

Adrian looked at the paper. He looked at Dr. Thorne. He looked at the paper again.

He reached out and took the paper.
