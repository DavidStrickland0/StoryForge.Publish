# Chapter 2: The Daily Grind

Adrian's alarm went off at 6:30 AM. He'd set it early to give himself time to eat before his first testing session, but his stomach was already cramping with hunger. He'd skipped dinner the night before—no point in wasting money on food he'd have to avoid before the test.

He stood in front of his bathroom mirror, studying his reflection. The fluorescent light was unkind, highlighting the dark circles under his eyes and the sharpness of his cheekbones. He looked thin. Sicker than he felt, maybe. The exhaustion was constant now, a weight he carried everywhere.

But he'd made it through the first night. One night down, who knew how many to go.

He dressed in his cleanest clothes—a faded t-shirt and jeans that hung loose on his frame—and grabbed his backpack. The four hundred-dollar bills were still inside, tucked into a side pocket. He'd counted them three times the night before, as if the number might change if he looked hard enough.

Four hundred dollars. Enough for rent. Enough to survive.

He left his apartment and started the walk to campus. The morning air was cold, and he could see his breath misting in front of him. His stomach cramped again, a sharp reminder that he hadn't eaten. But he'd made it this far on empty. He could make it a little further.

The campus was quiet at this hour, just a few students making their way to early classes. Adrian passed the library, the gym, the student union building. Familiar landmarks that felt different now. He was part of something else, something that had nothing to do with his classes or his studies.

He was a data point. A specimen.

The research hospital loomed ahead, a modern glass and steel building that stood out among the older campus architecture. Adrian had passed it a hundred times before, never thinking he'd have a reason to go inside. Now it was the center of his universe, the place where he'd spend his mornings for the foreseeable future.

He pushed through the revolving doors and stepped into the lobby. The space was all white tile and soft lighting, designed to be calming but somehow making him feel more exposed. A receptionist sat behind a curved desk, typing on her computer. She glanced up as he approached.

"Can I help you?" she asked.

"I'm here for the experimental medicine program," Adrian said. "Specimen collection. Room three."

She nodded, her expression neutral. "Take the elevator to the third floor. The collection room is at the end of the hall, on your left."

"Thank you."

He took the elevator up, his heart pounding. The car was silent except for the soft hum of the motor and the numbers lighting up above the door. Third floor. Room three. The end of the hall.

The elevator doors opened, and Adrian stepped out into a narrow corridor. The walls were painted a pale blue, the kind of color that was supposed to be soothing but just felt cold. He walked past several closed doors, each one labeled with a number. Room one. Room two. And then, at the end of the hall, Room three.

He stood in front of the door, his hand hovering over the handle. This was it. The moment he'd been dreading. The moment he'd signed away his dignity for four hundred dollars.

He took a breath and pushed the door open.

The collection room was small, barely large enough for a table and two chairs. The walls were the same pale blue as the hallway, and the fluorescent lights overhead were harsh, bright enough to make him squint. In the center of the room was a counter with a sink, and next to it, a small refrigerator.

A woman sat in one of the chairs, typing on a tablet. She looked up as he entered, her expression professional and detached.

"You must be Adrian Vale," she said. "I'm Nurse Elena. I'll be conducting your testing today."

"Hi," Adrian said, his voice coming out smaller than he intended.

"Please, have a seat." She gestured to the empty chair. "Before we begin, I need to confirm a few things. You've fasted since midnight, correct? Nothing to eat or drink?"

"Yes."

"And you've taken your medication?"

Adrian pulled the small bottle from his backpack. "Here."

Elena took it, examined the label, and set it aside. "Good. Now, I need to explain the procedure. It's straightforward, but I want to make sure you understand what we're doing. We're collecting a semen sample for analysis. You'll be given a specimen cup, and you'll provide the sample in the collection room next door."

She stood and walked to the refrigerator, pulling out a small plastic cup. "This is sterile. Once you've provided the sample, seal it carefully and bring it back to me. I'll need to check the volume and label it."

Adrian took the cup, his hands trembling slightly. The plastic was cold, the container somehow both insignificant and momentous in his palms.

"Is there anything else you need to know?" Elena asked.

"No," Adrian said. "I understand."

"Excellent. The collection room is through that door." She pointed to a second door on the opposite side of the room. "Take all the time you need. When you're finished, bring the sample back here."

Adrian nodded and walked to the collection room. The door closed behind him with a soft click, leaving him alone in the small space.

The room was even smaller than the testing room, barely large enough for a chair and a small table. The walls were painted the same pale blue, and the lighting was just as harsh. In the center of the table was a small monitor, displaying a nature scene—a forest, with trees swaying in the wind.

Adrian sat down, the plastic cup in his lap. He stared at it, his mind racing. This was what he'd signed up for. This was the price of survival. The humiliation, the invasion of privacy, the complete loss of dignity.

But he'd made his choice. He needed the money. He needed to survive.

He closed his eyes and tried to focus, but his thoughts kept drifting. The language in the consent form, the careful clinical terms that made the invasive requirements sound routine. The way Thorne had smiled, that small professional expression that didn't reach his eyes. The four hundred dollars in his backpack, the only thing standing between him and homelessness.

He opened his eyes and looked at the cup again. His hands were still trembling.

*Just get it over with*, he thought.

He tried to relax, to focus on the nature scene on the monitor, but the images only made him feel more exposed. The trees, the wind, the endless forest. All of it watched him, judged him, saw him for what he was.

A desperate kid. A specimen. A data point.

Minutes passed. Five. Ten. Fifteen. His body was tense, resistant, refusing to cooperate. The longer he sat there, the more humiliating it became. He was failing at the one thing he'd promised to do, the one requirement that earned his money.

He tried again, forcing himself to relax, but his mind kept wandering. He thought about his classes, his studies, his future. He thought about the other students, who'd never have to do this, who'd never have to choose between dignity and survival. He thought about his parents, who'd given up on him years ago, who'd probably be relieved to know he was finally taking care of himself.

But mostly, he thought about the money. Four hundred dollars. Enough for rent. Enough to survive.

He closed his eyes again and tried to focus, but his thoughts were scattered, fragmented, impossible to control. His stomach cramped, a sharp reminder of his hunger. His head ached. His body felt heavy, exhausted, like it was made of lead.

And then, finally, after what felt like an eternity, it happened.

He opened his eyes, his body trembling with relief and exhaustion. He sealed the cup carefully, his hands still shaking, and stood. The sample was smaller than he'd hoped, barely enough to fill the container, but it was done. He'd completed the first requirement.

He walked back to the testing room, the cup in his hand, and pushed open the door.

Elena looked up from her tablet, her expression unchanged. "All done?"

"Yes."

She took the cup, examined it briefly, and set it aside. "Excellent. That's the first step. Now I need to do a quick physical examination and collect some baseline data. This shouldn't take long."

She pulled out a stethoscope and blood pressure cuff from a drawer. "Let's start with vitals."

Adrian sat back down, his body still trembling slightly. Elena wrapped the cuff around his arm, inflated it, and listened to his heartbeat. She made a few notes on her tablet, her expression neutral.

"Your blood pressure is a bit low," she said. "That's consistent with your weight and activity level. We'll monitor it."

She set aside the stethoscope and pulled out a thermometer. "Temperature next."

Adrian opened his mouth, and she slid the thermometer under his tongue. He sat there, silent, while she waited for the reading. After a moment, she pulled it out and checked the display.

"Normal. Good." She made another note. "Now, I need to ask you a few questions. Have you experienced any side effects from the medication? Nausea, dizziness, headache?"

"No."

"Any changes in your mood or sleep patterns?"

"No."

"Good. That's all for now. You're free to go. Your next appointment is tomorrow at 9 AM. Same time, same procedure."

Adrian nodded and stood, his legs unsteady. He felt exhausted, hollowed out, like he'd just run a marathon. The testing had taken less than an hour, but it had drained him completely.

"Thank you," he said, his voice barely a whisper.

Elena smiled, a small, professional expression that didn't reach her eyes. "You did well. See you tomorrow."

Adrian left the room, walked down the hallway, and took the elevator back to the lobby. The morning was still young, the sun barely above the horizon, but he felt like he'd lived a lifetime in the past hour.

He walked home slowly, his body aching, his mind numb. His stomach cramped again, a sharp reminder that he still hadn't eaten. But he'd made it through. He'd completed his first testing session. He'd earned his first payment.

He was one day closer to surviving.

When he got back to his apartment, he collapsed on his bed and closed his eyes. Sleep came quickly, a heavy, dreamless weight that pulled him under. He didn't wake until the afternoon, when his stomach cramps finally drove him out of bed.

He stood in front of his refrigerator, staring at its nearly empty contents. A carton of milk, half-empty. A few slices of bread. A container of oatmeal, the only thing he could afford to eat.

He made the oatmeal, ate it standing at his counter, and tried not to think about the testing room. Tried not to think about the small plastic cup, the harsh fluorescent lights, the way Elena had looked at him like he was a data point rather than a person.

But he couldn't escape it. The routine was established now. The daily requirement was real. And he was bound to it, legally and financially, until he completed the trial or ran out of money.

He washed his bowl, put it away, and sat back down at his counter. The four hundred dollars were still in his backpack, untouched, a lifeline he'd earned by sacrificing his dignity.

He'd survive, he told himself. He'd have to.

But as he sat there, staring at the empty apartment, the silence pressing in around him, he couldn't shake the feeling that he'd just signed away more than he'd bargained for.

The trial had begun. The daily grind had started. And Adrian Vale, broke and exhausted and desperate, was now a specimen in an experiment he barely understood.

Tomorrow, he'd do it again. And the day after that. And the day after that, until the trial ended or his body broke or he found a way out.

He didn't know which would come first.

But he knew he had no choice but to keep going.

---

The afternoon passed slowly. Adrian tried to study, but his mind kept drifting back to the testing room, to the plastic cup, to the way Elena had looked at him. He tried to eat dinner, but his stomach was still cramping, still empty, still demanding more than he could give it.

By evening, he was exhausted again, his body aching from the tension of the morning. He lay in bed, staring at the ceiling, and tried not to think about tomorrow.

But tomorrow was inevitable. Tomorrow was always inevitable.

He closed his eyes and tried to sleep, but restlessness kept him awake. His thoughts were scattered, fragmented, impossible to control. His body was tense, his mind racing, his heart pounding.

And beneath it all, a single thought, persistent and unshakeable:

*What have I done?*

He didn't have an answer. He just knew that he'd made his choice, and now he had to live with it.

One day at a time. One testing session at a time. One humiliating requirement at a time.

He'd survive. He had to.

But as he lay there in the dark, alone and exhausted and terrified, he couldn't shake the feeling that his life had just changed in ways he couldn't yet understand.

The trial had begun.

And Adrian Vale, broke and desperate and alone, was now a part of something much larger than himself.

He just didn't know what that something was yet.
