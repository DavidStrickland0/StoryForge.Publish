## Blocking

Category: PhysicalLogic

The state of the refrigerator contents is contradictory. On Day 2, Adrian eats 'Two eggs' and 'A slice of bread' but leaves 'A carton of milk, half-empty' in the fridge. On Day 3, he eats 'the last of his eggs' and 'the last of his milk'. This implies he had eggs left over from Day 2 (which he ate) and milk left over from Day 2 (which he drank). However, on Day 2, he explicitly did not take the milk ('He didn't take the milk. He needed to make it last.') and ate all the eggs ('He ate them... He ate the bread, too'). Therefore, on Day 3, there should be no eggs left to eat, and the milk should still be half-empty, not 'the last of his milk' (implying it was nearly empty or finished).

Validation: The chapter explicitly states Adrian ate the two eggs and did not take the milk on Day 2. Consequently, on Day 3, there should be no eggs remaining to eat, and the milk should still be in the state it was left in (half-empty), not described as 'the last of his milk' which implies consumption or near-exhaustion. This is a direct contradiction of the established state of the refrigerator contents.
