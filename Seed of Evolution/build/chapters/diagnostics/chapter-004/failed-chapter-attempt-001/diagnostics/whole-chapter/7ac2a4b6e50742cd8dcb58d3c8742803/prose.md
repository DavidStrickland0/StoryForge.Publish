# Chapter 4: The Daily Grind

The clinic was sterile and cold, all white walls and fluorescent lighting that made Adrian's eyes water. He'd arrived twenty minutes early, as instructed, his stomach already churning with a combination of hunger and anxiety. The waiting area smelled of disinfectant and something else—something clinical and impersonal that made him feel small.

He sat in one of the plastic chairs, his backpack on his lap, and tried to calm his breathing. His hands were shaking. They'd been shaking since he woke up that morning, before his shift at the cafeteria, before he'd called in to say he wouldn't be coming in. The trial coordinator had been clear: the daily requirement was non-negotiable. He had to be here, had to take the medication, had to provide the specimen. Every single day. No exceptions.

He'd lost the shift. Another one. He was down two hundred dollars this week, maybe more if they decided to dock him for the notice. But the trial paid better—enough to cover tuition, enough to keep the rent current, enough to eat if he was careful. He just had to survive the routine.

A door opened, and a woman in scrubs stepped out. She was tall, with graying hair pulled back in a tight ponytail, and her expression was professionally neutral, as if she'd perfected the art of looking at people without really seeing them.

"Adrian Vale?" she said, consulting a clipboard.

He stood, his legs feeling unsteady. "Yes."

"I'm Nurse Elena. I'll be handling your daily testing sessions." She gestured toward the hallway behind her. "Follow me, please."

He followed, his footsteps echoing in the empty corridor. She led him to a small room with a table, two chairs, and a cabinet locked with a digital keypad. The walls were bare except for a framed medical diagram of the male reproductive system, which made his face burn.

"Sit," she said, closing the door behind them. "We'll start with the medication."

She pulled a small plastic cup from the cabinet and placed it on the table between them. Inside were two white pills, perfectly identical.

"Take these with water," she said, sliding a second cup—this one filled with tap water—across the table. "They're the fertility treatment. You'll take one dose every morning before your specimen collection. It's important that you take them exactly as directed."

Adrian looked at the pills, his stomach roiling. He thought of the consent forms he'd signed, the dense legal language he'd barely understood, the clauses that said he'd agreed to undergo "experimental treatments" and "invasive procedures" in exchange for payment. He thought of his empty bank account, his overdue rent, his cafeteria job that paid barely enough to eat.

He picked up the cup and swallowed the pills dry, then drank the water. The pills went down easily, but the moment they were in his system, he felt something shift. A warmth spreading through his chest, down his arms, into his stomach. It wasn't painful, but it wasn't comfortable either. It was strange. Wrong.

"Now," Nurse Elena said, her voice still flat and professional, "we'll need to wait about forty-five minutes to allow the medication to circulate. During that time, you'll need to remain in this room. I'll be back to begin the collection process."

She left without waiting for a response, and Adrian was alone with the locked cabinet, the medical diagram, and the growing warmth in his stomach.

He sat there, counting the minutes on his phone, trying not to think about what the pills were doing inside him. The warmth was spreading, creeping up his spine, making his skin feel hot and tight. He was sweating, he realized. His shirt was damp at the collar, at his back.

Forty-five minutes. He had to wait forty-five minutes before he could do what he'd come here to do, what he'd signed up for, what he needed to do every single day to keep the money coming.

He closed his eyes and tried to think of anything else. His classes. His roommate, who was always gone. His part-time job, which he'd just lost. His tuition, which was due in three weeks. His rent, which was already late.

The door opened again.

"Time," Nurse Elena said, stepping back into the room. She was carrying a small metal tray with a specimen cup, a label, and a pen. She placed the tray on the table and looked at him with those professionally neutral eyes. "We'll begin now. Remove your pants and underwear and sit on the examination table."

The examination table. He hadn't noticed it before—a padded surface with stirrups, positioned against the far wall. He stood, his hands fumbling with his belt, and stripped down to his skin. The room was cold, and he was shivering, but the warmth from the pills was still radiating through his core.

He sat on the examination table, his bare feet on the cold floor, and tried not to think about how exposed he was. Nurse Elena didn't look at him, not really. She was focused on the tray, on the specimen cup, on the label she was writing.

"Take this," she said, handing him the cup. It was sterile, perfectly clean, with a lid that snapped on. "When you're ready, provide the specimen. I'll be outside the door. Tap when you're finished."

She left, and Adrian was alone with the cup and the impossible task of performing on demand.

He closed his eyes, tried to relax, tried to think of anything that might help. But the pills were making him feel strange, making his body feel strange. The warmth was spreading, pulsing, making his heart beat faster. He felt light-headed, dizzy, and the room was spinning.

He tried to focus, to force his body to cooperate, but nothing was working. His mind was racing, his stomach was churning, and the warmth was becoming unbearable. He was burning up, he thought. He was on fire from the inside out.

He tried again, and again, but nothing came. He was failing. He was going to fail, and they'd stop paying him, and he'd lose everything, and he'd be back where he started—broke, desperate, drowning.

He opened his eyes, his face burning with shame, and looked at the empty cup in his hands.

"Nurse Elena?" he called out, his voice hoarse. "I... I can't. I don't think I can do this."

The door opened immediately, and she stepped back in, her expression unchanged. She looked at him, at the empty cup, and then at his face.

"It's fine," she said, her voice still professional, still detached. "The medication can sometimes have that effect, especially on the first day. It's normal. We'll try again tomorrow."

She took the empty cup from his hands and placed it back on the tray. "You can get dressed now. We'll schedule your next session for tomorrow morning, same time."

She left, and Adrian was alone again, sitting on the examination table, his body still burning, his mind still racing, and the terrible certainty settling over him that he'd just failed his first test.

He got dressed slowly, his hands still shaking, and left the room. The waiting area was empty, and the receptionist at the front desk was typing at her computer, not looking at him. He walked past her, out the front door, into the cold morning air.

He was halfway home when the nausea hit.

It came out of nowhere, a wave of sickness so intense it made him double over, his hands on his knees, his stomach heaving. He'd skipped breakfast to save money, and the pills were churning in his empty gut, making him feel like he was going to vomit up his own insides.

He made it to a dumpster behind a convenience store, barely, and retched until there was nothing left but bile and the taste of the pills. His body was shaking, his legs were weak, and he was dizzy with exhaustion.

When it was over, he stood there, gasping for breath, and felt the warmth spreading through his body again. It was stronger now, more intense, and it made him feel strange. Different. Wrong.

He walked the rest of the way home in a daze, his mind foggy, his body aching. His apartment was small—a studio with a kitchenette and a bathroom, barely enough room for one person, let alone two. But it was cheap, and it was his, and it was the only place he had to go.

He locked the door behind him and collapsed on the bed, fully clothed, and closed his eyes.

He was still wearing his coat when he woke up four hours later, his mouth dry, his head pounding, and his stomach growling with hunger so intense it felt like pain.

He checked his phone. Three missed calls from the cafeteria manager. Two texts from the trial coordinator, asking if he'd completed his session. One email from his academic advisor, reminding him about his tuition payment deadline.

He ignored them all and stumbled to the kitchenette, where he found a box of discount noodles, the kind that cost less than a dollar and tasted like cardboard. He ate them dry, standing at the counter, and felt the hunger recede just enough to think.

He'd failed. He'd taken the pills, and he'd tried to provide the specimen, and he'd failed. What was he going to do tomorrow? How was he going to make this work?

He didn't have an answer. He just had the growing certainty that he'd made a terrible mistake, that he'd signed up for something he couldn't handle, and that he was going to lose everything he'd fought so hard to keep.

His phone buzzed. Another text from the trial coordinator.

*Your payment for today's session has been processed. Please confirm receipt.*

He looked at the message, his stomach twisting. They were paying him for a session he'd failed. Why? What did that mean?

He didn't know. He just knew that he was hungry, exhausted, and terrified, and that tomorrow he'd have to go back and try again.

He fell asleep at the kitchenette counter, his head in his arms, and dreamed of burning.

---

The next morning, Adrian woke up to a notification from his bank: *Payment received. Current balance: $1,847.52.*

He stared at the number, his mind struggling to process it. That was more than he'd had in months. That was enough to cover his rent, his tuition, his food for the next few weeks. That was enough to survive.

But it was also a reminder that he'd failed. He'd taken the pills, and he'd tried, and he'd failed, and they were still paying him.

He didn't understand. He didn't know what that meant, or what it meant for tomorrow, or what it meant for the rest of the trial.

He just knew that he was alive, and he was hungry, and he had to figure out how to make this work.

He ate two more boxes of discount noodles for breakfast, then another for lunch, and still felt like he could eat more. The hunger was relentless, a gnawing emptiness in his stomach that the cheap noodles couldn't fill. He was burning through calories at an impossible rate, his body consuming fuel faster than he could replace it.

By the time he walked back to the clinic, his stomach was growling again, and his body was burning with that strange warmth. He felt different. Lighter. Stronger. But also strange, like something inside him had changed.

He arrived early, as instructed, and Nurse Elena was waiting for him in the same small room with the same locked cabinet. Her expression was professionally neutral, as if she'd never seen him before, as if yesterday had never happened.

"Good morning," she said, consulting her clipboard. "We'll begin with the medication."

She pulled out the same plastic cup, the same two white pills, the same tap water. Adrian looked at them, his stomach churning, and tried to think of any way out of this.

There was none. He had to take them. He had to try again. He had to make this work.

He swallowed the pills dry and drank the water, and the warmth spread through his body, stronger than before, more intense. He was burning up, he thought. He was on fire from the inside out.

"Forty-five minutes," Nurse Elena said, leaving him alone in the room.

He sat there, counting the minutes, and felt the warmth spreading through his body. It was pulsing now, a steady rhythm that matched his heartbeat, making him feel alive in a way he hadn't felt before. He was hot, burning, but it wasn't painful. It was strange. Wrong. But also strangely right.

When the forty-five minutes were up, Nurse Elena returned with the specimen tray.

"Remove your pants and underwear," she said, her voice still flat and professional. "We'll begin the collection process."

Adrian stripped again, his hands steady this time, and sat on the examination table. The room was cold, but the warmth from the pills was radiating through his core, making him sweat despite the chill.

He took the cup, closed his eyes, and tried to focus. The warmth was pulsing, steady, and it was making his body feel strange, but also strangely capable. He tried again, and this time, something shifted.

He felt it working, his body responding, the warmth pulsing in time with his heartbeat. And then, after a few long minutes, it was done.

He opened his eyes, his face burning with shame and relief, and looked at the cup in his hands. It was full, barely enough to fill the container, but it was done. He'd completed the requirement.

He tapped on the door, and Nurse Elena stepped back in. She looked at the cup, at the label she'd written, and then at his face.

"Good work," she said, her voice still professional, still detached. "We'll process this sample and schedule your next session for tomorrow morning. Same time."

She took the cup from his hands and placed it back on the tray, then left the room. Adrian got dressed slowly, his hands steady, and left the clinic.

He was walking home when he realized he felt different. Lighter. Stronger. The hunger was still there, gnawing at his stomach, but it was different now. It was sharp, focused, and it made him feel like he could eat the whole world if he had the chance.

He stopped at a convenience store and bought six more boxes of discount noodles, eating three of them standing at the counter. The hunger receded just enough to think, but it was still there, still gnawing, still demanding more.

He walked the rest of the way home in a daze, his mind foggy, his body aching, but also strangely energized. He was tired, exhausted, but he felt like he could keep going, like he could run a marathon if he had to.

When he got to his apartment, he checked his phone. Another payment notification: *Your payment for today's session has been processed. Current balance: $2,197.52.*

He was making money. Real money. Enough to survive, enough to pay his bills, enough to eat.

But he was also changing. He could feel it, the warmth spreading through his body, the hunger gnawing at his stomach, the strange energy coursing through his veins. He didn't know what it meant, or what it meant for tomorrow, or what it meant for the rest of the trial.

He just knew that he was alive, and he was hungry, and he had to figure out how to make this work.

He fell asleep on his bed, fully clothed, and dreamed of burning.

He was faster, he realized. His reflexes were sharper, his movements more precise. He was stronger too, he thought, though he didn't know how he knew. It was just a feeling, a certainty that settled over him like a second skin.

He ate four boxes of discount noodles for breakfast, another four for lunch, and still felt hungry. The hunger was relentless, a gnawing emptiness that the cheap noodles couldn't fill. He was burning through calories at an impossible rate, his body consuming fuel faster than he could replace it.

By the time he walked back to the clinic for the third session, his stomach was growling again, and his body was burning with that strange warmth. He felt different. Lighter. Stronger. But also strange, like something inside him had changed in ways he couldn't explain.

Nurse Elena was waiting for him in the same small room, her expression professionally neutral. She didn't acknowledge his presence, didn't greet him, didn't ask how he was feeling. She just handed him the medication, waited for him to take it, and left him alone in the room.

He sat there, counting the minutes, and felt the warmth spreading through his body. It was pulsing now, a steady rhythm that matched his heartbeat, making him feel alive in a way he hadn't felt before. He was hot, burning, but it wasn't painful. It was strange. Wrong. But also strangely right.

The payments continued, each one a small lifeline keeping him afloat. But the changes were becoming harder to ignore. His appetite was insatiable, his body was burning through calories at an impossible rate, and the warmth was spreading through his veins like a fever that never broke.

He was also getting stronger. He could feel it in his muscles, in his bones, in the way his body moved through space. He was faster, sharper, more coordinated. He felt like he could do anything, like he could survive anything, like he was becoming something more than human.

But he was also exhausted, constantly hungry, and terrified of what was happening to him.

He didn't know what the pills were doing, or why the clinic was paying him to endure this, or what would happen if he tried to quit. He just knew that he was trapped, and that the only way out was to keep going, to keep taking the medication, to keep providing the specimens, to keep surviving.

He was walking home from his fourth session when he saw her.

She was standing outside a convenience store, her face bruised, her clothes torn, and her eyes wild with fear. She looked like she'd been beaten, like she'd been hurt, like she was running from something.

He almost walked past her, almost pretended he hadn't seen her, almost kept his head down and focused on his own problems. But something made him stop. Something made him turn around and look at her.

She met his eyes, and for a moment, they just stared at each other. Then she ran.

He didn't think. He just followed, his body moving on instinct, his legs carrying him faster than he'd ever moved before. He caught up to her two blocks later, grabbing her arm and spinning her around to face him.

"Wait," he said, his voice hoarse. "Are you okay? What happened?"

She looked at him, her eyes still wild, and tried to pull away. But he didn't let go.

"Please," he said, his voice softer now. "I'm not going to hurt you. I just... I want to make sure you're safe."

She stopped struggling, her body going slack in his grip. And then she started crying.

He held her there, in the middle of the street, and let her cry. He didn't know who she was, or what had happened to her, or why he'd stopped to help her. He just knew that he couldn't walk away, couldn't pretend he hadn't seen her, couldn't leave her alone and afraid.

When her tears finally stopped, she pulled away from him, wiping her face with the back of her hand.

"I'm sorry," she said, her voice shaking. "I didn't mean to... I just... I need to go."

She turned to leave, but he grabbed her arm again.

"Wait," he said. "Please. Let me help you. Where are you going? What happened?"

She looked at him, her eyes searching his face, and something in her expression shifted. She seemed to see something in him, something that made her trust him, or at least made her willing to listen.

"My name is Lena," she said, her voice still shaking. "And I'm in trouble."

He didn't know what to say, so he just nodded.

"Can you help me?" she asked, her eyes pleading. "Please? I don't know what to do, and I don't know where to go, and I'm scared, and I—"

"You're safe now," he said, cutting her off. "I'll help you. I promise."

He didn't know why he said it, or how he was going to keep that promise. He didn't have money to spare, didn't have a place big enough for two people, didn't have any way to protect her from whatever danger she was running from.

But he said it anyway, because it was the right thing to do, because she needed to hear it, because he couldn't walk away and leave her alone.

She looked at him for a long moment, her eyes searching his face, and then she nodded.

"Thank you," she said, her voice barely a whisper. "Thank you."

He didn't know what he was getting into, or what it would cost him, or what would happen when the trial discovered he'd missed a session to help a stranger. He just knew that he couldn't walk away, couldn't pretend he hadn't seen her, couldn't leave her alone and afraid.

He was already late for his shift at the cafeteria, already late for his fifth session at the clinic, already in trouble with the trial coordinator. But it didn't matter. He had to help her. He had to make sure she was safe.

He just hoped it wouldn't cost him everything.
