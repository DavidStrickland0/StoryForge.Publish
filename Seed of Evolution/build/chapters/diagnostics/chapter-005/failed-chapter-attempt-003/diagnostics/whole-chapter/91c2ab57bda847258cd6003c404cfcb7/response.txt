# Chapter 5

## Day 2

Adrian's alarm went off at 6:00 AM. He reached over and silenced it, his hand moving with a precision that surprised him. The motion was smooth, controlled. He sat up, feeling the weight of the night's sleep lifting from his shoulders.

He looked at the clock. 6:01 AM.

He had time. He had more time than he'd had in weeks. The payment from yesterday—forty-four dollars—was tucked safely in his wallet. It wasn't enough to cover rent, but it was enough to keep him fed. It was enough to keep him in school.

He stood, stretching. His muscles felt different. Tighter. Stronger. He flexed his arms, watching the definition in his forearms. He had always been thin, but now there was something else. A density. A firmness.

He shook his head, dismissing the thought. It was just the medication. The trial. It was supposed to make him stronger, healthier. That was the point.

He walked to the bathroom, splashing cold water on his face. The mirror reflected back at him. His eyes looked sharper. Clearer. The bags under them had faded, replaced by a sharpness that made him look almost alert.

He dried his face and returned to his room. He pulled on his clothes—the same jeans and t-shirt he'd worn yesterday, the same worn sneakers. He didn't have enough clothes for a daily rotation, so he washed what he could and wore the rest until they needed cleaning again.

He grabbed his backpack and headed out the door.

The walk to the diner took fifteen minutes. He arrived at 6:45 AM, his shift starting at 7:00. He pushed open the door, the bell chiming overhead.

The manager, a tired-looking woman in her fifties, glanced up from the counter.

"You're early," she said. Her tone was flat, neither welcoming nor hostile.

"I know," Adrian said. "Is that a problem?"

She shrugged. "No. Just unusual." She turned back to the register, tapping at the screen. "You'll be on breakfast prep today. Dishes and grilling."

Adrian nodded. He had done this job before. It was simple. Repetitive. Perfect for someone who needed to keep his mind off the fact that he was broke, hungry, and enrolled in a trial that required him to masturbate into a cup every morning.

He tied on an apron and got to work.

The morning rush was busy. Orders came in fast, and Adrian moved through them with a mechanical efficiency that surprised him. His hands moved quickly, flipping pancakes, plating eggs, stacking toast. The work was familiar, but his body felt different. Faster. More responsive.

By 9:00 AM, the rush had slowed. Adrian wiped down the griddle, his movements clean and precise. He felt a pang of hunger, sharp and insistent. He hadn't eaten breakfast. He hadn't had time.

He looked at the clock. 9:02 AM.

He had to leave by 9:30 to make it to the clinic by 10:00. The medication was at 8:00, but he had to leave early to make sure he got there on time. The trial required punctuality. Missing a day meant losing the week's pay.

He untied his apron and hung it up.

"Leaving early?" the manager asked.

"I have a doctor's appointment," Adrian said.

She raised an eyebrow. "Again?"

"I told you yesterday. I'm enrolled in a trial."

She shrugged. "Fine. Just clock out properly."

Adrian clocked out and headed for the door. He paused, looking back at the diner. He had missed a shift yesterday to start the trial. He would miss today's shift too, leaving for the clinic early. That meant he would lose two days of pay from the diner this week.

But the trial paid better. Fourteen dollars a day, plus a thirty-dollar weekly bonus for compliance. That was forty-four dollars a day, six days a week. That was two hundred sixty-four dollars a week.

The diner paid eight dollars an hour, eight hours a day, five days a week. That was three hundred twenty dollars a week.

He was losing money. But he was gaining time. And the trial paid more per day. He could survive on less if he had to. He could eat cheaper. He could skip meals.

He pushed open the door and stepped out into the street.

The walk to the clinic took twenty minutes. He arrived at 9:50 AM, ten minutes early. He walked through the lobby, his footsteps echoing on the tile floor. He approached the counter, where the man in the white coat was typing.

"I'm here for the trial," Adrian said.

The man looked up, his eyes scanning Adrian's face. "You are early."

"I know."

"Good. Wait in the examination room. Room three."

Adrian nodded. He walked down the hallway, his steps light and quick. He pushed open the door to Room three and stepped inside.

The room was sterile, white, and cold. A small table sat in the center, with a chair on either side. A clock hung on the wall, its hands ticking steadily. 9:52 AM.

Adrian sat down, his hands clasped in his lap. He waited.

At 9:58 AM, the door opened. A nurse stepped inside, carrying a small paper cup. She set it on the table.

"Medication," she said. Her voice was professional, detached. "Take it now."

Adrian took the cup. The pill inside was small, clear, and unremarkable. He swallowed it dry, his throat clicking as it went down.

"Thank you," he said.

The nurse nodded. She left the room, closing the door behind her.

Adrian sat in silence, waiting for the medication to take effect. He felt a warmth spreading through his chest, a low, persistent burn that made his skin tingle. He touched his neck, feeling his pulse. It was steady. Strong.

He waited.

At 9:59 AM, the door opened again. The nurse stepped inside.

"Time for collection," she said.

Adrian stood, his body moving with an ease that surprised him. He walked to the collection room, the door closing behind him.

The room was small, sterile, and white. A small table sat in the center, with a chair on either side. A wastebasket sat in the corner. A sealed plastic cup sat on the table.

Adrian sat down, his hands clasped in his lap. He waited.

At 10:01 AM, he felt the heat rising in his chest, a low, persistent burn that made his skin tingle. He touched his neck, feeling his pulse. It was steady. Strong.

He closed his eyes, forcing his mind to focus. He thought about the trial. He thought about the money. He thought about survival.

He thought about the heat.

It took time. It took effort. And when it was done, he sat there, shaking, his face slick with sweat.

He sealed the cup, his hands moving with a mechanical precision that surprised him. He placed it in the designated bin, the slot swallowing the plastic with a soft *thud*.

He unlocked the door and stepped out into the hallway. The air was cooler now, or maybe it just felt that way because the heat inside him was receding, leaving behind a hollow, drained feeling.

He walked back to the counter. The man in the white coat was still there, still typing.

"Sample received," he said.

Adrian waited.

"You are due to return tomorrow at 0800 hours for medication intake and 1000 hours for collection," the man said. He didn't look up. "Do not be late."

Adrian nodded. He turned and walked out of the clinic.

The air outside was cool, carrying the scent of rain. Adrian stood on the sidewalk, watching the traffic pass. His stomach growled, a sharp, insistent pain that made him wince.

He was hungry. He hadn't eaten breakfast. He hadn't eaten lunch. He hadn't eaten anything since yesterday.

He pulled out his wallet, counting the bills. Forty-four dollars. He had spent none of it. He could afford to eat. He could afford to feed himself.

But he also needed to pay rent. He needed to pay his electricity bill. He needed to buy food for the rest of the week.

He closed his wallet and tucked it back into his pocket. He would eat. But he would eat cheap.

He walked to the nearest grocery store, a small, worn-down place that sold discount noodles, canned vegetables, and rice. He walked the aisles, his eyes scanning the shelves. He grabbed a bag of rice, a bag of noodles, and a can of vegetables. He paid with cash, the clerk handing him his change.

He walked home, his stomach growling. He entered his apartment, dropping his backpack on the floor. He opened the can of vegetables, eating them cold. He followed it with a handful of rice, chewing slowly.

The food was bland, tasteless, and unappetizing. But it was food. It was fuel. It was enough.

He finished eating and washed the dishes. He looked at the clock. 11:45 AM.

He had class at 2:00 PM. He had time.

He sat down at his desk, opening his textbook. He started reading, his eyes scanning the pages. The words were sharp, clear, and vivid. He absorbed them quickly, his mind retaining the information with an ease that surprised him.

He read for two hours, his mind sharp and focused. He closed the textbook, his eyes tired but satisfied.

He had time. He had money. He had food.

He had survived another day.
