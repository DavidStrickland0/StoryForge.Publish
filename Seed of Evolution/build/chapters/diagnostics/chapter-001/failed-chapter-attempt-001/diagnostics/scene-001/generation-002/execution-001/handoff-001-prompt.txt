# Chapter 1

The research hospital's waiting area smelled of disinfectant and sterile paper. Adrian sat hunched on a plastic chair, his stomach twisting with hunger that had nothing to do with the cheap noodles he'd eaten for breakfast. The fluorescent lights buzzed overhead, each pulse making his headache worse.

He'd used his last fifteen dollars on the bus fare. His bank account sat at negative forty-three dollars, a number the automated call had announced with cheerful incompetence. The tuition bill was due in three days. Rent in a week.

The receptionist's voice cut through his thoughts. "Mr. Vale? The coordinator will see you now."

Adrian stood on unsteady legs, adjusting the worn backpack that held everything he owned. The hallway stretched before him, white and endless, marked by doors labeled with numbers that meant nothing. He followed the receptionist's gesture toward Room 314, each step feeling like wading through water.

The door opened before he could knock.

"Adrian Vale?" The man inside was younger than Adrian expected, maybe thirty, with sharp features and eyes that moved too quickly to be warm. "I'm Dr. Aris Thorne. Come in."

The office was as sterile as the waiting room, all white surfaces and clinical efficiency. A tablet sat on the desk, already displaying something Adrian couldn't read from the doorway.

"Sit." Thorne gestured to the chair across from him without looking up from the screen. "You're here for the experimental-medicine program?"

"Yes." The word came out hoarse. Adrian cleared his throat, hating how desperate he sounded. "I need the payment. For school."

"Mm." Thorne finally looked at him, and the assessment was clinical, detached. "We have several trials running. Based on your application, you've been pre-screened for the male-fertility study. It pays four hundred per month, plus discounted medical care. The commitment is six months minimum."

Four hundred. Adrian's mind did the math automatically. Tuition covered. Rent mostly covered. Enough left over for food that wasn't just instant noodles.

"What's the catch?" The question slipped out before he could stop it.

Thorne's expression didn't change. "No catch. You'll take a daily medication, report any side effects, and visit the clinic each morning to provide specimens. The process is straightforward. The compensation is straightforward."

The way he said it made Adrian's skin crawl, but he pushed the feeling down. "How long does the screening take?"

"You're already pre-screened. We just need to go over the consent forms." Thorne turned the tablet toward him, and the screen filled with dense legal language. "Read through this carefully. Any questions, I'll answer them."

Adrian's eyes moved across the words, but they blurred together. *Participant agrees to undergo daily testing... participant understands the experimental nature... participant waives liability... participant agrees to confidential data collection...*

His stomach growled audibly.

Thorne didn't even blink. "Problem?"

"No." Adrian forced himself to focus. "How many specimens? Per day?"

"One. Daily. You'll come in each morning, provide the specimen, take your medication, and leave. The whole process takes ten minutes."

Ten minutes for four hundred dollars. For survival.

Adrian thought of his empty apartment, the eviction notice, the email from the university threatening to drop him from classes. He thought of his exhausted body, the way his hands shook from lack of food, the way he fell asleep in his lectures because he couldn't afford to eat.

He thought of the alternative: nothing. No money. No school. No future.

"I'll do it." The words came out flat, final.

Thorne nodded, as if he'd never expected anything else. "Sign here." He slid the tablet across the desk. "And here. And initial each page."

Adrian signed.

His name appeared on the screen, formal and binding. The coordinator's assistant appeared, then the hospital's name, then the words *PARTICIPANT CONSENT - EXPERIMENTAL MEDICINE PROGRAM*.

He'd just signed away something he couldn't quite name, but the alternative was worse.

"Excellent." Thorne stood, his smile not quite reaching his eyes. "You'll start tomorrow. Report to the testing wing at seven AM. Someone will show you the procedure." He paused. "One more thing. The medication will take a few days to become fully effective. You'll need to maintain the daily schedule without interruption. Is that clear?"

"Yes." Adrian stood, his legs still unsteady.

"Good. We look forward to working with you."

The door closed behind Adrian before he could respond.

He stood in the hallway, the weight of what he'd just agreed to settling over him like a physical thing. His stomach growled again, reminding him that even with four hundred dollars a month, he was still broke. Still hungry. Still exhausted.

But tomorrow, he'd have somewhere to be. Something to do.

Adrian made his way back to the waiting room, his footsteps echoing in the sterile corridor. The receptionist glanced up as he passed, her expression unreadable.

Outside, the afternoon sun felt wrong, too bright after the fluorescent lighting. Adrian squinted against it, his body aching with the kind of exhaustion that sleep wouldn't fix.

He had a job. He had money coming.

He also had a daily routine that would require him to masturbate into a cup every morning for six months, surrounded by strangers who saw him as data.

Adrian started walking toward his apartment, each step feeling heavier than the last. The weight of his decision pressed down on his shoulders, but underneath it all was a fragile, desperate relief.

He had tomorrow. That was enough.

For now.
