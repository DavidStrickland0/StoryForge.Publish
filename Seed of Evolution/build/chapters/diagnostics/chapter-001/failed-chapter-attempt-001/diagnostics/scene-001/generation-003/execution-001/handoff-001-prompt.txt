The research hospital gleamed with sterile efficiency. Adrian's sneakers squeaked against polished linoleum as he shuffled through the waiting area, his stomach churning with a hunger that had become familiar. The fluorescent lights overhead seemed too bright, making him squint against their clinical glare.

He'd spent his last three dollars on the bus fare. The receipt crinkled in his pocket—a reminder that he'd drained his account for this trip, for this chance. His rent was due in four days. Tuition in two weeks. The numbers sat in his head like weights, crushing any thought of alternatives.

The waiting room smelled of disinfectant and recycled air. A receptionist typed behind a glass partition, her fingers moving with mechanical precision. Adrian approached the desk, his throat dry.

"Name?" she asked without looking up.

"Adrian Vale. I have an appointment for the experimental-medicine program."

Her eyes flicked to a screen, then back to him. "Wait here. They'll call you."

He sank into one of the plastic chairs, its hard edges pressing against his spine. The chair next to him held a stack of pamphlets about various trials: sleep studies, nutrition programs, psychological evaluations. He'd chosen the fertility study because the pay was highest and the requirements seemed... He'd chosen the fertility study because the requirements seemed easy enough to meet.

His hands trembled slightly. He was utterly exhausted. Always exhaustion.

The waiting room was nearly empty except for an elderly man dozing in the corner and a woman flipping through a magazine with practiced disinterest. Adrian tried not to think about the specific nature of the daily testing he'd agreed to. The coordinator's explanation had been clinical, efficient, detached—as if describing a lab procedure rather than something so intensely personal.

But it paid.

The door to the examination wing opened, and a man in a white coat stepped out, holding a tablet.

"Adrian Vale?"

Adrian stood, his legs stiff from sitting. "Yes, sir."

"I'm Dr. Thorne. Follow me, please." The doctor's tone was professional, measured—nothing warm or reassuring. He turned without waiting for confirmation, expecting obedience.

Adrian followed through narrow corridors lined with diagrams of human anatomy, medical equipment he couldn't name, and framed certificates from institutions he didn't recognize. Dr. Thorne walked with confident strides, his white coat swaying slightly with each step.

They stopped at a small examination room. Inside, the sterile scent intensified. An examination table sat in the center, covered in fresh paper. A scale stood in the corner. A small desk held a laptop and a stack of forms.

"Strip down to your underwear and step on the scale," Dr. Thorne said, tapping at his tablet. "Then we'll take your vitals and discuss the program requirements."

Adrian's face heated. The clinical detachment in the doctor's voice made the instruction feel even more invasive. But he nodded, his throat too tight to speak, and began removing his clothes.

The scale's digital display blinked: 137 pounds.

Dr. Thorne made a note. "Blood pressure. Sit here."

Adrian sat on the examination table, the paper crinkling beneath him. The blood pressure cuff tightened around his arm, its mechanical hiss filling the small room. Dr. Thorne studied the reading, his expression unchanged.

"Underweight. Elevated cortisol levels, likely from stress and sleep deprivation. No contraindications for the trial." He set down the cuff. "The fertility study requires daily participation. You'll take the medication each morning, then return to the clinic between 2 PM and 4 PM for specimen collection and monitoring. Payments are made weekly, contingent on compliance."

Adrian swallowed hard. "And if I can't make the daily schedule?"

"Then you'll be in breach of contract. Payments stop, and you owe the program a penalty fee." Dr. Thorne's eyes finally met his, cool and clinical. "Do you have questions, or shall we proceed to the consent forms?"
