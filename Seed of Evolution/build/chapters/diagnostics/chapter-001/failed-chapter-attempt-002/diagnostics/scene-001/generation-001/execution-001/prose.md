# Chapter 1

Adrian Vale stood in the sterile waiting room of the research hospital, his thin frame hunched under the weight of an empty backpack and a hollow stomach. The fluorescent lights buzzed overhead, casting everything in a harsh, clinical white that made his exhaustion feel even more pronounced. He'd barely slept in two days—his cramped apartment was too cold without electricity, and his shift at the convenience store had run late again.

The tuition notice crinkled in his pocket as he shifted his weight from foot to foot. Three thousand dollars. Due in two weeks. His savings account held exactly $147. His rent was already two months past due.

The experimental-medicine program brochure had found its way into his hands three days ago, tucked between flyers for pizza delivery and student loan consolidation. *Participants receive $200 weekly stipend. All medical expenses covered. Flexible scheduling.* The words had blurred together as he read them in the fluorescent glare of the student union building, but the numbers had been clear enough.

He'd applied within the hour.

"Mr. Vale?"

Adrian jerked at the sound of his name, looking up to find a woman in scrubs standing near a clipboard. She had dark hair pulled back in a tight bun and eyes that suggested she'd seen this same scene play out a hundred times before.

"Dr. Thorne will see you now. Room 304, down the hall to your left."

Adrian nodded, his throat too dry to speak. He followed her past rows of identical chairs, past the smell of disinfectant and the faint hum of medical equipment, past the silent judgment of people who could afford to be here for reasons other than survival.

The door to 304 stood open.

Dr. Aris Thorne sat behind a desk that was immaculately organized, every pen aligned, every folder stacked with precision. He was younger than Adrian had expected—maybe thirty-five, with gray streaking through his dark hair and the kind of posture that came from years of maintaining control in a room.

"Adrian Vale," Thorne said, not asking, just stating it as he looked up from a file. "Nineteen years old. Freshman. Currently employed part-time at a convenience store. Financial aid denied. Rent delinquent."

The clinical assessment made Adrian's skin crawl, but he said nothing. Thorne had access to his records; there was no point in lying.

"You're here for the experimental-medicine program." Another statement, not a question. Thorne closed the file with a soft click. "Specifically, you've applied for the male-fertility trial."

"Yes, sir."

"Let's be clear about what this involves." Thorne leaned back in his chair, his expression neutral. "Daily visits to the clinic. Specimen collection. Medication administration. Monitoring. The schedule is non-negotiable. The requirements are specific. And your participation is contingent on your continued eligibility."

Adrian swallowed hard. The brochure had been vague about the specifics. *Daily visits* could mean anything from a quick blood draw to something far more involved.

"What exactly does specimen collection entail?"

Thorne's expression didn't change. "Masturbation into a collection cup. Daily. Your sperm count, motility, and genetic markers will be tracked throughout the trial. The fertility drug you'll receive is designed to increase production and correct genetic errors. We'll be monitoring how effectively it does both."

Heat crept up Adrian's neck. The clinical phrasing didn't make the requirement any less humiliating.

"And if I can't... if I'm too tired or—"

"You'll be paid to be here," Thorne interrupted smoothly. "Two hundred dollars weekly, paid biweekly. All medical expenses covered. You'll have access to the university's health network. And you'll be contributing to research that could help millions of men."

The words were polished, practiced. Adrian heard the subtext clearly: *You're desperate. We know you're desperate. This is the only way.*

"Can I think about it?"

Thorne smiled, but it didn't reach his eyes. "The application period closes tomorrow at five PM. I have three other candidates waiting to fill your slot. They're equally qualified. Equally motivated."

The threat was implicit but clear. Adrian had no leverage here. No alternatives. No safety net.

He thought of the eviction notice taped to his apartment door. The tuition bill burning a hole in his pocket. The empty refrigerator at home.

"I'll do it," Adrian heard himself say. "When do I start?"
