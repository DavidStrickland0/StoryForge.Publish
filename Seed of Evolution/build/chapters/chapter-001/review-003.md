# Chapter Review

## Overall Assessment

The chapter effectively establishes Adrian's desperation and the transactional nature of the trial enrollment. However, it contains a significant financial logic error regarding the payment amount versus the cash provided, and a minor continuity inconsistency with the room number established in the writing rules.

## Findings

- [FinancialLogic] The chapter states the first payment is $400, but Adrian counts forty $100 bills ($4,000). This contradicts the established compensation structure and creates an implausible cash flow for a student with limited funds.
  Evidence: Your first payment. $400, as promised... Forty crisp hundred-dollar bills.
- [Style] The writing rules prefer simple, rounded numbers or natural labels for incidental details. Using 'Room 314' and later 'Room 307' violates the guideline to avoid arbitrary specific room numbers unless they are plot-critical identifiers, which they are not here.
  Evidence: Room 314. Dr. Thorne will see you now... Report to the third floor, Room 307
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter completes the first ChapterTarget (signing consent) but fails to establish the 'first payment' as a distinct, received income event separate from the signing, as the ResolutionCriteria requires 'received his first payment' to be a completed state. The payment is handed over, but the financial relief is not fully realized in the narrative arc's progression.
  Evidence: Your first payment. $400, as promised... He could pay his rent. He could buy groceries.
- [FinancialLogic] The immediate cash payment of $400 contradicts the established compensation structure of $400 per month plus per-session fees, implying the protagonist has received a full month's base pay before performing any work, which is inconsistent with the 'first payment' description and standard trial protocols where base pay is typically prorated or paid after the first cycle.
  Evidence: Thorne states: 'The compensation structure was detailed in the materials: $400 per month, plus an additional $50 per completed testing session.' Later, Thorne hands him an envelope: 'Your first payment. $400, as promised.' Adrian counts: 'Forty crisp hundred-dollar bills.' - Correct the cash count to match the $400 payment (e.g., four $100 bills or forty $10 bills).
- Replace specific room numbers with generic labels like 'the examination room' or 'the third-floor office' to comply with writing rules.
