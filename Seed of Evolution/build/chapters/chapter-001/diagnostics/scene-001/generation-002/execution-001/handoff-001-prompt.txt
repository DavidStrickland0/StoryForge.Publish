# Chapter 1

Adrian sat in the sterile waiting room of the university-affiliated research hospital, his stomach hollow with hunger and his mind racing with calculations he'd made a hundred times before. The fluorescent lights buzzed overhead, casting everything in an antiseptic white that made his exhaustion feel even more pronounced. His backpack rested on the floor beside his chair, containing the last of his instant noodles and a granola bar he'd been saving for an emergency.

The emergency had arrived.

His tuition payment was due in three days. His rent was due in a week. The part-time job at the campus coffee shop paid enough for food—barely—but it wouldn't cover both obligations. He'd exhausted his meager savings months ago. The scholarship had fallen through. His parents couldn't help. There was no one left to ask.

The experimental-medicine program was his only option.

He'd found the advertisement posted on a bulletin board outside the student center: *Earn significant income through participation in cutting-edge medical research. Flexible scheduling. Generous compensation. All students welcome.* The fine print had been smaller, buried in legal jargon he'd barely skimmed.

Adrian's fingers drummed against his knees, his legs bouncing with nervous energy. The waiting room was nearly empty—just an elderly man reading a magazine three seats away and a receptionist behind a glass partition, typing at a computer with the focused efficiency of someone who had done this work a thousand times before.

"Adrian Vale?" a voice called out.

He looked up to see a woman in a white lab coat standing in a doorway, clipboard in hand. Her expression was professionally neutral, neither warm nor cold.

"That's me," Adrian said, standing too quickly. His head swam with the sudden movement, and he steadied himself against the chair.

The woman—her name tag read "Dr. Aris Thorne"—gestured toward the hallway. "Right this way, please. I'm Dr. Thorne. I'll be coordinating your intake today."

Adrian grabbed his backpack and followed her through the doorway into a corridor that smelled of disinfectant and felt several degrees colder than the waiting room. They passed several examination rooms, their doors closed, before stopping at a small office. Thorne held the door open for him, waiting.

The office was minimal: a desk, two chairs, a computer, and a filing cabinet. A window overlooked the parking lot, but the blinds were drawn, cutting off the afternoon sunlight.

"Please, have a seat," Thorne said, closing the door behind Adrian. She moved to her desk and sat down, placing the clipboard beside a manila folder. "I've reviewed your preliminary application. You're nineteen, a freshman in biology, and you're looking to participate in one of our compensation programs."

"Yes," Adrian said. His throat felt tight. "I need the income to cover my tuition and housing."

"Understood." Thorne opened the folder, scanning the pages inside. "Based on your health screening results, you qualify for several of our studies. However, I should inform you that the most lucrative option is currently our male-fertility trial."

Adrian's stomach dropped, but he nodded. "Okay."

"The trial involves daily specimen collection and medication administration. The compensation is substantial—four hundred dollars per week, with additional bonuses for successful completion of the study period. You'll also receive free medical care and access to our research facilities."

"Four hundred... a week?" The number seemed impossible. That was more than he made in a month at the coffee shop.

"Yes. However, the requirements are specific and non-negotiable." Thorne slid a thick document across the desk. "This is the consent form. You'll need to read it carefully before we proceed. The trial requires daily participation—you must report to our collection facility every morning between seven and nine a.m. to provide a specimen. You'll also take the fertility medication as prescribed."

Adrian took the document, his hands trembling slightly as he turned to the first page. The language was dense, filled with medical terminology and legal clauses. His eyes scanned quickly through the sections: *participant acknowledges the experimental nature of the treatment... participant agrees to provide daily specimens for analysis... participant understands that withdrawal from the study will result in forfeiture of all compensation...*

"I'll need you to sign this before we can enroll you," Thorne said, her tone matter-of-fact. "Do you have any questions about the terms?"

Adrian swallowed hard. The humiliation of what he was about to agree to sat heavy in his chest, but the alternative was equally clear: he would lose his place at the university, his housing, everything he'd worked for.

"No," he said quietly. "I understand."
