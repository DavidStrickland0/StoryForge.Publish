Twenty-eight-year-old maintenance worker Caleb Ward will be summoned to the magical world of Avarra, where he will expect the usual prophecy: a sacred weapon, an impossible destiny, and perhaps enough power to make up for an unremarkable life. Instead, the summoning will brand him with the lowest-ranked class in existence—Wayfarer—and grant him a single seemingly worthless ability: **Concord**, the power to form voluntary bonds with others.

Declared a failed hero, Caleb will be stripped of the kingdom’s support and sent beyond the frontier as bait for the monsters gathering there. He will survive only because Concord will have a hidden strength: those who willingly bind their paths to his will be able to share fragments of their talents, senses, and magic with him—and with one another. As their trust strengthens, the bond will grow more powerful. Pain, fear, and betrayal will pass through it just as easily as strength.

Caleb’s first companions will be women whom the kingdom will also deem expendable: a disgraced knight whose cursed armor will be slowly consuming her, an elven sorceress who will be unable to control the ancient spirit sealed inside her, a sharp-tongued beastkin scout who will be hunted by her own clan, and a sheltered priestess who will begin to suspect that her god will not be what the Church will claim. Each will have her own ambitions, secrets, and reasons to distrust him. Their growing affection will not be commanded by magic; it will have to be earned through shared danger, genuine loyalty, and Caleb’s stubborn refusal to treat anyone as a tool.

Starting with no weapons, no combat training, and no place in his new world, Caleb will have to transform a band of unwanted outcasts into a force capable of challenging monsters, conquering forgotten dungeons, and surviving the rival heroes sent to silence them. Yet every victory will reveal another piece of the truth: the summoned heroes will never have been brought to Avarra to save it. They will have been brought to feed the ancient power beneath its kingdoms—and Caleb’s supposedly useless class may prove to be the one thing capable of breaking the cycle.

To become a hero, he will have to do more than grow strong. He will have to unite people who will have every reason to stand alone, decide what kind of relationships they will build together, and prove that the greatest power in any world will come from freely given trust rather than domination.
