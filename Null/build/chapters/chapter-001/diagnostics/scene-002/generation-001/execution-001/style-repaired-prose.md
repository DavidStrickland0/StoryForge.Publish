The tension in the booth was a physical weight, pressing against Ethan’s chest. He watched the super’s hand drift toward the back of Elena’s neck, fingers curling with a casual, predatory grace. The air in the bar seemed to thicken, the ambient noise of the crowd fading into a dull roar as the super leaned in, his voice dropping to a register that only the three of them could hear.

"Tell me again," the super said, his tone dripping with a slow, venomous amusement. "Who is the boss here?"

Elena’s jaw tightened, her eyes hard. She did not flinch, though her fingers white-knuckled the rim of her wine glass. David, seated beside her, had gone completely rigid, his eyes darting between the two figures like a trapped animal. He looked as if he might faint, or perhaps simply vanish into the floorboards.

Ethan’s knuckles were white where he gripped his beer bottle. He knew the physics of the situation better than anyone else in that room. The super was a class-five entity, a walking fortress of muscle and invulnerability. Ethan was a thirty-year-old accountant who got winded climbing the stairs to his apartment. If he stood up now, if he tried to interpose his body between that massive hand and Elena’s throat, he would be a red mist on the floor before the super even registered the interruption.

It was a simple, cold calculation. Intervention meant death. Observation meant shame.

Ethan pushed the chair back. The legs scraped against the wooden floor, a sharp, grating sound that cut through the heavy silence. The super didn’t turn his head, but his eyes shifted, tracking the movement with the lazy precision of a hawk spotting a mouse.

Ethan stood, his heart hammering against his ribs. He didn’t look at Elena. He didn’t look at David. He looked straight ahead, his gaze fixed on the exit, which was a nightmare of obstacles. The path to the door was a straight line, but it required him to walk directly past the table where the super stood.

He had to pass within arm's reach.

The super’s hand was still resting on Elena’s shoulder, his thumb tracing a slow, deliberate circle on the fabric of her blazer. He was waiting for a reaction, for a display of dominance, for the world to acknowledge his superiority.

Ethan took a breath that tasted of stale beer and fear. He began to walk.

His steps were measured, steady. He could feel the super’s gaze on his back, a hot, physical presence that prickled his skin. He was ten feet away. Five feet. He was three feet away.

The super watched him approach, a smirk playing on his lips. He didn’t move. He didn’t need to. He was a god among insects, and Ethan was just another insect trying to scurry away.

Ethan reached the table. He could smell the expensive cologne the super wore, a cloying mix of sandalwood and something metallic. He could see the tension in Elena’s face, the desperate hope in her eyes as she watched this ordinary man walk past her.

Ethan kept his eyes forward. He kept his hands at his sides. He walked through the space between them, his body brushing against the edge of the table, his shoulder inches from the super’s bicep.

And then, he was past them.
