# Chapter Review

## Overall Assessment

The chapter fails to advance the active Short arc (Chapter 3 target) by repeating the internalization of the bar incident rather than showing Ethan's return to ordinary life. It introduces a continuity error regarding the Lens's function and mischaracterizes the protagonist's knowledge of the super's identity, violating established canon and narrative progression requirements.

## Findings

- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter fails to complete the Chapter 3 target of showing Ethan's return to ordinary life. Instead, it re-performs the internal processing of the incident (analyzing the anomaly, rationalizing the power loss) which was already established in Chapter 2, resulting in no material progression.
  Evidence: He needed to make sense of it... So why had FireMan fallen? Ethan frowned in the dark... A temporary failure. That was the only explanation that fit.
- [Canon] The description of the Lens contradicts established canon. The Lens is defined as a device for augmented reality navigation, but the chapter describes it as a band that projects cues and local data, implying a broader AR interface not supported by the character's established equipment.
  Evidence: The device was a sleek, unobtrusive band that fit over the ear, projecting navigation cues and local data into his visual field.
- [Character] Ethan's knowledge of the super's name 'FireMan' is inconsistent with his civilian status and the timeline. As a civilian who fled the scene, he would not have access to the specific media headlines naming the super immediately after the incident, especially given the police investigation is ongoing.
  Evidence: The name had appeared in the headlines after the incident, though the details were still sparse. The name FireMan had appeared in the headlines after the incident.
- [Style] The chapter relies heavily on repetitive, clipped narrative sentences and explanatory internal monologue that restates the obvious (Ethan is safe, ordinary, and the incident is over), violating the rule against unnecessary explanation of meanings the reader can infer.
  Evidence: But here, Ethan was safe. He was ordinary. And for now, that was enough... He didn’t need to solve it... He just needed to accept that it had happened.

## Engagement Findings

- The chapter lacks active engagement, consisting primarily of static internal monologue and repetitive reassurance of safety rather than character development or plot progression.

## Revision Guidance

- Remove the internal analysis of the power loss, as it was already covered in Chapter 2.
- Focus on Ethan's specific ordinary routines (work, hobbies) to satisfy the Chapter 3 target.
- Correct the Lens description to strictly navigation functions.
- Remove the reference to 'FireMan' headlines or justify how a civilian accessed this specific info.
