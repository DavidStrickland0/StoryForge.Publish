The hum of the refrigerator was a low, consistent drone, the kind of background noise that usually faded into the fabric of the room. Tonight, it felt loud. Ethan kept his eyes closed, his body loose on the mattress, the grey henley soft against his shoulders. He tried to find the rhythm of his own breathing, but his mind kept snagging on the image of the bar. The smell of spilled drinks. The weight of the silence that had dropped over the room.

He rolled onto his side, pulling the duvet up to his chin. The sheets were cool, a welcome contrast to the sweat that had broken out on his neck. He needed to make sense of it. The world had rules, even if they were weird rules. Supers were part of the landscape, like traffic or weather. They had skills. That was the official term, anyway. *They had skills.. A clinical way to describe a man who could punch through a concrete wall or a woman who could fly to work.

Ethan knew the basics. Everyone did. Skills were permanent. You didn’t wake up with them, and you didn’t lose them. They were locked into the body, tied to specific organs or systems. A guy with indestructible skin had a mutation in his dermis. A woman with super-strength had altered muscle fibers and bone density. It was biological. It was fixed. That was the one comforting truth in a world where physics seemed to apply selectively.

So why had FireMan fallen?

Ethan frowned in the dark. The name had appeared in the headlines after the incident, though the details were still sparse. *The name FireMan had appeared in the headlines after the incident.. A cheesy name for a guy with a penthouse and a slow-twitch strength that made him look like a statue carved out of granite. He’d been there, holding court, being abrasive. And then, for a split second, he’d been a man. Just a man.

A temporary failure. That was the only explanation that fit.

Maybe he’d hit his head. Maybe he’d had a stroke. Supers were human, after all. They could still get sick, still get drunk, still suffer from the same biological glitches as everyone else. A sudden drop in blood pressure, a spasm, a momentary neurological misfire. It happened. People passed out. People blacked out. It wasn’t a miracle; it was a malfunction.

He let the thought settle. A malfunction. It was mundane. It was boring. It was the kind of thing that happened in the real world, where things broke because they were made of meat and bone, not because some invisible force had decided to take their power away.

Ethan exhaled, the tension in his shoulders easing slightly. It was an anomaly, sure. It defied the standard understanding of how skills worked. But the standard understanding was just that: a standard. It wasn’t a law of the universe. It was just what everyone else said.

He didn’t need to solve it. He didn’t need to figure out why a guy with unbreakable skin suddenly felt pain. He just needed to accept that it had happened, that it was over, and that he was safe in his apartment. The mystery was there, sitting in the corner of his mind like a loose thread, but he could pull it away. He could let it fray on its own.

The refrigerator hummed. The clock on the nightstand ticked. The world outside was still turning, still full of people with powers and people without, still governed by the same weird, uneven rules. But in here, in the quiet, Ethan was just a guy trying to sleep. And for the first time since he’d walked out of that bar, that felt like enough.
