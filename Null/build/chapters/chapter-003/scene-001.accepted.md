The key card beeped against the reader, a small, reassuring chirp that cut through the ambient hum of the city outside. The magnetic lock disengaged with a heavy thud, and Ethan stepped inside, pulling the door shut behind him. The click of the deadbolt was the first normal sound he’d heard in an hour.

He stood in the entryway for a moment, letting the silence of the apartment settle around him. The air was still and cool, smelling faintly of the lavender detergent he used for his towels. The chaos of the bar—the clinking glasses, the low roar of conversation, the specific, heavy tension that had hung over Elena’s table—faded into the background noise of the street, muffled by the double-paned glass and the solid oak of the door.

Ethan unbuttoned his jacket. It was a dark, practical thing, designed to blend into the crowd rather than stand out, but right now it felt like armor he hadn’t asked for. He slid it off his shoulders and hung it on the hook by the door, the metal clinking softly against the nail. Beneath it, he wore a simple grey henley, soft from years of washing. He peeled off his shoes, setting them neatly by the mat, and stretched his legs out in the socks he wore underneath. The floor was hardwood, warm under his feet, a stark contrast to the cold, wet pavement he had been walking on.

He walked to the small kitchen, the light from the hallway spilling in to illuminate the counter. The coffee maker was empty, a testament to his habit of finishing the last cup before leaving the house. He poured a glass of water instead, taking a long drink that cooled his throat and settled his stomach. The water was clean, tasting of nothing in particular, which was exactly what he needed.

On the counter, his Lens sat in its charging dock. The device was a sleek, unobtrusive band that fit over the ear, projecting navigation cues and local data into his visual field. It had been guiding him away from the bar, suggesting routes that avoided the immediate vicinity of the incident. Now that he was home, the need for its constant, subtle guidance vanished.

He picked up the Lens and set it on the table, powering it down. The faint blue light that had been pulsing at the edge of his vision winked out, leaving his eyes to adjust to the dim, warm lighting of his apartment. The world outside, with its supers and its unexplained anomalies and its dangerous social dynamics, receded behind the glass. Here, in this small, quiet space, he was just a man with a job and a routine.

Ethan moved to the living area, a single room that served as his bedroom, workspace, and lounge. He sat down on the edge of the bed, the mattress dipping slightly under his weight. He looked out the window, past the blinds, toward the distant glow of the city skyline. Somewhere out there, in a penthouse or a bar, a super was probably trying to figure out what had just happened to him. Somewhere, Elena was filing a report, and David was calculating the reputational damage.

But here, Ethan was safe. He was ordinary. And for now, that was enough. He lay back on the bed, staring at the ceiling, letting the quiet of the apartment wrap around him. The memory of the bar was still there, a sharp edge in the back of his mind, but it was distant. It was something that had happened to someone else, in a place he didn’t want to be. He closed his eyes, listening to the hum of the refrigerator in the kitchen, and waited for sleep to come.
