David was already moving.

He abandoned his chair, the legs scraping a harsh shriek against the hardwood floor, and hurried to the woman’s side. For the first time since Ethan had entered the bar, the man didn’t just smile and nod. He didn’t offer a drink or a compliment on her evening. He moved with a frantic, protective urgency that matched the rigid, controlled fury radiating from the executive.

He didn’t touch her arm, knowing better, but he stood close enough to block the line of sight from the other patrons. His voice, when it came, was low and stripped of its earlier honeyed deference. It was the voice of a man who had just realized that the person he spent his evenings flattering was the only thing standing between him and a very painful evening himself.

"Let’s go," David said. It wasn’t a suggestion. It wasn’t a question.

The executive—Elena—didn’t look at him. Her eyes remained fixed on the super, who was still struggling on the floor, his broad shoulders hunched in confusion. But she nodded, a single, sharp dip of her chin.

David reached into his jacket and pulled out a credit card, sliding it across the bar top toward the stunned bartender. He didn't wait for a receipt. He didn't wait for change. He just needed the transaction to be final, the tab closed, the last thread of their evening at this establishment severed.

"Call the police," Elena said. Her voice was flat, devoid of the tremor that had marked her earlier panic. She sounded, for all the world, like she was scheduling a meeting. "And I want a statement filed. Now. And I want it on the record that he attempted to assault me."

"Ma'am, he's a super," the bartender whispered, his eyes darting between the broken man on the floor and the woman who had just commanded him to be arrested. "They don't... they don't usually go to jail. They just... they just file a report and go."

Elena finally turned her head. Her gaze was cold, precise, and utterly terrifying. "Then I will file the report myself. And if it isn't acted upon, my legal team will be in contact with yours before the sun comes up. Do you understand? I am not leaving this room until the authorities are on their way. And I am not leaving this city until he is in a cell."

David stepped in front of her, creating a physical barrier between Elena and the rest of the bar. He was shaking, his hands trembling slightly as he smoothed the lapels of his jacket, but his posture was straight, his shoulders squared. He was no longer the sycophant who laughed at her jokes. He was her shield, her bodyguard, her ally.

"We're going to get you out of here," David said quietly, his eyes never leaving the super. "And then we're going to make sure he never does this to anyone else."

Elena didn't answer. She didn't need to. The shift in the room was palpable. The super was no longer the apex predator. He was a man on the floor, and the woman who had been his target was the one holding the power.
