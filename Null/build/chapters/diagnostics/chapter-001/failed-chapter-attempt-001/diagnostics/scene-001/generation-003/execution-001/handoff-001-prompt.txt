A woman in a sharp, expensive suit sat at a high-top table, her posture rigid and commanding. Her companion, a man with a slicked-back haircut and a smile that didn’t reach his eyes, leaned in close, nodding emphatically as she spoke. Ethan couldn’t hear the words, but the body language was loud enough. She made a dismissive wave with one hand, and the man immediately adjusted his posture, straightening his tie and offering a deferential grin. She was a super, that much was obvious. The way the air seemed to bend around her, the subtle hum of energy that occasionally flickered at the tips of her fingers, and the way the other patrons gave the table a wide berth all pointed to it.

Ethan took a sip of his beer, his mind drifting back to the news clips he’d watched that morning. A city council meeting had been disrupted by a strength-type super who had thrown a microphone across the room after being criticized for his voting record. The footage had gone viral, and the comments section was a mess of anger and fear. Most people were outraged, but Ethan felt a different kind of unease. He remembered the time he’d been mugged by two normal guys outside a convenience store. He’d been lucky; they’d wanted money, not a fight. But what if one of them had been a super? What if one of them had simply decided that Ethan was in the way?

He looked back at the table. The man, the sycophant, was laughing at something the woman had said, though his laughter sounded forced, like a recording played at the wrong speed. The woman, meanwhile, was checking her phone, her thumb scrolling rapidly. She looked bored, or perhaps impatient. It was the look of someone who was used to getting exactly what she wanted, exactly when she wanted it. Ethan felt a pang of irritation, a low-grade annoyance that he couldn’t quite place. It wasn’t fear, exactly. It was the sensation of being a minor inconvenience in someone else’s grand plan.

He finished his beer and set the glass down. The bartender, a lanky guy with a beard, wiped down the counter and nodded at him. Ethan didn’t ask for another drink. He just watched the man at the table lean in even closer, his face a mask of eager agreement. The woman rolled her eyes, a gesture of mild contempt, but she didn’t push him away. She seemed to enjoy the attention, the constant validation of her own importance.

Ethan shifted in his seat, his leg bouncing slightly. He felt restless, a physical manifestation of the mental tension he’d been carrying all evening. He needed to get out of there, to clear his head. He stood up, buttoning his jacket, and glanced at the table one last time. The man was still talking, his hands moving in animated gestures. The woman was still on her phone. Nothing was happening. No one was in danger. It was just two people, one powerful and one compliant, existing in a bubble of their own making.

Ethan turned and walked toward the exit, his footsteps echoing softly on the wooden floor. He pushed open the door and stepped out into the cool night air, the sounds of the bar fading behind him. He breathed in deeply, the crisp air filling his lungs, and for a moment, the tension in his shoulders eased. He was safe. He was ordinary. And he was free.
