The neon sign outside the window buzzed with a low, electric hum, painting the rain-slicked street in bands of sickly pink. Ethan sat with his back against the cool, polished leather of the booth, a half-finished lager sweating condensation onto the sticky wooden table. He had come here for the cheap beer and the relative anonymity of the back corner, a small refuge from the city’s constant, low-grade anxiety. It was a Tuesday evening, unremarkable and quiet, the kind of night that allowed a person to forget that the world had been fundamentally altered by people who could fly through buildings or punch cars into scrap metal.

Inside the bar, however, the hierarchy was loud. A few tables over, a woman in a tailored charcoal suit occupied a space that seemed far too large for her, her presence radiating an aggressive, brittle confidence. She was talking to a man in a wrinkled dress shirt who leaned toward her with the eager, spineless devotion of a golden retriever. The woman—Ethan caught the name tag on her lapel, *Vance*—was dissecting a waiter’s mistake with the precision of a surgeon, her voice cutting through the ambient noise of the jukebox and the clinking of glasses.

"You think I came here to be inconvenienced by your lack of basic operational logic?" she said, her tone sharp and clipped. She didn't raise her voice; she didn't need to. The air around her seemed to vibrate with a subtle, localized pressure, a faint shimmer that distorted the light of the overhead pendant lamp. It was a minor display, a flex of power rather than a full-blown ability, but it was enough to make the air feel thin and cold.

The man, whose face was a mask of ingratiating concern, shook his head rapidly. "No, ma'am, absolutely not. It’s a complete oversight on our end. We’ll make it right immediately. Whatever you need, I am at your disposal."

"I need competence," Vance snapped, her eyes narrowing. She gestured vaguely at the empty air, and a few stray ice cubes in her water glass lifted slightly, hovering for a second before dropping back into the water with a soft splash. It was a petty, childish thing, a display of dominance that served no functional purpose other than to remind everyone within earshot of the disparity between their existences.

Ethan took a slow sip of his beer, his gaze fixed on the condensation dripping down the side of his glass. He watched the man at the table flinch, not from the cold, but from the sheer weight of her attention. He felt a familiar, dull ache in his chest, a sense of helplessness that had become a constant background noise in his life. In a world where strength was the currency of respect, being ordinary meant being invisible, or worse, being a target for the whims of those who could bend the world to their will without consequence. He looked around the bar, taking in the other patrons who had turned their heads, their expressions a mix of annoyance and resigned acceptance. They were used to it. He was tired of it.

He finished his drink, the bitterness a welcome anchor to reality, and set the glass down with a deliberate, quiet click. The sound was small, insignificant against the backdrop of the city’s distant traffic, but it felt like a declaration of intent. He needed to leave. The air in the bar felt too thick, too charged with the static energy of the woman who demanded the world bow to her. He stood up, his joints popping slightly in the cool air, and began to move toward the exit, his path taking him away from the table, away from the shimmering distortion in the light, and toward the door where the rain waited to wash the day away.
