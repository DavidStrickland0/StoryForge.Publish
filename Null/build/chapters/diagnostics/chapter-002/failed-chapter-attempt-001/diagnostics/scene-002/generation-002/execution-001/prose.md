The silence in the bar had a weight to it, thick and sudden after the earlier chaos. Ethan kept his back against the cool brick of the laundromat, his hands buried deep in his jacket pockets to stop them from shaking. He watched the glass, his breath fogging the pane slightly before dissipating in the cold night air. Inside, the super stood in the middle of the room, completely still. The man was large, broad-shouldered, with the kind of muscular frame that usually suggested something more than human, but right now he looked trapped in his own body. He flexed his fingers, then clenched them into fists, his face a mask of baffled frustration. He was waiting for something. Waiting for the strength to return, for the invulnerability to kick in, for the world to realign itself so that his physical dominance was absolute again. It didn’t happen.

Elena stood near the entrance, her posture rigid but composed. She had pulled her blazer straight, smoothing the wrinkles with a deliberate, practiced grace that spoke of a woman who refused to be ruffled, even when her life had just been threatened. David hovered a step behind her, his eyes darting between the super and the exit. He looked terrified, his earlier sycophancy stripped away by sheer survival instinct, but he didn't move to comfort Elena. He just waited, his hand hovering near the door handle, ready to bolt the moment she gave the signal.

The super took a step forward, his movements stiff and awkward. He was moving like a man who had forgotten how to coordinate his limbs, his center of gravity off, his steps heavy and clunky. He was no longer a god; he was just a large, angry man in a room full of broken glass and spilled drinks. The power dynamic had shifted so violently that the air in the bar seemed to crackle with the tension of it. The patrons who had scattered were beginning to return, their faces pale, their eyes wide. They didn't look at the super with fear anymore; they looked at him with a detached, horrified curiosity, as if he were a malfunctioning appliance.

Elena caught Ethan’s eye through the glass. For a second, their gazes locked. Her expression wasn't one of gratitude or recognition; it was a cold, calculating assessment. She saw a stranger, a witness, a variable in the equation of her survival. Then she turned her attention back to the super, her chin lifting in a challenge that seemed almost trivial, almost petty, in the face of the violence that had just occurred. She wasn't afraid of him. She was waiting for him to realize that he was no longer the apex predator in the room.

The super let out a sound, a low, guttural growl that lacked its usual thunderous resonance. He tried to flex his bicep, to show off the muscle, to reassert his dominance, but the movement looked pathetic, a weak spasm of flesh and bone. He was isolated, cut off from the source of his power, and the silence of the bar was amplifying his helplessness. Ethan watched, his heart still hammering against his ribs, the adrenaline fading into a dull ache in his muscles. He didn't understand what had happened. He didn't know why the super was powerless, or why the world had stopped to let a civilian executive stand up to a superhero. He only knew that the super was trapped, and the executive was free.
