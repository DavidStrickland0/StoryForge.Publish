Ethan stopped walking.

He hadn’t meant to. His feet had carried him three blocks on autopilot, his body executing the routine of leaving a place that had become unsafe, but his brain had refused to process the data. Now, standing on the sidewalk with the cold air biting through his jacket, the details rushed back with the force of a physical blow.

The super.

In the bar, the man had been a monolith. He had stood in the center of the room, his presence a heavy, suffocating weight that pressed against the air. He had been effortless. When he moved, it was with the smooth, terrifying grace of a predator that knew its prey could not escape.

But outside, through the grimy glass of the bar’s window, Ethan had seen something else entirely.

The super was still there. He was standing in the middle of the main floor, surrounded by overturned chairs and scattered glasses, but he looked... small.

He was flexing his fingers, staring at his own hands as if they belonged to a stranger. He had tried to straighten a bent chair leg, but his grip had slipped. He had tried to lift a heavy wooden table to block the exit, but it had stayed put, immovable under his palms. The fluid grace was gone. In its place was a clumsy, frantic flailing, the movements of a man who had suddenly forgotten how to control his own limbs.

Ethan watched, his breath hitching in his throat.

It wasn’t just that the man was failing. It was the look on his face. There was no anger, no rage. There was only a profound, bewildered confusion. He looked at his hands, then at the table, then back at his hands, trying to understand why the world had stopped obeying him.

And then, the door opened.

Elena walked out.

She did not run. She did not stumble or panic. She walked with the same deliberate, icy dignity she had worn while holding court at her table. Her head was high, her chin up, her gaze fixed on the street ahead as if the bar behind her was merely a smudge on her shoe.

David followed a few steps behind her. He was pale, his hands shaking, his eyes darting around the street with the wide-eyed fear of a rabbit in the open. But he was moving. He was following her.

They passed the window without a single glance back. They passed the super, who was still standing in the center of the room, frozen in his confusion. They did not acknowledge him. They did not offer thanks, or apology, or recognition. They simply walked past the man who had been holding court, the man who had been the most powerful thing in the room, and continued into the night.

Elena’s heels clicked on the pavement, a sharp, rhythmic sound that echoed in the quiet street. She adjusted her coat, a small, unconscious gesture of comfort, and kept walking.

The super watched them go.

He didn’t try to stop them. He didn’t call out. He just stood there, his shoulders slumped, his hands hanging limp at his sides. He looked defeated. He looked lost.

Ethan felt a strange, cold knot form in his stomach. He wanted to look away. He wanted to turn around and walk home, to lock his door, and pretend he hadn’t seen any of it. He wanted to believe that the super’s loss of power was a fluke, a momentary glitch in a system that was, for all practical purposes, unbreakable.

But the image of the man’s confused face lingered. The image of Elena’s calm, unbothered exit.

It was over. The incident was over. The super was powerless, and the executive was free.

Ethan took a deep breath, the cold air filling his lungs and grounding him. He turned away from the window, his back to the bar, and began to walk.

The city noise rushed back in around him, the distant wail of a siren, the rumble of a late-night delivery truck, the ordinary symphony of a world that continued to turn, indifferent to the small, violent disruptions that occurred in its corners. Ethan was just another shadow moving through the streets, a civilian in a city of gods, walking home to a life that felt, for the first time in a long time, both fragile and precious.
