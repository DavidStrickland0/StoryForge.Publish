The street was empty, save for the slow creep of shadows cast by the flickering neon sign of the laundromat three blocks down. Ethan stopped walking. His legs felt heavy, as if the ground had become a viscous fluid, but the cold air biting at his exposed neck was a sharp, clarifying presence. He turned back toward the Gilded Cage.

From this distance, the bar’s interior was a hazy glow of amber light and spilled drinks. The noise inside had settled into a low, uneasy murmur, the kind of silence that follows a sudden, violent disruption. Ethan’s gaze drifted to the large front window, the glass slightly smudged with condensation and the faint, lingering scents of stale beer and cigarette smoke.

He watched the figure in the center of the room. The super had not left. He stood near the bar, his posture rigid, his shoulders hunched as if carrying an invisible weight. The man’s hands were raised to chest height, fingers twitching and curling into fists, then releasing. He was testing them. Ethan watched the man flex his right bicep, the muscle bulging under the skin with a strength that no longer carried the terrifying, kinetic promise it had held moments ago. The man’s movements were jerky, lacking the fluid grace of someone accustomed to moving without consequence. He looked less like a god and more like a man who had just discovered his legs were made of water.

The super’s head turned sharply. His eyes were wide, darting across the room, searching for a source, a reason, a person to blame. The confusion in his expression was raw and unfiltered. He had been the strongest thing in the room, and now he was just another body in a space filled with other bodies.

Then, the door to the bar swung open.

Elena stepped out. She did not run. She did not stumble or look back over her shoulder. Her posture was straight, her chin lifted, her heels striking the pavement with a deliberate, measured rhythm. David followed a step behind her, his movements slightly more hesitant, his eyes fixed on the back of Elena’s head as he ensured their path to the sidewalk was clear. They moved with the same kind of authority they had possessed inside, the social hierarchy they had built at their table projecting itself outward into the night. They did not acknowledge the super. They did not acknowledge Ethan. They simply continued walking, their figures dissolving into the urban sprawl, the executive and her sycophant reclaiming their lives with a speed that felt almost cruel in its efficiency.

Ethan’s breath hitched in his throat. The sight of them leaving was a jolt to his system, a reminder that the world was still turning, that the people who had been caught in the center of that brief, violent flash had already moved on to their next appointment, their next dinner, their next life.

He looked back at the super through the window. The man was still there. He had not moved to follow them. He stood alone in the center of the room, his hands hanging at his sides now, his shoulders slumping. The other patrons had returned to their drinks, their attention shifting away from the spectacle and back to their own conversations, the bar resuming its nightly routine as if the previous twenty minutes had been a brief, unpleasant dream.

The super’s head tilted, his eyes finding the window. He stared at the dark glass, at the faint reflection of the streetlights, and then at the shadow of the man watching him. For a second, their gazes locked. There was no recognition in the super’s face, only a deep, frustrated bewilderment. He opened his mouth, as if to speak, but no sound seemed to leave him. The power that had allowed him to crush the world around him was gone, and with it, the certainty of his own invincibility.

Ethan felt a strange, cold knot in his stomach. He had no idea what had happened. He had no idea why the man had suddenly become ordinary. He had no idea why Elena had been able to walk away unharmed. He was just a civilian, a witness to a mystery that had no name, a glitch in a system he didn’t understand.

He looked away from the window. The street was quiet again. The siren wailed in the distance, a far-off cry that sounded like it belonged to a different city, a different world.

Ethan turned his back on the Gilded Cage. He pulled his jacket tighter around himself, the fabric rough against his skin, and began to walk. The city noise rushed back in, the distant wail of a siren, the rumble of a late-night delivery truck, the ordinary symphony of a world that continued to turn, indifferent to the small, violent disruptions that occurred in its corners. Ethan was just another shadow moving through the streets, a civilian in a city of gods, walking home to a life that felt, for the first time in a long time, both fragile and precious.
