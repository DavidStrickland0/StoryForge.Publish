The brick wall was cold against Ethan’s back. It seeped through his jacket, a damp chill that settled into his spine and refused to leave. He kept his eyes on his hands. They were trembling, a fine, rapid vibration that made the knuckles look white and brittle. He flexed his fingers, watching them move, waiting for the motion to stop. It didn’t.

A car horn honked somewhere down the street, a sharp, aggressive blast that cut through the lingering hum of the crowd. Ethan flinched, his body jerking forward before his mind caught up. The sound wasn't a threat; it was just a car. But his nervous system had forgotten that distinction. Every noise felt like a warning. Every shadow looked like a silhouette.

He pushed himself off the wall. His legs felt heavy, as if the pavement had become slightly more viscous. He needed to move. Standing still felt dangerous. Standing still meant thinking. Thinking meant confronting the geometry of what had happened: the stall, the fall, the blood. It meant confronting the fact that he had been standing there, three feet away, when the world’s most famous hero became just a woman in a puddle.

He began to walk. He didn’t know where he was going. He just needed to be away from the venue, away from the security barriers, away from the memory of the medics’ yellow vests. He walked until the buildings became less grand, the glass and steel giving way to older brick and painted storefronts. The air changed, too, losing the sterile scent of ozone and gaining the smell of exhaust and rain-washed asphalt.

He stopped at a small park, a square of concrete surrounded by a low chain-link fence. A few benches sat empty under the grey sky. Ethan sat on the edge of one, his hands resting on his knees. He looked at his shoes. They were scuffed, ordinary, and completely safe.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out his phone. The screen was dark. He unlocked it, the light flaring in his eyes. The news feeds were already scrolling, a cascade of red banners and breaking headlines. *Aurora Falls.* *Superhero Injured.* *Cause Unknown.*

He stared at the words. *Cause Unknown.*

They were true, in a legal sense. No one had seen anything. No one had touched her. But Ethan knew the truth, or rather, he knew the absence of a cause. He knew that his presence had been the variable. He knew that his body had done something to the air, to the energy, to the very fabric of her power. He didn't know how. He didn't know why. But he knew he was the cause.

He typed a message to Ben.

*Are you there?*

He stared at the text, the cursor blinking at the end of the sentence. It felt absurd. He had just watched a miracle fail, and his first instinct was to ask a friend if he was available for a beer. But the isolation was a physical weight, pressing down on his chest. He didn't want to be alone with the knowledge. He wanted someone to tell him it was a coincidence. He wanted someone to say it was a malfunction in her suit, a glitch in the system, anything other than the fact that he was a danger to the world.

He deleted the message.

He couldn't tell Ben. He couldn't tell anyone. If he spoke it, it became real. If he spoke it, he would have to explain. And he didn't have an explanation. He only had the memory of the silence, the heavy, vacuum-like silence that had replaced the roar of the crowd.

Ethan put the phone away. He stood up, his knees popping. The park was quiet now. The city hummed around him, indifferent and vast. He was just a man in a grey jacket, standing on a bench in a park, carrying a secret that would kill him if he let it out.

He looked at his hands one last time. They were still trembling.

He turned and walked toward the subway station, the concrete stretching out before him, a long, grey path leading back to his apartment, back to the silence, back to the life he had before the fall. He didn't look back. He didn't want to see the sky.
