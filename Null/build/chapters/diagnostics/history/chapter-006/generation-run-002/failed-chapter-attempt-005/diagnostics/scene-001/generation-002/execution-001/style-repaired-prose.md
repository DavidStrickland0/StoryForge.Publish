The brick wall was cold through his jacket. Ethan pressed his forehead against it, letting the chill seep into his skin, a sharp, physical anchor in a world that felt increasingly porous. His breathing was still ragged, shallow bursts of air that didn’t seem to fill his lungs. He waited for the vibration in his teeth to stop, for the roaring in his ears to fade into the mundane hum of the city.

It took a minute. Maybe two.

The street was quiet now. The crowd had thinned, dispersing back into the flow of traffic, their attention shifting to the next spectacle or the next delivery. A taxi idled at the curb, its driver looking out the windshield with the bored detachment of someone who had seen too much to be surprised by anything. Ethan pulled his hands out of his pockets. They were still trembling, but less violently now. He flexed his fingers, watching them move, checking that they were still his.

He checked his pockets again. He found his keys in his pocket. His wallet was there too. His phone was there too. All there. Nothing had been stolen. No one had touched him. The reality of it was absurd. He had been three feet from a broken god, and the world had continued to spin, indifferent and orderly.

He turned away from the wall.

The walk home was three blocks. He knew every crack in the pavement, every uneven tile, every shadow cast by the streetlights. Usually, this familiarity was a comfort, a map of his own making. Today, it felt like a cage. He kept his head down, his steps quick and deliberate, trying to match the rhythm of the pedestrians around him. He didn't look at their faces. He didn't want to see the reflection of his own fear in their eyes, or worse, their confusion.

A news van was parked near the intersection, its satellite dish angled toward the sky. A reporter was standing on the curb, speaking into a microphone, her voice amplified and distorted by the speaker on the van. Ethan walked past, his ears straining, not out of curiosity, but out of a desperate need to know what was being said. Was his name mentioned? Was he identified? Had someone seen him standing there, frozen?

"…unprecedented incident…" the reporter’s voice crackled. "…Aurora’s team is requesting privacy…" "…police are cordon-ing off the area…"

Ethan didn't stop. He kept walking. The words washed over him, meaningless noise, a static that filled the air. He was a ghost in his own city, moving through a world that was already reconstructing the events of the last ten minutes without him.

He reached his building. The doorman, Ray, was at his desk, looking up as the bell chimed. His eyes widened slightly, scanning Ethan’s face, looking for blood, for injury, for a reason to call security. He found none. He nodded, a professional gesture, and slid the door open.

Ethan stepped inside. The air in the lobby was stale, recycled, smelling of dust and old carpet. He walked to the elevator, pressing the button for his floor. The doors closed, sealing him in a small metal box. He stood there, listening to the hum of the machinery, the quiet tick of the floor indicator rising.

When the doors opened, he walked to his apartment. The key card took two attempts before the reader accepted it. He pushed through the door and waited for the deadbolt to engage behind him. The silence of the small space was immediate, a heavy, pressing weight that cut off the world outside.

He stood there, with his keys still in his hand, the door closed, the world sealed away.
