The corner was quiet, but the silence felt brittle, ready to snap. Ethan pressed his forehead against the cool brick, the rough texture grounding him against the vertigo that still threatened to pull him under. He breathed in, counting to four, and out, counting to four again. His lungs burned, a sharp, acidic ache that reminded him he was still alive, still flesh, still bound by the same fragile laws that had just claimed Aurora.

He straightened, his legs trembling so violently he had to brace a hand against the wall to keep from buckling. The street was empty, save for a few onlookers further down, their phones raised like talismans. They were recording, documenting, turning the impossible into data. Ethan pulled his jacket tighter, the fabric offering no comfort, only a thin barrier against the cold air that seemed to seep into his bones.

He started walking.

The path back to his apartment was longer than it should have been. He took the longer route, avoiding the main avenue where the news vans were already gathering, their red lights cutting through the morning haze like warning beacons. He walked past the bakery where the smell of yeast and burnt sugar usually made his stomach growl, but today the scent was just another intrusion, another reminder of a world that continued to function while his had just shattered.

People looked at him. He could feel their eyes on his back, the weight of their curiosity pressing against his shoulders. He didn't look back. He didn't know what face he was wearing, but he suspected it was the face of a man who had seen something he couldn't explain, something he wanted to forget. He kept his head down, his steps quick and deliberate, moving through the city like a ghost haunting his own life.

The transition from the public street to the private hallway of his building felt like crossing a threshold into another era. The lobby was dim, the air stale with the smell of carpet cleaner and dust. The doorman, Ray, looked up from his tablet, his eyes flicking to Ethan’s face, then to the street behind him. There was a question there, unasked, hanging in the air like smoke.

Ethan met his gaze, then looked away. He didn’t have an answer. He didn’t have words. He just nodded, a small, jerky movement, and pushed through the elevator doors.

The ride up was silent, the only sound the hum of the machinery and the rapid thumping of his own heart. He stood with his back to the mirror, refusing to see his own reflection. When the doors opened, he stepped out into the hallway, the quiet of the corridor wrapping around him like a shroud.

He entered his apartment, the door clicking shut behind him with a finality that echoed in the small space. The silence was immediate, thick and heavy. It was the same silence he had left behind that morning, but now it felt different. It was no longer a refuge. It was a waiting room.

Ethan stood in the center of the living room, his hands still at his sides, his fingers curled into fists. The apartment smelled of lavender detergent and old coffee, the familiar scents of a life that now felt impossibly distant. He looked at the Lens dock on the table, the small blue indicator light blinking steadily, indifferent to the chaos unfolding outside.

He didn’t touch it. He didn’t move. He just stood there, breathing in the stale air, waiting for the world to catch up with him.
