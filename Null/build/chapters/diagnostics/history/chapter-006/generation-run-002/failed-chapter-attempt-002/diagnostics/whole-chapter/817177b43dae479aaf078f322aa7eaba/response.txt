The siren cut off abruptly as the ambulance doors slammed shut. Aurora blinked against the sudden dimness, her head throbbing in time with the rhythmic rocking of the vehicle. The paramedic's face swam into view above her, his voice urgent but calm.

"Stay with me, Ms. Vale. You're doing great. Just keep breathing."

Aurora tried to focus on the sound of his voice, the steady beep of the monitors, anything to distract from the fire in her arm, the ache in her ribs, the grinding pain in her skull. She felt like a broken toy, her parts scattered and useless.

The paramedic's hands moved quickly, checking her vitals, adjusting the oxygen mask, recording the readouts on the screen above her head. Aurora watched him, marveling at the competence, the efficiency, the matter-of-fact concern.

"Your heart rate is still elevated," he said, glancing at the monitor. "But that's understandable given the circumstances. Just try to relax."

Relax. The word sent a spike of frustration through her. Relax was not an option, not when every breath sent a stab of pain through her chest, not when the slightest movement made her head swim and her vision blur.

She closed her eyes, fighting down the nausea rising in her throat. When she opened them again, the paramedic was holding a syringe, the needle glinting under the harsh lights of the ambulance.

"What's that for?" The words came out slurred, her tongue thick and uncooperative.

"Pain management," he replied, his voice gentle but firm. "It'll help with the discomfort. Just try to stay still."

Aurora felt the pinch of the needle, the slow spread of cool relief through her veins. The pain receded, replaced by a fuzzy warmth, a distant buzzing that seemed to come from inside her skull.

She looked down at her arm, the broken one, the one that had been invulnerable mere hours ago. The paramedic had splinted it, wrapped it in a thick layer of bandages, but even through the haze of the drugs, she could feel the wrongness of it, the foreign angle, the grating of bones against each other.

"Fracture," she said, the word strange and unfamiliar in her mouth. "Broken."

The paramedic nodded, his expression sympathetic. "That's right. A clean break, though. It'll heal."

Aurora shook her head, the movement sending a wave of dizziness through her. "No. It won't. Not like this."

The paramedic's eyebrows rose, a question forming on his lips. But before he could speak, a sound cut through the hum of the ambulance, a low, steady beep that seemed to come from the very walls themselves.

Aurora felt it in her bones, a vibration that made her teeth ache, her skin prickle. She looked at the paramedic, saw the change in his expression, the sudden tension in his shoulders.

"What is that?" Her voice was sharp, clearer now, the drug fading enough for fear to take its place.

The paramedic's eyes flicked to the monitor, to the screen that had been displaying her vitals, her heart rate, her oxygen levels. Now it was flashing, a series of red numbers that seemed to pulse in time with the beeping.

"Ms. Vale," the paramedic said, his voice calm but urgent. "I need you to listen to me very carefully. Your powers are back. They're... they're not stable, but they're present."

Aurora felt the words like a physical blow, a punch to the chest that stole her breath. She looked at her hands, at the way the light seemed to bend around them, at the way her skin seemed to glow with an inner fire.

"Impossible," she whispered, the word barely audible over the beeping of the ambulance. "They're not supposed to... to come back like this. Not after a fall. Not after..."

She couldn't finish the sentence, couldn't bring herself to say the word that hung between them, the one that explained the unexplainable, the impossible.

The paramedic was already moving, his hands flying over the controls, muting the beeping, silencing the alarms.

"Ms. Vale," he said, his voice low, intense. "I need you to do something for me. I need you to try to control it. To... to push it away from you. Can you do that? Can you make it go away?"

Aurora looked at her hands, at the way the light seemed to dance around her fingers, at the way the air seemed to bend and twist in her presence. She closed her eyes, focusing on her breath, on the steady rise and fall of her chest, on the rhythm of her heart, on the pulse of her powers beneath her skin.

She pushed.

It was like trying to hold back a tidal wave, like trying to stop a hurricane with a single thought. The power surged, a rush of energy that made her skin tingle, her hair stand on end, her body thrum with a force that seemed to come from within her very bones.

And then, as suddenly as it had come, it was gone.

The ambulance fell silent, the beeping fading away, the monitors returning to their normal, steady rhythm.

Aurora opened her eyes, looking at the paramedic, at the way he was watching her, his face a mask of awe and wonder and fear.

"It's gone," she said, her voice soft, uncertain. "I... I don't know how long it will last."

The paramedic nodded, his eyes never leaving hers. "That's okay," he said, his voice gentle, reassuring. "That's good, actually. It means we have a chance to figure this out. To... to understand what happened to you."

Aurora looked down at her arm, at the way it still ached, still felt wrong, still refused to heal the way it should.

"What happened to me," she said, the words tasting bitter in her mouth, "is that I fell. I... I broke."

The paramedic's face softened, a flicker of understanding, of sympathy. "Yes," he said, his voice careful, cautious. "You did. But... but you also came back. You survived. And that means something, Ms. Vale. It means that whatever happened, whatever caused this... this temporary loss of your abilities, it's not... it's not permanent. It's not irreversible."

Aurora looked at him, at the way he was watching her, at the way he seemed to be trying to reassure her, to comfort her, to give her hope.

But all she could feel was the ache in her arm, the pain in her head, the fear in her heart.

She closed her eyes, letting her head fall back against the pillow, letting the exhaustion, the pain, the confusion wash over her.

"Ms. Vale?" The paramedic's voice was soft, gentle. "Are you... are you all right?"

Aurora opened her eyes, looking at him, at the way he seemed to be holding himself back, at the way he seemed to be waiting for her to tell him what to do next.

She took a breath, a deep, shuddering breath that seemed to shake her entire body.

"No," she said, the word barely a whisper. "I'm not all right. I... I don't know what's happening. I don't know how to fix this."

The paramedic reached out, his hand hovering over hers, a question in his eyes.

"Then," he said, his voice soft, reassuring, "let's... let's take this one step at a time. Let's get you to the hospital, let's get you checked out. And... and from there, we can figure out what to do next. Together."

Aurora looked at him, at the way he seemed to be offering her a lifeline, a way out of the confusion, the fear, the pain.

She nodded, a small, jerky movement that sent a fresh wave of pain through her skull.

"Okay," she said, her voice barely audible over the hum of the ambulance. "Okay. To the hospital. Please."

The paramedic nodded, his hand moving to the controls, his voice murmuring into the radio, calling for the hospital, for the next step, for the chance to figure this out, to understand what had happened, what was happening, what would happen next.

And as the ambulance surged forward, as the lights of the city streamed by outside the windows, Aurora closed her eyes, letting the drugs pull her under, letting the exhaustion, the pain, the fear wash over her.

She would wake up in the hospital, she would face the doctors, the tests, the questions.

But for now, for this moment, all she could do was let herself fall, let herself be carried, let herself be taken care of, let herself be... be human.

For the first time in her life, Aurora Vale let herself be vulnerable.
