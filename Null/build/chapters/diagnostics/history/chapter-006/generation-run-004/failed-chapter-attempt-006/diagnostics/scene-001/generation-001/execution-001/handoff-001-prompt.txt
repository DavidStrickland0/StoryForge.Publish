It moved with the same deliberate, controlled speed as any other city vehicle, sliding into the flow of traffic that had begun to reassert itself around the blockade.

Ethan watched it go. He stood on the corner, his back still pressed against the cool brick of the building, his hands hanging loosely at his sides. The tremor in his fingers had subsided, replaced by a numbness that felt more permanent, a cold that seemed to have settled into his bones.

To catch the faces of the medics, the confused expressions of the onlookers, the blurred motion of the ambulance disappearing around the bend.

A woman in a bright yellow coat stepped forward, her phone extended on a selfie stick, her voice carrying over the din.

"Is this live? Can you see the spot? The concrete is actually cracked. Look at that. Who would do this? Who would let this happen?"

She was speaking to no one and everyone, her eyes wide with a mixture of fear and excitement. Ethan looked at her, really looked at her, and felt a strange detachment. She was part of the machinery of the event, a gear in the machine that turned tragedy into content, into discussion, into something that could be consumed and discarded.
