The walk home was a study in controlled disassociation.

Ethan moved with the rigid, mechanical precision of a man walking a tightrope. His feet struck the pavement in a steady, unvarying rhythm. *His feet struck the pavement in a steady, unvarying rhythm. Each step was a small, solid anchor to reality. Each step was a small, solid anchor to reality.* Each impact was a small, solid anchor to reality, a counterweight to the hollow, ringing sensation in his skull. He kept his eyes fixed on the ground, tracking the cracks in the concrete, the texture of the asphalt, the way the morning light caught the wet spots left by the night’s rain. If he looked up, he would see the world. And the world, he was beginning to suspect, was no longer safe.

The street, which had been a trickle of early commuters twenty minutes ago, was now a stagnant pool of stopped traffic and gathered bodies. Cars sat with their engines running, doors open, drivers leaning out of windows with their arms crossed. People stood in small, tight clusters on the sidewalks, their faces illuminated by the blue glow of their phones. The air was thick with the static charge of a thousand screens, a low-frequency buzz that seemed to vibrate in Ethan’s teeth.

He passed a group of teenagers, their heads bent together, speaking in rapid, urgent whispers. They didn’t look at him. They looked at the sky, then down at their devices, then back at the sky. The cycle repeated, a frantic, circular motion of verification and doubt.

A news van had pulled up to the curb three blocks back, its red beacon flashing a slow, rhythmic pulse that cut through the morning haze. A camera operator was wrestling a tripod into place, his movements hurried and clumsy. A reporter, a woman with a microphone clipped to her lapel, was speaking into a handheld mic, her voice amplified by a small speaker on the van.

“...unprecedented incident in the city center,” she said, her tone professional but tight, stripped of its usual polish. “Eyewitnesses report that Ms. Vale was suspended in mid-air when her powers appeared to fail completely. She fell a significant distance before impact. Medical personnel are on the scene, but the extent of her injuries remains unknown. We are seeing no signs of healing, which is highly unusual for a Class-1 super. Questions are mounting about the cause, and whether this is an isolated event or a new threat to public safety.”

Ethan slowed his pace. The words washed over him, detached from the speaker, floating in the cool morning air. *Class-1 super.* *No signs of healing.* *New threat.*

He felt a sudden, sharp pain in his chest, a physical ache that had nothing to do with his ribs. It was the weight of the unknown, pressing down on him with a force that was almost tangible. He was part of this now. He was a witness, a bystander, a piece of the puzzle that he didn’t understand and couldn’t explain.

He kept walking.

The crowd parted around him, a sea of faces turning toward the news van, toward the stage, toward the sky. They were looking for answers. They were looking for a culprit. They were looking for a reason.

Ethan kept his eyes on the ground. He kept his hands in his pockets. He kept moving, one step at a time, until the sound of the reporter’s voice faded into the background hum of the city, and the world felt solid again.
