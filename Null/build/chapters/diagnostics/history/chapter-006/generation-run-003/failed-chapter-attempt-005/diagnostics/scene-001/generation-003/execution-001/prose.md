The street was quiet, but it was the wrong kind of quiet. It wasn’t the peaceful silence of an early morning; it was the held breath of a city that had seen something it couldn’t explain.

Ethan kept his head down, his fingers digging into the fabric of his jacket. He had walked three blocks before he realized he was still holding his breath. He exhaled, a long, shaky release that fogged in the cool air, and forced his legs to keep moving. The rhythm of his steps—*step, step, step*—was the only anchor he had. Anything else was likely to shatter.

He passed the first news van. It was parked askew on the corner, its antenna slicing into the sky like a metal finger pointing at an invisible god. A cameraman was adjusting a lens, his face a mask of professional detachment despite the chaos unfolding behind him. Two reporters stood on the curb, their microphones out, shouting over each other, their voices distorted by the wind and their own desperation to be first.

"…confirmed that the incident occurred at the Meridian Center…"
"…officials have not released a statement…"
"…the cause remains unknown…"

Ethan walked faster. The words blurred into a static hum. He didn’t want to know what they said. He didn’t want to know if they were asking questions he was too afraid to ask. He just wanted to be somewhere that didn’t smell like ozone and fear.

He turned onto a side street, a narrower road lined with closed shops and parked cars. The news vans were still visible in the distance, a cluster of flashing lights that looked more like a distant storm than a local event. He could see the reflection of his own face in the window of a shuttered bakery. It looked pale. It looked like a ghost.

He stopped walking. His back hit the wall of the building, and he slid down until he was sitting on the cold concrete. The vibration of the city was different here. It wasn’t the hum of anticipation he had felt in the crowd; it was a low, thrumming anxiety. Sirens wailed in the distance, overlapping and indistinguishable.

He looked at his hands again. They were shaking.

*She is just flesh.*

The medic’s voice echoed in his head. *No bio-electric field. No energy signature.*

Ethan closed his eyes. He tried to remember the bar. He tried to remember FireMan, the way his skin had turned red and hot, the way he had laughed, confident and alive. And then he tried to remember the moment it stopped. The sudden drop. The silence.

He had thought it was a fluke. A glitch. A bad day.

But looking at the flashing lights in the distance, he felt a cold certainty settle in his stomach. It wasn’t a glitch. It was a rule. And he was the exception.

He didn’t want to be an exception. He wanted to be invisible. He wanted to be nothing.

But now, there were cameras. There were microphones. There were thousands of people who had watched a god fall, and they would be looking for the reason. They would be looking for the cause.

And he was the only one who knew what it felt like to stand in the middle of it.

Ethan stood up. He brushed the dust from his trousers. He straightened his jacket. He looked back toward the main street, toward the cluster of lights, and then he turned away.

He had a place to go. He had a life to maintain. And he had a secret that was growing heavier with every second that passed.

He started walking again. This time, he didn’t look back.
