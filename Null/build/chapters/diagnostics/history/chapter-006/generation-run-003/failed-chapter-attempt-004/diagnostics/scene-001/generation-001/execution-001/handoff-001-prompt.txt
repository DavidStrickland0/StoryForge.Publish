It was thinner, charged with a static tension that made the hair on his arms stand up.

People stared. That was the new thing. Not with awe, as they did when Aurora was flying, but with a wary, searching intensity. They looked at him as if he might be the next one to fall, or the one who had pushed her. He kept his head down, his hands buried deep in the pockets of his jacket, trying to make himself small.

The concrete was still dark with the stain of blood and dust.

Ethan turned a corner, his heart hammering against his ribs. He needed to be inside. He needed the four walls of his apartment to close around him, to separate him from the public gaze.

He froze, listening for any sound from the other apartments. There was nothing. Just the hum of the building’s ventilation system.

He bent down, his joints stiff and aching, and retrieved the key. It felt heavy in his palm, a small, cold metal anchor to reality.

And he had walked away.

The realization settled in his stomach, cold and heavy. He hadn’t run because he was guilty. He had walked away because he was afraid. Afraid of what he was. Afraid of what he had done. Or what he hadn’t done.
