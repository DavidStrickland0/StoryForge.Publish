The walk home was a blur of grey concrete and muted voices. Ethan kept his head down, eyes fixed on the pavement, counting his steps to keep the rhythm steady. One, two, three. The same pattern. The same safe, predictable cadence.

He passed the newsstand on the corner of 4th and Main. The paper was already out, the headline stark and black against the white newsprint. *AURORA FALLS.* The photograph was grainy, taken from a distance, but the white suit was unmistakable. He stared at it for a second longer than he should have, feeling the heat of the crowd’s anxiety radiating from the screen. He quickly looked away, tucking his hands deeper into his jacket pockets.

The streets were quieter than usual. People walked faster, glancing at the sky even though it was clear. A few cars had stopped at the red light, the drivers looking toward the venue, their faces tight with disbelief. Ethan felt like a ghost moving through a world that had suddenly remembered its own fragility.

He turned the corner onto his street. The building was three blocks away, but it felt like miles. His legs were heavy, each step a deliberate effort to move his body forward. He could still feel the vibration of the impact in his teeth, a phantom shudder that refused to fade. He could still see the way Aurora’s head had turned, the way her eyes had focused on the medic, the way her voice had cracked when she whispered for help.

*The word was flesh.*

The word echoed in his mind, a dark, heavy anchor. He had always known it was true, intellectually, but seeing it, feeling the reality of it settle into his bones, was different. It was terrifying. It was freeing.

He reached the building’s entrance and paused. The lobby was empty, the doorman, Ray, not yet at his post. Ethan stepped inside, the automatic doors sliding shut behind him with a soft hiss. The silence of the hallway was a relief, a thick, protective blanket that muffled the world outside.

He took the elevator up, standing still in the center of the car, his reflection staring back at him. He looked pale, his eyes hollow. He looked like someone who had seen something they couldn’t explain and had decided, for the sake of their sanity, to pretend it hadn’t happened.

The doors opened on his floor. He walked to his apartment, the key in his hand, and unlocked the door.

He stepped inside and closed the door behind him, the lock clicking into place with a finality that felt like a prayer.

He stood there for a moment, listening to the hum of the refrigerator, the tick of the clock, the distant rumble of the city.

He was home.
