Chapter 6: The Aftermath

Aurora's eyes fluttered open as the ambulance jolted over a pothole. The pain lanced through her like a thousand needles, sharp and insistent. She gasped, her breath coming in shallow, rapid bursts. Her hands twitched, fingers curling instinctively. The medic beside her reached out, his gloved hand steadying her shoulder.

"Easy," he said, his voice calm and soothing. "You're okay. We're almost at the hospital."

Aurora shook her head, wincing as the motion sent fresh spikes of agony through her injured arm. "My powers," she rasped, her throat dry and raw. "They're back. But... it hurts. Everything hurts."

The medic nodded, glancing at the monitor displaying her vitals. "That's normal. Your body's been through a lot. The doctors will take care of you."

Aurora looked down at her arm, at the angry purple bruise spreading from elbow to wrist. She flexed her fingers, the movement stiff and awkward. "I fell," she whispered, the realization still sinking in. "I fell, and... I couldn't fly. I couldn't do anything."

The medic's expression softened. "You're alive," he said simply. "That's what matters."

Aurora closed her eyes, letting the steady thrum of the ambulance's engine lull her into a restless doze. Her mind raced, replaying the incident in fractured, disorienting flashes. The sudden loss of power. The sickening drop. The impact, jarring and brutal. The faces of the crowd, a sea of shocked, concerned strangers.

She'd never been vulnerable like that before. Never helpless, never breakable. The fear that had gripped her in those moments, the desperate, animal panic, still lingered in her chest like a coiled spring.

The ambulance slowed, tires crunching on gravel. Aurora tensed, bracing herself for another jolt. But the vehicle rolled to a stop, the rear doors swinging open with a hiss of hydraulics. The medic guided her out, supporting her as she stepped down onto the concrete. The hospital entrance loomed before her, a blur of lights and motion.

Inside, the staff moved with practiced efficiency. They transferred her to a gurney, wheeling her through a maze of hallways. Voices murmured around her, low and urgent. The lights seemed too bright, the air too cold.

In the examination room, a doctor examined her arm, prodding and poking with gentle fingers. Aurora gritted her teeth, determined not to cry out. The doctor's face was a mask, eyes visible above it, concerned and careful.

"She's stable," the doctor said to someone out of Aurora's line of sight. "Broken arm, multiple contusions. No signs of internal injury. We'll need X-rays."

The words washed over her, a distant buzz of noise. Aurora looked down at her arm, at the bruises already darkening her skin. She could feel the bones, too, the way they shifted beneath the skin, grinding against each other. Her stomach churned, bile rising in her throat.

The doctor returned, X-ray images in hand. She pointed to a screen, showing Aurora the images of her arm, the bones clean and sharp against the white background. "You were lucky," the doctor said. "A clean break. It'll heal in time."

Aurora nodded, not trusting herself to speak. She looked at the images, at the evidence of her vulnerability, her humanity. It was strange, seeing herself like this, seeing the damage her body could do. She'd always thought of herself as invulnerable, as untouchable. Now, here she was, broken and hurting, just like everyone else.

The doctor wrapped her arm in a cast, the plaster cool against her skin. "Rest," the doctor said, "and physical therapy. You'll be flying again in no time."

Aurora managed a smile, a weak, wavering thing. "Thank you," she whispered.

She lay in the hospital bed that night, staring at the ceiling, at the shadows cast by the flickering lights of the city beyond the window. Her mind raced, thoughts tumbling over each other in a dizzying cascade. She'd fallen. She'd fallen, and she hadn't been able to stop herself. The memory of that helplessness, that moment when she'd realized she was just a person, just a woman, just flesh... it haunted her.

She closed her eyes, trying to will herself to sleep. But the memories wouldn't let her go. They played over and over in her mind, a constant, maddening loop of fear and failure.

She'd fallen. And she didn't know why.

The next morning, she would demand answers. She would find out what had happened, what had caused her to fall, to lose her powers. She would fix it. She would make herself better than before.

For now, though, she lay in the hospital bed, in the dark, and she wept.

She wept for herself, for her fear, for her pain. She wept for the world that had seen her fall, that now knew she was vulnerable, that now knew she could be broken.

She wept, and she didn't know if she would ever stop.

In the morning, the sun would rise, and the world would continue. And she would be there, ready to face it, ready to fight, ready to fly.

But for now, for this moment, she wept.

And she didn't know if anyone could hear her.
