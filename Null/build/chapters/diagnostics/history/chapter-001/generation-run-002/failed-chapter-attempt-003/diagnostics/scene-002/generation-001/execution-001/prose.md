Ethan stepped out of the main bar area into the dim light near the door, moving away from the woman and the super who were still at a table near the corner. The bass from the stage vibrated through the floorboards, rattling in his chest, but he kept his gaze fixed on the pavement outside where he could see the rain beginning to fall. He didn't look back to check if the man had followed him or to see how the woman was reacting to the situation. Self-preservation meant knowing when to walk away from things that weren't yours to fix.

He started moving again, heading for the exit on the opposite side of the establishment, his steps quiet and careful against the slick concrete. The music seemed louder now, drowning out everything else except the sound of his own breathing. He could feel the weight of the woman's fear pressing against him like a physical thing, as if the universe itself was trying to tell him something he wasn't ready to hear yet. But there was nothing more he could do but keep walking.

The bar felt too small for the violence that had just happened inside it, yet every corner seemed to hold another potential threat. Ethan kept his head down, scanning the perimeter for anyone who might follow him outside. He knew better than to make eye contact with people like that. Supers were celebrities in this city; they owned the streets and the laws that protected them. Normal people were invisible and vulnerable, existing only to serve as background noise or disposable collateral if things went wrong.

He reached the door and pushed it open, stepping into the cool night air. The rain felt refreshing against his skin, washing away some of the sweat from his earlier anxiety. He didn't stop to look back at the bar one more time. There was no point in checking on the woman or finding out what had happened inside. His only priority was getting home before anyone else decided to follow him.

As he walked down the street, he tried to convince himself that he hadn't just witnessed something terrible and done nothing about it. He told himself it wasn't his place to interfere with situations involving supers. He reminded himself that normal people didn't have any power in a world like this. But even as he repeated these rationalizations to himself, he felt the cold water of doubt seeping into his thoughts.

The street was mostly empty except for a few other pedestrians hurrying under their umbrellas or into nearby storefronts. He kept his pace steady, avoiding eye contact with anyone who might be watching him too closely. The rain continued to fall steadily now, mixing with the neon reflections on the pavement. He wondered how long it would take before someone noticed that he was missing from the bar, and then what would happen when they realized where he had been.

He didn't want to think about those questions yet. He just wanted to get home and pretend that nothing had happened at all.
