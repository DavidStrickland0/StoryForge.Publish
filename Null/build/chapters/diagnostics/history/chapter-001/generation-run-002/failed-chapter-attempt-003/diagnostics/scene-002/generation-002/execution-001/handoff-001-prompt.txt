He wasn’t a hero; he was a man with a job, rent due next month, and a life that required him to keep his head down when things got ugly. He was dangerous in the way only someone who could crush concrete with a casual grip could be. A normal person stepping between them would end up as broken glass before security even arrived to question why the crowd had stopped screaming.

Ethan slowed his pace, not because he wanted to watch, but because leaving looked like admitting defeat. The woman’s hands were still pressed together in her lap, trembling slightly beneath the table. She hadn’t moved since the super leaned closer, his voice low enough that only they could hear it, probably telling her exactly what he expected from her tonight. The bar was full of people who’d seen this kind of thing before and decided to look away, to pay their tab and walk out like nothing had changed.

He didn’t need to be a superhero to know the difference between helping someone and throwing yourself into a wall you couldn’t win against. He turned his collar up against the sudden chill that had nothing to do with temperature, more like the cold of realizing he’d just walked past something he should have stopped. The music swelled again, drowning out the sound of her breathing, but Ethan could still feel it like a second heartbeat next to his own.

His feet carried him forward without permission, toward the door where the air felt cooler and safer, even if only for a few seconds.
