The air inside *Neon & Nitro* tasted like expensive perfume, stale beer, and desperation. It was a place where the rules of physics bent to accommodate the wealthy and the powerful, and Ethan Vale felt like an insect trapped under glass in a room full of gods. He shifted his weight on his boots, keeping his posture loose, eyes scanning the perimeter for any sign that he needed to bolt.

The bar wasn't just loud; it was a cacophony of celebrity culture. On stage, a woman named Vortex spun through the air without touching the floor, her hair whipping in a wind she generated herself with a casual flick of her wrist. The crowd cheered as if witnessing a miracle, not just a Tuesday night at a dive bar. At the tables, men and women with glowing tattoos or eyes that shimmered like stained glass drank cocktails while normal people waited on their behalf, clutching menus they didn't want to read.

Ethan found himself standing near the kitchen entrance, a convenient blind spot for someone trying not to be seen. He wasn't there to party. He worked late shifts at his part-time job at the electronics store around the corner, and this was just the detour where he waited for the last shift bus. The bus schedule had been posted outside, but he’d checked three times already: it ran until midnight, which was an hour past when he needed to be home.

He kept his head down, focusing on the rhythm of a song playing too loudly in his ears. The bass thumped against his chest, a reminder that even here, music could rattle your bones without anyone needing to touch you. He watched a group of patrons laughing at a joke no one else seemed to hear. One of them was a man with skin like polished marble who had just finished telling a story about how he’d once caught a falling airplane. The crowd roared in appreciation, their faces flushed and eyes wide with the kind of reverence usually reserved for saints or saviors.

Ethan felt the familiar weight of invisibility pressing against his shoulders. It wasn’t fear—fear was too simple a word for what it felt like to be ignored by people who could crush you with a thought—and more like shame. He knew he looked ordinary, which in this world meant he was invisible. He didn’t have powers. He didn’t glow. He didn’t fly. He existed only because someone else had decided not to erase him from existence.

He took another step toward the door, his hand resting on the handle. The night air outside promised relief, but also danger. Supers could be dangerous anywhere, even when they weren’t trying to hurt anyone. He remembered reading about a man who’d been turned into a statue just for looking at the wrong person the wrong way. Normal people in this city lived their lives with one foot in a trap and the other in a cage.

Inside, the bass line hit again, vibrating through his bones. He paused, listening to the song finish, then let out a small breath. He wasn’t here to be heroic. He wasn’t even here to be noticed. He was just Ethan Vale, waiting for the bus, trying to avoid trouble while pretending not to care about the chaos unfolding around him.

He stepped back into the dim light of the bar’s interior, eyes darting toward the exit. The door opened a little wider, letting in a gust of cool air that smelled of rain and wet pavement. He could feel his heart rate rising slightly as he scanned the room again, looking for anything that might make him run.

Nothing yet. Just the usual noise, the usual mix of adoration and indifference. And then, near the bar’s edge where the exit stood, a figure caught his eye.

It was a woman. She looked young, maybe in her early twenties, with messy hair pulled back by a rubber band and wearing a jacket that had seen better days. She sat at a small table near the corner, staring into her drink like it held all the secrets of the universe. Her shoulders were hunched slightly forward, as if trying to make herself smaller than the space she occupied.

Ethan recognized the look immediately. It was the same expression he’d worn in front of mirrors for years when he felt like a ghost haunting his own life. This girl wasn’t special or powerful or lucky. She was just someone who had been handed a world where she didn’t belong, and she was trying to survive it by holding her breath until someone else noticed.

Then another figure appeared beside her. Tall, broad-shouldered, with skin that shimmered faintly in the bar’s neon lights. He wore a suit that cost more than Ethan made in a week, and he didn’t need to look at anyone to know they were afraid. His presence filled the air like static electricity before a storm, and the woman beside him flinched when he stepped closer.

Ethan’s stomach tightened as he watched the man reach out and touch her arm. She didn’t pull away. Not because she was weak or helpless, but because there was no point in resisting. He could feel the tension in her body, the way her muscles tensed beneath her clothes, ready to run if she could. But she couldn’t. Not yet.

The man smiled down at her, his expression soft but his eyes cold. “You’re not going anywhere,” he said, his voice low enough that only those nearby could hear. “Not unless I say so.”

Ethan turned away before anyone else could notice him watching. He didn’t want to be the guy who watched a woman get harassed by someone she couldn’t fight back against. It wasn’t bravery or courage or anything noble; it was just self-preservation. And in this world, self-preservation meant knowing when to walk away from things that weren’t yours to fix.

He started moving again, heading for the exit, his steps quiet and careful. The music seemed louder now, drowning out everything else except the sound of his own breathing. He could feel the weight of the woman’s fear pressing against him like a physical thing, as if the universe itself was trying to tell him something he wasn’t ready to hear yet.

But there was nothing more he could do but keep walking.
