The bass from the stage thumped against his ribs, a physical reminder of the hollowness waiting inside that room. Ethan kept his eyes on the floor tiles, counting steps rather than faces, trying to convince himself he was just another patron leaving before closing. The woman at the table near the corner was invisible to him now, just part of the static noise that filled his mind when he stared too long at something he couldn't fix.

He passed the kitchen entrance and felt a fresh wave of heat rising in his chest, not from the room’s air conditioning failing, but from the sheer weight of complicity pressing down on his shoulders. He wasn’t a hero; he was a man with a job, rent due next month, and a life that required him to keep his head down when things got ugly. The super standing beside her—Vortex, based on the shimmering veins running beneath skin that looked too smooth, too perfect for a Tuesday night—wasn’t just imposing. He was dangerous in the way only someone who could crush concrete with a casual grip could be. A normal person stepping between them would end up as broken glass before security even arrived to question why the crowd had stopped screaming.

Ethan slowed his pace, not because he wanted to watch, but because leaving looked like admitting defeat. The woman’s hands were still pressed together in her lap, trembling slightly beneath the table. She hadn’t moved since the super leaned closer, his voice low enough that only they could hear it, probably telling her exactly what he expected from her tonight. The bar was full of people who’d seen this kind of thing before and decided to look away, to pay their tab and walk out like nothing had changed.

He didn’t need to be a superhero to know the difference between helping someone and throwing yourself into a wall you couldn’t win against. He turned his collar up against the sudden chill that had nothing to do with temperature, more like the cold of realizing he’d just walked past something he should have stopped. The music swelled again, drowning out the sound of her breathing, but Ethan could still feel it like a second heartbeat next to his own.

His feet carried him forward without permission, toward the door where the air felt cooler and safer, even if only for a few seconds. He kept his gaze fixed on the floor tiles outside, avoiding the way the super’s shadow stretched across the exit, marking territory he didn’t belong in. The woman hadn’t moved since he passed her table, still sitting there as if rooted to the spot by something invisible, by something that made her too afraid to run even when the super was gone from view.

Ethan stopped at the threshold, one foot on the sidewalk where the streetlights hummed and cars idled in the distance. He could hear the bar’s music still pounding through the walls, but now it felt distant, like he was listening to someone else’s life play out behind a glass wall. He took another step outside, then paused, checking his reflection in the darkened window of a parked car. His face looked tired, older than it should be for someone who’d just spent two hours watching a woman get harassed without doing anything about it.

He started walking again, hands shoved deep into his pockets, trying to feel something other than the hollow ache spreading through his chest. The night air smelled like rain and exhaust fumes, nothing like the stale beer and perfume that filled the bar. He didn’t look back, knowing he wouldn’t see anything but empty space where the bar’s exit had been just moments ago.
