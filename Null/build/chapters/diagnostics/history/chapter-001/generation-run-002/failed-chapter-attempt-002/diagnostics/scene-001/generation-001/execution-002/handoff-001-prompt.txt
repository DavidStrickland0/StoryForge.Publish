Ethan Vale adjusted the collar of his jacket, the fabric feeling slightly stiff against the humid air drifting in from the street. The bar smelled of spilled beer, stale smoke, and the underlying metallic tang of anxiety that had become a permanent fixture of the city’s nightlife. He slid into the empty stool at the end of the wooden table near the back wall, careful not to scrape the legs against the floorboards. It was a minor precaution, but in a place where physics often bent to accommodate superhuman strength, friction and noise could be as dangerous as a direct hit.

The room was crowded, a sea of dim light filtering through grimy windows and reflecting off the polished bar top. Most patrons were regulars—people who knew the unwritten rules of survival: keep your head down, avoid looking at supers, and never draw attention to yourself if you’re not one. Ethan kept his eyes on his phone, scrolling through a list of mundane tasks, trying to occupy his mind with something that didn’t require constant, high-level threat assessment.

He wasn’t looking for trouble. He was simply trying to navigate an evening without becoming a statistic or a punchline in someone else’s story. Supers were celebrities, assets, and occasionally threats, depending on the day and the mood of the crowd. Ethan had learned long ago that intervention usually made things worse. A normal person with no superhuman abilities couldn’t stop a fight between two enhanced individuals without getting crushed in the crossfire or accidentally triggering something far worse. The law existed, but it was toothless when faced with someone who could punch through reinforced concrete or fly out of reach.

His phone buzzed, and he glanced at the screen. A reminder about his shift at the grocery store tomorrow morning, followed by a notification from a friend asking if he wanted to meet up later that week. He replied with a noncommittal emoji and set the device back down. It felt wrong to leave it on the table, as if the phone itself might attract unwanted attention or draw the gaze of someone who should be ignored.

A figure loomed in the corner near the entrance, drawing stares from nearby tables but receiving no real response. Ethan didn’t need to look up to know what he was seeing: a superhuman with glowing eyes and a posture that suggested effortless dominance over the space around him. The man wore a simple black t-shirt and jeans, but his presence filled the room like a storm cloud. He wasn’t trying to intimidate anyone; he just existed in a way that made everyone else feel small. That was enough for Ethan to keep his distance and avoid eye contact.

The super’s companion—a young woman with dark hair and nervous hands—sat at the adjacent table, her shoulders hunched as if bracing against an invisible weight. She kept glancing around, searching for a way out or someone who might help, but no one moved toward her. The crowd had already decided that this was not their problem. To intervene would be foolish, to ask for help would invite ridicule or worse. Ethan watched the scene unfold with detached curiosity, noting how quickly the atmosphere shifted when the super turned his head slightly.

The woman at the table whispered something to him, but the super didn’t even acknowledge her voice. Instead, he leaned forward, his arm brushing against hers as if testing the limits of her resistance. She flinched visibly, pulling back into her chair, but the motion was too slow to avoid contact. The crowd gasped softly, then fell silent again. Ethan shifted in his stool, trying to make himself less noticeable without losing sight of what was happening.

The super’s expression remained neutral, almost bored, as if the woman’s discomfort were beneath notice. He raised a hand and waved it dismissively toward a group of patrons nearby, causing them to stumble back in surprise. No one intervened, even though there were at least three other supers in the bar who could have stopped him. The social hierarchy was absolute; normal people had no say in how things played out when power imbalances tipped too far in favor of enhancement.

Ethan took a sip of his drink, letting the bitter taste settle on his tongue. He felt a familiar knot forming in his stomach—the same sensation he’d experienced whenever someone with extraordinary abilities decided that rules didn’t apply to them. This wasn’t about malice or cruelty; it was simply how things worked. Supers could do whatever they wanted, whenever they wanted, and the consequences rarely landed on their shoulders unless someone like Aurora had intervened—and even then, not without risk.

He glanced back at the woman, who now stared at her lap, refusing to meet anyone’s gaze. Her hands trembled slightly as she clutched a napkin in both palms. Ethan wondered if she’d been here before, if this was part of her routine or just another day in a life where ordinary humanity felt like a distant memory. He didn’t know her name, didn’t care enough to ask, and certainly couldn’t do anything to help beyond sitting quietly in the corner while others watched helplessly.

The super finally turned his attention away from the woman, scanning the room with a casual sweep that made several heads turn instinctively. Ethan followed his gaze, half-expecting him to pick a fight or cause some kind of spectacle. Instead, he just walked over to the bar, ordering another drink without speaking to anyone else in the vicinity. The bartender didn’t question anything, because questioning would have been pointless anyway.

Ethan returned to his phone, scrolling through news headlines that mentioned Aurora’s latest appearance and hinted at rumors surrounding her recent fall. He read the reports with mild interest, noting how quickly the public moved from concern to speculation once a supers’ image was compromised. It was ironic that she’d spent years building herself up as a symbol of hope and strength, only for one mistake to erase months of goodwill. She was still there, staring into nothingness, her body language screaming that she needed space more than anything else in the world. Ethan considered leaving before things got worse, but he couldn’t bring himself to abandon her completely—not when he’d already paid for his drink and taken up a seat near where this all started.
