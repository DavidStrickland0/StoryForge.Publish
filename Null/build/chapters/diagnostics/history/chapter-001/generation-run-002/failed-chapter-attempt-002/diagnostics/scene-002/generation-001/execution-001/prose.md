People knew something was wrong, but no one said anything. They watched, they waited, they hoped the next minute wouldn't be the worst one yet.

Her shoulders were hunched inward as if she were trying to make herself smaller, invisible. He wanted to walk away, to let this play out without getting involved, but the woman's silence weighed on him. She had done nothing wrong except exist in a world where she wasn't strong enough to defend herself.

The patrons around them didn't move, didn't speak, didn't even whisper. They waited for something to break, something to change, but nothing did.

Ethan turned toward the exit, pulling his jacket tighter around himself. He could hear the soft creak of the floorboards beneath his feet, the low murmur of voices from the bar's main area, and the distant hum of traffic outside. He took a deep breath, trying to steady himself, but the tightness in his chest hadn't loosened. Ethan pulled out his phone again, scrolling through the news feed, looking for anything that might distract him from what he'd just seen. All he knew was that he'd left someone behind, and he didn't know if he could ever forgive himself for it.
