He was a monument of casual dominance, his shoulders broad enough to block out the light from the ceiling fixtures. Ethan took another slow sip of his own drink, though he barely tasted it. The news on his phone had been relentless lately; headlines about Aurora’s fall had turned into speculative fever dreams in the social feeds, with everyone wondering if she was hurt or just playing a game to regain attention. The super waved at a passing patron who had approached the table, dismissing the man as if he were part of the furniture. The crowd around them was silent, a sea of faces turned away or lowered eyes, watching but refusing to intervene.

The super leaned forward, his voice low enough that only those immediately nearby would hear it, yet the tone carried an authority that demanded silence from everyone else. “You’re making this harder than it needs to be,” he said, the words dripping with a boredom that felt more insulting than anger. The woman didn’t respond; she just stared at her cup, her knuckles white where they gripped the ceramic rim.

Ethan shifted on his stool, his eyes narrowing as he watched the super reach out and tap the table lightly with two fingers. A faint vibration rippled across the wood, a physical manifestation of the man’s power that made the entire surface shiver under the touch. Ethan had seen this kind of casual display before in the news clips that played on loop in his mind during long nights at home—supers who treated reality like clay they could mold with a thought or a tap. It was a reminder that normal people lived in a world where their struggles were trivial against forces like this, where strength and invulnerability were not just abilities but social hierarchies that bypassed laws and reason.

He considered standing up and offering some kind of intervention, maybe waving at the bartender to call security or someone with enough authority to make a difference. But he knew what would happen if normal people got in the way of supers. The news had been clear: attempts to intervene made things worse, turning a simple confrontation into something chaotic that left civilians worse off than before. So he stayed seated, his arms folded loosely across his chest as he watched the super’s fingers trace the edge of the table with a slow, deliberate motion.

The woman flinched when he pressed the heel of his hand against the wood, not hard enough to damage it but enough to make the entire surface tremble. Ethan noted how the vibration seemed to ripple outward from the point of contact, affecting everything around them in a subtle way that made him wonder if he was overthinking the scene or if there was something real about the man’s power that went beyond simple strength.

“Why are you even here?” the super asked, his voice rising slightly as he turned to face the woman directly. “This isn’t your problem.”

The woman didn’t answer; she just lowered her gaze, her shoulders hunching inward as if trying to make herself smaller and invisible. Ethan watched her reaction, noting how her posture reflected a fear that had nothing to do with the super’s presence but everything to do with the understanding that some people could never escape being defined by others’ power.

He felt a flicker of unease in his gut, a sensation he had ignored too many times in his life as an ordinary person in a world run by extraordinary forces. The super’s dominance was absolute, a reality that left normal people with no choice but to accept it and move on. It was a system where power dictated behavior, where laws were suggestions for the powerful and threats to be avoided rather than rules to be followed.

Ethan’s phone buzzed quietly in his pocket, a notification from a friend who must have been trying to reach him. He glanced down at the screen but didn’t bother to respond, keeping his attention fixed on the unfolding drama. The super’s casual cruelty was something he had seen before, yet it still made his stomach tighten with that familiar mix of fear and helplessness.

The vibration from the table continued to ripple outward, a reminder that this man could alter reality with the slightest gesture. Ethan realized with growing unease that something was about to go wrong, not because he understood what would happen but because the tension in the room had reached a point where even the most powerful person couldn’t ignore it forever.
