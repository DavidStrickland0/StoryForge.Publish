The sound of bass-heavy music rolled out to meet him, vibrating in his chest as he navigated the threshold.

Ethan preferred to stay near the perimeter for events like this. It wasn't that he lacked courage or a desire to be interesting; it was simply a calculation of risk versus reward that his ordinary life demanded. In a world where physical strength could level a building and flight allowed one to leave a jurisdiction before an indictment could be filed, being visibly unremarkable was the safest strategy. He moved into the main hall, his eyes tracking the perimeter rather than fixating on any single individual. The lighting was low, colored by blues and purples from the disco balls spinning overhead, but it was sufficient for him to read the body language of those closest to him without needing to shout over the music.

He took a seat at an empty table near the wall, away from the dance floor where the crowds gathered in their own spontaneous circles. A waitress approached with two menus, offering them with a practiced smile that didn't quite reach her eyes. She was young, likely hoping for a tip that would cover her rent, and she moved through the room with the fluid grace that only came from years of navigating spaces filled with people who could end a fight before it started.

"Anything to drink?" Ethan asked, his voice kept low enough not to cut through the thrumming beat.

"Just water," he said when she looked at him expectantly. "And maybe a soda."

She nodded and turned away, merging back into the flow of service. Ethan picked up his glass of water from the previous patron and took a sip, letting the cold liquid settle his stomach. He watched the room, noting the way certain people moved through it. There were supers everywhere tonight, or at least the ones with enough ego to attend charity galas or local meet-and-greets without their powers being suppressed by something they couldn't control. The hierarchy was obvious in the seating arrangements and the casualness with which they treated space that would be sacred ground for anyone else.

His hair was silvered and short, his jawline sharp enough to cut glass, and he wore a suit that cost more than Ethan earned in a year. Around him, the other supers moved with a deference that bordered on worship. They weren't afraid of him physically—he could probably lift a car without effort—but they respected the reputation he carried. He was one of the big names, someone who had saved cities from collapsing tectonic shifts and brought down meteors before they hit the ground. His power was strength, the kind that made buildings look like toy blocks.

Across the room, a group of women were dancing on the floor, their laughter rising above the music as they moved in sync with each other. Ethan kept his gaze on the man at the bar, watching how he interacted with them. It wasn't malicious; it was just the way things were. He could see the way his hand rested on one of the women's shoulders, a gesture that might have been affectionate or protective but carried an implicit weight of dominance. The woman didn't pull away, and the others around her didn't complain either. They understood the reality of the situation. If he was strong enough to lift a car, then touching someone lightly wouldn't hurt them unless he chose to snap their neck like a twig.

Ethan turned his head slightly, watching as another figure approached the group at the bar. This one was different from the older man—smaller in stature but radiating an energy that made the air around him feel heavier. His skin had taken on a strange quality under the club lights, shimmering faintly even though he wasn't using any visible abilities yet. The way he stood there, arms crossed and eyes scanning the room, made him look like a predator waiting for prey to make a mistake.

Ethan felt a familiar tightness in his chest as he watched him go, knowing he had no choice but to stay put until he found a way out that didn't involve drawing attention to himself.
