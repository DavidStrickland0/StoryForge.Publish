The tension in the room felt heavy, pressing against Ethan’s eardrums until he could taste copper in the back of his throat. He watched as the super took a sip from his glass, the liquid sliding down with a casual indifference that made the violence of his previous moments look even more calculated. The victim kept her head down, shoulders hunched as if trying to shrink into the floorboards beneath her shoes. She was trembling now, not just from fear but from the sheer physical exhaustion of holding herself together while someone this large and strong treated her like furniture.

Ethan adjusted his jacket collar for the third time in a minute. The fabric felt scratchy against his neck, but he couldn’t stop moving it, unable to look directly at the woman who had paid for a drink that was now sitting untouched on the table beside him. The news headlines about Aurora still dominated his phone screen when he last checked, the bold typeface declaring her status as both savior and celebrity, yet here she wasn’t. Here, the power structure remained raw and unfiltered.

The aggressive super leaned forward, elbows resting on the sticky surface of the table. He didn’t need to shout anymore; his voice was a low rumble that seemed to vibrate through the floorboards and into the soles of the boots he wore, though Ethan couldn’t see them clearly. “You think you can walk away from me,” the super said, gesturing with one hand as if swatting away a fly, “like I’m some inconvenience in your little world.” The victim didn’t respond. She stared at the table, her eyes dry, her expression blank as if she had already accepted that anything could happen next.

Ethan felt the weight of the room settling around him like dust motes suspended in slow motion. The other patrons hadn’t moved; they sat with their drinks untouched, watching silently, their faces pale and unreadable. There were at least three supers in the bar tonight, all sitting at tables near the front where the lighting was brighter, none of them intervening. Their inaction confirmed what Ethan had already suspected without ever being able to name it: laws meant for ordinary people didn’t apply when strength and size tipped the scales. The law was toothless against something that could shatter concrete with a single punch.

The super laughed then, a dry sound that barely registered as humor but carried more weight than most threats he’d heard in his life. He reached across the table again, this time pressing a palm flat against the victim’s cheek, not to hurt her but to demonstrate how little effort it took to control her body. She didn’t flinch, didn’t pull away, didn’t even blink. Her skin was cool beneath his fingers, normal and human in a way that seemed alien to someone who could lift cars with one hand.

Ethan tried to tell himself that he should be getting up now, sliding out from behind the empty stool he’d chosen as his vantage point, walking toward the door before whatever happened next pulled him back into danger. But something held him in place. Maybe it was the drink he’d paid for, maybe it was the fact that he’d already sat down and watched too long to leave without looking foolish. Or maybe it was just the strange comfort of observation, the way the world seemed to pause around him when he stopped moving.

The super pulled his hand back from the victim’s face with a dismissive wave, leaving her sitting there like an object that had lost its purpose in someone else’s game. “I’m not done,” he said, standing now and looming over her. The crowd of patrons didn’t stir. They didn’t breathe louder or quieter than before. They existed in the same suspended space as Ethan, watching but not acting, afraid to do anything that might make them targets themselves.

Ethan shifted his weight on the stool, wincing slightly when the leg of the chair scraped against the floor. He knew he should be getting up, walking out, putting distance between himself and the bar that had become a cage. But something kept him rooted in place, some quiet part of him believing that if he left now, he’d never understand what was about to happen. Or maybe he didn’t want to understand it at all, just wanted to see what would break first when this went wrong.

The victim finally spoke, her voice barely rising above the hum of the refrigerator in the kitchen behind the bar. “Please,” she said, not looking up from the table, “I’m going home.” The super didn’t answer immediately. He walked around her chair and placed another hand on her shoulder, this one heavier, pressing down just enough to make her gasp for air. She made no sound but her posture tightened, muscles flexing under skin that had already begun to sweat despite the coolness of the room.

Ethan watched as the dynamic settled into a rhythm that made his stomach turn. The super was enjoying this not out of anger or malice, but because it gave him purpose, a chance to remind everyone in the room of where they stood in the hierarchy. No one would stop him. No one could help her. And yet, Ethan sat there, paying attention to every twitch of muscle and shift of weight, trying to remember that he was still a person, not just another observer who had decided to watch things happen because staying put felt easier than doing anything about them.
