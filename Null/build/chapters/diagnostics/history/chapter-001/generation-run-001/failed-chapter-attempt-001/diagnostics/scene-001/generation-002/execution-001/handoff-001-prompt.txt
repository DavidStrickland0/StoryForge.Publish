The neon sign for The Gilded Cage buzzed with a low, electric hum that seemed to vibrate in Ethan’s molars. He sat at the far end of the bar, his elbow propped on the sticky wood, watching the world through the haze of cigarette smoke and spilled beer. It was a Tuesday, which meant the clientele was a mix of people who wanted to be seen and people who wanted to be forgotten. Ethan fell into neither category. He was simply passing time until his shift at the community center started, a small, unremarkable existence in a city that had been reshaped by giants.

Above him, suspended on a thin, glowing thread of light that defied every law of physics Ethan knew, sat Kaelen.

Kaelen was one of the top-tier supers, a man whose public persona was built on a foundation of brute force and an almost aggressive charisma. His skin had the texture of granite, his muscles corded and dense beneath a tailored suit that cost more than Ethan’s annual rent. He floated three feet above the barstool, his boots crossed at the ankles, completely ignoring the fact that the stool was designed for feet. He was laughing at something, a deep, rumbling sound that didn’t seem to match the casual ease of his posture.

Beside him, or rather, trapped in the shadow of his presence, was a woman. She looked young, maybe mid-twenties, with tired eyes and a posture that spoke of long hours standing on her feet. She held a glass of water she hadn’t taken a sip of, her fingers white-knuckled around the stem.

Ethan took a sip of his lager. The beer was warm and flat, a reminder of the mundane reality that still existed in the cracks of this superhuman world. He watched Kaelen reach out and tap the table with one long finger. The wood splintered slightly under the pressure, a tiny crack spreading through the surface. Kaelen didn’t even look at the damage. He was too busy talking to the woman, or rather, speaking *at* her.

"You’re trembling, darling," Kaelen said, his voice carrying easily over the bar’s chatter. "Is it the cold? I can turn up the heat. Or is it fear? I like to think it’s the latter. It adds a certain... texture to the evening."

The woman flinched. She shifted her weight to the other foot, her eyes darting around the room, scanning for an exit, a helper, anyone who might be brave enough or foolish enough to intervene. She locked eyes with Ethan for a brief second, a silent plea that hung in the air before she looked away, resigned to the fact that no one was coming.

Ethan felt a familiar tightness in his chest. It was the same feeling he got when he saw a news report of a building crushed by a careless display of strength, or when he read about a lawsuit that had been dropped because the defendant could simply fly out of the courtroom’s jurisdiction. The laws were the same for everyone, on paper. In practice, the distance between a normal person and a super was as vast and unbridgeable as the gap between the earth and the sky.

He shifted in his seat, his chair scraping against the floor. The sound was small, insignificant, but it seemed to punctuate the silence between Kaelen’s words. He looked down at his hands, tracing the rim of his glass with his thumb. He was thinking about his apartment, about the stack of paperwork waiting on his desk, about the safe, predictable monotony of his life. It was a small life, yes, but it was his. It didn’t require him to be brave, or strong, or something more.

"Come on," he muttered to himself, more as an internal prompt than a thought. "You’re not a hero. You’re not anything. Go home."

He stood up, the movement slow and deliberate. He didn’t look at Kaelen. He didn’t look at the woman. He focused on the path to the exit, a clear line of sight that required him to pass within a few feet of the floating super. The air around Kaelen seemed to shimmer with residual heat, a byproduct of his invulnerable skin. Ethan could feel the warmth of it even from a distance, a physical manifestation of the power that kept normal people at bay.

He took a step toward the exit. The bar was crowded, but there was a lane of movement near the door, people leaving or arriving. Ethan merged into that current, his shoulders hunched slightly, his gaze fixed on the back of a man’s head in front of him. He was trying to be invisible, a ghost in the machine, a background character in a story that didn’t include him.

Behind him, he heard Kaelen laugh again. It was a bright, sharp sound that cut through the ambient noise.

"Did the little mouse run away?" Kaelen called out, his voice dripping with amusement. "I thought you had the nerve to stand up to me. How disappointing."

Ethan didn’t turn around. His heart was beating faster now, a steady drum in his ears. He could feel the eyes of the other patrons on him, a mixture of curiosity and pity. They were judging him, certainly, but they were also afraid. They all were. That was the unspoken rule of The Gilded Cage, and of the city beyond its walls. You did not engage with the powerful. You observed, you endured, and you left.

He reached the door, his hand hovering over the handle. The glass was cold under his palm. For a moment, he hesitated. He thought about the woman’s tired eyes, the way she had shrunk back from Kaelen’s touch. He thought about the crack in the bar, the splintered wood that would never be repaired because Kaelen would never pay for it.

But then he thought about his own body. The softness of his skin, the fragility of his bones, the fact that a single stray punch from Kaelen could turn him into a memory. The reality of the situation was cold and hard, and it outweighed any impulse to act.

He pushed the door open. The night air hit him, cool and smelling of rain and exhaust. The street was busy, filled with the hum of traffic and the distant wail of a siren. Ethan stepped out into the world, the heavy door clicking shut behind him.

As soon as he was through the threshold, the tension in his shoulders began to ease. The pressure that had been building in his chest since he entered the bar slowly dissipated, replaced by the familiar, grounding weight of his own feet on the pavement. He took a deep breath, the air filling his lungs with a sense of relief that felt almost physical.

He started walking, his pace steady and purposeful. He didn’t look back. He didn’t need to. He knew what was happening inside that bar. He knew that the woman was still there, still trapped, still afraid. And he knew that Kaelen was still floating, still smiling, still untouchable.

The city stretched out before him, a sprawling tapestry of light and shadow. Somewhere up in the sky, a streak of red light zipped past, the signature of another super on patrol. Ethan watched it disappear into the clouds, a fleeting reminder of the world he lived in. A world where strength dictated respect, and where the only way to survive was to know your place.

He adjusted the strap of his bag on his shoulder and continued down the street, blending into the crowd of ordinary people who walked, talked, and lived in the spaces between the giants. The night was young, and the air was crisp. For now, the world felt safe.
