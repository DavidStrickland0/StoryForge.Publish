The silence that followed Aurora’s smile was not empty; it was full, pressurized, and vibrating with a tension that Ethan felt in his molars. She hung in the center of the stage, suspended by nothing visible, her white and gold costume catching the harsh spotlights. To Ethan, she looked less like a person and more like an equation balanced perfectly, every variable accounted for, every force neutralized.

Then she moved.

It wasn’t a flight in the way Ethan understood movement. It was a dismissal of gravity. One moment she was still, the next she was a streak of light cutting across the sky above the venue, descending slowly, deliberately, toward the crowd. The roar of the spectators shifted from anticipation to a low, reverent chant. They parted like water around a stone, creating a path of empty air that led directly over the heads of the front rows.

Ethan’s hands were deep in his pockets, his fingers twisting the hem of his grey henley. He stood rigid, his shoulders hunched against the cold morning air, watching the figure draw closer. The altitude dropped. Fifty meters. Thirty. Twenty.

He could see the details now. The way her hair floated back as if she were moving through thick air, the absolute stillness of her expression. She was smiling, but it was a distant, public smile, the kind she gave to cameras. It didn’t reach her eyes.

As she passed directly overhead, drifting slowly above the densest part of the crowd, something shifted in the air. It wasn’t a sound. It wasn’t a gust of wind. It was a sudden, violent absence. The hum that had been vibrating in Ethan’s teeth for the last hour simply stopped. The pressure in his ears popped, equalizing as if they had just broken the surface of deep water.

Aurora’s forward momentum died.

For a heartbeat, she hung in the air, perfectly still, her arms outstretched as if she were trying to hold the sky in place. Then, the suspension failed.

She didn’t slow down. She didn’t drift. She fell.

The scream that tore from her throat was raw and human, a sound of pure, unadulterated terror that cut through the chanting crowd like a knife. Ethan’s head snapped up, his eyes locked on the woman plummeting toward him. The distance between her and the ground seemed to stretch and compress at the same time. He saw the panic in her face, the way her hands clawed at the empty air, grasping for an ability that was no longer there.

The crowd erupted. Not a roar this time, but a cacophony of shrieks, the thud of bodies being shoved, the frantic scrambling of people trying to get out of the way. Ethan tried to move, but his legs had turned to lead. He was frozen, anchored by a terror that was so profound it felt physical, a weight pressing down on his chest that made it impossible to breathe.

He watched her fall.

The impact was a wet, heavy sound, a crunch of bone and flesh that echoed off the steel beams of the stage. The air around the point of impact rippled with dust and debris. People screamed, some falling, others running, but the center of the chaos remained still for a second. Aurora lay in the middle of the crowd, a tangled heap of white fabric and gold trim. Her left arm was bent at an angle that no living limb should hold, the bone protruding sharply through the skin of her forearm. Blood, dark and thick, pooled around her head, spreading across the pavement in a spreading web.

Ethan couldn’t look away. His mind was racing, trying to process the impossibility of what he had just seen. Invulnerability. Flight. Strength. These were the pillars of her power, the things that made her a god among men. And they had vanished. Just like that. Just like the super at the bar.

The realization hit him with the force of a physical blow. *It’s me.*

The thought was so foreign, so alien to his understanding of the world, that it felt like a hallucination. But the evidence was there, splattered across the ground before him. The bar incident. The sudden loss of power. The woman’s escape. And now this. Aurora. The most famous super in the world. Fallen. Broken. Bleeding.

He looked at the people around him. They were shouting, pointing, some pulling out their phones, recording the scene with a detached, morbid curiosity. None of them looked at him. To them, he was just another face in the crowd, a civilian, a threat level zero. He was invisible.

But he wasn’t invisible to himself. He could feel the weight of his presence, the heavy, suffocating silence that hung in the air around him. He was the anomaly. He was the glitch. And he had just broken the one person he had ever truly admired.

The paramedics arrived in a blur of red and white lights, their vehicles screaming to a halt at the edge of the crowd. They jumped out, their faces grim, their movements efficient and practiced. They formed a circle around Aurora, their voices rising in a chorus of commands and questions.

“Vitals! We need vitals!”
“Bleeding on the head, major fracture on the left arm!”
“Who’s got the splint?”

Ethan watched from his spot, his heart hammering against his ribs. He was terrified. Not of the blood, not of the noise, but of the truth. If they started asking questions, if they started looking for the cause, they might find him. They might see the connection. They might realize that the man standing three feet away was the reason she was lying there, broken and bleeding.

The lead paramedic, a woman with short, dark hair and eyes that were hard and focused, crouched beside Aurora. She checked her pulse, her breathing, her eyes. Aurora was conscious, her head moving slightly, her lips moving as if she were trying to speak. The paramedic leaned in, listening, then shook her head.

“She’s asking for her power,” the paramedic said, her voice low enough that only those nearby could hear. “She’s asking why she can’t fly.”

Ethan felt a cold sweat break out on his forehead. He wanted to run. He wanted to turn and push through the crowd, to get away from this place, this scene, this nightmare. But his feet wouldn’t move. He was rooted to the spot, paralyzed by a guilt that was so heavy it felt like it was crushing him.

The paramedics worked quickly, stabilizing Aurora’s neck, applying pressure to the bleeding on her head. They wrapped her broken arm in a rigid splint, their movements gentle but firm. Ethan watched them work, his mind racing. He didn’t understand what was happening. He didn’t understand why her power was gone. He didn’t understand why he felt this way.

All he knew was that he was here. He was present. And for the first time in his life, he felt truly, completely alone.

The crowd began to thin out, people backing away from the scene, giving space for the emergency workers. The noise of the crowd faded into the background, replaced by the beeping of monitors and the murmur of medical equipment. Aurora was loaded onto a stretcher, her body limp, her face pale and drawn.

As they wheeled her toward the ambulance, Ethan saw her eyes open. They were wide, filled with a terror that was so profound it was almost beautiful. She looked around the crowd, her gaze searching, desperate. For a second, her eyes locked onto Ethan’s.

It was a fleeting moment, a fraction of a second. But it was enough.

She recognized him.

Or maybe she didn’t. Maybe she was just looking at him because he was the closest thing to a human face in a world that had suddenly become alien and hostile. But to Ethan, it felt like a verdict.

The ambulance doors closed. The vehicle pulled away, its lights flashing in the morning sun. Ethan stood there, watching it go, his hands still in his pockets, his heart still hammering in his chest.

He was safe. He was invisible. And he was terrified.

He turned and began to walk, moving against the flow of the crowd, his steps slow and deliberate. He didn’t know where he was going. He didn’t know what he was going to do. All he knew was that he couldn’t stay here. He couldn’t stay in this world, this place, where the rules had changed, where the power had shifted, where he had become something he didn’t understand.

He walked. And for the first time in his life, he didn’t know where he was going.
