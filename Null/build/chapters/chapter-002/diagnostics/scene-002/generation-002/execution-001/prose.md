The front door of The Gilded Cage remained open behind Ethan, a rectangle of cool night air cutting through the warm, stale scent of spilled wine and adrenaline. Inside, the bar had settled into a tense, buzzing silence. Dozens of smartphones hung in the air, their screens glowing with the live recording of the man who had been a monster an hour ago and was now just a man in a wrinkled suit, his shoulders slumped in defeat.

Elena turned back to face the room. The tremor in her hands had been replaced by a cold, rigid control. She smoothed the front of her blouse, her movements precise and deliberate, as if she were preparing to step onto a stage rather than flee a crime scene. David stood slightly behind her, his posture shifting from defensive to deferential. He scanned the room, his eyes lingering on the patrons filming with an expression of wary calculation. He was already thinking about who to call, what to say, and how to ensure that no one in this room could be traced back to them with any discomfort.

The super, whose name Ethan had never known and did not bother to ask, looked up from the floor where a shard of glass glittered. His face was flushed, sweat beading on his forehead in the cool air of the bar. He looked at Elena, then at David, and finally at the circle of onlookers. For a moment, a flicker of his old arrogance crossed his features, a ghost of the invulnerability he had felt just moments prior. But the weight of his own ordinary body pressed down on him, and the ghost vanished. He was trapped in a room full of people who had just seen him fail, and they were not afraid of him. They were angry, or they were curious, or they were already drafting the social media posts that would define the next twenty-four hours.

Elena picked up her purse from the table. The leather was expensive, and she handled it with a possessive grip. She did not look at the super. She did not acknowledge his presence at all. To her, he was no longer a threat; he was an inconvenience, a stain on the evening that needed to be cleaned up before she could leave.

"David," she said, her voice cutting through the ambient hum of the bar with sharp, commanding clarity. "Check the bill."

David nodded, his eyes dropping to the counter. "On it, Elena."

She turned to the bartender, who was hovering near the register, his eyes darting between the super and the executive. She did not raise her voice. She did not need to. "Put it on the corporate card," she said, holding out a black card without asking for the total. "And I want a copy of the incident report if one is going to be filed. My legal team will be in contact with your management by morning."

The bartender swallowed, his throat bobbing. "Of course, Ms. Vale. Right away."

Elena tucked the card back into her purse and turned to the door. David moved to flank her, his hand hovering near the doorframe, ready to push it open. The super watched them go. He could have stopped them. He could have shouted, or thrown a drink, or done something to reassert his dominance. But he had no strength left to spare, and his invulnerability was gone, leaving him exposed to the very things he had previously ignored. He was a civilian in a civilian bar, surrounded by civilian witnesses, and the silence in the room was heavier than any punch he could have thrown.

Elena reached the door. She paused, just for a second, her back to the room. She adjusted her earring, a small, delicate gesture that contrasted sharply with the aggression she had projected earlier. Then she stepped out onto the sidewalk, the cool air hitting her face. David followed closely behind her, the door swinging shut behind them with a soft, final click.

Ethan watched them go. The executive’s face was set in a mask of professional indifference, her eyes fixed on the street ahead. David’s shoulders were tense, his hands clasped behind his back. They moved with the same coordinated efficiency they had displayed at their table, a unit of social power that had just reasserted itself in the face of physical chaos.

The super remained inside, standing in the center of the bar, surrounded by the glowing screens of his captives. He looked down at his hands, flexing his fingers as if trying to remember how to grip something. The weight of his own existence, stripped of its supernatural armor, seemed to press in on him from all sides.

Ethan turned away from the window. The night air was crisp, carrying the distant sound of traffic and the faint hum of the city’s electric life. He adjusted his jacket, buttoning it up to his chin. The memory of the super’s sudden collapse played in his mind, a strange, unexplained anomaly that had no place in the logic of the world he inhabited. He took a step forward, then another, merging into the flow of pedestrians on the sidewalk. The bar disappeared behind him, its secrets locked within its walls, while the street stretched out before him, wide and indifferent to the small, strange incident that had just occurred in its shadow.
