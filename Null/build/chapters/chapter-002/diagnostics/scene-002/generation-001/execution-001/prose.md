Three blocks down, Ethan stopped. His legs felt like they belonged to someone else, stiff and unresponsive, so he leaned against the cold brick of a closed pharmacy to steady himself. He needed to see it. He needed to understand why a man who could bench press a sedan was standing frozen in a bar, looking as confused as a dog that had just realized it couldn’t jump onto the sofa.

He pushed off the wall and walked back toward The Gilded Cage. The night air was thick with the smell of wet asphalt and exhaust, and his breathing still hitched in his chest, a jagged rhythm that refused to smooth out. Every step felt deliberate, heavy, as if the city itself was watching him with a thousand indifferent eyes. He told himself he was just curious. Just a civilian checking to see if the world had made a mistake.

The bar’s neon sign buzzed with a low, electric hum, casting a sickly pink glow across the wet sidewalk. Through the glass, the interior was dim and chaotic. Tables were overturned, glasses shattered on the floor, and the air smelled of spilled whiskey and sweat. And there, near the center of the room, was the super.

He was standing in the middle of a mess of broken glass and torn fabric, his broad shoulders hunched, his head bowed. He was trying to pick up a shard of glass. Or rather, he was trying to move it. His hand hovered over the fragment, fingers twitching, but the glass didn’t budge. It wasn’t stuck to the floor; it was just sitting there, an ordinary piece of debris. But to the super, it must have felt like a boulder. He strained, his face reddening, his neck muscles bulging, but the glass remained exactly where it was.

Ethan watched through the window, his heart pounding a frantic rhythm against his ribs. The super was a celebrity, a name that appeared on billboards and news feeds. A man who could shrug off bullets and lift cars with one hand. And now he was standing in a bar, unable to move a single piece of broken glass, his face a mask of bewildered frustration.

The disconnect was staggering. In a world where supers were gods, this man was suddenly just a man. A large, confused, powerless man. And no one was coming to help him. The other patrons had fled, leaving the bar empty save for the super and the lingering scent of fear. The super looked around, his eyes darting, searching for an explanation, for a reason, for a way to regain the strength that had defined his life.

Ethan stood on the sidewalk, hidden by the shadows of the parked cars, and felt a strange, cold thrill run through him. He had no idea why this was happening. He had no idea what he had done. But as he watched the super struggle with a piece of glass, he realized something else: no one was holding the super accountable. The laws of the city, the police, the courts—they all bent and broke around the will of the powerful. And now that the power was gone, the super was left with nothing but his own confusion and the empty, echoing silence of a bar that had just witnessed the impossible.

Ethan took a deep breath, the cold air filling his lungs. He didn’t know what to do next. He didn’t know if he should go back in, if he should speak, if he should even exist. But he knew this: the world had just shifted, and he was standing in the middle of it, watching the pieces fall into place.
