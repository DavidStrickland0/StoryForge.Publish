The walk back to his apartment was three blocks. Ethan counted them. One, two, three. The distance was short, but the air between the venue and his building felt thick, resistant. He moved with a heavy, mechanical gait, his eyes fixed on the cracks in the sidewalk. The city hummed around him, a low vibration in the soles of his shoes, but the noise seemed to come from underwater. People brushed past him, their faces lit by the blue glow of handheld screens, their conversations a blur of static. He kept his hands in his pockets, fingers curled tight against his palms, grounding himself in the pressure of the fabric.

The door to his building was solid oak, heavy and warm to the touch. Ray was at the desk, the Security Level 4 badge pinned to his chest catching the lobby light. He looked up as the door swung open, his expression neutral but his eyes sharp.

"Evening, Ethan," Ray said.

"Hi, Ray." Ethan’s voice came out thin. He cleared his throat and tried again. "Hi, Ray. Long night."

Ray’s gaze flicked to the street behind Ethan, then back to his face. The doorman didn’t ask what had happened three blocks away. He didn’t have to. The news was already moving through the airwaves, through the phones in the hands of the people passing by, through the very air of the city.

"You look like you’ve seen a ghost," Ray said softly.

Ethan shook his head, a small, jerky motion. "Just tired."

"Right." Ray didn’t press. He knew better. Some things were too big for a lobby. "Take it easy, Ethan."

Ethan nodded. He swiped his keycard, the green light blinking in the dark, and stepped into the elevator. The doors closed, sealing him in a small metal box. The silence was heavy. He could hear the faint hum of the machinery, the whir of the cables. He stared at the numbers changing on the display, 1, 2, 3, 4, until the doors slid open with a soft chime.

His apartment was dark when he stepped in. The curtains were drawn, the only light coming from the streetlamps outside, filtered through the gaps in the fabric. It was a dim, blueish glow that painted the room in shades of shadow. He dropped his bag by the door, the sound of it hitting the floor loud in the quiet. He didn’t turn on the lights.

He moved to the kitchen, his movements slow. He opened the cabinet, his fingers brushing against the cold glass of the blue mug. Mara’s mug. It was a simple thing, a deep, calming blue, with a small chip on the handle. She had bought it for him at a street market years ago, a time when their life together felt solid, grounded. Now, it was just a piece of ceramic, a reminder of a past that felt increasingly distant.

He took the mug out, his fingers wrapping around the cold surface. He didn’t fill it with water. He just held it, feeling the weight of it, the solidity. It was a real thing. A tangible object. Unlike the world outside, which had just shattered.

He walked to the window, standing in the center of the room. The view was a blur of city lights, the distant glow of the venue three blocks away. He could see the red and blue lights of the ambulance, still pulsing in the distance, a faint, rhythmic throb.

He stood there for a long time, the mug in his hand, his eyes fixed on the lights. He tried to think, to process, to make sense of it. But his mind was a blank slate, wiped clean. All he could feel was the cold of the mug, the weight of it, and the hollow emptiness in his chest.

He didn’t sleep. He sat in the dark, the mug in his lap, and waited for the morning.

***

The morning light was harsh. It poured through the gaps in the curtains, slicing the room into strips of brightness and shadow. Ethan woke up on the floor, his back aching, the mug still clutched in his hand. He had fallen asleep sitting up, his head resting against the cabinet.

He blinked, his eyes adjusting to the light. The apartment was the same as it had been the night before. The kitchen counter was cluttered with the usual debris of his life: a stack of mail, a half-empty bottle of water, a few dishes in the sink.

He walked to the living room, his legs stiff from sleeping on the hard floor. He picked up his phone from the coffee table. It was dead, the screen dark. He plugged it in, waiting for the battery to charge. When the screen lit up, it was a flood of notifications. Hundreds of them. Texts, calls, messages from news apps, from social media, from friends he hadn’t spoken to in weeks.

He opened the news app. The headline was bold, red, impossible to ignore.

**AURORA FALLS: SUPERHEROINE INJURED IN MYSTERIOUS ACCIDENT.**

The story was everywhere. It dominated the feed, the screens, the conversations. Ethan scrolled through, his heart pounding in his chest.

*“Aurora, the city’s most beloved superheroine, suffered serious injuries early this morning during a public appearance at the Grand Hall. Witnesses report that she was flying over the crowd when she suddenly lost altitude, crashing to the concrete with a force that shattered the pavement. Paramedics rushed to the scene, treating her for what are believed to be multiple broken bones and internal bleeding.”*

Ethan’s hands trembled. He read on.

*“The cause of the accident remains unknown. Authorities are investigating, but there is no evidence of foul play. Aurora’s representatives have declined to comment, citing her private medical matters.”*

*“Experts are baffled. Aurora is known for her invulnerability, a skill that has protected her for years. How could she have been injured? Is this a malfunction in her skills? A new threat? Or something else entirely?”*

The speculation was wild, unchecked. Theories ranged from a secret weapon to a hidden illness. Some claimed it was a trap, a conspiracy. Others suggested a breakdown in the natural order. The public was in shock. They had relied on Aurora for so long, so completely, that the idea of her being vulnerable, of her being human, was a concept they couldn’t wrap their heads around.

Ethan read the comments. They were a mix of fear, anger, and confusion.

*“Who did this? Who would hurt Aurora?”*
*“Is this a new super? A villain we don’t know about?”*
*“This is a nightmare. What are we going to do without her?”*
*“I was there. I saw it. She just… stopped. Like she hit a wall.”*

That comment. *“Like she hit a wall.”*

Ethan closed the app. He felt sick, his stomach churning. He walked to the bathroom, splashing water on his face. The water was cold, shocking. It helped, a little. It grounded him.

He looked in the mirror. His face was pale, his eyes dark with sleeplessness.

He thought about the bar. He thought about FireMan. He thought about the way the super’s strength had vanished, the way the woman had escaped. He thought about the way Aurora had fallen, the way her invulnerability had failed.

Two incidents. Two different places. Two different people.

And he had been there. Both times.

The coincidence was impossible. It had to be a coincidence. It had to be.

He looked at his hands. They were ordinary hands. Pale, slightly dirty, trembling.

He picked up his phone again. He looked at the message to Ben, the one he had sent days ago, the one that had gone unanswered. *Hey, Ben. You okay? Just checking in.*

No response. Just silence.

Ethan set the phone down. He needed to leave. He needed to go out, to see the world, to see if it was still real. But he knew he couldn’t. He knew that if he went out, if he saw the news, if he saw the people, he would break.

He sat on the edge of his bed, the convertible sofa, the one that had been his refuge for so long. He pulled his knees up to his chest, wrapping his arms around them. He was small. He was fragile. He was a threat level zero.

The thought was a loop in his mind. He couldn’t stop it. He couldn’t think of anything else. He just sat there, in the quiet, waiting for the world to end.
