Ethan merged into the flow of traffic, his eyes fixed on the middle distance, refusing to look at the faces passing by. He was invisible. He was nothing. The thought offered a grim comfort.

The walk was a blur of sensory input that his brain refused to process. The green light. The red light. The squeal of brakes. The chatter of people discussing the news, their voices rising in excited, horrified speculation. *Did you see it? I think I saw it. She just stopped.*

Ethan walked faster. The pavement seemed to shift under his feet, the concrete cracking and receding like the scene he had just witnessed. He kept his hands in his pockets, his fingers curled into tight balls. He was afraid to let them unclench. He was afraid that if he let go, he would shake.
