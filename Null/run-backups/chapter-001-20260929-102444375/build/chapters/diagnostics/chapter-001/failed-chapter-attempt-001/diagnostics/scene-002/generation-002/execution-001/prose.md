The street outside was a different world. The heavy glass door clicked shut behind him, sealing in the low hum of conversation and the clatter of ice against glass. Ethan stood on the sidewalk, the cool night air hitting his face like a splash of water. He adjusted the strap of his messenger bag, his hands trembling slightly as he tried to steady his breathing. The adrenaline was still there, a tight wire in his chest, but the immediate pressure of the room had lifted. He turned and began to walk, his footsteps clicking against the pavement, the city noise rushing back in around him—distant sirens, the rumble of a bus, the indistinct chatter of other pedestrians.

He was just a man walking home. That was the thing he kept reminding himself. He was ordinary. He paid taxes, he had a job that involved spreadsheets and fluorescent lights, and he had a life that existed safely in the background of the superhuman spectacle. The hierarchy was simple, even if it felt brutal. Supers were the celebrities, the strategic assets, the untouchable icons who could reshape cities or escape justice with a smile. Normal people were the audience. We were the background noise.

Ethan reached his apartment building, a modest brick structure that had nothing special about it. He fished his keys from his pocket, the metal cold against his fingers. The lock clicked, and he stepped into the hallway, the smell of old carpet and cleaning product familiar and grounding. He dropped his bag by the door and ran a hand through his hair, his mind still replaying the scene at the bar. The way the woman had looked at him, not with hope, but with a kind of resigned recognition. She knew he was powerless. She knew he was safe. And that safety, in the presence of something like Kael, felt like a kind of complicity.

He walked to his kitchen, turned on the tap, and filled a glass with water. The sound of the running water was steady and normal. He drank it in long, slow gulps, the cool liquid soothing the dryness in his throat. He leaned against the counter, looking out the window at the street below. A car drove by, its headlights sweeping across the wet pavement.

Everything was quiet. The world continued, indifferent to the small, private moments of fear and relief that defined the lives of those without power. Ethan took a deep breath, letting the tension in his shoulders ease. He was okay. He was safe. And for now, that was enough. He turned off the kitchen light and headed toward his bedroom, the night stretching out before him, full of the same ordinary possibilities it had always held.
