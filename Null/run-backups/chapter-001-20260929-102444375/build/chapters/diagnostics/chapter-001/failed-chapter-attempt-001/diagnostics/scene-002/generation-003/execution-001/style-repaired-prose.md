I stepped into the street. The cool air hit my face, a sharp contrast to the humid, stale air of the bar. My heart was still hammering against my ribs, a frantic rhythm that didn’t match the quiet evening. I walked briskly, my boots slapping against the pavement, needing to put distance between myself and The Rusty Anchor.

The city buzzed around me. Neon signs flickered, advertising everything from cheap protein shakes to flight training for the less privileged. A bus hissed to a stop three blocks down, its doors sliding open with a mechanical sigh. I kept moving, my hands shoved deep into my pockets, fingers drumming against my thighs.

I needed to go home. I needed a shower, a glass of water, and maybe to stare at the ceiling until the image of Kael’s pearlescent skin faded from my mind. The way he had tapped the table, the way the laminate had cracked like dry ice under a heavy foot. It wasn’t just strength; it was a display. A reminder.

I turned onto 4th Street, the wind picking up slightly, carrying the scent of rain and exhaust. A group of teenagers walked ahead of me, laughing, their voices echoing off the brick buildings. They looked like they had nothing to worry about. Normal people. Background noise.

That was the thing about the hierarchy. You weren't even aware of the supers' presence unless they acknowledged you. And when they did, it was usually to remind you of where you stood.

I reached my apartment building, a modest structure on the east side of the city. I fished my keys out of my pocket, my hands trembling slightly. I unlocked the door and stepped inside, the heavy click of the lock providing a small, satisfying comfort.

My apartment was small, a single room with a kitchenette and a bathroom. It was tidy, clean, and entirely mine. I tossed my bag onto the sofa and walked to the window, looking out at the city lights.

A figure flew overhead, a streak of silver against the dark sky. That was Aurora. I watched her glide past, effortless and graceful. She was a celebrity, a symbol of what supers could be. Powerful, beautiful, untouchable.

I wondered if she knew what it was like to be ordinary. To be afraid. To have to calculate every move, every interaction, to ensure you didn't offend or provoke.

I turned away from the window and walked to the kitchen. I filled a glass with water and drank it slowly, the cool liquid soothing my throat. I felt a strange sense of relief, mixed with a deep, gnawing guilt.

I had left. I had left a woman trapped with a man who could crush her like a bug. I had chosen safety over justice, self-preservation over empathy.

Was that who I was? A coward? Or just a human being, trying to survive in a world that had no place for people like me?

I didn't have an answer. All I had was the silence of my apartment, the hum of the refrigerator, and the distant sound of the city, continuing its endless, indifferent rhythm.

I sat down on the sofa, the leather creaking under my weight. I stared at the blank wall, my mind racing.

I had to be more careful. I had to be smarter. I had to find a way to navigate this world without getting hurt, without getting involved.

But how?

The question hung in the air, unanswered.

I closed my eyes and tried to sleep, but the image of Kael’s face, twisted in that moment of casual cruelty, kept intruding.

I wasn't ready for this. I wasn't ready for any of it.

But here I was.
