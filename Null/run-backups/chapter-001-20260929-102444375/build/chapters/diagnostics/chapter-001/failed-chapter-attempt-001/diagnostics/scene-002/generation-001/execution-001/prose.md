The heavy glass door hissed shut behind him, sealing the low murmur of conversation and the clink of ice against glass into a distant, muffled rhythm. Ethan stood on the sidewalk, the cool night air hitting his face like a splash of water. He felt the tension in his shoulders begin to unknot, his fingers unclenching from the strap of his messenger bag.

He took a deep breath, tasting the faint exhaust fumes and the damp scent of rain that had passed an hour ago. The street was quieter than the interior of the bar, the neon sign of The Rusty Anchor buzzing with a faint, erratic flicker that painted his shirt in intermittent shades of blue.

He didn't turn back. He didn't need to. The others turned, their eyes flicking to Ethan with a mixture of curiosity and mild irritation. He had interrupted the flow. He had broken the silence.

Ethan offered a small, apologetic smile to the woman with the red hair, then turned and walked toward the door. His heart was beating fast, a frantic rhythm in his chest. He felt exposed, thin, and entirely ordinary. He pushed through the heavy glass door, stepping out into the cool night air, and didn't look back.
