Ethan Vale did not usually go to bars on a Thursday, which was why he was currently standing near the back of The Rusty Anchor, holding a glass of amber liquid he hadn’t taken a sip of in twenty minutes. The place smelled of stale hops, wet wool, and the faint, metallic tang of ozone that always seemed to cling to the patrons who weren’t quite human. He’d come for the cheap craft beer and the relative anonymity of the back corner, a small luxury in a city where the skyline was dotted with floating billboards and the news cycle was dominated by superheroes saving cats from trees or punching through bank vaults with their bare hands.

Ethan took a slow sip, the cold bitterness coating his tongue. He watched the room with the detached curiosity of a man observing a species he found fascinating but fundamentally alien. There were three supers here, he counted. One was a woman with skin like polished obsidian, sitting at the bar and laughing loudly at something a bartender said. Another was a man whose hair floated slightly above his head, defying gravity with a lazy, perpetual wave. The third was less obvious, a quiet guy in a corner booth who kept tapping his fingers on the table; when he tapped, the wood didn’t crack, but the air around his knuckles shimmered with a faint, heat-haze distortion.

Ethan had learned early on to keep his distance from these people. It wasn’t fear, exactly, or at least he told himself it wasn’t. It was a pragmatic assessment of the power differential. In a world where a person could bench-press a sedan, the social contract was written in a different ink. Laws existed, sure, but they were suggestions to those who could walk through walls. Ethan had seen a news report last week about a super who had “accidentally” leveled a city block during a misunderstanding. The man had been fined. A fine. For destroying a neighborhood. The victims had been offered counseling and a check that would cover their rent for two years, if they were lucky.

He shifted his weight, his eyes drifting to the window. Outside, the city hummed with its usual chaotic energy. A delivery drone buzzed past, trailing a banner for a new superhero insurance policy. *Total Coverage. Even for Collateral Damage.* The irony was so thick it was almost comical.

Ethan turned back to the interior. The woman with the obsidian skin was now standing, stretching her arms above her head. She was tall, easily six-foot-two, and moved with a fluid, predatory grace that made the ordinary patrons around her shrink into their seats. She didn’t seem to notice, or perhaps she didn’t care. She was a celebrity, one of the top tier. Her face was familiar from the billboards, the magazines, the constant stream of media that treated her like a god. She smiled at a group of men at a nearby table, and they leaned in, eager, their eyes bright with the kind of awe that Ethan found uncomfortable to witness.

He finished his beer, the glass emptying with a soft clink against the coaster. He felt a prickle of unease, a low-grade tension in his shoulders. It was the feeling of being small. Of being ordinary. In a world that had decided that strength was the only currency that mattered, Ethan was broke. He paid the tab, the transaction a small, mundane ritual that felt absurdly fragile in this space. He didn’t want to be near the woman. He didn’t want to be near any of them. He wanted the quiet safety of his apartment, where the walls were just walls and the air didn’t smell like ozone.

He pushed off the bar, navigating the tight space between the stools. The bartender, a man with tired eyes and a nametag that read *Gary*, looked up.

“Same time next week, Ethan?” Gary asked, wiping down the counter with a rag.

Ethan gave a small, tight smile. “Maybe.”

He headed for the door, his hand reaching for the handle. The air near the exit was cooler, carrying the damp scent of the street outside. He paused for a second, his hand hovering over the brass. He could hear the low murmur of conversation, the clinking of glasses, the distant thump of music from the jukebox. He could hear the woman with the obsidian skin laughing again, a sound that was almost musical, almost beautiful, and entirely devoid of any awareness of the fragility of the people around her.

Ethan pushed the door open and stepped out into the night, the heavy wood swinging shut behind him with a solid, reassuring thud. He didn’t look back. He didn’t need to. He knew where he was, who he was, and exactly how much of a target he was in this city. He walked briskly toward the street, his mind already turning to the warm, safe chaos of his own life, leaving the world of the powerful to its own heavy, glittering gravity.
