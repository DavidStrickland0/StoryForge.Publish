The heavy wood of the door swung shut behind him with a solid, reassuring thud, cutting off the low hum of conversation and the clink of glass. Ethan didn’t look back. He didn’t need to. He knew exactly where he was, who he was, and precisely how much of a target he represented in a city where strength was the primary currency.

He walked briskly toward the street, his mind already turning to the warm, safe chaos of his own apartment. He left the world of the powerful to its own heavy, glittering gravity, closing the distance between himself and the bar’s entrance with every step. The air outside was cooler, thinner, and carried the faint scent of rain on pavement. It was a relief, a tangible boundary that marked the end of the immediate danger zone.

As he reached the corner, his foot caught on a slight rise in the sidewalk, and he stumbled, his shoulder hitting the brick wall of the adjacent building. The pain was sharp and immediate, a grounding reminder of his own fragility. He froze, his heart hammering against his ribs, not from fear of the super, but from the sudden, visceral realization of how easily he could be broken.

He turned his head slowly, glancing back toward the bar. Through the foggy glass, he could see the silhouettes of the patrons. One figure stood out, a massive shape looming over a smaller one near the bar. The super was leaning in, his voice low, his body language a wall of unyielding force. The victim was barely visible, a shadow swallowed by the super’s dominance.

Ethan watched, his breath shallow. He could see the super’s hand resting on the back of a stool, the wood creaking under the pressure. He could see the way the victim’s shoulders hunched, her head bowed, her entire posture a surrender to the sheer physical disparity between them. The bystanders around them were still, frozen in a collective paralysis, their eyes fixed on the floor, their phones in their hands, recording nothing.

Ethan felt a cold knot tighten in his stomach. He knew what it felt like to be that small, that vulnerable, in the face of such power. He knew that no amount of moral outrage or social expectation would change the fact that he was just a man in a world of gods. He had a job to get to, a life to live, and a body that could be crushed without a second thought.

He turned away from the window, his back to the bar, and continued walking. The streetlights flickered overhead, casting long, distorted shadows on the wet pavement. He kept his head down, his hands in his pockets, his eyes fixed on the ground. He was not a hero. He was not a symbol. He was just Ethan, and he was going home.

The super’s voice was muffled by the glass, but Ethan could feel the weight of it, the sheer force of his presence. He could feel the victim’s distress, the way it radiated outward, invisible but undeniable. He could feel the fear of the bystanders, the way it permeated the air, thick and suffocating.

He kept walking. He did not look back. He did not stop. He did not save anyone. He simply walked, his steps steady, his breath slow, his mind already turning to the safety of his own home. The world of the powerful was not his world. It was a place of glittering gravity, of heavy, unyielding force, and he was not strong enough to stand in it.

He reached the street corner, turned, and disappeared into the night, leaving the bar, the super, and the victim behind. He left them to their own heavy, glittering gravity, and he left himself to the quiet, safe chaos of his own life.
