Ethan pushed through the sticky haze of the bar’s back room, the air thick with stale beer and the metallic tang of desperation. He felt small in a way that had nothing to do with height or weight. Here, in the center of it all, he was just meat and bone, a biological organism subject to gravity and consequences. The bar was packed with patrons who knew better than to challenge the celebrities who walked through its doors like gods descending from Olympus. A woman in a red dress at the next table caught his eye; she was smiling nervously while a man with skin that shimmered like oil on water leaned over her booth, his casual demeanor masking an aura of absolute entitlement. He looked away quickly, focusing on the exit sign above the door where the emergency light hummed quietly in the background.

The social contract here was simple and brutal: you either had power or you didn’t, and if you didn’t have it, you kept your head down until someone else decided to use their strength against you. Ethan had spent his entire life learning to navigate this invisible hierarchy, adjusting his posture when a celebrity entered the room, lowering his voice when news of a recent rescue mission made headlines, and pretending he wasn’t watching as people who could level blocks walked past him without a second thought.

He reached for his coat, feeling the familiar weight of it as if it were armor against something that wasn’t there. The place felt too crowded, too loud, with conversations overlapping in a chaotic symphony that made him want to disappear into the floorboards. He needed air, needed space where he could breathe without feeling like an intrusion on someone else’s moment of glory. His fingers tightened around the collar of his jacket as he started toward the door, trying not to think about how easy it would be for someone out there to just pick him up and throw him through a wall if they felt like it.

Every step he took felt deliberate, calculated, each movement measured against the possibility that someone might decide to intervene in their own favor. He could hear laughter from behind him, the kind of easy, carefree mirth that came from knowing you couldn’t possibly be hurt by anything anyone said or did. It was a sound that made his stomach turn over, a reminder of how unequal everything really was.

He stopped at the threshold, looking back one last time before stepping out into the cool night air. The street beyond was quiet compared to the bar’s chaos, but even here, he could see the signs of imbalance.
