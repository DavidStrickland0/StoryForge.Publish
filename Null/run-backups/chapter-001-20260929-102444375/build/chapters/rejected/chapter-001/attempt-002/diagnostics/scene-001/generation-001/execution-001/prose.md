The air inside *Neon & Nitro* smelled of synthetic lime and expensive cologne, a scent that felt heavy against the skin. Ethan Vale pulled his collar up slightly, trying to make himself smaller in a crowd where size was currency. It wasn't just physical bigness; it was the kind of presence that bent reality itself. He watched as a man with hair dyed the color of a bruised sky walked through the room, and everyone else simply parted like water around a stone. No one spoke to him. No one looked at him directly. They acknowledged his shadow, but they never engaged with the person inside it.

Ethan shifted his weight from one foot to the other near the bar's edge. He held his drink with both hands, the condensation cold against his palms. It was a small comfort in a place where power dictated the only laws that mattered. He knew the rules here better than anyone who didn't belong to the upper echelon: if a super wanted something, they took it. If they decided you were beneath notice, you ceased to exist until they changed their mind.

He scanned the room again, looking for an exit that didn't feel like surrendering to a giant's whim. The music was too loud to carry a conversation across even ten feet, but he could still hear snippets of the conversations drifting in from the center of the dance floor where the real celebrities gathered. A woman with skin that shimmered like oil on water laughed at something a man said without blinking. Another group, three women who looked identical except for the colors of their neon tattoos, were arguing about which new hero was most likely to fly into the atmosphere tomorrow morning.

Ethan felt the familiar tightness in his chest. He wasn't dangerous. He didn't have powers. He didn't own a jet or a fortress. He was just a guy who worked data entry and liked to read fantasy novels he couldn't afford to buy at the local store. But here, that ordinary life made him vulnerable. He could see the way the patrons treated the supers, not as people, but as celebrities they were allowed to admire from a safe distance or ignore entirely if their attention wasn't requested.

He turned toward the door again, stepping back into the stream of bodies moving toward it. The noise was deafening, a wall of bass that vibrated through his boots and up into his teeth. He kept his eyes on the exit, watching how the crowd flowed out like a river finding its channel. It felt strange to be an observer in a world where everyone else operated by different physics.

He paused near the doorway, checking his phone for the time. The bar’s schedule was packed with events that he knew nothing about—charity galas, launch parties, press junkets. He had been drawn here by curiosity, wanting to see if the rumors were true, but the moment he stepped in, the reality of the situation settled over him like a heavy coat.

There was no point staying for another hour. He needed air that didn't smell like expensive perfume and ozone from someone’s personal field generator. He took another step toward the door, feeling the pull of the crowd behind him, and then he saw it.
