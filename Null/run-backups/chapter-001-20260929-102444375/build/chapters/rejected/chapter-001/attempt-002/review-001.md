# Chapter Review

## Overall Assessment

The chapter fails to execute the required inciting incident (the bar scene) by having Ethan leave before the suppression occurs, leaving the plot unresolved. It also incorrectly describes the world's physics regarding supers and ignores the specific canon of the aggressive super's behavior.

## Findings

- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter fails to complete the required Short arc objective for Chapter 1: the bar incident. Ethan leaves before the super loses his powers, meaning the 'unexplained suppression' event never happens in this text.
  Evidence: With a deep breath, he turned away from the scene... He kept moving, letting the rhythm of his footsteps carry him forward without thought.
- [Canon] The chapter contradicts established canon by describing the aggressive super as 'perfectly still' and ignoring social norms only after Ethan leaves. The canon requires the super to lose powers *while* Ethan is present, causing the victim to escape.
  Evidence: The man remained perfectly still, leaning against the wall like a statue made of living muscle... He ignored her completely...
- [Canon] The chapter incorrectly states that supers 'bend reality' and wear suits carved from granite. Canon defines them as having specific abilities (flight, strength) without implying they alter physics or wear impossible materials by default.
  Evidence: ...presence that bent reality itself... His skin shimmered with a faint, unnatural sheen... muscles that looked like they had been carved from granite...
- [Canon] The chapter describes the super as 'bored' and ignoring the woman, whereas canon establishes he is physically imposing himself on her. The text fails to show the harassment that triggers the incident.
  Evidence: ...expression was one of bored entitlement... He ignored her completely...
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter ends with Ethan walking away, leaving the victim trapped and the mystery unresolved. This contradicts the 'NextMilestone' which requires the super's powers to fail and the victim to escape.
  Evidence: He kept moving... The guilt gnawed at him quietly in the background...
- [Canon] The chapter implies Ethan is 'just a guy' who works data entry, but fails to establish the specific social hierarchy where supers are celebrities and assets. It treats them as generic monsters rather than the specific 'celebrity/asset' class defined in canon.
  Evidence: He was just a guy who worked data entry... But here, that ordinary life made him vulnerable.
- [Canon] The chapter describes the super's suit straining against 'broad shoulders and chest muscles,' implying biological growth. Canon implies supers have specific powers; this description conflates biology with ability.
  Evidence: ...suit that strained against broad shoulders and chest muscles that looked like they had been carved from granite rather than grown by biology.
- [Canon] The chapter states the super 'ignored social norms,' but canon requires the specific incident of a super harassing a normal woman at a bar. The text describes a static scene without the active harassment required to trigger the plot.
  Evidence: ...expression was one of bored entitlement... He ignored her completely...
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter does not advance the 'CurrentPhase' of the story. The phase requires Ethan to witness the incident and flee *after* the suppression, but he flees *before* it happens.
  Evidence: He paused near the doorway... He took another step toward the door... Then he saw it.
- [Canon] The chapter describes the super as 'enormous' and filling space, but fails to mention his specific power (likely strength or invulnerability) which is required for the suppression incident.
  Evidence: ...frame filling the space so completely... His skin shimmered with a faint, unnatural sheen...
- [Canon] The chapter implies the super's presence alone makes him 'untouchable' without specifying his power. Canon requires specific abilities (flight, strength) to be suppressed.
  Evidence: ...presence that bent reality itself... No one spoke to him. No one looked at him directly.
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter fails to establish the 'ReservedLaterWork' for Chapter 1, which includes the super's reaction to losing strength and the victim's escape. These events are absent.
  Evidence: He kept moving... The guilt gnawed at him quietly in the background...
- [PhysicalLogic] Ethan's internal monologue claims he is not dangerous or powerful, yet his presence causes a super to lose their abilities, which contradicts the established premise that Ethan possesses an unknown nullification field.
  Evidence: The text states: 'He wasn't dangerous. He didn't have powers. He didn't own a jet or a fortress.' However, the scene immediately follows with the super losing his strength and invulnerability simply by being near Ethan, which is the core mechanic of the story that Ethan has not yet realized he possesses.

## Revision Guidance

- Rewrite the scene so Ethan stays until the super loses powers.
- Show the victim escaping immediately after the power loss.
- Remove descriptions of 'bending reality' or granite muscles.
- Ensure the super is actively harassing the woman before leaving.
- End the chapter with Ethan fleeing *after* the incident occurs.
- Remove or rephrase the explicit denial of his own power in the internal monologue. Instead, have Ethan believe he is powerless and unaware that his presence is causing the anomaly, preserving the mystery until later chapters.
- Ensure the narrative voice reflects Ethan's ignorance of his ability rather than stating facts about his nullification field as if they were common knowledge to him.
