The air inside the bar was thick enough to chew, a cloying mixture of synthetic lime from the ice machines, stale beer foam, and the sharp tang of expensive cologne that clung to people like invisible armor. Ethan shifted his weight, trying to find purchase on the sticky floor as he navigated the sea of bodies. Most patrons were just heads bobbing to the bass, phones glowing in their pockets, or staring upward with slack jaws at some new hero posing on a raised platform. He kept his eyes down, scanning for the nearest exit, counting steps in his head.

He had been drawn here by curiosity, wanting to see if the rumors about these celebrities were true. They said supers could level buildings and fly into orbit, that they ruled over governments and laws with a casual wave of a hand. He wanted to see the power that made normal people invisible. But the moment he stepped across the threshold, the reality of the situation settled over him like a heavy coat. There was no point staying for another hour. He needed air that didn't smell like ozone and perfume.

He took another step toward the door, feeling the gravitational pull of the crowd behind him, a tide of people who wanted to be seen but couldn't afford the price of attention. Then he saw it.

A figure dominated the floor near the exit, not by standing tall or shouting, but by simply occupying space with an unnatural density. The man was massive, his shoulders broad enough to block out the neon signs above him. He wore a suit that probably cost more than Ethan's annual salary, tailored to fit a frame that moved without effort. This wasn't just a large man; this was someone whose biology had been rewritten by something beyond human engineering.

Near him stood a woman who looked like she'd been picked from the front row of an ordinary university lecture hall. She wore sensible flats and a modest blouse, clutching her phone with both hands as if it were the only thing keeping her grounded. Her posture was rigid, shoulders hunched, eyes darting toward the door every few seconds. She wasn't looking at the music or the stage; she was watching the giant man with an expression that said *please don't look at me again*.

The giant turned his head slowly, his face a landscape of smooth skin and sharp jawline that made him look carved from marble rather than flesh. His eyes locked onto the woman near the exit, and something changed in his demeanor. The casual arrogance of a celebrity softened into something darker, more predatory. He didn't walk toward her; he extended an arm, fingers curling as if he were reaching for a simple object, but the air around them seemed to warp with the effort of his movement.

Ethan felt his heart rate spike, a sharp thud against his ribs that made his vision blur at the edges. The bass from the speakers vibrated through his boots, a physical pressure in his chest, but it was nothing compared to the feeling of being watched by something that could crush him without thinking about it. He knew he shouldn't be here. He knew he should just walk past and pretend he didn't exist. But curiosity had driven him into this trap, and now he was stuck watching a predator circle its prey.

The woman near the door took a half-step back, her heel catching on the threshold rug. She stumbled, her balance gone for a fraction of a second. The giant moved then, not with speed but with an inevitability that made the floor seem to tilt beneath her feet. His hand reached out again, closing around the space where she had been standing. She flinched, her eyes wide and wet with terror, as if she were watching a horror movie in real life.

Ethan's instinct screamed at him to run, but his body froze. He was an ordinary man in a world where ordinary people were invisible and vulnerable. If he moved now, he'd be caught between the giant's attention and the panic of the crowd behind him. His options were limited: stay and watch, or flee. Staying meant becoming part of the spectacle, which would only make things worse. Fleeing meant risking drawing attention to himself in a place where everyone was either too distracted or too afraid to care.

He decided there was no point staying for another hour. He needed air that didn't smell like expensive perfume and ozone from someone’s personal field generator. He took another step toward the door, feeling the pull of the crowd behind him, ready to leave before whatever happened next changed everything.
