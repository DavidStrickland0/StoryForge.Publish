# Chapter 1: The Accidental Hero

The alley stank of urine and rot.

Adrian pressed himself against the brick wall, trying to make himself invisible, which shouldn't have been difficult. He was thin enough that his windbreaker hung on his frame like a tent. His stomach had been empty for fourteen hours. His eyes burned from exhaustion. The fluorescent lights of the campus library had given him a headache that throbbed behind his left ear.

He'd been walking home from his evening shift at the cafeteria, taking the long route because the shortcut through this neighborhood cost thirty cents for the bus he couldn't afford. The paycheck wouldn't clear for another week. The rent was due in five days. He'd eaten two dollars' worth of leftover rice and beans for dinner, and even that had been a decision between eating and paying for the textbook he needed for tomorrow's exam.

The voices ahead made him freeze.

A man's voice, slurred and angry: "You think you can just walk away? You think you're done?"

A woman's voice, tired: "We're done. I'm done. Move."

Adrian peeked around the corner. The alley was narrow, lit by a single flickering streetlight that cast everything in sickly yellow. A woman stood near the far end, maybe thirty feet away, facing a man who had to be six-foot-two and built like a refrigerator. She was smaller, dressed in a jacket that was too thin for the October chill, her arms crossed defensively.

The man took a step forward. The woman didn't back down, but her jaw was tight, her shoulders tense.

Adrian's first thought was to keep walking. He had no business here. He had no money for trouble, no energy for heroics, no training for a fight. He was a broke nineteen-year-old who couldn't even afford to eat properly. Getting involved in someone else's violence was the stupidest thing he could possibly do.

The man's hand shot out and grabbed the woman's arm.

She tried to pull away. He yanked her closer, and she stumbled, and the anger in his face looked dangerous.

Adrian's body moved before his brain caught up.

He was walking toward them before he realized what he was doing, his hands raised, his voice cracking: "Hey. Hey, let her go."

The man's head snapped toward him. His eyes were bloodshot, his face flushed. "What?"

"Let her go," Adrian repeated, and his voice was still shaking, but he was standing between the man and the woman now, and he was close enough to smell the alcohol on the man's breath. "Just let her go."

The man's fist came at him with terrible speed.

Adrian didn't think. He couldn't think. His body twisted sideways, and the punch missed his face by inches, and he felt the wind of it, the rush of air, and then his own hand was swinging, clumsy and desperate, and he connected with the man's ribs.

It hurt his knuckles. It probably didn't hurt the man at all.

The man grunted and swung again, and Adrian's body twisted the other way, and he felt something strange—a clarity, a focus, like his vision had sharpened to the point where he could see individual pores on the man's face, could track the movement of his shoulders, could predict the arc of his next swing before his conscious mind had processed it.

The second punch caught Adrian's shoulder and sent him spinning. Pain exploded across his collarbone. He hit the brick wall and the air rushed from his lungs.

The man was coming at him again, and Adrian tried to raise his hands, tried to protect himself, but the man's weight was crushing him against the wall, his forearm across Adrian's throat, cutting off his breath.

Adrian's vision was starting to tunnel. His lungs burned. His shoulder screamed where the man had hit him. He could feel the man's breath, hot and alcoholic, could smell the sour stench of it, and he was going to pass out, he was going to die, he was going to—

His knee came up, hard and fast, into the man's groin.

The man's eyes went wide. His grip loosened. He stumbled backward with a choked gasp.

Adrian didn't wait. His body was moving again, pushing off the wall, and his fist connected with the man's jaw, and the man's head snapped to the side, and he was falling, crashing into the garbage bags that lined the alley, and the bags burst, and trash scattered everywhere, and the man was on the ground, groaning, trying to push himself up.

Adrian stood over him, breathing hard, his shoulder throbbing, his throat raw, and he didn't understand what had just happened. He didn't understand why his body had moved like that, why he'd been able to see the punches coming, why he'd been able to twist away from blows that should have broken his jaw.

The man was still trying to get up. Adrian kicked him in the ribs.

The man went down again with a strangled cry.

"Stay down," Adrian said, and his voice didn't sound like his own. It was hoarse, rough, and there was something underneath it that he didn't recognize. "Just stay down."

The man didn't move. He was curled up on the ground, clutching his side, breathing in ragged gasps.

Adrian turned to the woman.

She was staring at him, her eyes wide, her face pale. The bruise on her cheek was already starting to darken.

"Are you okay?" Adrian asked, and his voice cracked again. "Did he hurt you?"

She didn't answer. She was still staring at him, and there was something in her expression that he couldn't read. Fear, maybe. Or suspicion.

Adrian looked down at his hands. They were shaking. His knuckles were scraped and bleeding. His shoulder was going to be a massive bruise. His throat felt like he'd swallowed sandpaper.

"I'm sorry," he said. "I'm sorry, I shouldn't have—"

"You should go," the woman said. Her voice was steady now, but there was an edge to it. "Before he gets up."

Adrian looked back at the man on the ground. He was still breathing, still moving. He would get up eventually. He would be angry. He would be looking for someone to hurt.

Adrian nodded. He turned and started walking, his legs shaky, his vision swimming. He made it three steps before he had to stop and lean against the wall, gasping for breath.

His shoulder was on fire. His ribs ached. His throat was raw.

He'd just gotten into a fight. He'd just hit someone. He'd just—

The woman was standing in the alley, watching him. She hadn't moved.

Adrian straightened up, tried to look less like he was about to collapse. "Are you going to be okay?"

She didn't answer. She was still staring at him, her expression unreadable.

Adrian reached into his pocket. His fingers closed around the crumpled bills he'd been saving for the bus fare. He pulled them out and held them toward her.

"Take this," he said. "For a taxi. Or the bus. Whatever you need."

She looked at the money. Then she looked at him. Then she looked back at the money.

"That's all you've got?"

"I can't afford to lose it," Adrian said, and it was true. He couldn't. He needed that money for the bus. He needed it for food. He needed it for—

He shoved the bills into her hand before he could change his mind.

"Take it," he said. "Please."

She stood there for a moment, holding the money, her expression still unreadable. Then she pocketed it without a word.

Adrian nodded. He turned and started walking again, his legs still shaky, his vision still swimming. He made it to the mouth of the alley before he had to stop again, leaning against the brick wall, gasping for breath.

His shoulder was throbbing. His ribs ached. His throat was raw.

He'd just gotten into a fight. He'd just hit someone. He'd just—

He'd just survived.

He stood there for a moment, breathing hard, trying to process what had just happened. His body felt strange. His vision was sharp, too sharp, like he could see every detail of the street in front of him. His hearing was sharp too, like he could hear the distant hum of traffic, the rustle of leaves, the sound of his own heartbeat.

He shook his head, trying to clear it. He needed to get home. He needed to—

The sound of sirens made him turn.

A police car was approaching, its lights flashing. It slowed as it passed the alley, and Adrian saw the officers inside, saw them looking at the scene, saw one of them pointing.

Adrian didn't wait. He turned and started walking, his legs still shaky, his vision still swimming. He made it two blocks before he had to stop again, leaning against a building, gasping for breath.

His shoulder was throbbing. His ribs ached. His throat was raw.

He'd just gotten into a fight. He'd just hit someone. He'd just—

He'd just survived.

But he couldn't afford to survive. He couldn't afford the injuries. He couldn't afford the time off work. He couldn't afford the—

His phone buzzed.

Adrian pulled it out. It was a text from his landlord: *Rent is due Friday. Don't be late.*

Adrian closed his eyes. He couldn't afford this. He couldn't afford any of it.

He opened his eyes and started walking again, his legs still shaky, his vision still swimming. He made it home twenty minutes later, his shoulder throbbing, his ribs aching, his throat raw.

He climbed the stairs to his apartment, unlocked the door, and stepped inside.

He was alone.

He was always alone.

He was—

Adrian pulled it out. It was a text from an unknown number: *Thanks for the help. I'll find you.*

Adrian stared at the message. Then he stared at the number. Then he stared at his phone.

Who was that?

The woman from the alley?

Why would she—

His phone buzzed again.

Another text from the same number: *I know where you live.*

Adrian stared at the message. Then he stared at the number. Then he stared at his phone.

How did she—

His phone buzzed again.

Another text from the same number: *Don't worry. I'm not going to hurt you.*

Adrian stared at the message. Then he stared at the number. Then he stared at his phone.

He didn't know what to think. He didn't know what to—

His phone buzzed again.

Another text from the same number: *I just want to repay my debt.*

Adrian stared at the message. Then he stared at the number. Then he stared at his phone.

What debt?

The money he'd given her? That was—

His phone buzzed again.

Another text from the same number: *I'll come by tomorrow. Don't be late.*

Adrian stared at the message. Then he stared at the number. Then he stared at his phone.

He didn't know what to think. He didn't know what to—

His phone buzzed again.

Another text from the same number: *And Adrian? You should see a doctor about that shoulder.*

Adrian stared at the message. Then he stared at the number. Then he stared at his phone.

How did she know—

He looked down at his shoulder. The bruise was already starting to darken, spreading across his collarbone, down his arm. It hurt. It hurt a lot.

He couldn't afford a doctor. He couldn't afford—

His phone buzzed again.

Another text from the same number: *I know you can't afford it. I'll help.*

Adrian stared at the message. Then he stared at the number. Then he stared at his phone.

"No."

"Okay."

"The one that changed you."
