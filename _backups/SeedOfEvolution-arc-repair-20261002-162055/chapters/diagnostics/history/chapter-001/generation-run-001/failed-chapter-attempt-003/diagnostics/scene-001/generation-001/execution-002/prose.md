# Chapter 1

The bus lurched around the corner, its brakes hissing as it slowed for the next stop. Adrian Vale pressed his forehead against the cool window, watching the streetlights blur into streaks of yellow and white. His shift at the convenience store had run late—another manager asking questions about the inventory discrepancy that definitely wasn't his fault—and now he was the last passenger on a nearly empty bus heading home through the industrial district.

He was tired. Always tired. The kind of exhaustion that settled into his bones and made his thoughts move through molasses. His stomach growled, reminding him that he'd eaten nothing but a stale sandwich from the break room all evening.

The bus stopped. A woman got on.

Adrian barely noticed her at first—just another silhouette against the bright lights of the bus interior. She was young, maybe mid-twenties, with dark hair pulled back in a messy ponytail. She wore jeans and a faded jacket that had seen better days. She dropped some coins in the fare box and moved toward the back of the bus, her movements casual, unremarkable.

Then the bus started again.

The woman had barely taken three steps when the doors hissed open a second time. A man stepped aboard—tall, broad-shouldered, with a scowl that made the driver glance nervously in the rearview mirror. He didn't pay the fare. He didn't sit down.

He went straight for the woman.

"Lena," he said, his voice low but carrying in the quiet bus. "Get off."

The woman—Lena—turned, and Adrian saw her face for the first time. She looked tired too, but there was something else in her eyes. Wariness. Defiance.

"I'm not going anywhere with you, Marcus," she said.

The man called Marcus laughed, but there was no humor in it. "You think you have a choice?"

He grabbed her arm.

Lena jerked away, stumbling back against the seat. "Touch me again and I'll—"

"Or what?" Marcus cut her off, moving closer. "You're done, Lena. You know that. You've been done since you stopped bringing in the money."

Adrian watched from his seat, frozen. This wasn't his fight. He didn't know these people. Getting involved would only make things worse.

The bus driver, a middle-aged man with graying hair and a weary expression, glanced in the mirror again. "Sir, you need to sit down or get off."

Marcus ignored him completely. He grabbed Lena's arm again, this time with enough force to make her wince. "Come on. Now."

"No."

"You're making this harder than it needs to be."

The bus was still moving, turning onto another street. The other passengers—the elderly man near the front, the young couple holding hands—were all staring now, but no one was moving. No one was saying anything.

Adrian's hands clenched into fists in his lap. His heart hammered against his ribs.

*Don't,* he thought. *Don't do anything stupid.*

But Lena was struggling now, trying to pull her arm free. Marcus's grip tightened. The bus driver was muttering into his radio, probably calling for help, but help wouldn't arrive in time.

And Adrian was sitting there, watching, doing nothing.

The bus lurched again. Marcus stumbled, his grip loosening just enough for Lena to wrench her arm free. She bolted toward the front of the bus.

Marcus swore and lunged after her.

That's when Adrian moved.

He didn't think about it. Didn't plan it. His body just reacted, pushing him to his feet, propelling him forward. The bus hit a pothole, and he grabbed a pole to steady himself, then another, working his way down the aisle as the bus driver hit the brakes.

"Stop!" the driver shouted. "Stop the bus!"

Marcus had caught up to Lena near the front. He grabbed her by the hair, yanking her head back. Lena cried out, clawing at his hands.

And Adrian was there.

He didn't know how he got there. One moment he was in the back of the bus, the next he was between Marcus and Lena, his hands on the bigger man's chest, pushing.

Marcus stumbled back, his eyes widening in surprise. "What the—"

Adrian didn't let him finish. He shoved again, harder this time, and Marcus crashed into the side of the bus with a metallic bang. The other passengers gasped. The driver hit the brakes again, and the bus screeched to a halt.

Marcus straightened, his face twisting with rage. "You stupid little—"

He swung.

Adrian ducked, the punch whistling over his head. He felt the air displacement, the rush of movement, and his body moved before his mind could process it. He came up swinging, his fist connecting with Marcus's ribs.

It felt like hitting a wall.

Pain shot up Adrian's arm. Marcus grunted, barely flinching, and swung again. This time Adrian wasn't fast enough. The punch caught him in the shoulder, spinning him around, sending him crashing into the pole.

The bus was silent except for heavy breathing.

Marcus advanced, fists raised, a predator closing in on wounded prey. Adrian pushed himself upright, his shoulder screaming, his head spinning. He could taste blood. His knuckles were already swelling.

*Run,* his mind screamed. *Run now.*

But Lena was behind him, and Marcus was in front, and there was nowhere to go except forward.

Adrian planted his feet and waited.
