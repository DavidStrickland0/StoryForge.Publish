Adrian planted his feet and waited.

Marcus's fist came at his face with terrible speed. Adrian's body moved before his mind could process the threat—a duck, a twist, his hands coming up instinctively. The punch whistled past his ear close enough that he felt the wind of it. He didn't think. He couldn't think. His hands were already moving, driving forward into Marcus's ribs with the same desperate power that had shoved the man into the pole moments before.

Marcus grunted, stumbling back. His eyes widened with surprise and pain.

*Again,* something in Adrian's brain screamed. *Again.*

He didn't know why his body knew these movements. He didn't know where the strength came from. But he could feel it coursing through his limbs, hot and electric, demanding release.

Marcus recovered fast, swinging wide. Adrian caught the blow on his forearm—his shoulder screamed in protest, but he held firm. He pivoted, using Marcus's momentum against him, and shoved hard. The larger man crashed backward into a seat, then onto the floor.

The bus driver was still shouting into the radio, his voice tight with panic. The passengers had pressed themselves against the windows, watching with wide eyes. Someone was recording on their phone.

Marcus struggled to his feet, blood trickling from his split lip. His hands were shaking.

"Fucking crazy," he muttered, backing toward the front exit. "Fucking crazy kid."

The doors hissed open. Marcus stumbled out into the night, disappearing around the corner of a warehouse.

Silence fell over the bus. The driver lowered his radio, his face pale. The passengers slowly unglued themselves from the windows. An elderly woman in the back was openly crying.

Adrian stood in the aisle, breathing hard, his shoulder a throbbing mess of pain. His knuckles were swollen and split. His head was spinning. He could taste blood in his mouth.

And he could feel it—something wrong, something *different* coursing through his veins. His body was humming, his skin too tight, his senses too sharp. Everything felt wrong. Everything felt like it was burning.

He turned, searching for Lena. She was still near the front, her hand pressed to her bruised arm, her eyes wide and fixed on him.

"Are you—" he started, but his voice cracked. He cleared his throat. "Are you okay?"

Lena stared at him for a long moment. Then, slowly, she nodded.

The bus driver spoke up, his voice still shaky. "You can get off here if you need to. Police are coming. I'll tell them what happened."

Adrian looked at Lena. She looked back at him, her expression unreadable.

"Thank you," she said quietly. "For... for what you did."

Adrian nodded, not trusting his voice. He fumbled in his pocket, pulled out his wallet, and handed her what little cash he had. Twenty-three dollars. It wasn't enough for a cab, but it might buy her a bus ticket home.

"For transport," he said.

Lena took the money, her fingers brushing his. Her touch was warm. Her eyes were still wide.

Adrian turned and stumbled off the bus, his shoulder screaming with every step. He didn't look back. He couldn't. He didn't know what he'd see if he did.

The night air hit him like a slap. He was shivering, though he wasn't cold. His body was still humming, still burning. He walked home slowly, each step a small victory over the pain, the confusion, the terrible, electric wrongness that had taken root in his bones.
