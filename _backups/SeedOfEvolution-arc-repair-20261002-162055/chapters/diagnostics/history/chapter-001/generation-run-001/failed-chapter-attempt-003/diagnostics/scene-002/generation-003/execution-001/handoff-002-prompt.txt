Adrian saw Marcus's fist coming and his body moved before his mind caught up. He twisted, feeling the punch whistle past his ear, close enough that he could feel the wind of its passage. His injured shoulder screamed in protest as he pivoted, but he didn't stop.

He didn't think about what he was doing. There was no time. His hand lashed out, grabbing Marcus's wrist, and his other fist drove into the man's side—harder this time, fueled by adrenaline and something else he couldn't name. Something that felt like electricity running through his veins.

Marcus grunted, his grip loosening. Adrian didn't wait. He shoved the man back, using his momentum against him, and Marcus stumbled, crashed into the side of the bus again with a heavy thud.

The bus driver was still on the radio, his voice tight with urgency: "Officer needs assistance at—"

But Adrian barely heard him. He was breathing hard, his knuckles throbbing, his shoulder feeling like it was on fire. Marcus was struggling to his feet, face twisted with rage and pain, but there was something else there too. Marcus's eyes flickered with doubt.

The man had been confident before. Now he looked confused, as if he couldn't understand why the scrawny kid who'd looked ready to collapse was still standing.

Adrian raised his fists again, planting his feet. His heart hammered in his chest. He tasted blood. His vision swam at the edges.

*I'm going to die,* he thought. *I'm going to die here on a bus for someone I don't know.*

Marcus charged again—and Adrian's body moved without thought, ducking, weaving, his movements sharp and precise in a way that shouldn't have been possible for someone who'd never fought before in his life. His fist connected with Marcus's jaw, and the man's eyes rolled back. He went down hard, crashing against the pole again, sliding to the floor in a heap.

The bus was silent except for heavy breathing.

Adrian stood over Marcus's unconscious form, his fists still raised, his whole body shaking. He looked down at his hands. They were bleeding. His hands were swollen. His knuckles were split open.

*What just happened?*

Behind him, he heard Lena's sharp intake of breath. Felt her presence like a weight on his back.

The bus driver was staring at him, mouth open. The elderly passenger had pressed himself against the window, eyes wide. The young couple were clutching each other.

Adrian lowered his fists slowly. His shoulder sent another wave of agony up his neck. He tasted blood in his mouth—his own, probably, from where Marcus had caught him.

He turned, looking at Lena. Her face was pale, but there was something in her eyes he couldn't read. Not fear. Not quite.

Something else.

"Get him out," the bus driver said, his voice breaking the silence. "Get him out before he wakes up."

Adrian staggered back to his seat, his legs giving out. He collapsed into the chair, breathing hard, his whole body shaking. He could feel bruises forming on his ribs, his shoulder, his face.

*I survived. I actually survived.*

But that wasn't what scared him.

What scared him was the way his body had moved. The way it had known what to do without him thinking about it. The way it had been *faster* than it should have been.

The bus pulled away from the stop, leaving the streetlights behind. Adrian closed his eyes, pressing his palms against his face.

"Thank you," she said. Her voice was steady, but there was something underneath it. Something he couldn't place. "You didn't have to do that."

He didn't know what had just happened. He didn't know why his body had moved like that, or why he'd survived when he should have been beaten to death.

All he knew was that something had changed.

And he was afraid of what it meant.
