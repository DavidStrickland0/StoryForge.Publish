Rain continued to pour relentlessly outside, drumming against the jagged timber of the ruined keep’s shattered roof and echoing through hollow corridors that smelled of wet stone and old iron. The party had moved deeper into the storage chamber Elias had just left behind, their boots leaving faint impressions in the damp earth before vanishing into the gloom of deeper passages. Maren adjusted her hammer’s grip on her shoulder, wincing slightly as she shifted weight away from the bruised spot near her spine where the broken cart axle pressed against her. “Keep moving,” she muttered, more to herself than anyone else. “If we stop too long, they’ll hear us.”

Elias nodded in acknowledgment, though his attention remained on Kaelen standing beside a cracked stone table cluttered with maps and scattered looted goods. The mage held his brass gauntlet aloft, its inner glow flickering faintly against the rain-induced haze that clung to the air like mist. He had already cast a minor smoke spell earlier—a thin veil of gray vapor curling from beneath his palm—but now he raised it higher, letting a thicker ring of swirling gray drift forward into the corridor ahead. The smoke settled silently around their feet, obscuring the ground for several paces and masking any sound they might make stepping over loose stones or fallen debris.

“Good,” Elias said softly, keeping his voice low so it wouldn’t carry beyond Maren’s shoulder. “Let’s see what’s down that way.” He gestured toward a narrow archway leading into a twisting hallway lined with rusted iron doors and peeling frescoes of long-dead gods. The walls were damp, patches of moss growing between cracks in the stone where water pooled from leaking roof sections above. Torin moved ahead cautiously, testing each step before committing his full weight to the ground beneath him. Behind him, Elara followed closely with her satchel strapped tight against her chest, her spectacles fogged by humidity but still allowing her to see clearly enough to note every detail of their surroundings.

Inside the hallway, the air grew colder and thicker with the scent of damp earth and something faintly metallic—perhaps blood left behind during recent raids, or maybe just rust from old weapons hanging on hooks along the walls. Torin paused briefly at a corner where shadows seemed unusually deep despite Kaelen’s smoke veil, his hand instinctively resting on the hilt of his sword. “Something feels off here,” he murmured without turning around. “Too quiet for this far in.”

Elias stepped forward slightly, scanning the dimly lit corridor ahead. He could hear nothing but the steady drumming of rain against the ruins above them and the occasional drip of water falling somewhere unseen. “Torin, take point,” he said quietly, keeping his tone calm despite the tension radiating off the soldier’s frame. “Maren, stay close to Elara. Kaelen, hold your position near the entrance until we’re ready.”

Torin nodded once and moved forward again, moving deliberately as if expecting ambush at every turn. His boots sank slightly into soft mud where the floor had given way under pressure from years of neglect. Maren fell in beside Elara, her breath visible briefly before dissipating in the chill air. “Just keep eyes open,” she said simply. “And watch for traps.”

Elara didn’t respond immediately; instead, she stepped closer to Kaelen’s smoke ring and began examining its residue on the ground where it had settled moments earlier. Her fingers dipped into a small vial of quartz dust carried in her satchel, sprinkling a fine layer over the damp floor beneath their boots. The dust glowed faintly under Kaelen’s lantern light, revealing subtle patterns left behind by magical interference—faint outlines of wards or concealment spells embedded within the stone itself. “There,” she whispered after a moment, pointing to a patch of shimmering residue near the wall. “This isn’t natural magic. It’s been deliberately placed.”

Kaelen glanced down at her work, his expression unreadable in the dim light. “You mean wards?” he asked, though his tone suggested he wasn’t entirely surprised by Elara’s conclusion.

“Exactly,” she replied, brushing a stray strand of hair from her face as she continued her analysis. “Simple concealment spells designed to hide movement or presence within these corridors. They’re not active defenses yet, just markers indicating where goblins are likely patrolling.” She paused, tilting her head slightly as she studied the residue more carefully. “And there’s another layer here—something stronger on the northern edge of this section. Might be a trap or alarm mechanism tied into the keep’s structure.”

Elias stepped closer to inspect the glowing dust himself, leaning against the rough-hewn wall for support while doing so. “We’ll need to account for that when we move deeper,” he said, glancing at Kaelen again. “Can you reinforce the smoke veil around us? Make sure it covers whatever lies ahead.”

Kaelen gave a small nod and raised his gauntlet once more, letting another ring of gray swirl outward from beneath his palm. The spell cost him something noticeable—it drained him visibly, his brow furrowing slightly as he concentrated on maintaining the effect. But he didn’t complain about it; instead, he focused entirely on keeping the smoke thick enough to obscure their approach while Elara mapped out the layout of the keep’s defenses.

“Alright,” Elias said finally, watching how the smoke curled around the group like a protective curtain. “Let’s proceed carefully but without hesitation. If there are traps ahead, we’ll deal with them once we know what they are.”

Maren grunted in agreement, shifting her weight again to relieve pressure on her injured side before stepping forward alongside Torin. Together they moved deeper into the hallway, their footsteps muffled by Kaelen’s ever-present smoke and the sound of rain continuing outside. Elara followed closely behind them, her notebook tucked securely against her chest as she sketched notes about the keep’s layout based on Elara’s observations so far.

As they reached a junction where three narrow corridors branched off from one another, Elias stopped briefly to consider their options. One path led directly toward what appeared to be a guard post marked by crude wooden signage carved into the wall; another wound deeper into the keep’s interior, disappearing behind heavy iron doors that groaned faintly in the wind; and the third seemed dead-ended after only a few paces, blocked by rubble and debris from some earlier collapse.

“Which way do we go?” Torin asked, his voice low enough not to carry beyond their immediate circle. “Guard post or deeper chambers?”

Elias studied the map spread out before him—a crude sketch drawn from memory and reinforced by Elara’s annotations regarding magical residue patterns. “Guard post first,” he decided after a moment of consideration. “If goblins are active here, they’ll likely have someone stationed there keeping watch over the keep’s perimeter. We learn their routine before committing further.”

Maren nodded once in approval, though her expression remained grim as she adjusted her hammer’s grip on her shoulder again. “Agreed,” she said simply. “But if they’re expecting us…”

“They aren’t expecting anyone yet,” Elias interrupted gently. “We’re still invisible to them thanks to Kaelen’s smoke. Let’s take our time and observe first.”

Kaelen gave a small shrug, his expression unreadable in the dim light. “I’ve got enough left for smoke screens,” he repeated earlier words, though now his voice carried a hint of weariness beneath its usual calm tone. “But I can’t hold this indefinitely.”

Elias nodded again, trusting that judgment more than any spell slot count could explain. “Then let’s make every second count,” he said softly before gesturing toward the guard post corridor. “Torin, lead us there. Maren, keep watch behind us. Elara, note anything unusual along the way.”

Torin stepped forward without hesitation, his hand resting firmly on the hilt of his longsword as he scanned the path ahead. The road wound uphill through a patch of young oak saplings, their leaves already brown and brittle underfoot. Raindrops pooled in hollows between roots, forming small dark puddles that reflected the gray sky above. Torin moved deliberately, testing each step for hidden debris or loose stones before committing his full weight to the ground.

Behind them, Kaelen’s smoke continued to curl around their feet like a living entity, obscuring any sounds they might make stepping over loose stones or fallen debris. Elara walked slightly ahead of him now, using her own small lantern to check for loose stones or hidden dips in the trail. She carried a satchel filled with chalk and brushes, ready to mark any new evidence they found along the way.

“Alright,” Elias said finally, stepping into view with a weary smile despite the fatigue weighing heavily on each member’s shoulders. “We’ve got our supplies packed, and now we know where the goblins are heading next.” He gestured toward the treeline beyond the farmhouses—or in this case, the twisting corridors of the keep itself. “Time to follow their trail before they vanish deeper into the woods.”

Torin adjusted his grip on his sword as he spoke up. “I’ll take lead. Maren, you stay close to Elara—she’s still mapping things, right?”

Elara nodded absently, already pulling out her notebook again despite Elias’s earlier warning to pack up. “Almost done,” she muttered, adding a final note about the rope fragment before closing the book once more. “Just need to cross-reference with the tracks from yesterday’s raid.”

Maren rolled her eyes but held her tongue, falling into step behind Torin as they moved toward the guard post corridor. Kaelen followed close beside them, casting occasional glances at the ground beneath their boots—checking for signs of disturbance or movement that might indicate danger ahead.

Their progress was slow and deliberate, each member aware of their role within the group dynamic. Elias led from the front, keeping an eye on both the terrain and the party’s morale; Torin took point physically, scanning the horizon for any sign of approaching threats; Maren handled logistics—checking equipment, managing supplies, and ensuring everyone stayed together despite fatigue or distraction; Kaelen provided magical support where needed, using his limited abilities to obscure their movements or detect invisible hazards; and Elara analyzed evidence as they went, turning scattered clues into actionable intelligence.

Rain lashed against the rough-hewn bark of the oak where Elias stopped, turning the forest floor into a slick, mud-churned trap that demanded constant attention. The sky had opened up fully again, a sudden shift from the gentle drizzle of the village to a proper deluge that smelled of wet wool and turned earth. It was the kind of weather that made a traveler’s clothes heavy with water and his boots fill within minutes, yet Elias kept his stride steady, eyes scanning the line of trees for any unnatural break in the canopy or a shadow that didn’t belong. He held his cloak back against the wind, letting it flap slightly behind him, while keeping one hand near the hilt of his sword—not out of fear of immediate attack, but because the rain made every step into the unknown feel like a choice between caution and momentum. Behind him, Maren adjusted her pack with a grunt that sounded less like pain and more like satisfaction at having finished checking the gear. The broken cart axle she had dragged from the farm’s edge was strapped to her shoulder now, a clumsy but necessary weight that balanced against the hammer slung across her back. Her hands were gloved in thick leather, though the cuffs showed signs of dampness already soaking through; she’d ignored the earlier concern about her burned skin because the cold rain kept her from feeling the sting too much.

“We move now,” Elias said, his voice low and steady despite the wind trying to tear it away. “If we wait for the storm to break, the trail washes out.” Maren nodded once, tapping the axe haft against her palm as if checking its balance rather than comforting herself. “Mud’ll hide tracks in half an hour,” she replied, voice practical and clipped. “But goblins don’t care about mud. They leave their own mess regardless of rain or sun.”

Elara stepped forward with a notebook clutched to her chest, water dripping from the rim of her hood onto the pages where she’d sketched footprints earlier. She didn’t open it, but she did glance at Elias over the top of her spectacles, which were fogged by the humidity. “The arrowhead’s still sharp,” she murmured. “And the rope fragment—” She stopped mid-sentence as Kaelen flicked his gauntlet with a soft *click*, casting a faint ring of golden light through the gloom ahead. The brass relic pulsed gently, its inner glow steady despite the storm, offering enough illumination to read the trail markers without straining eyes or risking exposure to the dark corners of the woods.

“Good,” Elias said, glancing at the mage’s hand. “Keep it on until we’re clear.” Kaelen gave a small shrug, his expression unreadable in the dim light; he didn’t seem bothered by the effort it took to maintain the glow, nor did he comment on how much energy such magic must cost him over time.

Torin moved ahead into the tree line, boots sinking slightly into the mud as he tested each foothold with a practiced rhythm. He wasn’t scanning for threats so much as testing the ground itself—checking where branches had fallen, whether roots had shifted under pressure, and if any animal tracks might confuse their path. “Left side’s slicker,” he called back over the sound of wind rattling leaves. “Right bank holds better.”

Maren stepped up beside him, taking point on that flank now while Elias kept watch from the rear. “Lead me where it doesn’t slip,” she said, voice firm despite the rain. “I’ll follow your lead if you’re sure.”

Torin didn’t hesitate. He gestured toward a narrow ridge that ran parallel to the creek bend, its surface hardened by recent wind and exposed to less runoff than the lowlands below. “That way,” he said. “Steeper but firmer footing.” They began moving again, stepping carefully over roots and fallen branches that the rain had loosened. Elias walked near the center of the group, watching both ahead and behind, making sure no one lagged or strayed too close to the edge where the creek widened into a rocky embankment. The broken cart axle creaked against Maren’s shoulder with every step, a dull reminder of what they’d left behind at the farm. She didn’t complain about it; instead, she shifted her weight occasionally to redistribute the load, letting Elias see that she understood the necessity of carrying it even if it slowed them down.

“We’ll need to lighten up once we cross the tree line,” he said quietly. “Leave extra rope and tools there.”

“Agreed,” Maren replied without hesitation. “But I’m not leaving Nella’s straps or the iron fittings behind unless there’s no choice.”

“Not leaving anything vital,” Elias added. “Just the non-essentials. We’ll grab what we can once we’ve secured the captives.”

Elara walked slightly ahead of him now, using her own small lantern to check for loose stones or hidden dips in the trail. She carried a satchel filled with chalk and brushes, ready to mark any new evidence they found along the way. “The rope fragment’s still intact,” she said, holding it up so Elias could see it better. “It was tucked under straw near the doorframe. The goblins must’ve used it to drag something heavy out quickly.”

Elias nodded, studying the frayed ends where hemp fibers had been pulled apart by force. “Hasty retreat,” he confirmed. “They didn’t want to be seen here.”

Torin paused briefly at a cluster of bushes that seemed unusually bare of leaves, though the season wasn’t late enough for autumn shedding. He leaned forward and sniffed the air, his nose twitching slightly as if catching something faint beneath the scent of wet wood and rain. “Something’s off,” he said after a moment. “Not goblin smell, but… stale fear.”

Elias frowned, stepping closer to inspect the bush himself. Inside, near the base of the trunk, lay a small smear of dried blood—human, not animal—and something else that looked like charred fabric caught on the bark. “Burnt gear?” Elara asked, reaching out to take a sample with her gloved hand. She held it up for inspection, noting how the fibers had blackened unevenly, as if caught in a sudden fire or exposed to heat quickly before being discarded. “Could be from Nella’s crate,” she suggested. “Or maybe they burned something else first.”

Maren stepped forward and touched the charred cloth with her hammer’s grip, testing whether it was still hot enough to burn her skin. “Cold enough to touch,” she said. “But too fresh to have been there long.”

Torin nodded again, signaling them to move on without lingering. “Keep moving,” he said. “If they’re burning things, they’re preparing for something bigger than just raiding farms.”

Elias didn’t press further; he knew that speculation would only slow them down when they had limited time and energy. Instead, he focused on keeping the group together and watching for signs of goblin movement beyond what they could already see in the gloom. As they crossed into denser woods where light barely penetrated, Kaelen’s gauntlet dimmed slightly, its inner glow struggling against the rain-induced haze. He didn’t mention it, though Elias noticed the change immediately and adjusted his own pace accordingly.

“Save your energy,” he said quietly. “We’ll need you when we hit the keep.”

Kaelen gave a small smile that barely registered in the dim light. “I’ve got enough left for smoke screens,” he replied. “And maybe a shield if things get ugly.”

Elias nodded again, trusting that judgment more than any spell slot count could explain. The storm continued to pour down around them, rain falling hard enough to blur outlines and make speech difficult without raising voices. Yet the group kept moving forward, each member aware of their role in maintaining cohesion under these conditions. Maren led with her hammer ready, Torin watched the path ahead for traps or ambushes, Elara recorded findings as they went, Kaelen managed visibility and defense, and Elias coordinated everything while keeping an eye on morale. They weren’t heroes by some grand design—they were five people who had agreed to work together because no one else would help them survive another night of goblin raids. And tonight, with the rain masking their approach and the trail guiding them toward a ruined keep that sat beyond the tree line, they were closer than ever to proving themselves capable enough to face whatever lay ahead.
