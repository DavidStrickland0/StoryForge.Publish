The heavy iron doors of the storage chamber groaned, a sound like a dying beast that was quickly drowned out by the roar of rain against the keep’s crumbling walls. Inside, the air smelled of rotting grain, wet wool, and the sharp tang of dried blood that Elara had noted but not yet fully processed. The maps spread across a crude table made from a broken workbench were cluttered with pins marking raided farms: Nella’s place on the creek bend, Miller’s homestead near the stone bridge, and now a fresh notation for Blackwood Valley to the north.

Maren leaned against the wall, her hammer resting loosely in one hand while her other gripped the broken cart axle strapped to her back. She winced as she shifted her weight, the splintered wood digging into her shoulder. “Quiet enough,” she muttered, though her voice carried an edge of irritation that wasn’t entirely about the pain. “Too quiet. If those goblins sleep on a schedule, they’re waking up soon.”

Torin moved closer to the door, his hand still on the hilt of his sword, scanning the narrow corridor beyond for signs of movement. “They’ve been here at least three times since we left the village,” he said quietly. “The last patrol was an hour ago—enough time for them to regroup.”

Elias stood near the center of the room, reviewing the maps with Elara’s help. He pointed to a small circle marked with a red pin near the northern edge of the keep. “This is where Kaelen detected the ward,” he explained, gesturing toward the mage who was pacing slowly near the far wall. “He said there were three magical signatures total—two wards and one trap.”

Kaelen nodded, his brass gauntlet dimmed to a faint glow now that they were inside the keep. The residual light from earlier still lingered in the air, casting long shadows across the floor where puddles had formed around scattered debris. “The northern edge is the weakest point,” he said, his voice low and measured. “I can sense the ward there—it’s a simple concealment spell that masks movement within twenty feet of the wall. Beyond that, it fades.”

Elara stepped forward, pulling out her notebook again despite Elias’s earlier warning to pack up. She knelt beside a puddle, dipping her gloved finger into the water and examining the ripples formed by falling raindrops. “The ward is active,” she murmured, adding a note to her sketchbook. “But it doesn’t block detection entirely—it just hides motion. If we move deliberately, the goblins won’t see us until we’re close enough to be seen.”

Elias frowned slightly, studying the map again. “That means if we approach from the north, we risk being spotted once we cross that twenty-foot threshold. But if we come from another direction…” He paused, glancing at Maren and Torin. “Can you handle the southern approach? The rain makes it slippery, but the terrain is flatter there.”

Maren nodded once, adjusting her grip on the broken axle. “I can manage,” she replied. “But I’ll need to move slower than usual. My leg isn’t cooperating well enough for speed.”

Torin stepped forward, testing the ground with careful steps before committing his full weight to a muddy patch. “The southern bank is steep and slick,” he said. “It’s not ideal for quiet movement. But if we use the cover of the trees along the ridge, we can avoid detection until we’re within ten feet of the keep.”

Elias considered that possibility briefly before turning back to Kaelen. “Can you reinforce the ward? Or at least mask our approach?”

Kaelen hesitated, glancing at his gauntlet as it pulsed softly in response to his command. “I could try,” he said after a moment. “But it’ll drain some of my reserves. And if the goblins detect any disruption in the magic field, they might become more alert.”

Elias nodded slowly. “Understood. We’ll proceed cautiously regardless. Just keep us under cover until we’re within striking distance.”

Kaelen gave a brief shrug and raised his gauntlet again, this time focusing on masking their presence rather than revealing it. A faint haze of smoke curled around them, barely visible in the dim light but effective enough to obscure their outlines from any goblin eyes that might glance outward from the keep’s windows or open doors. The effect was subtle—a kind of visual ghosting that made them appear less solid, more like shadows slipping through the rain-soaked world outside.

Elara watched the smoke with interest, her pen moving quickly as she noted the nature of Kaelen’s spell. “It’s not a full invisibility,” she observed. “Just enough to blur our edges. Good for avoiding casual observation, but nothing against someone actively searching.”

“Exactly,” Kaelen replied with a small smile that didn’t quite reach his eyes. “And if we get too close, I’ll lose the effect entirely. So let’s stick to the plan and keep moving steadily.”

Torin stepped forward again, this time leading the way toward the southern approach as Elias had suggested. Maren followed closely behind him, her movements deliberate despite the added weight of the broken axle. Every step brought them closer to the keep’s perimeter, where the rain continued to fall hard enough to blur outlines and make speech difficult without raising voices.

As they approached the outer wall, the group paused briefly to assess the defenses Torin had identified earlier: natural barriers on three sides, with one open side toward the village. The broken outer wall provided a narrow passage that led directly into the courtyard beyond. Beyond that lay the main structure—a two-story building with rusted doors and leaning upper floors that seemed ready to collapse at any moment.

Elias signaled for caution as they reached the edge of the courtyard. “Remember,” he whispered, keeping his voice low even though no one else could hear him clearly over the rain. “We don’t engage unless necessary. Our goal is to locate the captives and get them out safely.”

“Understood,” Maren replied, her tone clipped and professional despite the strain on her face. “But if we encounter resistance, we’ll handle it quickly.”

Torin nodded once more before stepping through the broken wall and onto the uneven ground beyond. The courtyard was littered with debris from previous raids—broken crates, scattered tools, and scraps of cloth that had been dragged here or discarded in haste. Rainwater pooled in depressions between stones, reflecting the gray sky above while masking footprints that might have been left behind by recent activity.

Kaelen maintained the smoke screen as they crossed the courtyard, his gauntlet glowing faintly enough to obscure their forms without drawing attention from nearby wildlife—or worse, goblin scouts patrolling the perimeter. The haze dissipated slowly as they moved away from the keep’s walls, but it remained sufficient to mask their approach until they were well beyond the twenty-foot threshold where the ward would interfere.

Elara kept her notebook close at hand, sketching the layout of the courtyard and noting any potential hazards or cover options available for retreat or flanking maneuvers. She also examined the looted goods scattered around—the stolen grain sacks, bundles of cloth, and tools from various farms that had been piled haphazardly in corners of the courtyard. Some bore markings indicating they belonged to specific families or businesses, while others were simply generic items taken without care.

“Interesting,” she murmured under her breath, adding a note about the distribution patterns observed among the loot. “They seem to prioritize certain types of goods over others—grain and livestock take precedence over tools or clothing. Maybe they’re preparing for winter storage?”

Elias glanced at her briefly before turning his attention back to the path ahead. “Let’s keep moving,” he said quietly. “We’ll check the trapdoor soon enough.”

Torin led them toward a narrow corridor that wound deeper into the keep’s interior, its walls lined with rough-hewn stones and marked by signs of recent habitation—scratches in the wood, dried mud splattered against doorframes, and faint traces of footprints leading off in various directions. The air grew colder as they ventured further inward, the damp chill seeping through their clothing despite the rain outside.

Maren adjusted her pack one more time before continuing forward, her movements slower than before but no less determined. “If we find the captives,” she said after a moment, “we’ll secure them immediately and bring them back out. No delays.”

“Agreed,” Elias replied with a nod. “And if we encounter any guards or traps along the way, we’ll deal with them quietly. Remember—we’re not here to fight unless absolutely necessary.”

Torin nodded again before leading them down another twisting corridor that sloped gently downward toward what appeared to be a deeper section of the keep. The layout was unfamiliar yet familiar enough to suggest it had been used frequently for storage or as a temporary base camp. As they descended, the sound of dripping water echoed softly around them, accompanied by occasional distant noises that suggested activity elsewhere within the structure—perhaps goblins resting or preparing their next raid.

Kaelen watched his gauntlet intently as they moved deeper into the keep, noting how its glow dimmed slightly with each step taken away from the northern ward. “The magic is fading faster than expected,” he observed quietly. “I’ll need to conserve it for later use.”

“Understood,” Elias said before turning back to Elara. “Keep tracking any new evidence we find. If there are additional wards or traps, mark them clearly so we can avoid them.”

Elara nodded again, her pen moving steadily across the page as she added notes about the magical residue detected so far and their implications for navigating the keep’s defenses. “There’s a slight disturbance in the air near this corner,” she noted after a moment. “Could be another ward or just residual magic from previous use.”

Elias paused briefly to examine the spot herself, leaning forward slightly as if trying to detect something invisible to normal senses. After a moment, he shook his head and continued onward. “Just residual magic then. Let’s keep moving.”

As they descended further into the keep, the corridor opened into a larger chamber that seemed designed specifically for storage or temporary shelter. Crates of grain lined one side, while stacks of cloth and bundles of rope occupied another corner. In the center stood a crude table covered with maps similar to those Elias had reviewed earlier, along with scattered tools and other miscellaneous items scavenged from various farms.

Maren stepped forward cautiously, her hammer drawn slightly as she scanned the room for signs of goblin presence. “No movement yet,” she reported quietly. “But that doesn’t mean we’re safe.”

“Agreed,” Elias replied before gesturing toward the map-covered table. “Let’s check these maps again. Maybe we can determine exactly where the captives are being held based on the raid patterns noted here.”

Elara nodded and stepped forward to examine the maps more closely, her pen moving quickly as she marked routes and locations with fresh observations. “These maps show recent raids,” she explained after a moment. “Nella’s farmhouse was hit first, then Miller’s, and now Blackwood Valley is next on their list. If they’re following a pattern like this, the captives are likely being held in one of the deeper chambers beyond this area.”

Torin stepped forward again, his hand still on the hilt of his sword as he scanned the room for exits or entry points that might lead to those deeper chambers. “There’s a door at the far end,” he noted quietly. “Looks like it leads to a stairwell going down further. Probably where they keep prisoners.”

Elias nodded once before signaling for caution as they approached the door. “Let’s proceed carefully,” he said softly. “If anyone’s there, we’ll find out soon enough.”

The group moved forward together, stepping quietly over debris and puddles that dotted the floor. The air remained still and quiet save for the occasional drip of water echoing softly from above. Every sound seemed amplified in the confined space, making it easy to detect even the faintest creak of a settling beam or rustle of fabric shifting in the draft.

As they reached the door, Torin tested its weight before pushing it open slightly to peek inside. The chamber beyond was dimly lit by flickering torches mounted along the walls, their flames casting long shadows across stone steps that descended into darkness below. Faint screams had recently echoed softly through thin walls before fading into silence as rain continued to pour relentlessly outside.

Elias exchanged a glance with Maren and Torin before stepping forward himself. “This is where we need to go,” he said quietly. “If the captives are being held down here, we’ll find them soon enough.”

Maren nodded once more before drawing her hammer fully from its sling. “Stay close,” she warned softly. “And if we encounter any resistance, deal with it quickly but efficiently.”

Torin stepped forward again, his hand still on the hilt of his sword as he prepared to lead them down into the darkness below. The group descended carefully, each step bringing them closer to completing their mission—and further away from safety that awaited them back at the Crooked Stag. Yet despite exhaustion weighing heavily on each member’s shoulders and injuries reminding them constantly of how fragile life truly was under such conditions, determination drove them forward nonetheless.
