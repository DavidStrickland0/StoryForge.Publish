The heavy wooden door groaned as Maren pushed it open, revealing the interior of the keep’s deepest chamber. The air inside was colder and stiller than the storage area, carrying a distinct metallic tang that made Elias wince. It smelled of old blood, wet stone, and the faint, acrid scent of burning oil.

“Quiet,” Torin murmured, his hand resting on the pommel of his sword as he stepped across the threshold. His boots made no sound against the flagstones—likely damp with condensation or dust that had settled over years of abandonment. He scanned the room ahead, noting how the light from Kaelen’s smoke veil filtered through a high window in the far corner, casting long, distorted shadows against the walls.

Elara stepped forward immediately, pulling her notebook from her satchel again. She knelt beside a cluster of crates stacked near the center of the room—crates that had once held grain or tools but now sat half-empty, their contents scattered across the floor in small piles. Some of them were crushed underfoot; others bore fresh scratches where something sharp had dragged through them recently.

“Look here,” she whispered, pointing to one of the crates with her gloved finger. “These markings—they’re not random. Someone’s been cataloging what they took.” She ran a finger along the side of a crate marked with crude symbols scratched into the wood: a bird, a fish, and a strange symbol that looked like a broken circle with a dot in the middle.

Elias leaned down to examine it more closely. “That looks like an attempt at communication,” he said softly. “They’re signaling each other—or maybe leaving instructions for whoever comes next.” He traced the pattern with his thumb, feeling the rough texture of the scratchings beneath his skin. “If they’re planning raids on Blackwood Valley, this could be part of a larger operation.”

Maren stepped over to inspect the crates herself, lifting one slightly to check its weight before setting it back down carefully. “They’ve been here for days,” she noted, her voice low and pragmatic. “Enough time to restock supplies, set up traps, and organize patrols. But no sign of anyone moving around right now.”

Torin nodded slowly, scanning the perimeter again. “No guards at least. Not yet. We need to be careful though—if they’re using wards like Kaelen mentioned earlier, stepping too loudly could trigger something worse than a simple alarm.”

Kaelen remained near the entrance, his brass gauntlet glowing faintly as he maintained the smoke screen around them. He watched the group’s movements closely, ready to adjust the veil if needed. “I can sense the wards,” he said quietly, his tone calm but serious. “They’re active. They don’t just hide us—they react. If we force a breach…”

“They won’t let us pass without consequences,” Elara finished for him, her voice steady despite the tension in the air. She stood up and walked over to the wall where a series of faint, glowing lines ran vertically along the stone surface—remnants of magical residue that seemed to pulse with a soft blue light when she touched them with her quartz-dusted fingers.

“See this?” she asked, holding up her hand so Elias could see the faint shimmering trails left behind by her analysis. “This isn’t just fog or smoke—it’s a membrane. A barrier designed to conceal whatever’s inside from outsiders. Touch it too hard, and it reacts.”

Elias studied the glowing lines carefully, trying to piece together what kind of magic had been used here. “Concealment wards,” he repeated softly. “So we have to move silently, avoid triggering anything, and find another way in if necessary.”

Torin shifted his weight slightly, testing the floorboards underfoot before stepping forward again. “Then we proceed slowly. One step at a time. No sudden movements.”

Maren grunted in agreement, adjusting her pack as she moved toward the far end of the room where a narrow staircase led downward into darkness. “Down there,” she said, gesturing toward the stairs. “Captives are probably held in the lower levels. Let’s check it out.”

As they began their descent, Elias noticed how the walls changed texture—the rough-hewn stone giving way to smoother, more deliberately crafted surfaces that suggested recent activity. Near the base of the staircase, a small door stood slightly ajar, its hinges rusted but still functional. Beyond it lay a short corridor lined with torches whose flames flickered weakly in the drafty air.

“Wait,” Elara called out, stopping abruptly as she approached the doorway. Her eyes widened as she pointed to something on the floor just inside the room beyond—a pile of scattered items including ropes, lanterns, and several bound figures lying face-down against the wall.

Elias stepped forward cautiously, peering into the dimly lit chamber where three women sat huddled together in the corner, their faces pale and drawn with exhaustion. One of them glanced up as they entered, her expression shifting from fear to relief when she recognized them as rescuers rather than raiders.

“Nella!” Maren exclaimed, lowering her hammer slightly as she stepped forward. “You’re alive.”

The woman nodded weakly, struggling to sit upright before collapsing back against the wall. Beside her lay another figure—Hobb, who groaned softly when he heard his name called out. Both men were bruised and exhausted but clearly conscious, which meant the rescue was already underway.

“Where’s Skrit?” Elias asked quietly, keeping his voice low as he scanned the rest of the room for signs of the goblin chief or other captives being held elsewhere.

Maren shook her head sharply. “Not here. Somewhere else in the keep. Probably up near the top floor.”

Torin moved silently toward the stairs again, preparing to ascend and locate any additional prisoners while Maren attended to Nella and Hobb. Elara stayed close by, documenting their condition and noting how much food and water they’d been given—or denied—during captivity.

Elias remained at the center of the group, observing everything with careful attention. He knew that rescuing these villagers was only half the battle; defeating Skrit and his band required strategy, patience, and courage—all qualities the party possessed in abundance. But as he looked around the cramped, dimly lit chamber, he couldn’t help but wonder how many others were being held deep within these walls—and whether they would survive long enough to be freed.

With steady resolve, Elias gestured for the group to follow him up the stairs, toward whatever challenges awaited them in the upper levels of the ruined keep. The rain continued to lash against the outside world, masking their approach with its relentless drumming, but inside the keep, silence reigned supreme—a fragile peace that could shatter at any moment if someone made a mistake or triggered an alarm.

They moved forward together, each member aware of their role in maintaining cohesion under these conditions. Maren led with her hammer ready, Torin watched the path ahead for traps or ambushes, Elara analyzed evidence as they went, Kaelen managed visibility and defense, and Elias coordinated everything while keeping an eye on morale. They weren’t heroes by some grand design—they were five people who had agreed to work together because no one else would help them survive another night of goblin raids. And tonight, with the storm continuing outside and the captured villagers waiting for rescue inside, they were closer than ever to proving themselves capable enough to face whatever lay ahead.
