The heavy wooden door groaned as Maren pushed it open, sealing out the sound of the rain for a moment before letting the damp chill seep back in. She stepped into the threshold first, her boots sinking slightly into a layer of dust and debris that hadn't been disturbed for some time. "Smells like copper," she called out, keeping her voice low. "Old blood, or maybe just rust on the iron bars of these cages."

Elias followed closely behind, his hand resting near the hilt of his sword but not drawn yet. He paused to look around the chamber, noting the layout with a critical eye that had honed itself over years of navigating ruins and narrow passages. The room was long and narrow, flanked by rows of empty cages on one side—likely where livestock were held before being driven away—and what appeared to be storage shelves stacked with sacks of grain or dried meat on the other. In the center of the room stood a single table covered in maps and scattered papers, which Elias recognized immediately as raid plans.

"Look at this," Elara whispered, stepping forward with her satchel already open. She pulled out a small vial of quartz dust and sprinkled it carefully onto one of the maps spread across the table. The powder settled, forming a faint shimmer that caught the dim light filtering through cracks in the ceiling stone. "These wards aren't just simple concealment membranes anymore," she murmured, her eyes narrowing as she studied the residue left behind by Kaelen's earlier smoke spell. "They're adapting. They feed off the magic used to hide them."

Kaelen nodded from where he stood near the back of the room, his brass gauntlet still glowing faintly despite the strain it seemed to put on him. His expression was unreadable, but there was a tension in his shoulders that Elias hadn't noticed before. "I didn't mean to overdo it," the mage said quietly. "But if we'd waited until morning, they might have moved us out already."

"And now?" Maren asked, glancing toward the heavy steel door at the far end of the chamber where captives were supposedly being held. "What happens now that we know how these wards work?"

"We don't force them," Elias replied, his tone firm but not harsh. "Not yet anyway. If we try to break through without understanding exactly what triggers an alarm, we risk alerting anyone inside who's been waiting for us. Remember—the goblins aren't just opportunistic thieves; they're planning larger raids. These wards are part of a system designed to keep them safe while they organize."

Torin stepped up beside Elara, scanning the corridor ahead with practiced caution. "Then we need another way in," he said, gesturing toward the map on the table. "There's a narrow passage leading off to the left. If we can get past that trap without setting it off, we might be able to slip into the storage area where they keep their prisoners."

Elara nodded again, pulling her notebook out once more to sketch a quick diagram of the room. "Right here," she pointed at a specific location on the map. "That passage connects directly to the northern edge of the cell block. But there's also a chute leading down from above—it looks like it could collapse under pressure, though I'd need to test it first."

Maren crossed her arms over her chest, shaking her head slightly. "Testing traps isn't exactly my forte," she muttered. "Leave that to someone else if you want me doing the heavy lifting."

"I wasn't asking you to do anything dangerous," Elias interjected gently. "Just careful observation. You're not alone here; we've got Torin watching your back, and Elara analyzing every detail before we act."

Maren grunted in acknowledgment, though her expression remained skeptical. She turned her attention back to the door at the far end of the room, listening intently for any sign of movement or noise coming from beyond. "If they're expecting us," she said finally, "then there's probably someone posted outside. And if there is, we'll need a plan that accounts for both the trapped passage and whatever else lies ahead."

Elias nodded once more, glancing toward Kaelen as he spoke. "You mentioned earlier that forcing those wards would trigger an alarm," he reminded him softly. "So our goal here is to avoid drawing attention while still getting inside. That means we'll have to move quietly and carefully, using what little knowledge we've gained so far."

Kaelen gave a small smile that barely registered in the dim light. "I can help with that," he said. "My smoke magic can obscure our movements further, making it harder for anyone outside to see us even if they're looking directly at us. Just keep an eye on how much energy I'm using—I won't be able to maintain this indefinitely."

"Good enough for me," Elias replied. "Let's get moving then. Torin, take point and watch for traps. Maren, stay close to Elara so she has someone to rely on if things go wrong. And remember—we're trying to rescue two people, not destroy a stronghold. We'll do whatever it takes to bring them home safely."

With that, the group began moving toward the trapped passage, each member aware of their role within the dynamic. Elias led from the front, keeping an eye on both the terrain and the party's morale; Torin took point physically, scanning the horizon for any sign of approaching threats; Maren handled logistics—checking equipment, managing supplies, and ensuring everyone stayed together despite fatigue or distraction; Kaelen provided magical support where needed, using his limited abilities to obscure their movements or detect invisible hazards; and Elara analyzed evidence as they went, turning scattered clues into actionable intelligence.

As they approached the narrow opening leading off to the left, Torin paused briefly to inspect the area around it. There was a loose stone near the entrance that looked like it might have been placed deliberately to trigger a mechanism if disturbed. "This thing's tricky," he muttered under his breath. "If we push too hard against it, it'll probably fall and set whatever trap's rigged here."

"How about we lift it instead of pushing?" Elias suggested quietly. "Torin, grab that rope over your shoulder and pull gently while Maren helps stabilize the weight from below."

Maren hesitated for a moment before nodding in agreement. She stepped forward and placed her hammer against the base of the loose stone, using its flat surface to distribute pressure evenly as she and Torin worked together to lift it carefully off the ground. Once the stone was free, they moved aside just enough to peek into the passage beyond, revealing a steep incline leading down toward what looked like a series of cages stacked along the walls.

"Looks promising," Elara whispered, stepping forward cautiously with her lantern in hand. "But we'll need to be extra careful here. One wrong move and we could wake everyone up."

"Don't worry about that," Torin said, his voice steady despite the tension building inside him. "We've got this under control. Just follow my lead and don't let anyone else panic us into making mistakes."

Elias nodded once more, watching closely as his companions navigated the steep incline with practiced precision. They reached the bottom of the slope without incident, finding themselves face-to-face with a group of goblins huddled near a crude fire pit in the corner of the chamber.
