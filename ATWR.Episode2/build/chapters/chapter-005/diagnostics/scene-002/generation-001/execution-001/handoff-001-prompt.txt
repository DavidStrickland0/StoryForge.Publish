Elias did not wait for the goblins to finish their frantic shouting. He stepped out from the shadows of the gallery railing, his boots making a soft, deliberate sound on the worn stone floor above.

Below, in the dim light of the main hall, the movement of the goblin horde slowed. They were a chaotic tangle of matted furs and rusted iron, huddled around a central pile of stolen grain sacks. The noise in the room was a low, jagged roar of confusion, a sound like a hive of angry wasps that had been kicked.

Elias raised his voice, letting it carry down into the open space without shouting. He projected it with the calm, steady authority of a man who had every intention of staying exactly where he was.

"Skrit," he called out. "I know you are in there."

The silence that followed was heavy, pressurized. The goblins below stopped their shuffling. A dozen yellow eyes snapped upward, fixed on the figure standing on the gallery. The air in the hall grew thick with a sudden, sharp tension, the kind that precedes a strike.

Elias turned to his companions. He nodded toward the group standing in the shadows behind him. Torin, still bearing the weight of Hobb on his back, shifted his stance to face the center of the hall. Maren moved to the edge of the gallery, her hand resting on the haft of her hammer, her eyes scanning the goblins below with cold, clinical precision. Elara and Kaelen flanked them, their postures relaxed but ready, a silent wall of competence behind Elias.

He looked back down at the goblins. He could see the way their eyes darted, looking for the others, looking for the threat. He saw the fear in their wide, dark pupils, a fear that was easily readable in the way their shoulders hunched and their hands gripped their crude weapons.

He knew Skrit Ironjaw was not a warrior. He was a raider, a scavenger, a petty king of a small, frightened band. He was not a hero. He was a man who had taken what he could and was now facing a group of people who had the strength, the skill, and the will to take it all back.

Elias took a step forward, placing one foot on the lowest rung of the gallery’s support ladder.

"Come down, Skrit," Elias said, his voice cutting through the silence of the hall. "We have your people. We have your grain. And if you don’t show yourself, I’m going to start taking it all. Piece by piece."

The goblins held their breath. The silence stretched, taut and brittle.

Then, from the darkness of the chief’s room, a door creaked open.

A figure emerged. He was shorter than Elias, thicker, with a head that looked like it had been squeezed in a vice. He wore a patchwork of leather and chainmail, a crude crown of twisted iron on his brow. His face was a map of scars, his eyes small and hard, like two beads of black glass.

Skrit Ironjaw stepped into the light.

He did not run. He did not shout. He walked with a slow, deliberate pace, his eyes fixed on Elias, his hand resting on the hilt of a short, curved knife at his hip.

The goblins in the hall shifted, their bodies leaning toward their chief, their fear now tempered by a sense of hope. They had their king. They had their leader. And he was coming.

Elias watched him approach, his own posture steady, his gaze unblinking. He could see the calculation in Skrit’s eyes, the rapid, frantic search for an opening, a weakness, a flaw in their formation.

But he found none.

He was faced with a wall of steel, of silence, of a quiet, deadly certainty that was more frightening than any shout, any blow, any display of power.

Skrit stopped at the center of the hall, his eyes sweeping over the gallery, over the five figures standing there, over the captured man on Torin’s back, over the woman in Maren’s arms.

He looked at Elias.

And for the first time, in the long, dark history of his small, frightened band, Skrit Ironjaw felt the weight of the world settle onto his shoulders.

He felt the silence.

And he felt the end.
