The corridor narrowed, the ceiling dropping until Torin had to tilt his head back to avoid the rough-hewn flagstones. The air grew heavier, thick with the scent of damp rot and something sharper, like rusted iron. Elias kept his hand on Maren’s shoulder, not to push, but to maintain the spacing Torin had established. They were a single, breathing unit, moving through the dark with the slow, deliberate grace of water finding its level.

Torin paused at a junction where the stone floor split into three paths. To the left, the passage descended steeply, the air rushing out in a cold, steady draft. Straight ahead, the corridor continued into a gloom so complete that even Kaelen’s dimmed light seemed to die a few feet out. To the right, a narrow fissure in the wall, barely wide enough for a man to slip through sideways.

Torin pointed to the right-hand gap. "There," he whispered. "See the scuff marks?"

Elias moved closer, his boots silent on the uneven stone. He saw it now: a series of parallel scratches in the mortar, deep and fresh, leading into the fissure. Above the gap, a single, crude hook was embedded in the stone, from which dangled a length of frayed rope.

"A trap?" Elias asked.

"Maybe," Torin said, his eyes narrowing. "Or a shortcut. Goblins aren't builders, Elias. They take what’s there. If they’ve used that rope, it means they’ve moved through it recently. And if they’ve moved through it, it’s probably not guarded. Not by anyone who wants to stay hidden."

Elara crouched beside the floor, her fingers tracing the dust. "The dust is disturbed here," she murmured. "But not by feet. By dragging." She looked up, her face pale in the faint glow of Kaelen’s gauntlet. "Someone was dragged out of that passage. Recently. The drag marks are fresh. The mud is still damp."

A chill that had nothing to do with the temperature settled in Elias’s chest. He looked at the rope, then at the left-hand passage where the cold draft was strongest. The left passage led down, toward the cellar levels they had been expecting. The right passage was a dead end, or a trap, or a way out.

"We don’t know what’s down that draft," Maren said, her voice low and steady. She was staring at the left passage, her hammer resting on her shoulder, her posture tense. "Cold air usually means a room with a fire. Or a hole in the roof. But it could also mean a pit."

"Or the captives," Elias said.

Silence held the group. The weight of the names—Nella, Hobb—hung in the air between them, heavy and real.

Torin shook his head. "We can’t risk the left without knowing what’s at the bottom. If it’s a pit, we lose Maren before we can even react. If it’s a room, we walk in blind. The right side is known. We know they’ve used it. We know they’ve dragged something through it."

"It’s a gamble," Elara said.

"It’s the only one we can see," Torin replied. He turned to Elias. "Your call."

Elias looked at Maren. She met his gaze, her expression unreadable, but her hands were steady on her hammer. She didn’t look afraid. She looked ready.

"We take the right," Elias said.

Torin gave a sharp nod. He stepped into the fissure, his body twisting to navigate the tight space. He emerged on the other side, where the passage opened into a wider, dimly lit chamber. The light here was different—yellow, flickering, casting long, dancing shadows against the stone walls.

"Fire," Torin whispered. "A small fire. And... voices."

Elias moved past him, stepping carefully into the new space. The chamber was small, circular, and cluttered with the debris of goblin life. There were piles of stolen grain sacks, half-eaten food, and broken tools. In the center, a small fire burned in a ring of flat stones, its flames low and controlled.

And there were goblins.

Three of them sat around the fire, huddled together for warmth. They were speaking in low, guttural murmurs, their eyes half-closed. One of them was sharpening a crude knife against a stone, the sound of the rasp echoing softly in the still air.

Elias held his breath. The others followed him in, one by one, their movements fluid and silent. They formed a semicircle behind a stack of empty crates, their bodies pressed against the cold stone wall.

From their position, they could see the goblins clearly. They could hear the rustle of the fire, the sharp scrape of the knife, the wet sound of chewing. They could smell the sour stink of goblin sweat and the sweet, cloying scent of rotting fruit.

And they could see the door.

It was a heavy, iron-banded door, set into the far wall of the chamber. It was closed, but not locked. A small gap at the bottom revealed a sliver of darkness beyond. But it was the sound that came from that darkness that made Elias’s blood run cold.

A cough. Weak, raspy, human.

Then, a voice. Thin, cracked, but unmistakable.

"Please," it whispered. "If there’s anyone there... please."

Elias closed his eyes. He knew that voice. He had heard it in the tavern, in the village square, in the dreams he had refused to have.

It was Nella.

He opened his eyes and looked at Torin. The soldier’s jaw was tight, his hand resting on the pommel of his sword. He looked at Elara, who was staring at the goblins, her face a mask of concentration. He looked at Maren, who was watching the door, her eyes sharp and bright with a fierce, protective anger.

He looked at Kaelen, who stood slightly behind them, his gauntlet dim, his face pale with exhaustion.

"We’re here," Elias whispered, his voice barely a breath. "We’re here, Nella."

The goblins did not hear him. They were too deep in their own world, their own small, cruel rituals of survival. They were unaware of the five people standing in the shadows, watching them, waiting for the right moment to strike.

Elias raised a hand, signaling for silence. He pointed to the door.

Torin nodded. He moved to the left, positioning himself to cover the exit if the goblins panicked. Maren moved to the right, her hammer resting against her leg, ready to swing. Elara and Kaelen stayed close to Elias, a wall of quiet competence behind him.

The cough came again. Weak, but persistent.

Elias took a step forward.

The goblin sharpening the knife looked up. His yellow eyes widened. He froze, the knife hovering in the air.

Then, slowly, he began to stand.
