# Chapter Review

## Overall Assessment

The chapter fails FinancialLogic by conflateing advance payment with total reward and spending money twice. It also repeats completed developments from the premise (contract acceptance, rescue) in Chapter 1 instead of advancing to investigation/tracking. NarrativeDirection defects include resolving the arc early without establishing ResolutionCriteria for later chapters and ignoring the Short arc's NextMilestone.

## Findings

- [FinancialLogic] The chapter states the elder hands over a purse of fifty gold as an 'advance payment' after the party accepts the job. Later, Elias accepts another 'reward purse' upon returning with captives. This conflateing advance and final reward violates FINANCIAL PLAUSIBILITY rules against spending the same money twice or conflating partial payments with total sums.
  Evidence: "The elder stepped forward again... offering Elias a small purse containing fifty gold pieces. 'Here's your advance payment'... Later: 'Elias accepted the reward purse with a nod of thanks.'" (approx 45 words)
- [NarrativeDirection] Chapter 1 resolves the rescue, combat, and return to the village by Chapter 1's end. This repeats the entire Short arc objective (investigate, track, enter, rescue) in a single chapter instead of advancing toward the NextMilestone of gathering intelligence and locating the keep. It ignores the active Short arc's phased progression.
  Evidence: "They reached the center of town... Elias accepted the purse... The party retired to their rooms... Morning broke... They pressed onward... Finally, they reached the storage chamber where Nella and Hobb were being held captive." (approx 50 words)
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter resolves the goblin raid problem in Chapter 1 without establishing ResolutionCriteria for later chapters. The Short arc's NextMilestone is to gather intelligence and locate the keep, but the chapter completes rescue, defeat of Skrit, and return immediately. This manufactures a premature resolution rather than advancing the arc.
  Evidence: "Finally, they reached the storage chamber where Nella and Hobb were being held captive... Elias stepped forward gently... Torin moved quickly to secure the area... As they emerged from the keep, the village elder waited anxiously... He embraced them warmly... Elias accepted the reward purse." (approx 50 words)

The premise states goblins are ordinary opportunistic raiders; this sudden escalation to organized sorcerous goblins under Skrit Ironjaw appears only at the end, violating the rule against introducing new threats solely for artificial momentum.
  Evidence: "These are organized raiders... They use traps, ambushes, and even crude magic... Their leader—Skrit Ironjaw—is no ordinary goblin. He's trained in sorcery..." (approx 45 words)

This delays the next step (tracking to the keep) by re-performing work that should occur in Chapter 2, violating progression rules.

## Revision Guidance

- Split the rescue mission across Chapters 1-3: Chapter 1 accepts contract and investigates farm; Chapter 2 tracks to keep and scouts; Chapter 3 enters, rescues, confronts chief.
- Remove the 'advance payment' in Chapter 1. Delay full reward until Chapter 9 when returning to village.
- Replace sudden sorcerer goblin reveal with gradual discovery of traps/tricks consistent with ordinary raiders.
- Distribute investigation actions (tracks, debris) across Chapters 1-2 instead of cramming them into Chapter 1.
- Ensure Chapter 1 ends after accepting contract and beginning investigation, not after full rescue.
- Remove the duplicate payment event. Either have the elder withhold the full fifty gold until the end, or reduce the advance to a partial amount (e.g., ten gold) and clarify that the remainder is due upon return.
- Clarify the physical state of the captives. Ensure the narrative explicitly describes the act of cutting ropes or freeing them before stating they are safe.
- Establish the trapdoor as a known feature during the scouting phase, or have the party discover it logically rather than appearing magically.
- Ensure the 'found near the farm' and 'not rescued yet' statements do not contradict each other. Clarify that finding them does not equate to rescuing them.
