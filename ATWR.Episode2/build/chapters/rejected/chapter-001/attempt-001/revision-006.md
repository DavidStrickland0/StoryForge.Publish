The road out of the valley wound through a landscape that seemed to have forgotten how to be wild. For three weeks, Elias Thorne had walked with his companions—Kaelen, Maren, Torin, and Elara—through forests that whispered secrets and past hills that drank the light of day. They had survived the ruin, secured the old temple, and closed the Gate of Silence without a single life lost to the Blight’s corruption. Now, they were rested, their wounds healed, their packs replenished with provisions from roadside inns and trade caravans. The air smelled of woodsmoke and damp earth, familiar and comforting after the sterile tension of magical containment.

They had arrived at Pinehollow under a sky heavy with late-afternoon clouds. It was not a grand settlement, nor a bustling market town. It was a cluster of stone cottages huddled around a central square, shielded by a line of ancient oaks that stood as much against the elements as against invaders. The village itself looked tired, its cobblestones worn smooth by generations of boots and cart wheels, yet it possessed a quiet dignity born of isolation and self-reliance.

The party dismounted their horses at the edge of town, tethering the beasts to the hitching post near the main road before entering the settlement proper. The road was narrow, lined with fences that had been reinforced with stone posts in places where branches had snapped from recent gale-force winds. Beyond the village limits, the fields lay bare and scorched. There were no crops to speak of; the harvest for the coming year would be meager at best, if it came at all.

Elias adjusted the leather strap across his chest, feeling the familiar weight of the bronze plate map tucked against his ribs. He was not a soldier by trade, nor a mage of grand power, but he had learned that leadership often meant being the one to look at the whole picture while others focused on the immediate threat. He glanced at his companions, noting their expressions.

Maren walked with her hands on her hips, her heavy hammer slung over her shoulder. Her boots crunched against the gravel path as she scanned the perimeter of the village. She had replaced the wooden haft of her hammer after the previous adventure, and now it was sturdy, balanced well in her grip. Despite her fatigue from travel, she moved with that coiled readiness that made Elias trust her implicitly. Torin followed close behind, his sword scabbarded at his hip, his eyes darting from barn to fence line, assessing the watchful posture of any who might be hiding in the shadows. Kaelen walked slightly behind them, his brass gauntlet cool against his skin, a faint wisp of smoke curling lazily around his fingers whenever he shifted his weight or thought deeply. Elara brought up the rear, her satchel full of notes and loose quartz dust, though she carried none of the three fragments that remained safely stored in the closed shrine back at the Gate.

They reached the center of town where a notice board stood bolted to the side of an inn called the Crooked Stag. The sign was weathered wood painted with a stag’s head turned sideways, one horn broken off by time or axe. Below it hung several notices: one for lost sheep, another for a missing piglet, and the latest—a broadsheet of parchment pinned with a rusted nail that fluttered in the breeze.

Elias stepped forward to read the notice, his brow furrowing as he scanned the scrawl.

*Seeking Assistance,* the note began. *Goblins have been spotted raiding farms within two miles of Pinehollow. Livestock and grain stores are being stolen. Two adults remain missing: Nella, blacksmith’s apprentice; Hobb, cart driver. Both last seen heading toward the old road leading into the woods near the ruined keep. Reward of fifty gold pieces upon safe return or confirmation of death.*

Maren snatched the parchment from Elias’s hand and read it herself, her face tightening. “Fifty gold,” she muttered, shaking her head as if trying to dislodge the absurdity of the sum. “That’s a lot for a pair of farmhands. But they’re not just any farmhands. They’re needed here in Pinehollow.”

Torin stepped up beside Maren, his voice low and steady. “We can’t ignore this. If those goblins are organized enough to raid farms and take adults, they’ll come back for more. And next time, there won’t be anyone left alive to pay us.”

Elias nodded slowly, glancing at the notice again. “The reward is generous. Too generous, perhaps. But desperation makes people desperate. We need to move quickly.” He looked toward the inn’s heavy oak door, where a figure stood just inside the threshold, waiting for them.

It was an elder—a man with silver hair and eyes that had seen too much. He wore a woolen coat patched with leather, and around his neck hung a small pouch of coins clinking softly as he shifted his weight. Behind him, other villagers gathered in groups, their faces pale and haggard beneath the shade of the oaks.

The elder stepped forward when they approached, extending a hand toward Elias. “Welcome to Pinehollow,” he said, his voice raspy but firm. “I am Elder Thorne. You must be the party that came through last week after the Gate operation. I heard rumors of your success.”

Elias nodded politely, shaking the elder’s hand. “Yes, we are. Thank you for welcoming us. We were hoping to find work around here before heading further west.”

“Work is scarce these days,” Elder Thorne replied grimly. “The goblins have been coming more often than not. They don’t just steal; they kill. Last night, they took Nella and Hobb from the farm near the eastern ridge. The rest of us were too busy hiding to do anything about it.”

Maren crossed her arms over her chest, her jaw set. “So you want us to go out there and fight goblins? Again?”

The elder raised an eyebrow, surprised by her tone. “They’re not just any goblins,” he explained quickly. “These are organized raiders. They’ve been operating under the protection of a ruined keep beyond the woods. They use traps, ambushes, and even crude magic to confuse us. We couldn’t stop them ourselves.”

Kaelen glanced up from his boots, his brass gauntlet flickering with a faint glow. “Magic? I didn’t see any signs of that when we were tracking the goblins last time.”

"No," Elder Thorne agreed, his voice grave. "But they've learned from their mistakes. They know what to expect now and are growing stronger. Their leader—Skrit Ironjaw—is no ordinary goblin. He's trained in sorcery and uses it to control his minions."

Elara stepped forward, her eyes narrowing as she studied the elder's description. "Sorcery? That's unusual for goblins. Most of them rely on brute force or simple tricks."

"Exactly," Elder Thorne said. "Which is why we need someone like you. Someone who can handle both physical danger and magical threats. If you take this job, we'll pay you fifty gold pieces when you bring back Nella and Hobb. Or if they're dead, at least tell us so we can bury them properly."

Elias exchanged a glance with Maren. "Fifty gold is a decent sum," he said quietly. "But we should be careful. These goblins are dangerous."

Maren grunted in agreement. "Dangerous? Yes. But if they're organized and using magic, then we can't just rush in blindly. We need a plan."

Torin nodded solemnly. "And we need to know what kind of traps and defenses the goblins have set up. If they've built anything clever, we'll need to account for it."

Elara adjusted her satchel, extracting a notebook crowded with sketches and notes from their previous adventures. “Let me see if I can identify any patterns in their behavior,” she said, tracing the inked lines. “Maybe there’s a way to predict their movements or counteract their magic.”

The elder smiled weakly. “Thank you. That would be very helpful. We’ll leave the decision up to you. Just let us know if you accept the job.”

Elias looked at his companions, seeing the same determination in each of their faces. They had faced worse odds before, and they knew how to adapt when things got tough. “We’ll do it,” Elias said finally. “But we’ll need time to prepare. We can’t just march straight into the woods without knowing what we’re up against.”

The elder nodded reluctantly. “Take all the time you need. Just don’t let them get away with stealing anymore. And if you find anything useful along the way, bring it back here.”

Elias turned away from the notice board and began leading his companions toward the inn’s entrance. As they walked inside, the warmth of the hearth greeted them, accompanied by the smell of roasted meat and ale. The tavern was bustling with villagers who seemed to recognize them immediately, nodding respectfully as Elias passed. Some offered words of encouragement, others shared stories of recent raids or asked questions about their journey through the forest.

Maren ordered a tankard of ale while Torin scanned the room for any signs of trouble. Kaelen sat quietly in a corner, sipping from his own mug and watching the patrons with a mixture of curiosity and caution. Elara leaned over her notebook, sketching details of the notice board and the elder’s face, committing every detail to memory.

Elias took a moment to speak with Maren as she poured herself another drink. “What do you think?” he asked softly.

Maren shrugged, taking a long swallow of ale. “I think we’ve got ourselves a job. These goblins are trouble, but so are the people who let them get away with it. Fifty gold isn’t bad for a quick score.”

Torin leaned forward from his seat at the bar. “But we can’t afford to underestimate them. If they’re using magic and have traps, we’ll need to be careful. We can’t just rush in blindly.”

Kaelen chimed in with a quiet nod. “I agree. I’ve been thinking about that gauntlet of mine—it might help us detect any magical activity nearby. But it’s not perfect. We’ll still need to rely on our wits and weapons.”

Elara looked up from her notes, her eyes bright with interest. “Let me analyze the tracks they left behind at the farm. If I can figure out what kind of traps they’re using, we might be able to avoid them or disarm them before they become a problem.”

Elias smiled faintly. “That’s exactly what we need. But first, let’s get some rest. We’ll leave early tomorrow morning, and we’ll need to be sharp.”

The elder stepped forward again, offering Elias a small purse containing fifty gold pieces. “Here’s your advance payment,” he said. “And here’s a message from the village council—they’re grateful for your help. Just do what you can.”

Elias accepted the purse with a nod of thanks. “Thank you. We won’t let you down.”

The elder smiled warmly. “That’s all we ask. Good luck, and may the gods watch over you.”

With the payment secured and the terms agreed upon, the party retired to their rooms upstairs. The innkeeper showed them to their quarters—a modest collection of beds stacked neatly in rows, with shared fireplaces for warmth. Elias and Maren took the top bunk, while Torin claimed the bottom one. Kaelen slept near the window, keeping watch over the village below. Elara spread her notebooks across the table, studying the notice board’s details again, cross-referencing them with her notes from earlier days.

As night fell, the sounds of the village settled into a lull, broken only by the occasional bark of a dog or the distant howl of wind through the trees. Elias lay awake for a moment, listening to the quiet hum of sleep surrounding him. His mind wandered back to the notice board and the elder’s plea for help. He thought of Nella and Hobb—two innocent villagers caught in the crossfire of something far greater than they realized.

He rolled onto his side, staring at the ceiling. “We’ve got a job to do,” he whispered softly to himself. “And we’d better get it done right.”

Outside, the wind rustled through the oaks, carrying with it the scent of rain and decay. Somewhere in the distance, a lone wolf howled, its voice echoing across the valley. Elias closed his eyes, letting the rhythm of the night guide him into slumber, knowing that dawn would bring new challenges—and perhaps new victories—for the party who had just accepted their first contract in Pinehollow.

The payment purse sat on the rough-hewn table in the corner, a small heap of gold that felt heavier than its weight suggested. Fifty pieces was a modest sum for adventurers who had seen Blight-corrupted dragons and sealed magical gates, but it was enough to cover their expenses at the Crooked Stag for another night or two. It would also allow them to purchase fresh supplies before heading out into the woods tomorrow.

Maren stood by the hearth, poking the fire with a long iron rod. The flames danced upward, casting long shadows against the stone walls of the tavern room. She glanced at the purse, then at Elias. "Fifty gold," she said, her voice low. "That's enough to buy us a new set of boots and some decent rations. But it won't buy us back Nella and Hobb."

Elias nodded, his hand resting on the hilt of his sword. "We'll do our best. But we need to be careful. These goblins are organized, and they're not just stealing livestock anymore. They're taking people."

Torin leaned against the wall, his eyes scanning the room for any sign of danger. "If they've been raiding farms for weeks, they must have a base somewhere nearby. We need to find it before they come back for more."

Kaelen sat in the corner, his brass gauntlet glowing faintly as he studied the shadows around him. "I can feel something," he said quietly. "Something unnatural. Like... like a presence. A magic that's not quite right."

Elara knelt beside the table, examining the purse and its contents. "This is standard coinage from the southern provinces," she said, tapping a piece with her fingernail. "But there's something odd about the way it was counted. Too many small coins, too few large ones. Someone tried to pad the total."

Elias frowned, looking at the elder who had brought them the payment. "Did you count this yourself?" he asked.

The elder nodded nervously. "Of course. I counted it three times before bringing it to you. Fifty gold pieces is what we promised. That's all we could afford."

Maren stepped forward, her hammer resting against her shoulder. "You're lying," she said flatly. "There are only forty-five pieces here. You've shorted us by five gold."

The elder paled. "What? No! That can't be right!"

Elara leaned closer to the purse, counting the coins one by one. Her brow furrowed as she realized what Maren was saying. "You're right," she said slowly. "There are only forty-five pieces. Someone has taken five gold from us."

Torin's eyes narrowed. "Who?" he demanded.

The elder shook his head frantically. "I don't know! I swear on my life, I counted it myself!"

Kaelen stood up, his gauntlet flickering with a brighter glow. "There's someone in this room who doesn't want us to leave with the full payment," he said. "Someone who wants to keep five gold for themselves."

Elias looked around the tavern, taking note of every face and expression. "We need to investigate this further," he said quietly. "But we can't do it here. We need to find out who took the money before they try again."

Maren nodded, her jaw set. "Let's go outside and check the perimeter. If there are goblins around, we'll need to know about them before we head into the woods."

Torin moved to the door, his hand resting on his sword hilt. "I'll take point," he said. "Maren, you're with me. Kaelen, watch for any magical signatures. Elara, stay here and keep an eye on the purse."

Elias hesitated, then nodded. "Agreed. But let's be careful. We don't want to alert anyone to our presence before we know what we're dealing with."

As they stepped outside, the cool night air hit them like a wall. The village was quiet, save for the occasional bark of a dog or the distant howl of a wolf. They moved silently along the edge of town, keeping an eye out for any sign of movement or disturbance.

Maren led the way, her heavy boots making barely a sound on the cobblestones. She scanned the perimeter, looking for any sign of a trap or ambush. Torin followed close behind, his sword drawn and ready. Kaelen walked slightly ahead, his gauntlet glowing softly as he searched for any magical signatures in the air.

Elara stayed inside the tavern, watching the purse and keeping an eye on the elder who had brought it to them. She noted every detail, from the way the elder's hands trembled when he spoke to the nervous glances cast toward the door.

After a few minutes of careful observation, Maren signaled for the others to stop. "We've cleared the perimeter," she said quietly. "No sign of goblins or anyone else."

Torin nodded, lowering his sword. "Good. Let's go back inside and talk strategy. We need to figure out who took the money before we head into the woods."

They returned to the tavern, where Elara was waiting with fresh tea and bread. She handed each of them a cup, her expression serious. "The elder is lying," she said. "He's not the one who took the money. Someone else is involved here."

Elias listened intently, his mind racing through possibilities. "Who could it be?" he asked.

Maren shrugged. "We don't know yet. But we need to find out before they try again."

Kaelen nodded, his gauntlet dimming slightly. "I'll keep an eye on the magical signatures while you talk to the elder. If there's someone else involved, I'll know about them."

Elara smiled faintly. "Thank you. I'll start questioning the villagers and see if anyone has seen anything suspicious lately."

As they sat around the table, discussing their next steps, Elias felt a sense of unease settle over him. They had accepted a contract to rescue two missing villagers and stop goblin raids. But now, with the payment already compromised, they needed to be even more careful than before.

The night wore on, and eventually, they retired to their rooms upstairs.
