The shrill scream tore through the damp air, a jagged sound that shattered the fragile silence they had cultivated. Elias did not hesitate. His sword cleared the scabbard in a single, fluid motion, the steel humming as it bit the cold air. He stepped forward, not toward the screaming goblin who was already bolting for the far door, but toward the nervous creature slumped against the wall.

The second goblin, the one with the twitching lip and the spear that had dropped to the floor, looked up with wide, yellow eyes. He did not run. He did not reach for a weapon. He simply sat there, his hands raised in a futile gesture of surrender, his breath coming in short, panicked hitches.

"Stay," Elias said, his voice low and hard. He did not threaten. He did not need to. The presence of the four other figures in the doorway, steady and silent, was threat enough. The goblin nodded, a jerky motion, and pressed his back further into the stone.

Elias turned his attention to the center of the room. Nella and Hobb were still bound to the wooden post, their heads bowed, their shoulders trembling. The sight was not what the village elder had described in his hopeful, if vague, briefing. It was not a simple detention. The rope bit deep into their wrists, leaving red, swollen welts. Hobb’s head lolled against Nella’s shoulder, his chest rising and falling with the shallow, labored rhythm of a man who had run out of strength.

Elara was already moving. She did not speak; she simply crossed the room with the quiet efficiency of someone who had learned that words were a luxury they did not have. She knelt beside the post, her chisel already in hand. The blade was small, designed for precision work on stone, but in her hands it was a surgical instrument. She positioned the tip under the thick fiber of the rope around Hobb’s wrist.

"Maren," Elias said, not looking up from the goblin. "If anything moves, you’re on it. Keep your hammer ready, but do not swing unless you have to. I want that goblin alive for questions, not dead on the floor."

Maren did not answer. She simply shifted her weight, the broken cart axle creaking softly against her shoulder as she braced herself. Her eyes were fixed on the trembling creature by the wall, her jaw set in a line of grim determination. She was the only one in the room who could end the problem physically, and she knew it.

Kaelen stood near the door, his face pale and drawn. The light from his gauntlet flickered, a faint, amber pulse that illuminated the dust motes dancing in the air. It was not much, but it was enough to see the ropes, enough to see the fear in the captives’ eyes. Elias could see the effort it cost him, the slight tremor in his hands that he tried to hide by resting them on the doorframe.

Elara worked quickly. The chisel scraped against the rope, a soft, rasping sound that was loud in the quiet room. She did not rush, but she did not hesitate. One by one, the fibers snapped. The first loop fell away from Hobb’s wrist. Then the second. She turned to Nella, her movements no less precise.

Nella looked up. Her eyes were red, swollen, but clear. She saw Elara, saw the chisel, saw the others standing guard. A small, fragile smile broke across her face, a thing of pure, exhausted relief.

"Thank you," she whispered. Her voice was rough, barely a breath.

Hobb stirred. His eyes opened, clouded with confusion, then focused on the light. He blinked, his head lolling back against the post. He did not speak. He simply stared at the goblin, then at the open door, and then at the five people who had come into the dark to pull him out of it.

Elara finished the last knot. She straightened up, her breathing slightly uneven, and handed the bundle of rope to Maren. "It’s done," she said.

Maren took the rope, her hands steady despite the pain in her shoulder. She looked at Hobb, who was trying to stand, his legs weak and shaking. She moved to his side, offering a hand. He gripped it, his fingers weak but desperate.

"We need to move," Elias said, his voice calm. "The first goblin is gone. He will be back with Skrit. We have minutes, not hours."

Torin stepped forward from the shadows of the doorway. He had been listening, his head tilted, tracking the sound of the running feet. "He went left," Torin said. "Toward the upper levels. I heard the footfalls on the wooden stair. It’s a narrow path. If we go right, we can take the service corridor. It’s tighter, but it avoids the main hall."

Elias nodded. He looked at Kaelen. "Can you manage the light for the corridor?"

Kaelen closed his eyes for a second, a shudder running through his frame. He opened them, his gaze hard. "I can hold it. But we move now. I don’t have enough for a long walk in the dark."

Elias looked at the captives. Nella was standing, leaning on Hobb, who was leaning on her. They were a pair of fragile, broken things, but they were alive. That was enough.

"Let’s go," Elias said.

They moved out of the chamber, the door closing softly behind them. The corridor was narrow, the air thick with the smell of damp stone and old smoke. Kaelen’s light flickered ahead of them, a small, defiant flame in the encroaching darkness. Elias led the way, his hand on his sword, his eyes fixed on the shadows that stretched out before them.

Behind them, the goblin by the wall sat in silence, his hands over his head, his eyes wide with a fear that was no longer silent. The rescue was done, but the escape had just begun. And in the deep, breathing dark of the keep, they knew they were not alone.
