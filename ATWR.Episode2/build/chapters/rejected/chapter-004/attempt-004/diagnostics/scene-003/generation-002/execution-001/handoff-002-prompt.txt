The silence that followed the spill was not empty; it was heavy, filled with the sharp, acrid scent of burnt grain and the low, guttural grumbling of the goblins who had lost their dinner. One of them, a stocky creature with a broken ear and a tunic held together by mud and twine, was dragging a heavy iron pot toward the fire, his movements jerky and angry. His companion, thinner and more nervous, was wiping his hands on his loincloth, his eyes darting toward the doorway where the wood had been pried loose.

Elias did not move. He stood in the shadow of the doorframe, his hand resting lightly on the pommel of his sword, his breathing controlled and shallow. The room was small, the stone walls sweating with condensation that dripped into the shallow pools of muddy water on the floor. The light was almost non-existent. Kaelen’s gauntlet, hidden in his sleeve, gave off a faint, pulsing amber glow, just enough to pick out the shapes of objects against the dark, but not enough to cast a shadow that might betray their position. It was a trick of the light, a fragile shield against the absolute blackness that pressed in from the corners.

Elara moved first. She did not run; running would be a mistake that could not be unmade. She stepped into the room with the careful, deliberate grace of a scholar crossing a library floor, her eyes fixed on the two figures in the corner. They were bound to a rough-hewn wooden post, their wrists tied with thick, fibrous rope that bit into their skin. Nella, the blacksmith’s apprentice, sat slumped against the post, her head bowed, her shoulders trembling slightly. Hobb, the cart driver, leaned against her, his face gaunt, his eyes closed, though the slight movement of his chest indicated he was still alive.

The goblins were not guarding them with the vigilance of professional soldiers. They were guarding them with the casual indifference of those who believed no one could reach them, or that no one would care enough to try. The one with the broken ear turned, his yellow eyes narrowing as he caught the scent of oil and old leather. He froze. The pot slipped from his grasp, clattering loudly against the stone floor. The sound was a gunshot in the quiet room.

He looked up, his gaze landing on Elara, then sweeping across the group as they stood in the doorway. For a heartbeat, there was only the hiss of the fire and the ragged breathing of the captives. Then the goblin let out a shrill, high-pitched scream, a sound of pure, undiluted panic that echoed off the damp walls.

"Help!" he shrieked, grabbing a rusty short-sword from a pile of loot near the wall. "Skrit! Skrit! Intruders!"

He turned and ran, not toward the exit, but deeper into the keep, his feet slapping against the stone. His companion, the thinner one, did not follow. He stood rooted to the spot, his mouth open in a silent O of fear, his hands raised in a gesture of surrender or perhaps just paralysis.

Elias stepped forward, his sword drawn in a single, fluid motion. The steel sang as it left the scabbard, a clear, resonant note that cut through the goblin’s panic. He did not attack. He did not need to. The sight of the five companions, armed and ready, was enough to break the spirit of the nervous guard. He dropped his spear, his knees buckling, and he slumped against the wall, sliding down until he sat on the floor, his hands covering his face.

Kaelen stepped into the room, his face pale, his eyes dark with exhaustion. The effort of maintaining the light, even in this dim state, had taken a toll. He could feel the magic draining from him, a slow, persistent leak that left him feeling hollowed out, as if he had been carved from the inside. He moved to the captives, his movements careful, his hands raised in a gesture of reassurance.

"Nella," he said softly, his voice steady despite the tremor in his hands. "Hobb. It is us. We are here to help."

Nella lifted her head. Her eyes were red, swollen with tears, but they were clear. She recognized Kaelen. She recognized the group. A small, trembling smile broke across her face, a fragile thing in the harsh light of the chamber.

"Thank you," she whispered, her voice barely audible.

Elias turned to the nervous goblin, who was still sitting on the floor, his hands over his face. "Stay there," Elias said, his voice calm and authoritative. "Do not move. Do not speak. You will not be harmed if you stay here."

The goblin nodded, his hands trembling. He did not move.

Maren stepped into the room, her hammer resting on her shoulder, her face set in a grim line. She moved to the post where the captives were tied, her eyes scanning the ropes. They were thick, well-made, and tied with a knot that would take time to undo. She looked at Elara, who was already kneeling beside the captives, her hands working quickly to loosen the bindings.

"Can you cut them?" Maren asked, her voice low.

"I have my chisel," Elara said, her fingers moving with precision. "It will take a moment, but I can do it."

Elias watched them, his mind already turning to the next step. The goblin had run. He would have alerted Skrit. They did not have much time. The keep was a maze of cold, damp corridors, and they were deep inside it. They needed to move, quickly and efficiently.

"Torin," Elias said, his voice cutting through the quiet. "Check the exits. See if the goblin took the main path or a side route."

Torin was already moving. He stepped out of the room, his sword in his hand, his eyes scanning the shadows. He did not rush. He moved with the quiet, deadly efficiency of a soldier who had spent his life in the field. He checked the first corner, then the second. He listened. He smelled the air. He was mapping the keep, identifying the routes, the blind spots, the places where they could hide or fight.

Elias looked at Kaelen. "How are you holding up?"

Kaelen did not look up from his work. He was using a small knife to cut the rope around Hobb’s wrists, his hands steady, his concentration absolute. "I am fine," he said, but his voice was thin, strained. "I can manage. Just... do not let them see me falter."

Elias understood. He did not push. He knew that Kaelen was a mage, but he was also a man, and his magic was a resource, not an infinite well. He had to be careful with it. He had to be careful with everything.

The room was quiet now, save for the soft sound of Elara’s chisel scraping against the rope and the ragged breathing of the captives. The light from Kaelen’s gauntlet flickered, a small, defiant flame in the darkness. It was not enough to light the whole keep, but it was enough to guide them. It was enough to get them out.

Elias took a deep breath, the cold air filling his lungs. He felt the weight of the moment, the responsibility, the danger. But he also felt the strength of his companions, their trust, their competence. They were not alone. They were a team, a unit, a force that could not be broken.

They had found the captives. Now they had to get them out. And they had to do it before the goblins realized what they had done. The clock was ticking, the shadows were closing in, but they were ready. They had prepared for this. They had trained for this. And they would succeed.
