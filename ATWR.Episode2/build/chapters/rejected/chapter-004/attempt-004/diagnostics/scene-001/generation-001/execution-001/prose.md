The corridor narrowed, the stone pressing in closer on both sides until the air felt thick and heavy, smelling of wet mortar and old dust. Torin moved with the slow, deliberate grace of a man who had learned that speed was often the enemy of survival. His boots found purchase on the uneven flagstones, each step a calculated test of weight and balance. He did not look back. He did not need to. The formation had settled into a rhythm that required no words, a silent understanding born of years spent navigating hostile terrain.

Elias followed, his eyes scanning the low archway ahead. The passage twisted sharply to the right, a blind turn that offered no view of what lay beyond. It was the kind of spot where a trap would feel most at home, a place where the architecture itself seemed to conspire against the unwary. He raised a hand, a subtle gesture that paused the group before they committed to the turn.

Torin stopped. He crouched, his body low and fluid, and extended a hand toward the floor. His fingers trailed over the surface, testing the stone for loose edges, for the telltale give of a pressure plate. He found nothing. Instead, he lifted his head, listening. The silence was profound, broken only by the distant, rhythmic drip of water somewhere deep within the keep.

"Clear," Torin murmured, his voice barely a breath. He straightened, signaling for them to proceed.

They moved into the turn. The passage opened slightly, revealing a series of small, alcove-like nooks cut into the walls. They were empty, save for a few scattered pieces of rusted iron and a tangle of coarse rope that lay in a heap on the floor. Elias stepped over the rope, his eyes flicking to the shadows in the nooks. Nothing moved.

Elara walked beside him, her satchel swinging gently at her hip. She was watching the walls, her gaze tracing the lines of the masonry with an intensity that suggested she was reading a text rather than inspecting a ruin.

"Look at the wear patterns," she said, her voice low and steady. She pointed to a section of the floor where the stone was smoother than the rest, polished by feet that had come and gone repeatedly. "These paths are well-trodden. The goblins use them for movement, not for hiding. They don't set traps in their own walkways. It’s inefficient."

Torin glanced at her, a ghost of a smile touching his lips. "You think they’re too lazy to be thorough?"

"I think they’re practical," Elara corrected. "They raid for food and tools. They don’t build fortifications. They occupy. The defenses we’ve seen so far are the remnants of the keep’s original owners, modified just enough to serve their immediate needs. They rely on the structure, not on active surveillance."

Elias nodded, integrating her assessment. It made sense. The goblins were opportunists, not architects. Their strength lay in their numbers and their unpredictability, not in the sophistication of their traps. If that was the case, then the greatest danger was not a hidden mechanism, but a human presence. A guard who saw them, a sound that carried, a shadow that moved when it should not.

They continued down the passage, the air growing colder with every step. The stone walls were damp, beaded with condensation that caught the faint light from Kaelen’s gauntlet. The mage walked with a slight limp, his movements careful to conserve his strength. His gauntlet glowed with a soft, steady pulse, a beacon in the darkness that they all relied on, though none of them wanted to think about what would happen if it failed.

Maren brought up the rear, her hammer resting against her shoulder, the broken cart axle strapped to her back creaking softly with each step. She watched the entrances to the side passages, her eyes sharp and alert. She was the last line of defense, the one who would ensure that if they were cut off, they could still fight their way out.

The passage ended at a junction, a small, circular chamber where three paths met. One led back the way they had come. One led straight ahead, down a steep set of stairs that disappeared into the darkness below. The third branched off to the left, a narrow gap that barely allowed enough room for one person to pass through.

Torin stopped at the center of the junction, his hand resting on the pommel of his sword. He looked at the stairs, then at the narrow gap.

"The stairs go down," he said. "The gap... I don't know where that leads. It’s tight. Too tight for Maren."

Elias looked at Maren, who was standing with her weight shifted to one leg, her face set in a mask of quiet endurance. She nodded, a small, firm movement. "I can manage," she said. "But if it’s a bottleneck, I’m not going first."

Torin nodded. "That’s why I’m checking it first."

He moved to the narrow gap, crouching to peer through the opening. The darkness beyond was absolute, swallowing the light from Kaelen’s gauntlet. He listened, straining to hear any sound of movement, any breath, any sign of life. There was nothing. Just the cold, still air of the deep earth.

He turned back to the group, his face unreadable. "It’s empty. But it’s a dead end. The walls are solid stone. No way through."

Elias frowned. "Then the stairs are the only way forward."

Torin looked at the stairs, then at the gap. "The stairs are open. Visible. If there’s a guard at the bottom, they’ll see us coming. The gap is hidden. If we can squeeze through, we might be able to bypass the main path."

Elias looked at Elara, who was already studying the gap with a keen interest. "What do you think?" he asked.

Elara crouched beside the gap, her fingers trailing over the rough stone. "The wear patterns here are different," she said. "This path is less used. It’s likely a service passage, used for moving goods or waste. It’s not a primary route. But it connects to the lower levels."

Torin looked at Elias, a question in his eyes. "Do we take the risk?"

Elias considered. The stairs were the direct route, but they were exposed. The gap was dangerous, but it offered stealth. They had come this far to avoid a direct confrontation. They couldn’t afford to throw that away now.

"Take the gap," Elias said. "One by one. Torin, you go first. Elara, you’re next. Kaelen, then Maren. I’ll bring up the rear."

Torin nodded, already moving to the gap. He squeezed through, his body folding into the narrow space with an ease that spoke of his physical capability. He emerged on the other side, shaking the dust from his shoulders.

Elara followed, her satchel banging against the stone as she maneuvered through the tight space. She made a face, but said nothing.

Kaelen came next, his gauntlet dimming slightly as he concentrated on keeping the light steady. He emerged, wiping his brow.

Maren stepped up to the gap. She looked at the narrow opening, then at the others. She adjusted the straps of her cart axle, her jaw tight. She squeezed through, her movements careful and precise. When she emerged, she was breathing hard, but she stood straight, her hammer resting against her shoulder.

Elias was the last to go through. He squeezed into the gap, the stone pressing against his back, his elbows. He emerged on the other side, his heart beating fast.

They were in a new passage, wider than the gap they had just navigated, but still narrow. The air was colder here, and the smell of damp earth was stronger. The light from Kaelen’s gauntlet illuminated the path ahead, revealing a series of small, low-ceilinged rooms branching off to the left and right.

Torin stopped, his hand raised. He pointed to a door at the end of the passage, a heavy oak door reinforced with iron bands. It was slightly ajar, a sliver of darkness visible beyond.

"Guard," Torin whispered.

Elias looked at the door. Through the sliver, he could see the faint glow of a torch, and the shadow of a figure standing in the doorway. The goblin was holding a spear, its eyes fixed on the darkness beyond the door.

They froze, their breaths held in their chests. The goblin did not move. It was listening, its head tilted, its ears twitching.

Torin’s hand moved to his sword, then stopped. He was calculating. If he attacked, the noise would carry. The goblins in the other rooms, the ones guarding the captives, would hear. They would secure their prisoners, grab their loot, and slip out through the drainage channels before the party could even find the door.

Instead, Torin relaxed his grip. He let his sword hang loose at his side. He took a step back, a single, slow movement that broke the line of sight between the goblin and the party’s main cluster. He moved into the shadow of a broken pillar, becoming indistinct, a smear of dark leather against the ancient stone.

Kaelen’s gauntlet dimmed further, the light fading until it was indistinguishable from the ambient gloom. The mage was expending the last of his reserves to maintain the illusion of invisibility, a final, silent investment in their safety.

The goblin lowered its spear slightly, its posture relaxing from defensive to curious. It took a step forward, peering into the darkness where the five companions had been moments before. It sniffed the air, its whiskers twitching. It was looking for heat, for breath, for any sign that they were still there.

The party flowed past the goblin, not running, not rushing, but moving with the steady, inevitable pace of a tide. They passed within a foot of the creature, close enough for Elias to smell the sour, musky odor of goblin skin and old sweat. The goblin flinched, its spear jerking upward, but by then Torin was already three steps ahead, his back to the creature, his body a solid wall of quiet confidence.

As they rounded the corner, the last sliver of the goblin’s figure vanished from view. The torchlight flickered, casting long, wavering shadows that danced across the stone floor. The party continued down the passage, their breathing steady, their hearts beating in a synchronized rhythm that was as much a testament to their training as it was to their relief.

They had slipped past the second guard. They had maintained their cover. The goblins remained unaware of their presence. The stealth approach had worked.

Elias allowed himself a small exhale, a sound barely audible over the rain. He glanced back once, just to be sure. The passage behind them was empty. The goblin was gone, lost in the shadows, confused and defeated without ever knowing how.

They moved on, deeper into the keep, toward the storage chamber where Nella and Hobb waited.
