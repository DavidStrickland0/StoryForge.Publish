The corridor ahead was a throat of shadow, narrow and cold, the stone walls sweating with condensation that caught the faint, wavering beam of Kaelen’s gauntlet. The light did not reach the far end of the passage; it died twenty feet out, leaving the rest of the keep to guess at its own shape.

Elias stopped. He did not raise his hand to signal a halt; the group had already slowed to a crawl, their breathing synchronized in the oppressive quiet. He turned to Elara, who was crouching near the wall, her fingers trailing over the rough masonry.

"Elara," he said, his voice low and steady. "You heard the vibration earlier. Rhythmic. Steady. Is it still there?"

Elara nodded, her expression tight. She didn't speak immediately, pressing her ear closer to the stone. A moment later, she withdrew, her face pale in the dim light. "It’s louder here," she whispered. "And... different. It’s not just settling. It’s a mechanism. A counterweight, maybe. Or a bell. Something that responds to pressure or movement."

Elias looked at Kaelen. "Can you feel it?"

Kaelen shook his head slowly, his face gaunt. "I can feel the magic, Elias. Faint, but present. It’s woven into the stone. If we step on the wrong flagstone, or touch the wrong wall, it will trigger. And I don’t have the energy to stop it once it starts."

The group stood in the narrow passage, the weight of the keep pressing down on them from above. The rain drummed against the roof, a constant, monotonous reminder of the world outside, but in here, the air was still, thick with the smell of old stone and damp wood.

Elias took a deep breath, steadying himself. He looked at Maren, who was leaning against the wall, her hammer resting on the ground, her injured shoulder aching with a dull, persistent throb.

"Maren," he said. "We need to be careful. This section is likely trapped. If we move, we move together. No sudden movements. No loud noises. We rely on sight and sound, not on Kaelen’s magic."

Maren nodded, her eyes sharp despite the pain. "I can handle the front. I’ll watch the floor. Elara, you watch the walls. Kaelen, keep that light steady, but don’t push it. If it flickers, we stop."

Elias nodded. "Agreed. Elara, do you see any loose stones? Any cracks?"

Elara pointed to a section of the floor near the center of the passage. "There’s a hairline fracture here. It’s subtle, but it’s there. If we step on it, it might shift. I don’t think it’s a trap, but it’s a risk."

Maren crouched beside the fracture, running her gloved hand along the edge. "It’s old stone," she murmured. "Old stone cracks. But it’s anchored. If we move slowly, it should hold."

Elias looked at the group. They were huddled together in the dim light, a small island of order in a chaotic, ruined world. The rain continued to hammer above them, a constant reminder of the world outside, but in here, the air was still, thick with the smell of old stone and damp wood.

"We have the maps," Elias said, tapping the parchment in his hand. "We have the layout. We know where the captives are, and we know where the chief is likely to be. What we don’t know is how to get there without tripping whatever they’ve set up to stop us."

He looked at Elara, who was already crouching beside him, her eyes scanning the rough-hewn stone floor. "Elara, you’ve been looking at the ground. Is there anything there? Anything that looks... wrong?"

Elara didn’t answer immediately. She was tracing a faint line in the dust with her finger, her expression thoughtful. "There’s a draft," she murmured. "Not from the wind. It’s coming from under the floor. And there’s a vibration. Faint, but steady. Like something is moving below us."

Elias frowned. "A trap?"

"Maybe," Elara said, her voice cautious. "Or maybe just the structure settling. But it’s not random. It’s rhythmic."

Maren leaned forward, her eyes narrowing. "Rhythmic. Like a heartbeat."

They all looked at each other, the unspoken question hanging in the air. What were they walking into? And how were they going to get through it without losing someone?

Elias took a deep breath, steadying himself. "We’ll figure it out," he said. "One step at a time. We don't need to know everything right now. We just need to know enough to take the next step safely."

He stood up, brushing the dust from his knees. "Torin, keep watching. Kaelen, save your energy. Maren, stay close. Elara, keep listening."

He looked at the iron-bound door at the far end of the hub, a dark rectangle against the dim light. Beyond it, Nella and Hobb were waiting. And beyond them, Skrit Ironjaw was waiting too.

"We’re ready," Elias said, though the word felt heavy, laden with the weight of what was to come. "Let’s go find them."
