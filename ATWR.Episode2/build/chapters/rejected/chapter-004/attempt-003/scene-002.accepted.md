The corridor opened into a space that smelled of wet straw, stale sweat, and the sharp, metallic tang of fear. It was not the grand, vaulted hall Elias had imagined when he’d studied the maps; it was a cramped, low-ceilinged cellar, the air thick and heavy with the exhalations of two people who had been held here for days.

Elias raised a hand, halting the group. He didn’t need to speak. The silence in the room was different from the silence of the corridors. The corridors had been empty, waiting. This room was full.

Through the gap in the doorframe, lit by the faint, dying ember of a torch on the far wall, Elias saw them.

Nella sat against the far wall, her back straight despite her exhaustion. She was wrapped in a stolen woolen blanket that was far too thin for the damp cold. Her hair was matted, her face pale, but her eyes were clear. She was watching the door with the wary intensity of a hawk, not a victim. Beside her, Hobb slumped in a heap of rags. He was younger, perhaps twelve, and he was shivering violently. His hands were bound with rough rope, the knots tight enough to cut into his wrists. He wasn’t looking at the door. He was looking at the floor, his chin tucked into his chest, a posture of such profound defeat that it made Elias’s chest tighten.

They were alive. That was the first and most critical fact.

Elias signaled to Torin, who stepped forward to flank the door. Maren remained behind Elias, her hand resting on the pommel of her hammer, her breath shallow and controlled. Kaelen leaned against the wall, his face gray, his eyes closed. He had given them everything he had. Now, the magic was gone, and they were just five people in a dark room with a goblin who knew they were there.

The goblin stood in the center of the room, a small, hunched figure with skin the color of bruised plums. It held a club, but it wasn’t raised. It was lowered, resting on the floor, its tip tapping a slow, rhythmic beat against the stone.

It wasn’t a threat. It was a count.

Elias watched the creature’s eyes. They weren’t fixed on him. They were fixed on the door behind him. The goblin was listening.

Then, from the darkness beyond the door, a sound. Not a footstep. A cough. A wet, rattling cough that echoed in the narrow space.

The goblin’s head snapped toward the door. Its ears flattened against its skull. It didn’t turn to face Elias. It didn’t raise its club. It simply turned its head slowly, deliberately, and looked at the space where the party had entered.

It knew.

It didn’t know *who* was there, or *how* many. But it knew that the silence was broken. It knew that the intruders were close.

Elias felt the shift in the room’s tension. The air seemed to thicken. The goblin’s tapping stopped. It held its breath.

Elias looked at Maren. She gave a single, sharp nod. *We are ready.*

He looked at Torin. Torin’s hand was on his sword, but he hadn’t drawn it. He was waiting for the signal.

Elias looked at Elara. She was crouching by the door, her eyes scanning the floor near the threshold. She raised two fingers. *Two guards. Outside the door.*

The goblin took a step back. Then another. It was retreating toward the back of the room, toward a small, barred window that looked out into the night. It wasn’t fleeing. It was positioning itself.

It wanted to be seen.

It wanted to be known.

It was buying time.

Elias understood. If they attacked now, the goblin would likely kill one of the captives before they could reach it. It was a simple, brutal calculation. One life for time. One life for a chance to escape.

Elias didn’t move. He didn’t lower his guard. He didn’t raise his voice.

He simply stood there, in the doorway, and waited.

The goblin watched him. Its eyes were wide, unblinking. It was a small, pathetic thing, really. A creature of instinct and fear, playing a game of cat and mouse with people who had the strength to crush it.

But it was playing well.

Elias saw the shift in the goblin’s weight. It was preparing to strike. Or to flee. Or to do something else entirely.

He didn’t know which.

But he knew that if he moved, he would lose the advantage.

So he didn’t move.

The seconds stretched. The rain drummed against the roof above. The cough from the corridor faded into silence.

The goblin’s club shifted in its grip.

Elias took a deep breath.

He stepped forward.

Not into the room. Not toward the goblin.

He stepped toward the door.

He placed his foot on the threshold, his weight shifting forward. He didn’t cross. He just stood there, a boundary between the darkness outside and the light within.

The goblin froze.

Its eyes flicked from Elias to the door, and back again.

It understood.

It knew that if it struck, Elias would be there. It knew that if it fled, Torin would be there. It knew that if it screamed, Maren would be there.

It was trapped.

Not by walls. Not by bars.

By the certainty of their presence.

Elias held his position. The goblin held his.

The air between them was charged with the static of impending violence.

And then, from the darkness of the corridor, a sound.

A footstep.

The movement was slow and deliberate. The weight was heavy.

The goblin’s head snapped toward the door. Its eyes widened.

Elias didn’t turn. He didn’t need to.

He knew who was coming.

He knew what was coming.

And he knew that this was the moment.

The moment of choice.

The moment of action.

The moment of rescue.
