The footstep stopped.

It did not continue. It did not rush. It hung in the air, a heavy, deliberate beat that seemed to suspend time itself.

Elias felt the shift in the room’s atmosphere before he heard it. The tension, which had been a taut wire, suddenly slackened, replaced by a different kind of pressure. The weight of expectation.

He turned his head slowly, his eyes locking onto Torin.

The soldier stood in the shadows of the doorway, his sword drawn now, the blade catching the faint, dying light from Kaelen’s gauntlet. He was not looking at the goblin. He was looking at Elias. His expression was unreadable, a mask of professional calm, but Elias knew that look. It was the look of a man who had finished his calculation and was waiting for the commander’s order.

Behind Torin, Maren shifted her weight. The creak of her strap was the only sound, a dry, wooden warning. She was ready. Her hammer was in her hand, low and steady, her injury forgotten in the face of imminent violence.

Kaelen, leaning against the wall, closed his eyes. He was saving his last shield. He was saving it for the moment when the plan failed. He trusted Elias not to let that moment come.

Elara crouched low, her hand resting on her dagger. She was watching the goblin, but her eyes were also on the door. She knew what was coming. She knew the two guards outside were waiting for the signal.

Elias looked at the goblin.

The creature had stopped retreating. It stood frozen, its club raised, its eyes wide with a mixture of fear and defiance. It was a small, pathetic thing, a shadow of a monster, but it was still a threat. It could kill one of the captives in a heartbeat. It could ring the alarm bell. It could run.

Elias took a step forward.

The goblin flinched.

"Stay where you are," Elias said, his voice low and steady. He did not raise his sword. He did not threaten. He simply stated a fact. "If you move, we move. If you kill them, we kill you. If you run, we catch you. It is your choice."

The goblin trembled. Its eyes darted between Elias and the captives. It was calculating. It was weighing the odds.

And then, it made a decision.

It lowered its club.

The movement was slow. The movement was slow and deliberate.

It backed away from the captives, moving toward the barred window. Its movements were jerky, uncoordinated, but they were purposeful. It was giving up. It was surrendering.

Elias did not relax. He did not lower his guard. He watched the goblin’s hands. He watched its feet. He watched its eyes.

The goblin reached the window. It turned to face Elias.

It raised its club again.

But this time, it did not point it at the captives. It pointed it at the door.

At Torin.

At the two guards outside.

Elias understood.

The goblin was not surrendering. It was stalling. It was buying time. It was waiting for its friends.

Elias’s eyes narrowed.

He looked at Torin.

Torin nodded.

Elias looked at Maren.

Maren nodded.

Elias looked at Kaelen.

Kaelen opened his eyes.

Elias looked at Elara.

Elara was already moving.

Elias raised his hand.

"Wait."

The word was a command. It cut through the tension. It froze the moment.

The goblin froze.

The captives froze.

The air froze.

Elias took a deep breath.

He looked at the goblin.

Then he looked at the door.

Then he looked at his companions.

He raised his hand again.

This time, he pointed to the door.

Then he pointed to the goblin.

Then he pointed to himself.

The message was clear.

*We are not going to fight you.*

*We are going to talk to you.*

The goblin’s eyes widened.

It was confused.

It was afraid.

But it was listening.

Elias stepped forward.

The goblin did not move.

Elias stopped.

He was three feet away.

He was close enough to hear the goblin’s breathing.

He was close enough to see the fear in its eyes.

He was close enough to kill it.

But he did not.

He spoke.

His voice was soft.

His words were simple.

"Who are you?"

The goblin did not answer.

But it did not move.

And for the first time since they had entered the keep, the party was not fighting.

They were talking.

And the goblin was listening.

The standoff had ended.

The rescue had begun.

Not with a sword.

Not with a spell.

But with a question.

And the answer, Elias knew, would decide everything.

He waited.

The goblin waited.

The captives waited.

The rain waited.

And the world held its breath.
