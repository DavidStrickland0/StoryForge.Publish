The corridor twisted sharply to the right, the air growing noticeably colder and drier as they descended further into the keep’s substructure. "Elara," Elias murmured, his voice low and steady. "You felt that vibration earlier. Is it still there?"

Elara was already crouching near the center of the passage, her lantern extinguished to preserve the darkness. She held a small vial of quartz dust between her fingers, letting a single grain drop onto the stone floor with a precision that belied the tension in her shoulders. The grain landed with a faint *tick*, and for a moment, nothing happened. Then, a faint, pulsing blue light shimmered across the surface, not in a line this time, but in a complex, spiraling pattern that radiated outward from a specific point on the floor.

"It’s not a simple pressure plate," Elara whispered, her eyes tracing the glowing spiral. "It’s a resonance trap. The stone is tuned to a specific frequency. If anyone steps on that center point, the vibration amplifies. It won’t just alert the goblins; it will likely collapse the ceiling section above us. Or at least, it’s designed to make enough noise to bring every goblin in the keep running to this spot."

Maren shifted her weight, the broken cart axle strapped to her back creaking softly. She winced, a sharp intake of breath escaping her lips as the movement sent a jolt of pain through her injured shoulder. "Can we bypass it?" she asked, her voice tight.

Elara shook her head slowly. "Not without breaking the mechanism. And breaking the mechanism triggers the resonance. It’s a dead end. We have to find another way around."

Elias looked at Kaelen, who had been standing slightly behind them, his brass gauntlet dimmed to a faint, pulsing amber. The mage looked exhausted, his face pale in the gloom, the effort of maintaining his magical field taking its toll.

"Kaelen," Elias said quietly. "Can you disrupt the resonance field? Just for a moment, enough for us to cross?"

Kaelen hesitated, his jaw tightening as he considered the cost. "It would require a focused burst of counter-frequency magic," he said, his voice hoarse. "I can do it. But it will drain whatever reserves I have left. And if I miscalculate the frequency, the trap will still trigger. It’s a risk."

"We don’t have a choice," Elias said. "We can’t go back, and we can’t wait. If we stay here, they’ll find us eventually. If we move, we might get through."

Kaelen closed his eyes for a second, a flicker of pain crossing his features. He nodded slowly. "Alright. But you need to move fast. The disruption won’t last long, and I won’t be able to maintain it if I have to hold the formation."

The group moved into position. Torin took the lead, his boots finding purchase on the uneven stones. Elias and Maren flanked Elara, who was now standing directly over the center of the spiral, her hands hovering just above the stone, ready to guide Kaelen’s magic.

Kaelen raised his gauntlet, the amber light intensifying slightly. He focused his mind, channeling the last of his magical energy into a precise, counter-harmonic pulse. The air around the trap seemed to shimmer, the blue light of the resonance field flickering and wavering. For a brief moment, the spiral faded, leaving the stone floor inert.

"Now!" Kaelen hissed, his voice strained.

The party moved. Torin stepped over the center point first, his movements fluid and precise. Elara followed, her eyes fixed on the floor, ensuring she didn’t stray from the safe path. Maren came next, her steps heavier, her body struggling against the pain in her shoulder. Elias brought up the rear, his eyes scanning the shadows for any sign of movement.

"Maren!" Elias called out, moving to steady her.

"I’m fine," she gritted out, though her face was pale. She forced herself to straighten, her jaw set in determination. "Just a little off-balance. Let’s keep moving."

Kaelen lowered his gauntlet, the light fading completely. He leaned against the wall, breathing heavily, his hands trembling slightly. He had used the last of his magic to disrupt the trap, leaving him with no reserves left.

"That’s it," he said quietly. "I’m empty. If we need magic, we don’t have any."
