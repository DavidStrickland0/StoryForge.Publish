The silence in Skrit’s chamber was not empty; it was heavy, pressurized, and thick with the smell of wet fur and fear. Elias kept his hands visible, palms open, a deliberate counter to the tension that still coiled in the air. He watched the goblin chief’s eyes dart from the open doorway to the dagger lying useless on the stone floor, then back to the party. Skrit was small, hunched under the weight of his own defeat, and for the first time since they had entered the keep, the room felt larger.

"Move," Elias said, his voice flat and final. He did not look at Skrit. He looked at Torin.

Torin was already shifting his weight, a grimace tightening his jaw as he adjusted his grip on Hobb. The cart driver was limp against his back, a dead weight that pressed painfully into Torin’s right shoulder. The old injury, dormant for weeks, was now a sharp, hot spike that radiated down his arm with every micro-movement. He did not complain. He simply breathed through his teeth, a low, controlled hiss, and began to walk toward the door.

Maren followed, her arm locked firmly around Nella’s waist. The blacksmith’s apprentice was trembling so violently that her knees seemed to buckle with each step, but she kept her chin up, her eyes fixed on the door as if it were the only thing in the world keeping her tethered to reality. Maren’s own posture was rigid, the broken axle strapped to her shoulder acting as a counterweight to the strain of supporting another human being. She moved with the careful, grinding precision of a machine running on frayed gears.

Kaelen brought up the rear, his steps light but hurried. He was not carrying anyone, but the air around him felt thinner, drained of the ambient magic that usually helped him balance. He was exhausted, his eyes hollow, but he kept his gaze on the corridor behind them, listening for the clatter of boots that had not yet arrived.

Elara walked beside Elias, her satchel clinking softly against her hip. She was watching the door with the intensity of a hawk, her head tilted, listening to the silence beyond the threshold.

They emerged into the corridor, a narrow throat of stone that stretched away into the dark. The air here was colder, carrying the scent of damp moss and old iron. Elias signaled for them to stop, not because of a threat, but because they needed a moment to assess the situation without the immediate pressure of the room behind them.

"Torin," Elias said, keeping his voice low. "How long can you carry him?"

Torin stopped, his boots scuffing against the flagstones. He shifted Hobb slightly, the movement causing the cart driver to groan in his sleep. "A hundred yards, maybe more," Torin said, his voice strained. "But if I have to climb, or if the ground gets slick, I’m dropping him. And if I drop him, I’m done."

Maren looked up from where she was checking Nella’s footing. "I can take the weight for a bit," she said, her tone practical, devoid of sentiment. "But I’m not climbing. And I’m not running. If we have to go vertical, we need a rope. Or a ladder. Or a miracle, and I don’t see one of those in my satchel."

Elias nodded. He looked at the corridor ahead. It was straight, but the ceiling was low, and the walls were rough-hewn, offering little cover. To their left, a passage branched off, leading down. To their right, the path continued toward the main exit, but it passed through an open area where the goblins had been storing their loot.

"We can’t go right," Kaelen said quietly. He was leaning against the wall, his eyes closed, but his voice was sharp with alertness. "The goblin who fled... he’s going to lead them back here. If we take the open route, we’ll be seen. If we take the left, we’re going down. Into the dark. Into the unknown."

"Into the exit," Elara corrected. She had been tracing the floor with her finger, her eyes narrowed. "There’s a draft. It’s coming from below. And there’s a vibration. Faint, but steady. Like something is moving below us. Or above us."

"Or the structure settling," Maren said. "Old stone cracks. It’s not magic. It’s just a ruin."

"Maybe," Elara said. "But we don’t know. And we can’t afford to guess."

Elias looked at the group. They were tired. They were hurt. They were carrying two people who were not going to make it easy. But they were alive. And they had the job to finish.

"We take the left," Elias said. "It’s slower. It’s darker. But it’s quieter. And if the goblins are moving, they’re moving toward the exit, not toward the cellar. We go down, we wait, and we listen. If they pass, we move. If they don’t, we wait."

Torin nodded. He shifted Hobb again, the pain in his shoulder flaring up, sharp and bright. "I can do that," he said. "But I need a break. Every twenty steps. Or I’m going to pass out."

"Agreed," Maren said. She adjusted her grip on Nella, who had stopped trembling enough to look around, her eyes wide and unseeing. "I need a break too. My shoulder is screaming at me, and I’m not sure if it’s the axle or the fact that I’m carrying a woman who weighs less than my hammer."

A small, dry laugh escaped Kaelen. It was a fragile sound, barely audible, but it broke the tension. "I can carry the hammer," he said. "If you need to put it down."

Maren looked at him, her eyes sharp. "No. I’m good. Just... keep an eye on the dark. That’s your job."

Kaelen nodded. He straightened up, his eyes opening, scanning the shadows. "I’ll do it."

Elias turned to Elara. "You’re with me. Keep listening. If you hear anything, you tell us. Immediately."

Elara nodded. She pulled a small vial of quartz dust from her satchel, holding it up to the faint light that seeped in from the cracks in the stone. The dust swirled, catching the gloom, revealing the faintest traces of movement in the air.

"Nothing yet," she said. "But the air is still. That’s a good sign. Or a bad one. I can’t tell."

Elias looked at the passage to the left. It was dark, cold, and inviting in a way that felt dangerous. But it was the only way forward.

"Let’s go," he said.

They moved into the passage, one by one. The air grew colder, the darkness deeper. Elias kept his hand on Maren’s shoulder, not to push, but to maintain the spacing Torin had established. They were a single, breathing unit, moving through the dark with the slow, deliberate grace of water finding its level.

And in the silence, they waited.
