"Let's go," Elias said, his voice flat and final. He turned his back on the chief, a deliberate dismissal that was louder than any shout. "Maren, keep her steady. Torin, if his shoulder buckles, we stop. We don't rush a man who's already broken."

They moved toward the door, a slow, heavy procession that seemed to stretch the seconds. The air in the room was thick with the scent of wet fur and old iron, a smell that clung to the back of Elias’s throat. He could feel the weight of the moment pressing against his ribs, the solid, undeniable fact that the hardest part was over, and the boring, painful part was beginning.

Skrit Ironjaw did not follow immediately. He stood in the center of his room, looking small against the backdrop of stolen grain sacks and broken furniture. He looked at his two kneeling guards, then at the party’s retreating forms. There was no anger in his eyes, only a profound, exhausted relief. He reached up and unclasped the heavy leather strap of his bow, letting it drop to the floor with a dull thud. The sound echoed in the silence, a final punctuation mark to the end of his reign.

Elias paused at the threshold, his hand resting on the doorframe. He didn’t turn around. "Leave your weapons where they are," he called out, his voice carrying easily in the stillness. "We are not your friends, Skrit. But we are not going to kill you in the dark. If you want to live to see a trial, you walk with us. If you try to run, you will find that we are much faster than you think, and much harder to stop."

It was a bluff, mostly. They were exhausted, injured, and carrying a dead weight. But Skrit didn't know that. He only knew that the man who had just dismantled his entire power structure with a few words was now standing in the doorway, waiting.

The goblin chief picked up his sword. He sheathed it with a slow, deliberate clink, the sound of steel sliding into a worn leather scabbard. He straightened his spine, the movement stiff and painful, and then he began to walk.

The corridor outside was cold. The dampness seeped into Elias’s bones, a physical reminder of the keep’s decay. He kept his hand on his sword hilt, not because he expected an attack, but because the muscle memory was stronger than his better judgment. He watched Skrit’s back, the greenish skin of his neck, the way his shoulders hunched under the weight of his own surrender.

Torin was struggling. Elias could hear the faint, rhythmic wheeze of the cart driver’s breathing, muffled by the heavy wool blanket wrapped around him. Torin’s steps were uneven, his weight shifting constantly to compensate for the strain on his right shoulder. He didn't complain. He didn't ask for help. He just kept moving, a silent, enduring pillar of strength that Elias felt he owed a debt he couldn't pay.

Maren was close to Elias, her hand gripping Nella’s elbow with a firm, protective strength. The blacksmith was leaning heavily on her, her head lolling against Maren’s shoulder. Maren’s own face was pale, her jaw set in a tight line that spoke of the pain she was ignoring. She walked with a careful, measured gait, one foot in front of the other, conserving every ounce of energy.

Kaelen walked beside Elara, his steps light and quick. He was holding a piece of flint in his hand, turning it over and over, a small, nervous tic that betrayed his exhaustion. He looked at Elias, his eyes dark and tired. "We're almost there," he whispered. "Just a little further."

Elias nodded, though he couldn't hear him. He was too busy watching the shadows, too busy listening to the silence that followed them. The keep was quiet. The goblins were gone. The traps were dead. Maren lifted Nella’s leg, helping her over the edge. Elias watched them, his heart pounding in his chest, a strange, hollow feeling of triumph mixed with a deep, abiding fatigue.

They were out. The air was cold and fresh, carrying the scent of rain and wet earth. The stars were out, bright and clear, a vast canopy of light that seemed to stretch on forever.
