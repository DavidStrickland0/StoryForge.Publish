The iron pot did not roll forever. It was heavy, a solid chunk of blackened metal that had once held stew for a dozen hungry raiders, and gravity, combined with the slight incline of the flagstones, gave it a momentum that felt almost sentient. It clattered into the left corridor, a dull, rhythmic *thud-thud-thud* that echoed off the wet stone walls.

Elias did not wait for the echo to die. He spun on his heel, his boots scraping against the damp floor, and signaled the others with a sharp chop of his hand. *He signaled the others to move.*

Torin was already moving. He had abandoned his post at the cellar entrance, the old pain in his shoulder flaring with a white-hot spike as he shifted his weight, but he ignored it. He moved toward the center of the main hall, his sword drawn, the steel catching the faint, ambient light from the high windows. He positioned himself between the left corridor and the main shaft, a broad wall of leather and steel.

Maren did not move toward the center. She stepped back, placing herself directly in front of Nella and Hobb. The blacksmith was trembling, her knees knocking together, but she did not collapse. Maren’s hand rested on the haft of her hammer, her fingers tight, her knuckles white. She looked at the cellar entrance, then at the left corridor, her eyes calculating angles, distances, and the precise moment when fear would turn into action.

Elara had climbed to the edge of the stone table, her back against the wall, her eyes fixed on the window. The window was high, a narrow slit of glass that looked out onto the rain-slicked night. It was not an escape route for Hobb, whose leg was broken, but it was a possibility. A hope. She kept her hands on the window frame, ready to unlatch it if the goblins broke through Maren’s line.

Kaelen stood beside Elias, his face gray, his breath coming in short, shallow gasps. He looked at the left corridor, where the sound of the pot had finally ceased, replaced by a silence that was heavier than the noise.

"They’re coming," Kaelen whispered. His voice was thin, strained. "I can hear them. Multiple sets of feet. And... shouting."

Elias nodded. He could hear it too. The wet slap of bare feet on stone. The guttural, panicked yells of goblins who had been broken by the darkness and the silence, now driven by the promise of a noise that sounded like a threat.

They were coming to investigate. They were coming to see who had made the sound.

And they were coming to the center of the hall, where the light was brightest, and the exit was widest.

Elias took a step forward. Then another. He moved into the center of the main hall, his sword unsheathed, the blade held low, the tip pointing toward the left corridor. He stood in the open, in the light, a single figure of defiance in the middle of the goblins’ domain.

He was not afraid. He was tired. He was hungry. He was ready to be done with this.

The goblins emerged from the left corridor, one by one, then in a cluster. They were hunched, their eyes wide and yellow, their hands clutching crude weapons. Spears, knives, a broken axe. They stopped at the edge of the light, their movements jerky, uncertain. They looked at Elias. They looked at Torin, who stood to his left, his sword drawn. They looked at Maren, who stood to his right, her hammer resting on her shoulder, her posture rigid, her eyes cold.

They looked at the captives.

Nella stood behind Maren, her face pale, her hands clasped tightly in front of her. Hobb leaned against the wall, his face a mask of pain, but his eyes were open, alert. They were alive. They were free. And they were being watched by a group of armed men who had the upper hand.

The goblins hesitated.

The silence stretched, thin and taut. The rain drummed against the roof, a steady, rhythmic sound that filled the void left by the goblins’ hesitation.

Elias spoke. His voice was low, steady, and clear. It carried across the hall, bouncing off the stone walls, filling the space with a quiet authority.

"Skrit Ironjaw," he said. "I know you are here. I know you are the one who gave the orders. I know you are the one who held them."

He paused. He let the name hang in the air, a weight, a threat.

"I am not here to kill you. I am here to take you. And I am here to take your friends. And I am here to take your keep."

He looked at the goblins. He looked at their weapons, their fear, their uncertainty. He looked at their leader, who was not visible, but was surely listening, surely watching, surely calculating.

"You can come out and surrender," Elias said. "You can give up your weapons, your loot, and your claim to this keep. And you can walk out of here, into the rain, into the night, and into the hands of the village guard."

He paused again. He let the words settle.

"Or you can stay here. And I can come to you. And I can take you. And I can take your keep. And I can take your life."

The goblins looked at each other. They looked at their weapons. They looked at Elias.

The silence stretched again.

Then, a sound. A soft, shuffling sound. From the shadows, from the darkness, from the place where the leader should be.

A goblin stepped forward.

He was tall for a goblin, his skin a mottled gray-green, his eyes sharp and intelligent. He wore a leather tunic, patched and repaired, and a belt with a short sword. He held no weapon in his hands. His hands were open, his palms out, a gesture of surrender.

He looked at Elias. He looked at Torin. He looked at Maren.

He looked at the captives.

And then, he lowered his head.

"I am Skrit Ironjaw," he said. His voice was rough, tired, and resigned. "I am here. I am yours."
