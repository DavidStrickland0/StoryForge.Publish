# Chapter Review

## Overall Assessment

The chapter repeats narrative work completed in Chapter 4 (rescue, assessment, decoy plan) without advancing the Short arc's NextMilestone. It also introduces a new threat (Skrit Ironjaw) at the end without establishing his presence or capabilities earlier, violating pacing and engagement rules.

## Findings

- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter substantially re-performs the rescue and assessment actions completed in Chapter 4. The party is already moving out with captives; this chapter replays the stealth approach, goblin intimidation, and exit planning as if they are happening for the first time.
  Evidence: "Torin moved first, his sword drawn now... Maren followed, her eyes scanning the shadows behind the crates... She found nothing. The room was small, cramped, and smelled of wet dog and ash."
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter introduces Skrit Ironjaw as a new threat at the very end without prior establishment. This violates the rule against manufacturing a larger replacement threat solely to create artificial momentum.
  Evidence: "And behind the table sat Skrit Ironjaw... He was larger than the others, his skin a mottled green-grey, his eyes sharp and intelligent."
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter resolves the arc by having Skrit surrender and give keys/silver without establishing ResolutionCriteria (goblin band incapacitated, captives returned, reward received). The ending feels premature and unearned.
  Evidence: "Skrit looked at the stolen goods on the table. Then, slowly, he stood up... 'Take them,' Skrit said. 'Take the goods. Take the keep.'"
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter ignores the active Short arc's NextMilestone (secure exit route and return) by spending its primary action on a confrontation that should have been resolved in Chapter 5 or earlier.
  Evidence: "They moved out of the room, the door closing behind them. The corridor was empty. The goblins were gone. The keep was silent."
- [FinancialLogic] The chapter mentions a pile of silver coins on the table but never reconciles this against the agreed fifty gold piece reward or any outstanding debts, creating an unexplained financial state.
  Evidence: "On the table sat a pile of stolen goods: a wheel of cheese, a loaf of bread, a handful of silver coins... 'Take the silver. It is yours.'"
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter uses activity (confronting Skrit) without changing character, relationship, knowledge, or circumstances meaningfully before resolving the arc, violating the requirement for meaningful change.
  Evidence: "Skrit laughed... 'You think you can just walk out?'... 'Your reputation is that of a thief,' Maren said... 'I am a chief.'"
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter ends with the party leaving the keep but does not narrate the return to Pinehollow, reward collection, or rest at the Crooked Stag, which are required by the ResolutionCriteria.
  Evidence: "They began to walk... moving toward the exit, toward the light, toward the end of the dark... The village was ahead. And the story, for now, was finished."
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter repeats the decoy plan and its execution (dropping flint) which was already established as a completed development in Chapter 4.
  Evidence: "Kaelen tossed the flint. It skittered across the flagstones... The effect was immediate. The goblins froze... Two of the goblins spun toward the left passage..."
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter resolves the arc without establishing that the goblin band is incapacitated or captured, contradicting the ResolutionCriteria.
  Evidence: "There is no chief,' Skrit said... 'Thieves run.' The goblin ran. The door opened and closed, and the sound of shuffling feet faded down the corridor."
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter contradicts the hierarchy between Short arcs by resolving the Medium arc's objective (rescue and return) in Chapter 5 instead of reserving it for Chapters 7-9.
  Evidence: "We did it,' Kaelen said... 'We actually did it.' They began to walk... toward the end of the dark."
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter introduces a new attack (Maren's stunning blow) and arrival (Skrit) only in its ending without earning them through the chapter, violating engagement rules.
  Evidence: "Maren's hammer flashed. It was not a killing blow, but it was a stunning one... The other goblin, the one holding the spear, flinched back..."
- [FinancialLogic] The party accepts a handful of silver coins as sufficient payment for their rescue mission, which contradicts the established contract value and the scale of the thefts.
  Evidence: Elias took the key. He looked at the silver coins on the table. They were not enough to buy a new farm, or a new life, or a new world. But they were enough to pay for the work they had done. And that was enough.

## Engagement Findings

- The chapter relies on danger and shock rather than investment in characters or progression.
- The ending feels artificial due to the sudden introduction of Skrit and immediate resolution.

## Revision Guidance

- Advance the Short arc's NextMilestone by securing the exit route and beginning the return journey.
- Remove the Skrit confrontation or establish his presence earlier in the chapter.
- Narrate the return to Pinehollow, reward collection, and rest at the Crooked Stag.
- Replace repeated decoy execution with new obstacles or complications on the retreat.
- Ensure ResolutionCriteria are met before ending the arc.
- The contract in Chapter 1 established a reward of fifty gold pieces (or equivalent value) for rescuing Nella and Hobb.
