The iron-bound door stood between them and the rest of the keep, its surface pitted with rust and pockmarked by old knife strikes. It was not locked, but it was not open, either. It hung slightly ajar, just enough to admit a sliver of draft from the corridor beyond, a draft that Elara had already identified as the source of the rhythmic vibration beneath their feet.

Elara knelt beside the threshold, her lantern casting a weak, trembling pool of light across the flagstones. She did not look at the door. She looked at the floor.

"Watch this," she murmured, her voice barely a whisper. She held a single grain of loose quartz dust between her thumb and forefinger, letting it drop onto the stone directly in front of the door’s lower hinge. The grain landed with a faint *tick* that seemed impossibly loud in the silence.

For a moment, nothing happened. Then, a thin line of pale blue light shimmered across the flagstone, running from the grain’s resting place straight toward the base of the doorframe. It did not glow brightly; it pulsed, weak and steady, like a heartbeat.

"That’s not a tripwire," Elara said, her eyes tracing the invisible line. "It’s a pressure plate. Or something like it. The vibration we felt isn’t random settling. It’s a counterweight. If you step on that spot, the mechanism behind the door shifts. I don’t know what it does—drop a beam, release a gas, ring a bell—but it’s designed to trigger the moment a foot crosses that threshold."

Maren crouched beside her, her hammer resting on her knee. Her face was tight with concentration, the pain in her shoulder a dull, persistent throb that she had learned to ignore. She looked at the floor, then at the door, then at the rough-hewn stone wall to the left of the entrance.

"Pressure plates are easy to break," Maren said. "Hard to bypass if you don’t know where the weight rests. But this is old stone. Old stone cracks."

She stood up, moving with a careful, deliberate grace that belied her injury. She walked to the wall beside the door, running her gloved hand along the mortar between the blocks. Her fingers found a gap, a hairline fracture that widened slightly as she pressed deeper.

"Here," she said, tapping the stone with her knuckles. The sound was hollow, not solid. "This block isn’t anchored. It’s loose. If we pull it out, the floor structure changes. The pressure plate loses its support. It drops out of alignment."

Elara looked up, a flicker of surprise in her eyes. "You think we can just pull it out?"

"I think we can pry it out," Maren corrected. "It’s not load-bearing for the whole wall. Just for that one section. If we remove it, the trap disengages. No magic needed. No alarms."

Elias stepped forward, his sword sheathed but his hand resting on the hilt. He looked at the loose stone, then at Maren. "Can you do it without making too much noise?"

Maren nodded. "Give me your dagger. And a wedge. Something thin and hard."

Elias handed her his dagger, then picked up a flat piece of iron from the pile of debris nearby—a scrap from a broken plow, perhaps. He handed it to her.

Maren wedged the iron into the crack, then leveraged the dagger behind it. She braced her good foot against the floor, her injured shoulder bearing the strain. Her face flushed with effort, but she did not grunt. She pushed, then pulled, then pushed again. The stone groaned, a low, grinding sound that echoed faintly in the corridor.

Elara held her breath, her eyes fixed on the quartz dust. The blue line of light flickered, then faded. The vibration beneath their feet ceased.

Maren wrenched the stone free. It fell to the floor with a heavy thud, cracking the flagstone beneath it. The gap it left was dark and empty, revealing the rough, unworked stone behind the masonry.

"There," Maren said, wiping her brow with the back of her hand. "The trap is dead. The mechanism has nothing to rest on."

Elara nodded, a small smile playing at the corners of her mouth. "And the door is still open. The alarm didn’t ring. The goblins didn’t hear a thing."

Torin, who had been watching the corridor, relaxed slightly. "Good work," he said. "That’s one less variable to worry about."

Kaelen, who had been leaning against the far wall, straightened up. His gauntlet was dim, almost dark. "I can confirm the magical signature is gone," he said quietly. "The ward is broken. Or rather, it’s been physically disrupted. It won’t reactivate unless they rebuild it."

Elias looked at the loose stone, then at the open door. The way forward was clear. The path to the captives was no longer guarded by a hidden mechanism. It was just a door, open and waiting.

"We go through now," Elias said. "Stay close. Torin, take point. Maren, cover the rear. If anything moves, we freeze until we see what it is."

Maren nodded, picking up her hammer and adjusting her grip. "Lead the way," she said.

The group moved through the doorway, stepping carefully over the cracked flagstone. The air in the corridor beyond was colder, drier, and smelled faintly of iron and old blood. But it was silent. No voices. No footsteps. Just the distant sound of rain falling on the keep’s roof.

They had bypassed the trap. They had established their position. And now, they were ready to move deeper into the keep, toward the storage chamber where Nella and Hobb were being held.

The path was clear. The next step was theirs to take.
