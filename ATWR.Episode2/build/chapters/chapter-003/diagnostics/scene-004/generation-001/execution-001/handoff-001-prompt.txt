The goblin’s spear tip wavered, a faint tremor in the crude wood betraying its owner’s uncertainty. It did not shout. It did not drop into a combat stance. It simply stood there, its yellow eyes fixed on the darkness where five people were pretending to be shadows.

Torin raised a single finger, holding it up in a silent command to freeze. He didn’t breathe. His eyes, sharp and unblinking, tracked the goblin’s weight distribution. The creature was leaning slightly forward, its foot poised to step backward, but it hadn’t committed to a retreat. It was waiting to see if they were real.

Elias felt the tension in his own shoulders, a physical knot of adrenaline that he consciously worked to loosen. He kept his sword sheathed. Drawing it now would shatter the illusion. If they moved, they had to move as if they were part of the stone, part of the draft, part of the rain.

It was barely visible, a slow heartbeat of gold in the gloom, but it was enough to anchor the scene. The mage didn’t speak. He didn’t need to. The silence was their weapon.

The goblin tilted its head, a jerky, bird-like motion. Its nostrils flared, catching the scent of wet wool and leather. It was a young goblin, younger than the chief, with a missing tooth that gave its snarl a lopsided, unimpressive quality. It looked less like a monster and more like a shrewd, frightened animal that had cornered a larger prey and was hoping the prey would run.

Torin’s hand moved to the hilt of his sword, then stopped. He was calculating. If he attacked, the noise would carry. The goblins in the other rooms, the ones guarding the captives, would hear. They would secure their prisoners, grab their loot, and slip out through the drainage channels before the party could even find the door.

Instead, Torin relaxed his grip. He let his sword hang loose at his side. He took a step back, a single, slow movement that broke the line of sight between the goblin and the party’s main cluster. He moved into the shadow of a broken pillar, becoming indistinct, a smear of dark leather against the ancient stone.

Elias followed suit, stepping back into the darkness with deliberate slowness. He watched the goblin. The creature’s eyes followed Torin’s retreat, then shifted to where Elias had been. It was confused. The threat had vanished. The prey had dissolved.
