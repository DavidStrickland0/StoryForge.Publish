The corridor stretched ahead of them, a narrow throat of cold stone that seemed to swallow the faint light from their lanterns. The air here was still, lacking the damp chill of the outer courtyard, but it carried a heavier scent—a mix of wet earth, old wood, and the faint, metallic tang of rusted iron. Torin moved first, his boots finding purchase on the uneven flagstones with a practiced silence that spoke of years spent navigating hostile terrain. He did not rush. Rushing was a luxury they could not afford, and more importantly, it was a mistake they could not afford to make twice.

He paused at the first junction, a low archway that branched off to the left. His hand rested lightly on the pommel of his sword, not in threat, but in readiness. He listened. The rain drummed against the distant roof, a steady, rhythmic backdrop that masked any subtle sounds from deeper within the keep. But beneath that, there was a silence that felt deliberate. The goblins were not moving. They were waiting.

Torin raised a hand, halting the group. Elias stepped up beside him, his own blade sheathed but his eyes sharp, scanning the shadows. The others followed, Maren bringing up the rear with her hammer resting against her shoulder, the broken cart axle strapped to her back creaking softly with her every movement. Kaelen stood slightly behind, his brass gauntlet dim, the light it cast just enough to see the immediate floor but not far enough to reveal what lay beyond the next turn.

"Left," Torin said, his voice low and steady. "That passage leads down. It’s narrow, but it connects to the lower cellar levels. If we go that way, we’re moving away from the main guard post. It’s a longer route, but it’s quieter."

Elias glanced at the right-hand passage, which was wider and more direct. "And the right?"

"Direct," Torin replied. "Straight to the storage chamber. But it passes within twenty feet of Skrit’s room. If he has a lookout on the gallery above, they’ll see us before we even reach the door. And if they alarm, we’re trapped in a corridor with no cover."

Maren shifted her weight, a wince crossing her face as she adjusted the axle. "Longer route means more time in the dark. More chances for something to go wrong. But I agree with Torin. We don’t need a fight we can’t win yet. We need to be in position before we start it."

Elara crouched by the wall, her fingers trailing over the rough stone. She didn’t speak, but her silence was answer enough. She had seen the traps, the wards, the crude but effective defenses. She knew that speed was not the same as safety, and that in a place like this, patience was a weapon.

Torin turned back to the group, his gaze meeting Elias’s. "Here’s the plan," he said. "I’ll go first. I’ll map the route to the storage chamber and identify the best exit point for the captives. I’ll mark the path with chalk—subtle, but visible to us. Kaelen, you’ll provide light and cover. If anyone moves, you’ll know before I do. Maren, you’re on rear guard. If we’re cut off, you’re the one who ensures we get back out. Elara, you’re with me. You’ll check for traps and wards as we go. Elias, you’re with me too. We move together, we watch each other’s backs."

Elias nodded. "And if we find the captives?"

"Then we bring them back," Torin said. "But we do it quietly. No shouting, no dramatic entrances. We get them out, we get out ourselves, and we leave the goblins confused and angry, but not organized. That’s when we strike. Not before."

Maren let out a short breath, almost a laugh. "I like that. Let them be angry. Angry goblins make mistakes."

"Exactly," Torin said. "And we’ll be ready for them."

The group moved into the left-hand passage. The air grew colder, the stone walls damp with condensation that beaded on the surface like sweat. Torin led, his movements fluid and precise. He checked the floor with the toe of his boot before stepping, testing for pressure plates, loose stones, anything that might betray their presence. Elara followed closely, her quartz dust vial in hand, ready to test the air for magical residue.

The passage twisted and turned, leading them deeper into the keep. The light from Kaelen’s gauntlet flickered, casting long, dancing shadows that seemed to move independently of their source. The silence was oppressive, broken only by the soft scrape of their boots and the occasional creak of the stone settling under their weight.

After a few minutes, Torin stopped. He raised a hand, and the group froze. He pointed ahead, where the passage opened into a small, circular chamber. The walls were lined with shelves, mostly empty, but a few crates remained, stacked haphazardly. In the center of the room, a single torch burned in an iron sconce, its flame flickering in the draft that seeped in from the cracks in the stone.

"The storage chamber," Torin whispered. "It’s ahead. But we’re not the only ones here."

Elias followed his gaze. In the shadows beyond the torchlight, a figure stood. It was small, hunched, its eyes gleaming with a malevolent intelligence. A goblin. It held a crude spear, its tip pointed directly at the entrance of the passage.

It had been waiting for them.
