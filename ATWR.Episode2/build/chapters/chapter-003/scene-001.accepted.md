The rain drummed a steady, monotonous rhythm against the stone roof, a sound that seemed to seep into the very marrow of the keep. Elias signaled for silence, his hand raised in a sharp, decisive gesture that cut through the low murmur of their debate. He pointed toward a narrow alcove behind a stack of empty grain sacks, a space tucked away from the main path of the central hub where the drafts were weaker and the light from their lanterns was dimmed by the surrounding clutter.

"Here," he said, his voice low. "We need to stop guessing at the layout and start respecting the variables we actually have."

The group moved quickly, shuffling into the alcove to avoid the open floor where any stray footstep might echo. Maren went first, her movements controlled and deliberate despite the weight of the broken cart axle strapped to her shoulder. She settled onto a low stone ledge, her back against the cold wall, and immediately began adjusting the straps across her spine. The leather creaked, a sharp sound in the sudden quiet. She didn’t hide the pain in her face; it lived there, a tightness around her eyes that she refused to acknowledge with a grimace.

"Torin, keep the main corridor covered," Elias instructed, not looking up from where he was unrolling a fresh sheet of parchment on the floor. "If anything moves toward the iron door, we need to know before it sees us."

Torin nodded, his hand resting lightly on the pommel of his sword as he stepped back into the shadows of the corridor. He didn’t speak, but the set of his jaw indicated that the order was accepted and executed simultaneously.

Elias looked at Kaelen, who was leaning against the opposite wall, his brass gauntlet dimmed to a faint, pulsing amber. The mage looked tired, his shoulders slumped slightly, the kind of exhaustion that came not from lack of sleep but from the constant, draining effort of maintaining a magical field in a hostile environment.

"Kaelen," Elias said, keeping his tone measured. "How much do you have left?"

Kaelen gave a dry, humorless smile. "Enough for one shield, Elias. Maybe two, if I’m feeling generous and the air is particularly still. But that’s the ceiling. If I try to push more smoke or light right now, I risk a headache that will last until we’re back in Pinehollow. And I’d rather not spend the rest of our lives picking my teeth with a fork because I can’t lift my hand."

"Then we don't use you for movement," Elias said firmly. "Your magic is a reserve. It’s for the moment when something goes wrong, not for the moment when we’re walking. We move by sight and sound. We rely on the darkness, not on your gauntlet."

Kaelen nodded slowly, the tension in his brow easing slightly. "Understood. One shield. One chance. I’ll make it count."

Elias turned to Maren, who was watching him with an appraising, critical eye. She had stopped fiddling with her straps and was now examining the edge of the broken cart axle, running her thumb over the splintered wood.

"Maren," Elias said. "We need to be clear about your limits. You’re carrying that weight, and you’re moving slower than the rest of us. If we have to sprint, you won’t keep up. If we have to climb, you’ll be the bottleneck."

Maren’s gaze sharpened. "I’m not a bottleneck, Elias. I’m a hammer. Hammers don’t need to run fast to break things. They need to hit hard." She paused, her expression softening just a fraction. "But I’ll admit, the axle is getting heavier. If we’re doing a lot of vertical movement, I might need a hand. Or a rope. But I can still fight. I can still hold a position. I just can’t do both at the same time if the ground is slick."

Elias considered this. He didn't want to micromanage her, but he also didn't want to put her in a situation where her injury compromised the whole group. "Then you stay in the middle of the formation," he decided. "Torin takes point, I take rear. Kaelen and Elara stay close to you. If we need to move quickly, we move together, or we don't move at all. Speed is secondary to safety. We are not racing against the goblins; we are outsmarting them. We wait for their patrols to shift. We use the gaps."

Maren nodded once, a sharp, affirmative motion. "Gaps. I like that. Gaps are where the work gets done."

Elias looked around the small group. They were huddled together in the dim light, a small island of order in a chaotic, ruined world. The rain continued to hammer above them, a constant reminder of the world outside, but in here, the air was still, thick with the smell of old stone and damp wood.

"We have the maps," Elias said, tapping the parchment. "We have the layout. We know where the captives are, and we know where the chief is likely to be. What we don't know is how to get there without tripping whatever they’ve set up to stop us."

He looked at Elara, who was already crouching beside him, her eyes scanning the rough-hewn stone floor. "Elara, you’ve been looking at the ground. Is there anything there? Anything that looks... wrong?"

Elara didn't answer immediately. She was tracing a faint line in the dust with her finger, her expression thoughtful. "There’s a draft," she murmured. "Not from the wind. It’s coming from under the floor. And there’s a vibration. Faint, but steady. Like something is moving below us."

Elias frowned. "A trap?"

"Maybe," Elara said, her voice cautious. "Or maybe just the structure settling. But it’s not random. It’s rhythmic."

Maren leaned forward, her eyes narrowing. "Rhythmic. Like a heartbeat."

They all looked at each other, the unspoken question hanging in the air. What were they walking into? And how were they going to get through it without losing someone?

Elias took a deep breath, steadying himself. "We’ll figure it out," he said. "One step at a time. We don't need to know everything right now. We just need to know enough to take the next step safely."

He stood up, brushing the dust from his knees. "Torin, keep watching. Kaelen, save your energy. Maren, stay close. Elara, keep listening."

He looked at the iron-bound door at the far end of the hub, a dark rectangle against the dim light. Beyond it, Nella and Hobb were waiting. And beyond them, Skrit Ironjaw was waiting too.

"We’re ready," Elias said, though the word felt heavy, laden with the weight of what was to come. "Let’s go find them."
