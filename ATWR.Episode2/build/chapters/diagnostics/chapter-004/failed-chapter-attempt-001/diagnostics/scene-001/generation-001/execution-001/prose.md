The corridor narrowed, the ceiling dipping low enough that Torin had to tilt his head back to avoid scraping his pauldron against the rough-hewn stone. The air grew heavier here, thick with the scent of damp moss and something sharper, like burnt resin. Elias moved behind Torin, keeping his hand on the pommel of his sword, his eyes scanning the uneven floor for any signs of disturbance.

Ahead, the passage opened into a T-junction. To the left, a steep set of stone steps descended into darkness. To the right, a wider hallway stretched out, blocked by a pile of rubble that looked suspiciously deliberate. A single goblin stood at the far end of the right-hand passage, hunched over a fire pit, its back turned to them. It was eating something that sizzled and smoked, the fat dripping into the flames with a violent hiss.

Elias raised a hand, stopping the group. He waited. The goblin burped, a wet, gurgling sound that echoed off the narrow walls. It didn’t look up. It didn’t seem to notice the five shadows coalescing in the darkness behind the rubble pile.

"Torin," Elias whispered, his voice barely a breath. "The steps."

Torin nodded, already moving. He crouched, his boots finding purchase on the slick stone. "Left. It’s a dead end or a trap. But it’s away from the fire. Away from the noise."

He began his descent, one careful step at a time. The stone was cold and wet, slick with condensation. He checked his footing with the toe of his boot before committing his weight, a habit born of long years in treacherous terrain. Behind him, Maren shifted her weight, the broken cart axle strapped to her shoulder creaking softly. She winced, a tight expression of pain crossing her face, but she didn’t complain. She adjusted the strap, locking it in place, and followed Torin into the dark.

Elias waited until they were out of sight before signaling Kaelen and Elara. The mage stepped forward, his brass gauntlet dimmed to a faint, pulsing amber. He didn’t cast a light; he simply let the residual warmth of his magic bleed into the stone, a subtle trick that required more concentration than he had left. His face was pale, his eyes heavy.

"Kaelen," Elias said quietly. "Can you do it again?"

The mage looked at him, a flicker of irritation in his eyes. "I can do it once. Maybe twice. If I try to light this entire staircase, I’ll be seeing stars that aren’t there. And I’d rather not spend the next hour picking my teeth with a fork because I can’t lift my hand."

Elias nodded. "Then we go blind. Trust your feet. Trust the stone."

Kaelen sighed, a long, weary exhale that fogged in the cold air. "Blind it is."

They followed Torin down the steps. The darkness was absolute, a heavy blanket that pressed against their eyes. Elias could feel the vibration of the stone under his boots, a faint hum that seemed to come from deep within the earth. He reached out, his fingers trailing along the rough wall, feeling for handholds, for cracks, for anything that might betray a hidden mechanism.

Elara moved close to him, her satchel clinking softly against her hip. She didn’t speak, but he could feel her presence, a steady anchor in the dark. She was listening, her head tilted, her ears catching sounds that Elias missed.

"Here," she whispered, her voice barely audible. "The air is different. It’s colder. And there’s a draft coming from the left."

Torin stopped. He raised a hand, silencing the group. He listened for a long moment, his eyes closed, his face a mask of concentration. Then he opened his eyes and pointed to the left.

"Through there," he said. "It’s a narrow gap. Just wide enough for one person at a time. It leads to a small chamber. I can see the light from the torch above."

Elias looked at Maren. She gave a short nod, her jaw set. She moved to the front, her hammer resting against her shoulder, the broken cart axle strapped to her back creaking softly with her every movement. She squeezed through the gap, her body twisting to fit the narrow space. The stone scraped against her leather armor, a sharp, abrasive sound that made Elias flinch.

Kaelen followed, his gauntlet dim, his face grim. Elara stepped through next, her satchel catching on a jagged edge of stone. She grunted, adjusting her grip, and pulled herself through. Elias was last. He stepped into the gap, the stone pressing against his back and chest. He could feel the cold seeping into his bones, a deep, penetrating chill that made his muscles tighten.

He pushed forward, his fingers finding the rough edge of the stone. He pulled himself through, his boots finding purchase on the uneven floor of the chamber. He straightened up, his breath misting in the cold air.

They were in a small, circular room. The walls were lined with shelves, mostly empty, but a few crates remained, stacked haphaphazardly. In the center of the room, a single torch burned in an iron sconce, its flame flickering in the draft that seeped in from the cracks in the stone.

And there, in the shadows, stood a goblin.

It was holding a spear, its eyes wide and yellow, its mouth open in a silent scream. It didn’t move. It didn’t breathe. It was frozen, a statue of terror and confusion.

Elias looked at Torin. Torin looked at the goblin.

Then Torin relaxed his grip on his sword. He let it hang loose at his side. He took a step back, a single, slow movement that broke the line of sight between the goblin and the party’s main cluster. He moved into the shadow of a broken pillar, becoming indistinct, a smear of dark leather against the ancient stone.

Kaelen’s gauntlet dimmed further, the light fading until it was indistinguishable from the ambient gloom. The mage was expending the last of his reserves to maintain the illusion of invisibility, a final, silent investment in their safety.

The goblin lowered its spear slightly, its posture relaxing from defensive to curious. It took a step forward, peering into the darkness where the five companions had been moments before. It sniffed the air, its whiskers twitching. It was looking for heat, for breath, for any sign that they were still there.

The party flowed past the goblin, not running, not rushing, but moving with the steady, inevitable pace of a tide. They passed within a foot of the creature, close enough for Elias to smell the sour, musky odor of goblin skin and old sweat. The goblin flinched, its spear jerking upward, but by then Torin was already three steps ahead, his back to the creature, his body a solid wall of quiet confidence.

As they rounded the corner, the last sliver of the goblin’s figure vanished from view. The torchlight flickered, casting long, wavering shadows that danced across the stone floor. The party continued down the passage, their breathing steady, their hearts beating in a synchronized rhythm that was as much a testament to their training as it was to their relief.

Elias allowed himself a small exhale, a sound barely audible over the rain. He glanced back once, just to be sure. The passage behind them was empty. The goblin was gone, lost in the shadows, confused and defeated without ever knowing how.

They moved on, deeper into the keep, toward the storage chamber where Nella and Hobb waited.
