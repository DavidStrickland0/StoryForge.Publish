The rain outside hammered a relentless rhythm against the rotting timber of the keep’s outer wall, turning the courtyard into a slick, reflective mirror that swallowed their footsteps without a sound. Inside the storage chamber, the air was thick with the smell of wet grain, stale meat, and the metallic tang of rusted iron tools left to dry on stone shelves. It was quiet, too quiet for a place occupied by goblins, but Kaelen’s brass gauntlet hummed softly against his thigh—a low-frequency vibration that registered disturbances in the air before they became audible. He had cast *obscurity smoke* earlier, a trick of light and particulate matter designed to mask their presence, but the dampness of the keep seemed to be eating the spell’s edge. The golden haze he’d summoned was already thinning near the ceiling, clinging to soot-stained beams like dust motes in sunlight.

Elias leaned against a stack of crates filled with looted hammers and sickles, watching Kaelen work. The mage stood near the northern edge of the room, where three faint, shimmering lines of residual magic marked the perimeter. Elara was crouched beside him, her chisel already out, scraping at a patch of wall that looked ordinary to anyone else but not to someone trained to read arcane residue. She held a small pouch of quartz dust in her hand, tapping a pinch onto the damp plaster. The dust settled in a fine gray cloud, revealing faint tracings beneath the grime—circles drawn with charcoal and sealed with a binding charm that had long since faded into nothingness.

“Three wards,” Elara murmured, more to herself than to anyone else. “Not traps. Not alarms. Just… presence-masking.” She stood, brushing her knees against the stone floor, and looked up at Elias. “They use *silent warding* spells here. Nothing fancy—just enough to hide their movement from normal sight or hearing. If we try to sneak past the northern gate, they’ll see us before we do, even if Kaelen keeps us obscured.”

Kaelen nodded, his fingers twitching as he adjusted the brass gauntlet’s setting. “I can reinforce the smoke for a bit longer,” he said, voice quiet but steady. “But only if I don’t push too hard. If I overextend it now, I might lose my ability to cast anything else until we’re back in the village.” He glanced at Maren, who was sitting on a crate near the far wall, her hammer resting across her lap. Her breathing was shallow and irregular; the broken cart axle strapped to her shoulder had shifted again during their climb through the keep’s collapsing corridors, and now it pressed hard against her side with every breath. She hadn’t complained, but Elias could see the way she flinched when a drop of water slid down the wall beside them.

Torin stood near the entrance gap that led to the courtyard, scanning the darkness beyond with his sword held low. His boots were silent on the wet stone, and he kept one hand on the hilt while his other tracked the movement of shadows. “No lights outside,” he said finally. “Just rain and wind.” He stepped forward, crouching beside Elara to examine the wall markings herself. “If they’re using silence wards, then noise is our biggest risk. We’ll have to move slow and keep our voices down. Even a footstep might give us away if they can hear vibrations through the floorboards.”

Elias nodded, adjusting his grip on his short sword as he looked around the chamber. It was clearly a storage area—rows of crates lined the walls, filled with farming tools taken from nearby villages, sacks of grain, and bundles of rope tied neatly with rough hemp. A crude map pinned to the back of one crate showed routes marked in charcoal ink, connecting several farmsteads to the keep’s location. Names were scrawled beside certain points: *Nella’s Farm*, *Miller’s Place*, *Blackwood Valley*. Elias recognized Nella’s name immediately; the others didn’t seem to notice it until he pointed it out.

“Wait,” he said quietly, pulling the map closer to inspect it. “These aren’t just random raids. They’re organized. Look here—the routes avoid forests and stick to roads whenever possible. And this note says they’ll hit Blackwood Valley next week.” He glanced up at Kaelen. “That’s outside your range of influence. But if we take out the northern gate, we can cut off their escape route and force them into open ground where Maren has the advantage.”

Kaelen frowned slightly, his brow furrowed as he considered Elias’s words. “If we close that gate, they’ll likely try to breach it from the inside. Which means we’ll have to fight in tight quarters. That won’t be easy.” He paused, then added, “But if we leave it open, they’ll just use it to retreat or reinforce. Either way, we need to decide what our priority is: speed or control?”

Elias didn’t answer immediately. Instead, he walked over to the map and traced a finger along the northern route marked in charcoal. “We don’t have time for debate,” he said finally. “Maren’s getting tired. Torin’s been watching the perimeter long enough. Kaelen’s magic is fading faster than I thought. And Elara’s right—we can’t afford to miss our chance while they’re still gathering supplies.” He turned back to face them, his expression serious but calm. “We move now. Quietly. Use the smoke to mask us as we cross the courtyard. Once we get inside, we deal with whatever we find. That includes any goblins still awake or asleep in the storage area.”

Maren grunted, shifting her weight slightly to relieve pressure on her injured side. “I’m not arguing,” she said flatly. “Just tell me where I need to go next.” She stood slowly, testing the weight of her hammer as it swung freely despite the pain in her shoulder. The wood of its haft was dry now, but she could feel how much harder it had become to swing it with precision. Elias had already suggested they lighten their load once they reached the tree line, but Maren hadn’t agreed to leave anything behind—not tools, not weapons, and certainly not her hammer. Still, she’d managed to redistribute the broken cart axle’s weight across her back and shoulders in a way that minimized further injury.

Torin stepped forward again, his sword drawn low and ready. “I’ll take point,” he said without hesitation. “Maren, stay behind me but keep close. Elara, follow Elias—he’ll guide you to the northern gate where Kaelen can reinforce the smoke there.” He glanced at Maren briefly before turning toward the entrance gap. “And if anyone tries to ambush us from the shadows, shoot first and ask questions later.”

Elias nodded once, then signaled for them to begin moving. They stepped out of the storage chamber and into the courtyard beyond, where rain continued to lash down in sheets. The ground was slick with mud, and their boots sank slightly with every step, leaving faint impressions that were quickly washed away by new drops. Kaelen activated his gauntlet again, casting a fresh ring of golden light around them as they approached the northern wall. The smoke he summoned this time was thicker than before, swirling gently around their forms like mist in sunlight. It didn’t hide them completely—but it made them appear less distinct to anyone watching from the shadows or rooftops nearby.

They moved toward the northern gate, which stood slightly ajar despite being reinforced with chains and iron bars. Torin checked the hinges carefully before stepping through, his sword ready for any sign of movement inside. Maren followed close behind, her hammer raised defensively as she scanned the interior of the keep’s main hall. Elias brought up the rear, keeping an eye on Elara as she adjusted her satchel and prepared to examine whatever they found next. The smoke began to dissipate near the ceiling again, but Kaelen managed to maintain a steady glow around their lower bodies, enough to obscure them from view while still allowing them to see clearly.

Inside the main hall, the air was colder than it had been in the storage area, and the floorboards groaned underfoot as they crossed the threshold. Torin stopped short before entering fully, his sword lowered slightly but still held ready. “Something’s wrong,” he whispered, his voice barely audible over the sound of rain outside. “They’re waiting for us.”

Elias signaled for them to hold their positions until he could assess the situation. He stepped forward cautiously, his eyes scanning the darkness ahead as Elara lit a small lantern from her satchel. The light revealed rows of crates stacked against the far wall, some of which had been pried open and emptied into piles on the floor. Others were still intact, their lids bolted shut with rusted iron clamps. But what caught Elias’s attention immediately was the faint hum coming from somewhere deeper within the keep. It wasn’t magical—it was mechanical, a low rumble that seemed to come from beneath the floor itself.

Elara knelt beside one of the open crates and pulled out a small vial of quartz dust again, tapping another pinch onto the damp wood inside. The dust settled in a fine gray cloud, revealing faint vibrations traveling through the stone foundation. “They’re using resonant wards,” she murmured, her voice low and analytical. “Something to trigger traps or alarms if anyone tries to disturb the floor too much.” She stood again, brushing her knees against the stone once more. “If we step wrong, we’ll set something off that could wake everyone up instantly.”

Kaelen nodded, his fingers twitching as he adjusted his gauntlet’s settings again. “I can dampen the hum slightly,” he said, casting a second spell designed to interfere with magical resonance. The golden light around them flickered briefly before stabilizing again. “But only for a few seconds. After that, I’ll need time to recharge.” He glanced at Elias with an apologetic expression. “Sorry. But it’s better than setting off an alarm and getting caught in the open.”

Elias nodded once, trusting Kaelen’s judgment even though he knew how much effort such spells must cost him over time. “Keep us hidden,” he said quietly. “Move slow and carefully. We’ll deal with whatever we find before anyone else wakes up.” He turned back to Torin, who was already scanning the shadows ahead with his sword held low. “Stay alert. If they’re expecting us here, they’ll be ready for us too.”

Torin didn’t respond verbally—he simply nodded and stepped forward again, moving silently along the edge of the room as if testing each step before committing his full weight to it. Maren stayed close behind him, her hammer raised defensively as she kept watch over the rest of the group. Elias followed Elara’s lead, examining the crates and crates until they found one that looked particularly suspicious. It was larger than the others, filled with sacks of grain and bundles of rope tied neatly with rough hemp. But something about it felt… off. There were faint scorch marks around its edges, as if someone had burned it recently before moving on to something else.

Elara opened it carefully, pulling out a handful of quartz dust again. The dust settled in a fine gray cloud, revealing faint traces of blood mixed with soot and ash. “They’ve been burning things,” she murmured, her voice barely audible over the sound of rain outside. “Gear, maybe weapons. Or worse—people.” She stood again, brushing her knees against the stone once more. “We need to get inside before they finish whatever they’re doing here.”

Elias nodded once, then signaled for them to begin moving again. They stepped deeper into the keep’s interior, following the faint hum of magical resonance as it led them toward the source of the disturbance. The smoke around them began to thin again near the ceiling, but Kaelen managed to maintain a steady glow around their lower bodies until they reached the next chamber. There, the hum grew louder, vibrating through the floorboards beneath their feet and making each step feel unstable. Torin paused briefly to test the ground with his sword, checking for hidden traps or loose stones that could trigger something dangerous.

“Something’s wrong,” he said finally, his voice low and tense. “They’re not just gathering supplies. They’re preparing for something bigger.” He glanced at Elias with a questioning look, his expression serious but calm. “Whatever it is, we’ll deal with it when we get there.”
