The air inside the storage chamber tasted of wet wool, rusted iron, and the sharp tang of unwashed fear. Maps pinned to rough-hewn beams detailed routes through pinehollow and blackwood valley, their edges frayed by damp fingers or claws. Elias stepped over a pile of stolen grain sacks that had burst open hours ago, releasing clouds of musty dust into the gloom. The rain outside hammered against the keep's broken roof, a chaotic rhythm that masked the sound of their own breathing.

Kaelen stood near a rusted iron door that led to the deeper levels, his brass gauntlet humming softly. He had already cast detect magic three times since they crossed the threshold, each casting revealing faint lines of residual energy clinging to the northern wall and two corners of the central chamber. Elara was crouched beside a crude table, her chisel hovering inches above a map that depicted the village grid with surprising precision for something drawn by goblins. She held a small vial of loose quartz dust in one hand, using it to trace the patterns Kaelen had flagged.

"The wards are thin," Elara said without looking up, her voice barely rising above the storm's roar. "Simple concealment spells anchored to structural weaknesses. Not much protection against a determined party with proper tools." She dipped her brush into the quartz dust and swept it across the floor near the northern wall. The powder caught on the faint shimmer of air that marked where Kaelen's magic had burned through, leaving behind tracks that looked like footprints but lacked the drag marks of boots. "These lines show where they tried to hide their presence," she murmured, marking the spots with a charcoal stick pulled from her satchel. "Three sources detected by your gauntlet match these concentrations."

Kaelen nodded once, keeping his eyes on the ceiling where water dripped steadily from a cracked beam. "I felt them as well," he admitted quietly, though his tone lacked any hint of embarrassment about failing to detect something so obvious. "But I couldn't maintain the shield long enough to see through all three at once without draining myself completely." He shifted his weight, favoring his left leg slightly—a minor strain from carrying the group's supplies earlier that day—but made no mention of it. His brass gauntlet pulsed with a faint golden light as he prepared another casting, though this time he chose to cast detect magic rather than a shield spell. "There's less need for concealment now," he added, gesturing toward the door. "We know what we're dealing with."

Elias watched them work in silence, listening to the soft scratch of Elara's chisel against stone and the occasional click of Kaelen's gauntlet as he adjusted its settings. Torin had taken position near the entrance gap where they'd entered, his hand resting lightly on the hilt of his sword while scanning the shadows beyond the broken outer wall. Maren stood beside him, checking her hammer's balance against the weight still strapped to her shoulder from the cart axle she'd brought along. She hadn't complained about the pain anymore; instead, she adjusted her grip every few steps, letting the dull throb in her burned hands fade into background noise.

"Don't thank me yet," Elias replied with a wry smile that didn't quite reach his eyes. Outside, the rain continued to pour relentlessly down around them, washing away some of the finer details but leaving enough evidence for their current needs. Elara tucked her notebook into her satchel along with a small vial of quartz dust she'd collected during their earlier travels—a reminder that even mundane tasks could have unexpected consequences in worlds where magic existed alongside ordinary life.

As they emerged once more into the clearing beyond the keep's broken walls, Elias felt a strange mix of relief and unease wash over him simultaneously. The contract was signed, the gold counted twice, and now they had a purpose beyond mere survival. But then he looked ahead toward the village lights visible in the distance through gaps in tree lines, knowing that safety awaited them there if only they could hold on long enough to reach it.

"We did it," Maren said finally after a moment of silence, breaking the quiet that had fallen over them all. "We got them back."
