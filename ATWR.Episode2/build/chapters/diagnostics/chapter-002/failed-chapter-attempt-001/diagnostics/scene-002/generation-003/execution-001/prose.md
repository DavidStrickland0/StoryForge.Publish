The rain continued to lash against the broken roof of the keep, a relentless drumming that seemed to swallow the sound of their footsteps. Inside the storage area, Elias Thorne stood near a crude table piled with looted sacks and stolen grain, reviewing the maps spread out before them. These were not the detailed charts of the temple he had seen in the previous adventure; these were crude sketches drawn on repurposed leather, marked with jagged lines indicating recent raid routes and arrows pointing toward Blackwood Valley.

“Look at this,” Elias murmured, tracing a red marker that indicated the next farm to be hit after Miller’s place. “They move systematically. If they’re hitting farms in a pattern, they must have established a routine here.”

Kaelen nodded, his brass gauntlet dimmed now that the party was no longer hiding from direct threats but moving through the interior of an enemy stronghold. He had used his smoke magic earlier to mask their approach as they crossed the courtyard, creating a veil of gray mist that clung to the floor and walls before dissipating into the damp air. Now, however, he stood silent, conserving his resources until they needed them for something more than simple concealment.

“Smoke screens are good for hiding movement,” Kaelen said quietly, his voice barely rising above the sound of rain. “But if there are wards active inside, they’ll still detect us when we get too close.”

Elara stepped forward, her spectacles catching the faint glow from the torches burning in iron sconces along the walls. She pulled a small vial from her satchel and uncorked it, letting a few grains of loose quartz dust drift down onto one of the maps. The dust settled silently, forming a fine layer over the ink lines.

“The wards,” Elara said, kneeling beside the map to inspect where the particles clung most tightly. “They’re not magical barriers in the sense of force fields or energy shields. They’re more like… sensory traps. Something that alerts the goblins when someone gets within twenty feet of certain spots.” She pointed to a series of faint markings along the northern wall, where the dust seemed to react as if disturbed by invisible currents. “And look here—this is where they’ve reinforced their defenses after the last raid failed.”

Torin approached cautiously from the shadows of a nearby corridor, his hand resting near the hilt of his sword. He had been keeping watch at the entrance while Elias and Elara worked over the maps. Now he moved closer, scanning the dim light with practiced eyes. “Any traps?” he asked, though his tone suggested he already knew the answer.

“Nothing active right now,” Elara replied, gesturing toward a small grate in the floor that had been sealed with fresh mud and covered by a thin sheet of leather. “But if they’ve set up wards like this along their perimeter… we’ll need to be careful how we move forward.”

Maren adjusted her pack again, shifting her weight off her injured leg before stepping into the central chamber. Her movements were slower than usual, every step deliberate and measured. She carried the broken cart axle still strapped across her shoulders, its jagged ends digging slightly into her skin where it pressed against her leather armor. Despite the pain, she kept her posture rigid and focused on scanning the room for signs of recent activity.

“These maps show two levels,” Maren noted, pointing to a sketch showing what looked like an upper floor connected by a rickety wooden staircase that led nowhere visible from ground level. “One part’s intact enough for them to store supplies and maybe sleep there. The other part’s collapsed or flooded. Which one did they take the captives into?”

Elias shook his head slowly, studying the map again. “If they’re holding prisoners down here… they’d need a secure room somewhere accessible but hidden from casual view.” He tapped on another section of the map where a narrow passage wound off toward the rear of the structure. “Let’s check that corridor. If anyone’s held in there, it’ll be near an opening big enough to drag someone through without raising suspicion.”

Kaelen stepped forward again, his gauntlet glowing faintly as he cast a low-level spell designed to reveal concealed features or detect magical residue lingering on surfaces. A thin wisp of smoke curled around the air between them, swirling into shapes that briefly suggested hidden passages behind walls or loose stones beneath their feet.

“Nothing obvious here,” Kaelen reported after a few moments. “Just the usual dampness and whatever’s left over from previous spells being cast here.” He glanced at Elara, who was still examining the quartz dust on the map. “You sure these wards aren’t linked to something bigger?”

Elara capped the vial and tucked it back into her satchel. “Not necessarily. Goblins are opportunistic raiders, not architects of grand magical schemes. These wards are simple concealment spells meant to keep intruders from noticing traps or ambushes until they’re too close.” She paused, then added, “That said… if they’ve set up a network of them around the perimeter, we’ll need to be extra cautious about noise and movement when we go outside again.”

Elias nodded, turning back toward the corridor Maren had pointed out earlier. “Alright. We’ll move quietly through this passage first. Then we’ll scout the upper area if we find anything useful there. Torin, you take point. Maren, cover our rear with that hammer of yours—just in case they’ve left any stragglers behind.”

Elara trailed last, clutching her satchel close to her chest as if protecting something precious within it.

“It’s quiet now,” Torin said after a moment. “They’ve probably settled down for the night. If we move slowly and stay low…” He gestured toward the floor again, indicating how much easier it would be to slip past unseen if they kept their voices down.

Elara followed last, keeping a close watch on the walls and ceiling for signs of additional traps or hidden mechanisms.

We need to get into deeper chambers before morning breaks.” She gestured toward a set of stairs leading upward along one side of the room. “Upper level first. Then we’ll head downstairs again once we’re ready.”

Elara knelt beside another table cluttered with maps and notes, carefully examining each one as she went. She paused occasionally to make additional sketches or jot down observations in her notebook before moving on to the next item of interest. Her focus remained sharp despite fatigue weighing heavily on everyone’s shoulders, including hers.

“They’ve got a good grasp of local geography,” Elara said finally, closing her notebook and tucking it back into her satchel. “Knowing where every creek bend or ridge line lies gives them advantages we wouldn’t expect.” She glanced up at Elias with a knowing smile. “Which means we’ll need to adapt our strategy accordingly.”

Kaelen followed last, his gauntlet dimming once more as they moved deeper into enemy territory without being detected.

Every step brought them closer to completing their mission—and further away from safety that awaited them back at the Crooked Stag.
