He moved first, his longsword drawn half its length so he could cut through any hanging branches or brush that might snag their progress. Behind him, Maren followed closely, her hammer resting against her shoulder where it balanced awkwardly with the broken cart axle strapped to her back. Elias walked near the center of the group, watching both ahead and behind while keeping his voice low enough not to carry over the sound of rain hitting the canopy. Maren didn’t stop, though she shifted her stance slightly to relieve pressure on her left side. “Steady enough,” she said, voice clipped. “But if we’re going to be crawling through brambles and climbing over roots for hours, I’ll need you to watch my back more than usual.”

Torin nodded without turning. “I’ve got your flank covered,” he replied, his tone matter-of-fact rather than reassuring. “Just keep moving where the ground holds.”

Elara walked slightly ahead of Elias, using her small lantern to check for loose stones or hidden dips in the trail that the rain had loosened. She carried a satchel filled with chalk and brushes, ready to mark any new evidence they found along the way. The goblins must’ve used it to drag something heavy out quickly.”

Elias nodded, studying the frayed ends where hemp fibers had been pulled apart by force. “Hasty retreat,” he confirmed. “They didn’t want to be seen here.”

The structure was partially collapsed, its outer wall broken at one end and its roof long since gone, leaving only stone foundations and scattered timber framing what remained of it. Smoke curled lazily from a vent near the top of the tower section, indicating that someone—or something—was still inside despite the storm.

Torin halted the group before they entered the clearing, signaling for silence. He crouched low beside a fallen log, peering around its edge to inspect the perimeter. “There’s a guard post up there,” he whispered, pointing toward a raised platform adjacent to the keep’s main gate. A crude alarm system hung from a rusted hook near the entrance—a bell suspended by thin rope that could be pulled manually from within. “And looks like they’ve got two lookouts rotating every hour or so.”

Maren grunted in agreement, pulling her cloak tighter around herself as she assessed the damage to the wall. “Broken outer wall,” she observed, tracing the jagged line of rubble with her boot toe. “We can slip through there if we’re quiet enough.”

Elias stepped forward to examine the gap more closely. The stones had been displaced cleanly, suggesting they hadn’t stood long after collapse—but whoever had knocked them down must have done so carefully to avoid triggering whatever mechanism held the rest of the wall upright. “Looks like it could hold us,” he said, running a gloved hand along the rough surface. “But we’ll need to move fast once we’re through.”

Torin stood up and wiped rainwater from his brow with the back of his gauntlet. “Fast enough to avoid detection?” he asked, voice flat but not unkind. “That’s the real question.”

Kaelen stepped forward then, raising his brass gauntlet once more. The faint golden glow pulsed gently again, casting a soft light through the gloom ahead without drawing attention from nearby wildlife—or worse, goblin scouts patrolling the perimeter. Elara paused to observe the phenomenon, her notebook open again despite Elias’s earlier warning to pack up. “Interesting,” she murmured, dipping her quill into ink before sketching how the smoke behaved in the wind. “The particles are forming irregular shapes—almost like they’re reacting to something magnetic.”

Elias frowned slightly but didn’t press further; he knew better than to question a mage’s explanations when his own understanding of magic was limited to what had been demonstrated so far. “Let’s gather what we can before heading out,” Elias called softly, his voice carrying just enough authority to remind them they weren’t alone in this mission. “Pack the tools if you think they’re useful. Leave the rest for now.”
