The rain had begun to ease, though the sky remained a bruised purple, heavy with the promise of another storm before dawn. Elias led the group away from the central hub of the keep, stepping out through the broken outer wall into a small ravine that sheltered them from the wind and the most direct rain. The ground here was rocky and uneven, covered in loose stones that crinkled underfoot, but it offered enough cover to speak without shouting.

Maren moved ahead of them, her hammer still strapped across her back with the broken cart axle. She didn't complain about the weight anymore; she had adjusted her gait until the strain felt like a familiar companion rather than an injury. Torin scanned the perimeter, his eyes moving over the dark treeline that bordered the ravine. Elara knelt beside a cluster of ferns, sketching something on a scrap of parchment before standing and wiping charcoal from her cheek. Kaelen stood with his back against a mossy rock, the brass gauntlet on his left hand dimmed to a faint glow that barely pierced the gloom.

"We're out of the main storage area," Elias said, gesturing toward the broken wall behind them. "The maps are useful for seeing what they've taken, but we need to talk about how we get back in there without getting ourselves killed before we even start." He kept his voice low, mindful that goblin ears were sharper than their own when the wind died down.

Maren leaned against a boulder, crossing her arms over her chest. The movement sent a fresh wave of pain through her side, but she didn't flinch or make a sound about it. "The broken wall is tempting," she said. "It's clear of debris, and the angle gives us high ground on whatever guard posts they have along the perimeter. But I've seen goblins use tripwires on walls that look like nothing."

Elara nodded, holding up her lantern to show the small group the faint outlines of the ravine wall. She had marked several potential weak points with chalk, lines that glowed slightly in the dim light thanks to Kaelen's residual magic. "The wards are active here too," she said, pointing to a faint shimmering distortion in the air near the top of the cliff face. "They're not as dense as inside, but they'll still mask movement if someone tries to climb or jump over them without being detected."

Kaelen shifted his weight, the gauntlet's hum dipping slightly before stabilizing again. "I can keep us obscured for a while longer," he said. "Just enough to cross the ravine and reach the entrance ledge above the guard post. But after that... I don't know if I'll have much left to spare."

"Good enough," Torin replied, stepping up beside him. "We don't need invisibility to sneak past a single lookout. We just need to make sure they don't see us until we're ready for them." He glanced at Maren. "You can take point on the climb down. I'll cover your left flank while Elias handles the right side and coordinates with Elara on ward detection."

Maren nodded, though she didn't smile. "That's how it should be," she said. "But let me handle the descent first. If the path is slick or has hidden roots, I can clear them before anyone else tries." She stepped forward, testing the footing carefully before committing her weight to a new step.

Elias watched her go, his hand resting lightly on the hilt of his sword. He wasn't sure how much time they had left before dawn broke and the goblins became more active. The maps showed that Blackwood Valley was only two days away, but the party knew better than to assume the raiders would stick to a rigid schedule if something went wrong. They needed to get inside now, secure the captives, and bring them back before nightfall forced them into a longer journey.

"You're right," Elias said finally. "We don't have time for second-guessing." He turned his attention to Elara, who was still examining the ward lines on the chalk marks. "What about that passage behind the guard post? Is it trapped?"

Elara shook her head slowly, pulling a fresh piece of parchment from her satchel and beginning to sketch again. "It's definitely trapped," she said. "I can see faint traces of activation residue along the floor edges. Something happens when someone tries to enter or leave without permission. Probably pressure plates or motion sensors."

"Can we bypass it?" Torin asked, scanning the area around them for any sign of movement beyond the ravine walls.

"Not directly," Elara replied. "But I think there's a way around if we use the terrain against the trap. If we can get above the entrance point and drop down inside before the mechanism triggers, we might be able to slip through without setting anything off." She looked up at Kaelen. "Can you create a distraction or mask our presence temporarily? Just something that draws attention away from us while we move into position?"

Kaelen hesitated for a moment, then nodded. "I can do smoke again," he said. "But only if you're ready to coordinate with me on where to place it. And remember—this won't last long."

"Understood," Elias said. "We'll stick close and move fast once we cross the ravine. If anyone tries to ambush us from above, Torin and Maren will handle them immediately while Elara and I cover the rest of the group."

Torin gave a short nod, his expression unreadable but his stance firm. "Agreed," he said. "Just keep moving and trust each other. We've done this before, even if things don't always go perfectly."

Elara packed her supplies away and stood up, brushing dirt from her knees. "Let's get ready," she said. "We'll need to be precise about our movements once we're in position. One wrong step could mean disaster."

The group began moving toward the entrance ledge above the guard post, stepping carefully over loose stones and avoiding areas where roots had shifted or fallen away. The rain continued to fall, though less heavily now, creating a mist that clung to the rocks and made visibility poor. Kaelen raised his hand again, and a faint ring of golden light spread outward from the gauntlet, illuminating their path just enough for them to see where they needed to go.

As they reached the top of the ravine wall, Maren stepped forward and began clearing the path ahead, removing loose stones and checking for hidden traps or tripwires. Elias followed behind her, keeping watch over the group while also looking for signs of goblin activity along the perimeter. Torin flanked them on the right side, scanning the treeline for any movement that might indicate an approaching threat. Elara stayed near the rear, recording observations and marking potential escape routes in case things went wrong.

They moved slowly but steadily, each member aware of their role in maintaining cohesion under these conditions. Maren led with her hammer ready, Torin watched the path ahead for traps or ambushes, Elara recorded findings as they went, Kaelen managed visibility and defense, and Elias coordinated everything while keeping an eye on morale. They weren't heroes by some grand design—they were five people who had agreed to work together because no one else would help them survive another night of goblin raids. And tonight, with the rain masking their approach and the trail guiding them toward a ruined keep that sat beyond the tree line, they were closer than ever to proving themselves capable enough to face whatever lay ahead.

Once they reached the entrance ledge above the guard post, Maren paused and signaled for the group to hold still. She knelt beside the edge of the wall and looked down into the darkened courtyard below, where faint torchlight flickered from within the keep's windows. The sound of distant voices drifted up from inside, accompanied by the clatter of metal boots on stone floors.

"They're awake," Maren whispered. "And they've got a lookout posted outside."

Elias nodded, keeping his voice low as he signaled for Kaelen to prepare another burst of smoke. "Okay," he said. "We move in now. Stick together and trust your instincts. If anything goes wrong, we deal with it as best we can."

Torin gave a short nod, his eyes scanning the area around them one last time before stepping forward to join Maren at the edge of the wall. Elara followed close behind, her lantern casting faint beams of light against the crumbling stone walls. Kaelen raised his hand, and a thick veil of gray smoke began to spiral outward from the gauntlet, masking their presence as they descended into the ravine below.

The descent was slow and deliberate, each step calculated to avoid triggering any hidden traps or alarms. The air grew colder as they moved lower, carrying with it the scent of damp earth and woodsmoke that lingered stubbornly despite the rain washing away most traces of violence. Once they reached the bottom of the ravine, Maren stepped forward and signaled for the group to spread out slightly, allowing them to reach the entrance ledge above the guard post.

They waited there for several moments, listening intently for any sign of movement or noise that might indicate an approaching threat. Finally, Torin gave a short nod, signaling that it was time to proceed. The group moved forward again, stepping carefully over rubble and avoiding puddles that might conceal hidden pitfalls or traps. The smoke from Kaelen's gauntlet swirled around them like a protective curtain, masking their presence as they passed through the narrow corridor and into the next chamber beyond.
