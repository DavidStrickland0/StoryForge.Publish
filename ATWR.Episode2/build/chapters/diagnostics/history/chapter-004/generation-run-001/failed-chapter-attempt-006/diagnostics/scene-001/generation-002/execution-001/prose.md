The corridor narrowed as they descended, the stone walls pressing in with a damp, claustrophobic intimacy that seemed to absorb the last of the light. The air here was heavier, thick with the scent of wet moss and the distinct, metallic tang of old iron. There was no rain here, no sound of it; only the subtle, rhythmic tick of condensation dripping from the ceiling onto the flagstones below. Each drop fell with a precision that felt almost deliberate, a metronome marking their progress into the keep’s deeper bowels.

Elias kept his hand on the pommel of his sword, not out of necessity, but out of habit. The steel was cold against his palm, a grounding weight in the suffocating dark. He focused on the space between his boots and the uneven stone, feeling for the slight give of loose mortar or the hollow ring of a hidden panel. The ground was treacherous here, slick with moisture that had seeped through the ancient masonry. A single misstep, a sudden slip, would be enough to shatter the silence they had worked so hard to maintain.

Behind him, the creak of the cart axle against Maren’s shoulder was a constant, low-frequency hum. It was a sound that did not belong in a stealth approach, a rhythmic reminder of the physical cost of their intrusion. Elias heard the shift in her weight as she adjusted her grip, the subtle grinding of leather against leather as she compensated for the drag of the broken wood. He did not turn to look at her, nor did he offer a word of encouragement. Words were unnecessary. They both understood the equation: her pain was a variable they had to manage, not a variable to be eliminated. To stop would be to risk the goblins regrouping; to move faster would be to risk her footing. So they moved at the speed of her worst moment, a slow, agonizing crawl that stretched the minutes into something indistinguishable from hours.

Kaelen moved beside Elias, his presence a dim, pulsing glow in the darkness. The brass gauntlet on his right hand had dimmed to a faint, amber flicker, barely enough to cast a shadow on the immediate floor. It was a fragile light, a spark struggling against the overwhelming weight of the dark. Elias could see the strain in Kaelen’s posture, the way his shoulders hunched inward, protecting the gauntlet as if it were a live coal. Every few steps, Kaelen’s breath hitched, a sharp, silent intake that spoke of the cost of maintaining even this minimal output. The magic was not just draining his energy; it was sapping his physical strength, leaving him hollowed out, trembling at the edges.

"Keep it low," Elias whispered, the words barely a breath, lost in the damp air. He didn't look at Kaelen; he didn't need to. The dimness of the light was enough.

Kaelen nodded, a jerky motion that seemed to cost him dearly. "It’s holding," the mage murmured, his voice rough, stripped of its usual dry humor. "But it’s… thin. Like oil on water. One drop, and it’s gone."

Elias nodded. He knew the feeling of resources running out, of the gap between what was required and what was available widening with every step. He adjusted his own breathing, slowing it down, forcing his heart rate to settle. He needed to be calm. He needed to be steady. If he panicked, if he rushed, the group would unravel.

Maren stopped.

The sudden halt was jarring. The creak of the axle ceased, replaced by a sharp, silent inhale. Elias froze, his hand tightening on his sword hilt. He didn't move. He didn't breathe. He waited for the sound of footsteps, for the hiss of a blade, for the shout of a goblin. But there was only the dripping of the condensation, the faint, rhythmic pulse of Kaelen’s gauntlet, and the heavy, labored breathing of the four of them.

Maren was leaning against the wall, her head bowed, her hands resting on her knees. She was not looking at them. She was looking at the floor, her eyes closed, her face contorted in a silent struggle. The pain was there, raw and unmasked, a physical weight that had finally become more than she could simply carry.

Elias didn't offer a hand. He didn't speak. He simply waited, his eyes fixed on the darkness ahead, his body rigid, ready to react. The silence stretched, taut and fragile. Kaelen’s light flickered, the amber glow dimming further, struggling to maintain its coherence. The air was still, thick with tension and the shared, unspoken acknowledgment of their limits.

After a long moment, Maren straightened. The movement was slow, deliberate, a careful redistribution of weight that spoke of immense effort. She adjusted the straps of the axle, her hands moving with a precision that was almost mechanical. She took a breath, a deep, shuddering draw of air that filled her lungs, and then released it slowly.

"Ready," she said, her voice flat, devoid of emotion.

Elias nodded. He stepped forward, his boots finding the next secure footing. The group resumed their movement, slower now, more cautious. The corridor continued to narrow, the darkness pressing in, the light of Kaelen’s gauntlet a small, defiant spark in the vast, indifferent dark. They were moving forward, step by step, into the unknown.
