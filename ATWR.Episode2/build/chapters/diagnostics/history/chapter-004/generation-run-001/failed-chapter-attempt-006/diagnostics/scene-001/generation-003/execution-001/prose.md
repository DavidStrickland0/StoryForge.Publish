The corridor narrowed as they progressed, the stone walls pressing in close, damp and slick with condensation that beaded on the rough-hewn surface. The air here was colder, carrying a distinct smell of wet earth and stale iron that clung to the back of Elias’s throat. He kept his stride steady, his boots finding purchase on the uneven flagstones with a practiced silence that he had cultivated over years of navigating hostile terrain. He did not rush. Rushing was a luxury they could not afford, and more importantly, it was a mistake they could not afford to make twice.

Torin moved first, his body angled slightly so that his sword hung loose at his side, the steel catching no light. His hand rested lightly on the pommel, not in threat, but in readiness. He listened. The rain drummed against the distant roof, a steady, rhythmic backdrop that masked any subtle sounds from deeper within the keep. But beneath that, there was a silence that felt deliberate. The goblins were not moving. They were waiting.

Elias felt the tension in his own shoulders, a physical knot of adrenaline that he consciously worked to loosen. He kept his eyes on the path ahead, scanning the shadows for any shift in posture, any flicker of movement. The light from Kaelen’s gauntlet was dimmed to a faint, pulsing amber, just enough to see the immediate floor but not far enough to reveal what lay beyond the next turn. It was a deliberate choice, a trade-off between visibility and stealth that Kaelen had made after checking his reserves.

"Kaelen," Elias murmured, his voice barely a breath, "how much longer can you hold that?"

Kaelen’s eyes were fixed on the wall ahead, his expression tight. The brass gauntlet on his left hand glowed softly, the light casting long, dancing shadows that seemed to move independently of their source. "The field is stable," he replied, his voice low and strained. "But it’s draining. Every second we spend in this damp, the magic wants to leak. I can hold it for a few more minutes, maybe ten, if we keep moving. But if I need to expand the radius or increase the intensity to hide us from a direct line of sight, I’ll lose the shield capability entirely."

Elias nodded, understanding the weight of that limitation. One shield. One chance. If they were discovered, Kaelen would have to choose between hiding them or protecting them. There was no middle ground.

Maren, bringing up the rear, shifted her weight. A wince crossed her face, subtle but unmistakable, as she adjusted the broken cart axle strapped to her back. The leather creaked, a sharp sound in the silence that made Elias’s head snap toward her.

"Careful," he whispered, his eyes sharp.

Maren didn’t look at him. Her gaze was fixed on the corridor ahead, her hands resting on the haft of her hammer. "I’m fine," she said, her voice steady, though there was a tightness in it that betrayed the effort it took to keep her voice level. "The axle is heavy, but it’s balanced. I’m just managing the load. Don’t look at me like I’m going to drop."

"I wasn’t going to do that," Elias said, though the concern was evident in his tone. "We need you at the back. If we’re cut off, you’re the one who ensures we get out. And you need to be able to move."

Maren gave a short, sharp nod. "I can move. I just need to manage the pace. If we start sprinting, I’ll be the bottleneck. If we have to climb, I’ll need a hand. But I can hold a position. I can fight. I just can’t do both at the same time if the ground is slick."

Elias considered this. He didn’t want to micromanage her, but he also didn’t want to put her in a situation where her injury compromised the whole group. "Then we move together," he decided. "No sudden stops. No sudden starts. We maintain a steady, even pace. Kaelen, keep the light low. Just enough to see the floor. Torin, keep your eyes on the walls. If you see a seam, a crack, anything that looks wrong, tell us before we step on it."

Torin nodded, his hand moving slightly away from the pommel. "I’m watching," he said. "The stone here is old. It’s settled. I don’t see any obvious traps, but I’m listening for the air. If there’s a draft, it’s coming from above. The ventilation shafts."

"Ventilation shafts?" Elara asked, her voice coming from just behind Elias, quiet and focused. She was crouching slightly, her lantern held low, casting a weak, trembling pool of light across the flagstones.

"Yes," Torin said. "If the goblins are hiding in the upper levels, they’ll use the shafts to move. We need to be aware of the ceiling."

Elias looked up. The ceiling was high, lost in the darkness above. It was easy to forget that they were not alone in the keep. The goblins were there, in the shadows, in the walls, in the air. They were waiting for them to make a mistake.

"Keep moving," Elias said, his voice firm. "We’re close to the junction. Torin, lead. Maren, stay close. Kaelen, hold the light. Elara, keep listening."

The group moved forward, stepping carefully over the cracked flagstones. The air in the corridor was colder, drier, and smelled faintly of iron and old blood. But it was silent. No voices. No footsteps. Just the distant sound of rain falling on the keep’s roof.

They had bypassed the first guard. They had maintained their cover. The goblins remained unaware of their presence. The stealth approach had worked.

The corridor stretched ahead of them, a narrow throat of cold stone that seemed to swallow the faint light from their lanterns. The air here was still, lacking the damp chill of the outer courtyard, but it carried a heavier scent—a mix of wet earth, old wood, and the faint, metallic tang of rusted iron.

Torin paused at the first junction, a low archway that branched off to the left. His hand rested lightly on the pommel of his sword, not in threat, but in readiness. He listened. The rain drummed against the distant roof, a steady, rhythmic backdrop that masked any subtle sounds from deeper within the keep.

"Left," Torin said, his voice low and steady. "That passage leads down. It’s narrow, but it connects to the lower cellar levels. If we go that way, we’re moving away from the main guard post. It’s a longer route, but it’s quieter."

Elias glanced at the right-hand passage, which was wider and more direct. "And the right?"

"Direct," Torin replied. "Straight to the storage chamber. But it passes within twenty feet of Skrit’s room. If he has a lookout on the gallery above, they’ll see us before we even reach the door. And if they alarm, we’re trapped in a corridor with no cover."

Maren shifted her weight, a wince crossing her face as she adjusted the axle. "Longer route means more time in the dark. More chances for something to go wrong. But I agree with Torin. We don’t need a fight we can’t win yet. We need to be in position before we start it."

Elara crouched by the wall, her fingers trailing over the rough stone. She didn’t speak, but her silence was answer enough. She had seen the traps, the wards, the crude but effective defenses. She knew that speed was not the same as safety, and that in a place like this, patience was a weapon.

Torin turned back to the group, his gaze meeting Elias’s. "Here’s the plan," he said. "I’ll go first. I’ll map the route to the storage chamber and identify the best exit point for the captives. I’ll mark the path with chalk—subtle, but visible to us. Kaelen, you’ll provide light and cover. If anyone moves, you’ll know before I do. Maren, you’re on rear guard. If we’re cut off, you’re the one who ensures we get back out. Elara, you’re with me. You’ll check for traps and wards as we go. Elias, you’re with me too. We move together, we watch each other’s backs."

Elias nodded. "And if we find the captives?"

"Then we bring them back," Torin said. "But we do it quietly. No shouting, no dramatic entrances. We get them out, we get out ourselves, and we leave the goblins confused and angry, but not organized. That’s when we strike. Not before."

Maren let out a short breath, almost a laugh. "I like that. Let them be angry. Angry goblins make mistakes."

"Exactly," Torin said. "And we’ll be ready for them."

The group moved into the left-hand passage. The air grew colder, the stone walls damp with condensation that beaded on the surface like sweat. Torin led, his movements fluid and precise. He checked the floor with the toe of his boot before stepping, testing for pressure plates, loose stones, anything that might betray their presence. Elara followed closely, her quartz dust vial in hand, ready to test the air for magical residue.

The passage twisted and turned, leading them deeper into the keep. The light from Kaelen’s gauntlet flickered, casting long, dancing shadows that seemed to move independently of their source. The silence was oppressive, broken only by the soft scrape of their boots and the occasional creak of the stone settling under their weight.

After a few minutes, Torin stopped. He raised a hand, and the group froze. He pointed ahead, where the passage opened into a small, circular chamber. The walls were lined with shelves, mostly empty, but a few crates remained, stacked haphazardly. In the center of the room, a single torch burned in an iron sconce, its flame flickering in the draft that seeped in from the cracks in the stone.

"The storage chamber," Torin whispered. "It’s ahead. But we’re not the only ones here."

Elias followed his gaze. In the shadows beyond the torchlight, a figure stood. It was small, hunched, its eyes gleaming with a malevolent intelligence. A goblin. It held a crude spear, its tip pointed directly at the entrance of the passage.

It had been waiting for them.
