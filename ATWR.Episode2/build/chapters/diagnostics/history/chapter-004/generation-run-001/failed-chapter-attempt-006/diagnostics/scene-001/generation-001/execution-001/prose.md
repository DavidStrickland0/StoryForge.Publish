The corridor narrowed as they moved deeper into the keep, the stone walls pressing in until the air felt thick and heavy, saturated with the damp chill of the earth above. Kaelen walked second, his brass gauntlet held low at his side. The faint, pulsing amber light that had served them so well in the wider chambers was now a burden. In this confined space, even the dimmest glow cast long, sharp shadows that danced against the rough-hewn blocks, betraying their presence with every slight tremor of his hand.

He focused on the mechanism of the light, a mental exercise he had performed a dozen times before. *Less. Less light. Less energy.* The gauntlet hummed against his palm, a low vibration that traveled up his forearm and settled in his teeth. It was a physical sensation, a constant reminder of the limit he had hit. He could feel the reserves draining, not in a sudden rush, but in a slow, steady leak. He kept the output to the absolute minimum, enough to see the next three steps, no more. The light was a whisper now, barely distinguishable from the ambient darkness, a pale halo that seemed to shrink with each passing second.

Behind him, the sound of Maren’s breathing was a rhythmic counterpoint to the silence. It was controlled, deliberate, and deeply tiring. The broken cart axle strapped to her shoulder shifted with every step, a dull, grinding creak that she had learned to sync with her gait. It was a sound that should have been impossible to ignore in a place where silence was survival, but Maren had turned it into a metronome. *Step. Creak. Step. Creak.*

Elias walked at the rear, his back against the cold stone of the wall. He watched Maren’s profile, the way her jaw tightened with each adjustment of her weight. She was managing the pain, but he could see the effort it took. Her movements were precise, economical, but there was a stiffness to them that hadn’t been there an hour ago. The injury was not healing; it was settling, becoming a permanent fixture of her current state. She was carrying the weight of the group’s mobility, and the cost was written in the tension of her shoulders.

He wanted to say something. To tell her to slow down, or to ask if she needed a moment to rest. But he knew better. Maren did not rest. She endured. To offer help now would be to admit that the plan was failing, that the physical reality of their situation was outpacing their tactical advantage. So he said nothing. He simply adjusted his own pace, matching hers, ensuring that the distance between them remained constant.

Torin, at the front, moved with a fluidity that seemed to defy the cramped space. He did not bump into the walls, did not stumble over the uneven flagstones. His boots found purchase with a familiarity that spoke of years spent in places like this. He was a map of the keep, of the gaps and the shadows, of the places where the stone was solid and the places where it was loose.

They moved for what felt like an eternity, though time had become a relative concept in the dark. The corridor twisted, leading them down a slight incline. The air grew cooler, drier, the smell of damp earth giving way to the sharper, metallic tang of old iron and rust.

Kaelen’s gauntlet flickered. It was a subtle change, a dip in the intensity that he felt as a sudden drop in pressure in his chest. He steadied his hand, forcing the light to stabilize. The effort was disproportionate, a small adjustment that cost him more than it should have. He frowned, a line of concentration deepening between his brows. The magic was thinning, becoming brittle. He was spending his last reserves not on power, but on stability.

Maren stopped.

The sudden halt was sharp, a break in the rhythm of their movement. Elias braced himself against the wall, his hand tightening on the hilt of his sword. Torin froze, his body coiled, ready to move.

In the silence, the creak of the axle was the loudest sound in the world. It echoed off the walls, a clear, wooden complaint that seemed to hang in the air, waiting for a response.

Maren did not move. She did not speak. She simply stood there, her weight shifted to her good leg, her injured shoulder locked in a rigid, protective position. The pain had crossed a threshold. It was no longer a background noise; it was a screaming, bright thing that demanded her full attention.

Elias waited. He could feel the tension in the air, the fragile balance of their stealth. One wrong move, one sound, and the silence would shatter. He looked at Maren, his eyes searching for a sign, a nod, a shake of the head.

After a long moment, Maren exhaled. It was a slow, controlled breath, the kind used to steady the nerves before a jump. She adjusted the straps of the axle, a small, precise movement that she executed with a grimace of effort. Then, she took a step forward.

The creak came again, softer this time, muffled by the way she shifted her weight. She took another step. And another.

The rhythm returned. *Step. Creak. Step. Creak.*

Elias allowed himself a small exhale, his heart rate slowing. They had passed the point of immediate danger. The corridor opened slightly, the walls pulling back to reveal a wider space ahead. The light from Kaelen’s gauntlet, though dim, was enough to show the outline of the next section of the keep.

They had reached the midpoint. The physical constraints of their movement were now a shared reality, a dance of pain and precision that they had all learned to navigate. They were tired, they were hurting, and their resources were dwindling. But they were still moving. They were still together. And the path ahead, though dark and uncertain, was clear.
