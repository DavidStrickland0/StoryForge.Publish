Elias stepped back from the wall, his boots finding purchase on the smooth stone of the junction floor. The air here was colder than in the outer corridor, carrying a damp chill that seemed to settle deep into the bones of anyone standing still too long. He looked at Elara, who was already crouching by the base of the masonry where they had slipped through the fracture. Her lantern cast a wavering pool of light across the floor, revealing the faint, rhythmic pulse of the secondary trap mechanism beneath their feet.

"Torin," Elias said quietly, his voice low enough not to carry beyond the immediate circle of companions. "Keep your eyes on the corridor ahead. If we hear anything, you're first to react."

Torin nodded, his hand resting lightly on the pommel of his sword. He didn't speak, but the set of his jaw indicated that the order was accepted and executed simultaneously. His gaze swept over the darkened passage beyond the junction, scanning for any sign of movement or breath in the gloom. The silence felt heavy, broken only by the distant drumming of rain against the keep's roof and the soft creaking of Maren's cart axle as she shifted her weight.

Elara stood slowly, brushing dust from her knees as she handed Elias a small vial of loose quartz dust. "I found it," she said, pointing to a hairline crack in the floor near the junction of two walls. It was barely visible without the right lighting, but under Elara's lantern, it glowed with a faint, violet residue—similar to what they had seen at the gate, but far weaker. "This isn't just structural settling," she explained, her voice tight with focus. "It's a secondary ward. A mechanical trap disguised as a pressure plate. If someone steps on that spot without distributing their weight evenly, it releases."

Elias took the vial and nodded. "And if we distribute our weight?"

"Then it might not trigger," Elara said. "But there's also vibration sensitivity here. The keep itself is old stone, prone to settling, but this mechanism reacts to that kind of movement too. We need to move like ghosts."

Maren winced as she adjusted the broken cart axle strapped across her shoulders. The weight was already pulling at her injured shoulder and lower back; every shift sent a sharp sting down her spine. She didn't make a fuss about it, though her face betrayed a grimace that she tried to hide behind a look of professional stoicism.

"I can help," Maren said, her voice steady despite the pain. "If you're worried about weight distribution, I'll take point on this section. I'll carry the load for two steps before stepping down. If anyone else needs to cross, they do it light and quick." She paused, then added, "And if you need someone to check for loose stones or debris ahead, that's me too. Just don't ask me to sprint uphill with a broken axle while holding my breath."

Elias smiled faintly at her practicality. "We'll keep the group together," he said. "No running unless we have to. If anything moves, we stop and assess."

Kaelen stepped forward from the shadows of the corridor behind them, his brass gauntlet dimmed to a near-invisible pulse. His face was pale, sweat beading on his forehead despite the cold air. He hadn't spoken much since they entered the keep; most of his energy had been spent maintaining the smoke screen that concealed them from the goblin scouts.

"I've got enough left for one shield," Kaelen said quietly, his voice raspy with exhaustion. "Maybe two if I'm careful. But don't expect me to be much of a mage right now. My head feels like it's filled with bees." He glanced at Elara. "If you need me to disrupt any wards, do it fast. I can't afford to cast twice in a row without resting."

Elara nodded appreciatively. "I'll handle the ward detection for now," she said. "But if you need me to reinforce something or break a line, just ask. We're all here to support each other."

Kaelen gave a weak grin. "Just don't make me look like a wizard who forgot how to breathe. I'm trying my best."

Elias looked around the junction. The corridor ahead split into two paths: one leading deeper into the cellar levels, where the captives were held, and another that seemed to loop back toward the main guard post. The maps on the walls indicated that the loop was shorter but more exposed, while the deeper path was longer but safer if they moved quietly.

"We go straight," Elias decided. "Torin, you lead with Maren behind you. Elara checks for traps ahead of us. Kaelen, you're in the middle with me. Keep an eye on the walls and floor. If anything feels wrong, we stop immediately."

"Got it," Torin said, stepping forward into the darkened corridor. His movements were fluid and silent; he tested the floor with the toe of his boot before committing his full weight to a step. Maren followed close behind him, wincing slightly as she shifted her load but keeping pace without complaint.

As they moved, Elara crouched low, her lantern casting narrow beams across the path ahead. She swept the dust onto the floor in front of them, watching how it reacted to their footsteps and any ambient vibrations. When they reached a junction where the corridor branched off to the left, she held up a hand and motioned for them to stop.

"There's another ward here," she whispered, pointing to a faint shimmer in the air that rippled like disturbed water when Kaelen's magic brushed against it. "It's designed to mask movement within a specific radius. If we stay inside this zone and don't make sudden noises, we should be fine."

Kaelen nodded absently, his gauntlet dimming further as he monitored the magical signatures around them. "As long as we don't trigger anything physical," he muttered. "This place is full of stupid traps."

Maren rolled her eyes but kept moving. "Stupid traps don't scare me," she said dryly. "Just weird ones that don't make sense until you walk into them. Like this one." She gestured to the shimmering ward. "I bet there's something in here that thinks it's being clever, but it's just making mistakes because of bad design."

Elara chuckled softly. "You'd be surprised how many goblins think they're geniuses," she said. "They build traps based on what they've seen in books or heard from older raiders, not on actual experience. So we have a chance if we play our cards right."

They continued down the corridor, their footsteps muffled by layers of debris covering the floor. The air grew progressively colder as they ventured deeper into the keep, with occasional gusts of wind rattling loose shutters high above them. Torin led the way this time, scanning each corner for signs of movement or traps while Elara noted details about the ward patterns and their possible origins on her ever-present pad.

After several minutes of careful movement, they reached a second junction where the corridor branched again. This time, one path led directly down into the cellar levels, marked by a steep flight of stairs that descended into darkness. The other path looped back toward the main guard post, passing within twenty feet of Skrit's room.

"We go straight," Torin said, gesturing toward the downward path. "It's longer, but it avoids the guard post entirely. If we keep moving quietly, we shouldn't meet anyone until we reach the storage chamber."

"Agreed," Elias said. "But let's not rush. We're still in enemy territory, and every step counts."

Maren nodded solemnly. "And if we need to climb anything later, I'll be ready for it. Just don't expect me to jump over walls or scale cliffs while carrying this axle."

Elara laughed softly. "No worries," she said. "We'll find a way around any obstacles. And if you do need help climbing, just let us know."

With renewed determination, the party began their descent into the cellar levels. The stairs were narrow and uneven, lined with rusted railings that groaned under the weight of their boots. At the bottom, they found themselves in a small antechamber that led into the main storage chamber where Nella and Hobb were being held.

The room ahead was dimly lit by flickering torches mounted along the walls. Stacks of looted goods—grain sacks, metal tools, farming implements—lined one side, while rough-hewn tables and crates occupied the center of the space. Maps pinned to the walls displayed routes between farms and villages, marking paths taken by goblin raiders over recent weeks. Arrows pointed toward neighboring settlements like Pinehollow itself, while other markers indicated future targets far off in the Blackwood Valley region.

"They're planning bigger raids soon," Elara murmured, tracing a line on one of the maps with her gloved finger. "If they hit Blackwood Valley next, that'll mean we've got less time to prepare for them returning here."

"We can't afford to let them win," Torin said quietly, his voice tight as he stepped forward to join the group. "We need to stop them before they grow too confident."

"And how do we do that?" Elias asked, turning back toward Maren, who was already inspecting the heavy wooden door leading further into the keep. "We don't have an army at our disposal. Just five people and a few spells between us."

Maren paused in front of the door, placing one hand on its cold surface to feel for vibrations or signs of recent activity. Her expression remained grim but focused as she shook her head slightly. "That's exactly why we're here. To show them that their little band of raiders isn't invincible. If we take out their leader and secure those captives, they'll scatter and stop raiding for a while."

"Unless someone takes charge," Torin added dryly, glancing at Elias with a faint grin. "Because if you're going to lead us straight into trouble again, I hope you've got a plan ready."

Elias chuckled softly, shaking his head as he followed Maren toward the door. "I always have a plan. It's just that sometimes it doesn't work out exactly as intended."

"That's why we need backup," Maren replied, pushing open the heavy wooden door and stepping into the next chamber beyond. The air inside was colder still, with a faint smell of blood and fear clinging to the stone walls. "Come on. Let's find these people before it gets worse."

Kaelen followed close behind, his gauntlet pulsing softly now, maintaining its steady ring of golden light as he monitored the ward readings alongside Elara. "Just remember," he muttered under his breath, "you only get one chance to pull this off cleanly."

Elias nodded again, considering their options. "You're right, Kaelen. But we've come too far to turn back now. Besides, you're counting on me to keep everyone safe."

Torin rolled his eyes slightly but said nothing further. Instead, he gestured toward another group of maps spread across a nearby table near the center of the room. Those papers contained detailed plans showing troop deployments, patrol schedules, and even notes about food supplies being transported daily from outside sources. One particular section depicted routes leading directly into Blackwood Valley, complete with estimated travel times based on terrain conditions and weather forecasts drawn up weeks earlier than that.

"See?" Elara pointed excitedly at a specific annotation scribbled in red ink near the bottom margin. "They're planning bigger raids soon. If we don't stop them here, they'll be coming straight for Pinehollow within days."

Maren stared silently at the map for several moments before nodding slowly. "Then we'd better hurry up." She turned to Elias once more. "What's your call on this? Do we stick with the original plan or think of something else?"
