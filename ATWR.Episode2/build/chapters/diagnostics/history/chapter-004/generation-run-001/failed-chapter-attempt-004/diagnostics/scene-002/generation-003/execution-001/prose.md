The corridor ahead was a throat of cold stone, narrowing as it approached the junction where the right-hand passage opened into a wider, more exposed section. The air here was stale, thick with the scent of damp moss and old iron. As they rounded the bend, the light from Kaelen’s gauntlet, restricted to a narrow, steady beam, illuminated a crude assembly hanging from the ceiling by a frayed rope.

It was an alarm. Or rather, a collection of alarms. A cluster of hollowed-out gourds, strung together with wire, hung directly above the path. Each gourd was pierced with tiny holes, and inside, small metal rings clattered softly against one another. A single tripwire, barely visible in the dim light, stretched across the width of the passage, connecting the gourd cluster to a weighted lever on the floor. It was a simple, mechanical thing, but effective. Step on the wire, or even brush against it, and the weight would drop, triggering the clatter. The noise would carry for miles in the silence of the keep.

Elias stopped, raising a hand. The group froze. Maren leaned heavily against the wall, her face pale, her breath coming in short, shallow bursts. Kaelen’s gauntlet flickered, the light dimming by a fraction, a subtle sign of his exhaustion.

"Look at that," Elias murmured, his voice barely audible. "Gourds and wire. If we step on that line, the whole keep knows we're here. And if we cut it, the weight drops, and the noise is just as bad."

Torin stepped forward, his eyes narrowing. "We can't go around it. The passage is too narrow, and the wall is solid stone. We can't climb over a weighted lever without making noise."

"Then we don't go around it," Elias said, his tone calm but firm. "We disable it. Quietly."

He turned to Maren. "Maren, your hands are steady. You know how these things work. Can you reach the wire without triggering the lever?"

Maren looked at the assembly, her jaw tightening. She ignored the pain that shot through her shoulder, the ache that threatened to bring her to her knees. "I can reach it," she said, her voice steady. "But I need someone to hold the light steady. If the beam moves, I lose focus."

Kaelen stepped forward, his face grim. "I can hold it," he said, his voice strained. "But I can't hold it long. And I can't cast anything else. If I focus on the light, I have nothing left for a shield."

"Then we work fast," Elias said. "Maren, you disable the trigger. Kaelen, you provide the light. Torin, you cover the entrance. If anything moves, you signal, and we freeze. Elara, stay back. Keep an eye on the floor for any secondary mechanisms."

Elara nodded, her eyes scanning the ground. She spotted a faint discoloration in the stone, a patch of wear that suggested a trap or a loose flagstone. She marked it mentally, her mind already calculating the angle of approach.

Maren moved forward, her steps slow and deliberate. She approached the wire, her hand reaching out. The wire was taut, humming faintly with the tension of the weight. She needed to cut it, but not so close to the lever that the weight would drop. She needed to cut it high, near the gourd cluster, where the wire was less taut and the weight was less likely to trigger.

She looked at Elias, then at Kaelen. "Give me the light," she said.

Kaelen nodded, his gauntlet flickering but holding steady. The beam of light, narrow and focused, illuminated the wire and the gourd cluster. Maren’s hand moved, her fingers finding a small blade in her belt. She held her breath, her eyes fixed on the wire.

The moment stretched, taut as the wire itself. Maren’s blade touched the wire. She cut.

The wire snapped, but the weight did not drop. Instead, the gourd cluster swung slightly, the metal rings inside clattering softly. The sound was faint, barely a whisper, but it was there.

Maren let out a breath she hadn’t realized she was holding. "Done," she said.

Torin relaxed slightly, his hand still resting on his sword hilt. "Good work," he said. "That was close."

Elias looked at the disabled alarm, then at the path ahead. The way was clear. They had neutralized the threat without alerting the goblins. It was a small victory, but it was a victory nonetheless.

"Keep moving," Elias said. "We’re not done yet."

The group continued forward, their footsteps muffled by the damp stone. The air grew colder, the scent of fear and iron growing stronger. They were closer now. The storage chamber was just ahead. And beyond it, Nella and Hobb were waiting.

Elias glanced back at Maren, who was leaning against the wall, her face pale but her eyes determined. He wanted to tell her to rest, to take a moment, but he knew she wouldn’t. She was a hammer, and hammers didn’t rest until the work was done.

Kaelen’s gauntlet flickered again, the light dimming. He was running out of time, out of energy. But he held the light steady, his face a mask of concentration.

Elara moved beside Elias, her eyes sharp. "There," she said, pointing to a faint draft coming from a crack in the wall. "It’s coming from inside. Someone is in there."

Elias nodded. They were close. Too close.

"We need a plan," he said. "Quietly."

The group gathered, their faces illuminated by the faint, flickering light of Kaelen’s gauntlet. The air was thick with tension, with the weight of what was to come.

Maren straightened, her hand resting on her hammer. "I can hold the door," she said. "Keep them out while you get the captives."

Elias looked at her, then at Kaelen. "Kaelen, you’re with me. We go in. Torin, cover the rear. Elara, watch for traps."

They moved into the storage chamber, their hearts pounding, their breath held. The door was heavy, iron-bound, and locked. But they had the tools, the skills, and the will to break it open.

And beyond that door, Nella and Hobb were waiting.
