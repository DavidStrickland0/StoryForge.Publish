The corridor narrowed, the stone walls pressing in like the ribcage of some ancient beast that had long since gone cold. Torin led with a careful rhythm, his boots finding purchase on the uneven flagstones without the squeak of fresh leather or the slip of wet grit. He moved low, keeping his head down, eyes scanning the junction where the corridor split into three directions: left down to the cellar levels, right toward the storage hub they had just passed, and straight ahead into a blind wall that seemed to offer no passage at all.

Maren followed in the middle of the formation, her weight shifting constantly as she bore the broken cart axle strapped across her shoulders. The wood groaned under the strain, a dull, wooden creak that sounded less like a tool failing and more like a limb snapping under stress. She kept her eyes forward, jaw tight, refusing to let pain dictate her pace unless it became too much. When Elias glanced at her, she didn't acknowledge it with a grimace or a complaint; she simply adjusted the straps and kept walking.

Elias brought up the rear, his sword sheathed but his hand resting lightly on the hilt. He wasn't looking back at them, though. His eyes were fixed on the floor, on the faint patches of dust that had been disturbed by the passage of time and the intrusion of their own boots. The lantern light from Torin’s belt cast long shadows ahead, illuminating the first few feet of the junction before fading into darkness.

“Wait,” Elias murmured, stopping just short of the junction. He raised a hand slightly, a gesture that didn’t stop anyone but signaled caution. “Elara, check the floor.”

Elara was already stepping forward before he finished speaking. She crouched beside Torin’s boots, squinting into the gloom where the torchlight ended and the true darkness began. Her fingers trailed along the stone, picking up small grains of dirt and debris to examine. She didn’t speak immediately, her concentration absolute, as if the silence itself was part of the test.

There were no visible traps—no tripwires, no loose stones that seemed ready to give way with a step, no pressure plates marked by glowing runes or strange symbols. But Elara’s expression shifted subtly. Her eyes narrowed, and she tapped a specific spot near the junction wall with her knuckle. The sound was hollow, barely audible over the rain outside, but it carried a rhythm that didn’t match the random settling of old stone.

She stood slowly, brushing dirt from her hands. “This isn’t just loose masonry,” she said quietly. “There’s something beneath this layer.” She gestured to the floor. “If we step here, or over here, or right there—we trigger a mechanism that I can’t see from above.”

Torin glanced at her, then down at the floor where she’d pointed. His eyes were sharp, scanning for anything out of place—cracks in the stone, discoloration, residue. “You mean it’s a secondary ward? Or something mechanical?”

“Both,” Elara said. “This keep was built to survive fire and siege, not just theft. But someone wants to make sure we don’t slip past without paying a price. There’s a hidden latch under this slab. It’s connected to a pulley system that drops beams from above.” She stepped forward again, pressing her hand flat against the floor near the junction point. “It’s designed to release when weight is applied unevenly. If we cross the threshold with one foot heavier than the other, it trips.”

Maren grunted, shifting her weight off the axle and onto her good leg. “So we walk evenly? Like ballet dancers?”

“Not quite,” Elara replied, glancing at her with a faint smile despite the tension in the air. “We need to distribute our weight carefully. And if possible, avoid stepping on that exact spot altogether.” She pointed to a patch of stone near the junction wall where the flagstones had been patched together centuries ago. “But even walking evenly won’t guarantee safety if the mechanism activates on slight vibrations or sound. This one’s rigged for both.”

Torin nodded once, then moved back to flank Elara, his eyes scanning the walls above for anything that might reveal a hidden pulley or trigger mechanism. Elias stepped up beside her, examining the floor himself with practiced precision. He reached into his pack and pulled out a small vial of quartz dust, pouring a thin line across the junction where she’d indicated. The dust glowed faintly blue under Kaelen’s residual magical field, spreading outward to reveal faint traces of energy residue embedded in the stone itself.

“No magic here,” Elias noted, watching as the dust settled into the cracks and crevices of the floor. “Just raw engineering.” He looked up at Torin. “Can you cut through the wall?”

Torin hesitated. “It’s solid stone. No weak points that I can see.”

“Then we go around,” Elias decided. “We don’t force it open. We find a gap in the architecture that doesn’t involve breaking anything.” He turned to Maren. “Can you hold the axle steady while I check for an alternate route?”

Maren nodded once, bracing herself against the wall with her good arm. “Give me a minute.” She closed her eyes briefly, letting her body settle into stillness, conserving strength until Elias returned. “I’ll keep my weight even. Don’t worry about me holding up the group; I know how to adjust for this kind of thing.”

Elias moved forward, crouching low and feeling along the base of the wall with his hands. He traced the mortar lines between stones, looking for any that had been disturbed or weakened over time. After a moment, he found what he was looking for—a hairline fracture running along the seam where two large blocks met. It wasn’t obvious from a distance, but up close it revealed itself as a natural weakness in the masonry.

“Perfect,” he muttered, rising to his feet and gesturing toward the crack. “We don’t break anything. We just slip through here.” He looked at Elara. “Help me clear a bit of debris so we can get through without triggering anything.”

Elara nodded, kneeling beside him and using her dagger to scrape away loose dust and small stones from the seam. She worked quickly, careful not to disturb anything else in the process. After a few moments, enough space had been cleared for them to squeeze through. They lowered themselves slowly, inching forward along the narrow gap until they reached the other side of the junction without making any sudden movements or sounds.

Behind them, the corridor remained sealed, untouched by their passage ahead. The trap had been bypassed—not destroyed, but avoided entirely. A clever use of observation and patience, not brute force or magic.

Torin gave a brief nod of approval as they emerged on the other side. “Good work,” he said, his voice low. “That was tighter than I expected.”

Elias straightened up, brushing dust from his knees. “Not exactly what we planned,” he admitted with a faint smile. “But it works.” He looked at Maren. “How’s your shoulder?”

Maren adjusted the axle again, wincing slightly as she moved. “Still sore, but manageable. Just don’t ask me to carry anything heavier than usual.” She looked at Elara. “You found something else, didn’t you?”

Elara nodded. “There’s a secondary ward down here that we’ll need to handle carefully next.” She glanced ahead toward the darkened corridor beyond the junction. “But for now, we’re clear.”
