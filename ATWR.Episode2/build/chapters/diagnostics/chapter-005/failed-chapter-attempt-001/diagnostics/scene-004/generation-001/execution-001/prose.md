The rain had stopped, leaving the world outside slick and silent, but inside the keep, the air was thick with the damp smell of wet stone and the sharper, metallic scent of fear. Elias turned his back on Skrit, a gesture that felt heavier than it should have. The goblin stood frozen in the center of the room, his hands still raised, his yellow eyes wide and unblinking. He looked less like a chief of raiders and more like a boy who had been caught stealing apples from an orchard and was waiting for the farmer’s son to come out with a stick.

Elias didn’t look back. He walked to the door, where Torin stood with Hobb slumped against his side, the cart driver’s head lolling dangerously close to the floor. Torin’s face was a mask of grim endurance, his jaw tight, a sheen of sweat visible on his forehead despite the chill in the room. He looked ready to drop, but he didn’t.

"Can you move him?" Elias asked, his voice low.

Torin shifted his weight, wincing slightly as the old injury in his shoulder flared with a familiar, grinding pain. "I can carry him a little further," he said. "But if we go up the stairs, I’m done. I don’t want to drop him, and I don’t want to drop myself."

Maren stepped forward, her boots scuffing against the stone. She was supporting Nella, who was leaning heavily on her, her face pale and drawn. Nella’s eyes were wide, fixed on the door as if she expected the goblins to vanish into the shadows the moment she looked away.

"We need a stretcher," Maren said. It wasn’t a question. It was a statement of fact, delivered with the blunt pragmatism that Elias had come to rely on when his own plans started to fray at the edges.

"There’s no time to build one," Torin said, though his voice lacked its usual conviction.

"Then we use what’s here," Maren replied. She looked at the pile of stolen grain sacks in the corner, then at the length of rough rope coiled nearby. "Elara, can you find something we can lash them together? And Kaelen, I need you to keep that goblin from having a heart attack. If he screams, we’re all in trouble."

Kaelen, who had been leaning against the doorframe, straightened up. He looked exhausted, his face pale and drawn, but there was a flicker of dry humor in his eyes. "I can do that," he said. "I’m quite good at being terrifying when I’m not tired. It’s a rare skill, but I have it."

Elias allowed himself a small, tight smile. It was the first time he had felt something other than tension in the last hour. "Stay with him," Elias said to Kaelen. "Make sure he doesn’t try anything clever. And if he does, remind him that we have a hammer that is significantly larger than his entire body."

Kaelen nodded, a ghost of a grin playing on his lips. "Understood. I’ll be the bad guy. It’s a role I’ve always wanted to try."

Elias turned back to Maren and Torin. "We need to move. The guard is gone, but the others are out there. We don’t know how long it will take them to realize what happened. We need to be out of this keep before they get their heads back."

Maren was already moving, her hands moving with practiced efficiency as she began to untangle the rope. "Torin, help me with the sacks. We’ll make a frame. It won’t be comfortable, but it’ll work."

Torin nodded, moving to join her. He didn’t speak, but his movements were sharp and decisive, the pain in his shoulder clearly being ignored in favor of the task at hand. Elara crouched beside them, her fingers working quickly to secure the knots, her face set in a mask of concentration.

Elias watched them work, a sense of relief washing over him, though it was tempered by the knowledge that they were not yet safe. The goblins were still out there, still dangerous, still waiting for them to make a mistake. And they all knew that mistakes were a matter of time, not probability.

But for now, they were alive. And they had a plan.

"Ready?" Maren asked, her voice steady.

Elias looked at the makeshift stretcher, then at the captives. Nella was staring at him, her eyes wide and unseeing. Hobb was unconscious, his breathing shallow but steady.

"Ready," Elias said.

They moved out of the room, the heavy door closing behind them with a dull thud. The corridor was dark, the only light coming from the faint, pulsing glow of Kaelen’s gauntlet, which he kept low to the ground.

As they walked, Elias heard the soft scrape of boots on stone, the rustle of cloth, and the quiet, focused intensity of five people who understood that their survival depended on each other’s competence.

They were not out of danger. The keep was still full of goblins, full of traps, full of secrets. But for the first time since they had entered the keep, they had a clear objective.

They had the people.

And that was enough.
