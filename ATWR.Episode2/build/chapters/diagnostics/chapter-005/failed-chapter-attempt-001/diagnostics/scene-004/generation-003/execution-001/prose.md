The silence that followed Skrit’s question was not empty, but heavy with the immediate, unglamorous reality of their situation. The goblin waited for a command, his yellow eyes darting between the open door and the group of armed, unimpressed humans. He was a scavenger who had found a stick and believed it made him dangerous, and now that the stick had been kicked out of reach, he was simply a small, frightened creature in a room that suddenly felt very large.

Elias didn’t answer. He didn’t need to. The air in the chamber shifted as the tactical weight of the confrontation dissolved into the logistical nightmare of extraction.

Torin let out a long, controlled breath, the sound whistling through his teeth as he released Hobb. The cart driver slumped against the cold stone floor, his unconscious body a heavy, limp burden that Torin seemed to have been holding up purely by will and the rigid lock of his joints. For a moment, the soldier stood over the man, his right hand hovering near his shoulder, a subtle, protective gesture that was quickly suppressed. He straightened, his spine cracking audibly in the quiet room, and rubbed the back of his neck.

"He's stable," Torin said, his voice rough. "Elara’s water did its job. But I’m not carrying him out a stairwell. Not with this." He touched his right shoulder, a brief, grimace crossing his face that he hid with a casual shrug. "And I’m not risking dropping him on the way down because I pull a muscle."

Maren didn’t look up from Nella, who was trembling violently against her shoulder. The blacksmith’s apprentice had been leaning on Maren for support, her weight significant against the soldier's already compromised posture. Maren adjusted her grip, her knuckles white around Nella’s arm, the broken cart axle strapped to her back digging into her bruised shoulder with every small adjustment.

"We need to reorganize," Maren said, her tone flat and practical. She didn't ask for help; she stated a fact. "Torin, you’re the strongest, but you’re hurt. I can carry the girl, but I’m limping. If we run into something—something that moves—my reaction time is gone. I’m a stationary target with a bad back."

Elara stepped forward, her satchel clinking softly as she pulled out a strip of waxed linen. She looked at the door, where the fleeing goblin had vanished into the darkness of the corridor. "The perimeter is open," she observed, her voice low. "He ran. He’ll come back. Or he’ll find others. We don’t have time to argue about who carries whom while we’re standing in the middle of his room."

Elias moved to the center of the group, his hand resting on the pommel of his sword, not in threat, but in readiness. He looked at the five of them—the weary mage leaning against the wall, the injured soldier, the limping hammer-wielder, the scholar, and himself. They were a broken machine that had just managed to stop a clock, and now the clock had to be unwound.

"Kaelen," Elias said.

The mage straightened. He looked pale, his forehead beaded with sweat that had nothing to do with the heat of the room. He had used his last spark to distract the guard, and the cost was a dull ache behind his eyes that felt like a physical weight.

"I'm here," Kaelen said, his voice thin.

"Can you make noise?" Elias asked. "Not magic. Just... physics. A stone. A coin. Something that sounds like a body hitting the floor."

Kaelen blinked. "I can throw a rock with a bit of force. I can make it sound like a goblin falling. It will draw their attention to the far end of the corridor. If they investigate, we have a window. But if I’m wrong, if the sound doesn't travel right, we’re sitting ducks."

"It’s a gamble," Torin said.

"It’s a plan," Elias corrected gently. "And it’s the one we have. Kaelen, find a stone. Maren, check your pockets. Elara, mark the route on the parchment. Torin, ready Hobb for movement. Maren, get Nella on her feet."

The group moved with a synchronized efficiency that masked their exhaustion. There was no shouting, no frantic gesturing. There was only the soft scrape of boots, the rustle of cloth, and the quiet, focused intensity of five people who understood that their survival depended on each other’s competence.

Elias watched them work. He saw Kaelen’s hand steady as he picked up a loose flagstone from the floor. He saw Maren’s jaw tighten as she helped Nella stand, the blacksmith gripping her forearm for balance. He saw Elara’s charcoal scratching rapidly on the parchment, mapping the dark. And he saw Torin, who was already lifting Hobb’s upper body, distributing the weight across his good arm and his back, his face a mask of disciplined endurance.

They were not out of danger. The keep was still full of goblins, full of traps, full of secrets. But for the first time since they had entered the keep, they had a clear objective. They had the people.

And that was enough.
