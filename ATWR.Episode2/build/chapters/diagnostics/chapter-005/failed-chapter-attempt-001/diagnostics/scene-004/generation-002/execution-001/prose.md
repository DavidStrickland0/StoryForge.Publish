He waited until the silence in the room settled into a specific, heavy kind of quiet. The kind that follows a decision. The door was open behind him, but the goblin who had been standing there was gone. The hallway beyond was dark, the only movement a faint ripple in the air where the creature had fled.

"Torin," Elias said, his voice steady despite the tremor in his own hands. "Check the hallway. Make sure he’s gone. Make sure no one else is coming back."

Torin moved to the doorway first. He didn’t look at Skrit, who remained standing in the center of the room, his hands still raised, his eyes wide and unblinking. Torin stepped out, his sword drawn, his body angled to cover the narrow passage. He moved with a caution that belied his size, his weight shifting carefully to spare the old injury in his right shoulder.

"Clear," Torin whispered from the corridor. "He ran. Fast. I can hear the other goblins shouting in the distance. They haven't come yet."

Elias turned back to the group. The room was small, cluttered with the detritus of goblin leadership: a pile of stolen coins, a few jars of preserved fruit, a map pinned to the wall with rough nails. It was a far cry from the grandeur of a lord’s keep, but it was enough to sustain a band of raiders. And it was enough to make the next few minutes feel like the hardest part of the entire night.

"Maren," Elias said. "How is Nella?"

"She’s shaking," Maren replied, her voice low and practical. She was standing beside the blacksmith, her hand resting on Nella’s shoulder, a steady, anchoring weight. "She’s in shock. Her body is trying to process the last few days all at once. But she’s conscious. She’s aware. That’s good."

Elias nodded. He looked at Hobb, who lay on the stone floor, his breathing shallow but rhythmic. The water of life had done its job; his heart was steady, his color returning. But his leg was still broken, a jagged angle that spoke of pain that no amount of magic could fix.

"Torin," Elias said. "Can you carry him?"

Torin turned back into the room. He looked at Hobb, then at his own shoulder, then at Elias. The soldier’s jaw tightened, a muscle jumping in his cheek. "I can," he said. It was a statement of fact, not a question. "But I need a moment. And I need to know the route is clear."

"It is clear," Elias said. "For now."

"And Maren?" Torin added. "She’s carrying the axle. She can’t carry two people."

Maren stepped forward, her expression hard. "I can carry one. But I’m not strong enough to carry two. And I’m not fast enough to run with two. So we move slow. We move together. Or we don’t move at all."

Elias looked at Kaelen, who was leaning against the wall, his face pale, his eyes closed. The mage looked exhausted, his body still trembling from the effort of maintaining their cover.

"Kaelen," Elias said. "Can you do anything? A small spell? A light?"

Kaelen opened his eyes. They were sharp, but tired. "No," he said. "I’m empty. I have nothing left. Not even a spark."

Elias nodded. He had expected that. He turned back to the group.

"Here’s what we do," Elias said. "Torin, you carry Hobb. Maren, you support Nella. Kaelen and Elara, you stay in the middle. If anything moves, you freeze. If anything attacks, Torin deals with it. Maren, you cover the rear. Kaelen, you watch the exits. Elara, you listen."

He paused, looking at each of them in turn.

"We move slowly. We move quietly. And we do not stop until we are out of this keep."

Torin nodded. He moved to Hobb, crouching down to assess the weight. He wrapped his arms around the cart driver’s torso, lifting him with a grunt of effort that was more sound than pain. Hobb groaned, but did not wake.

Maren helped Nella to her feet. The blacksmith swayed, her legs unsteady, but she kept her chin up. She looked at Elias, her eyes red-rimmed and fierce. "Is it over?" she whispered.

"Almost," Elias said gently. "Can you stand?"

Nella nodded, but her hand trembled as she pushed against the cold stone. She managed to rise, swaying slightly. She looked at Hobb, then back at Elias. "He's hurt. Badly."

Elias turned to Kaelen and Elara, who were standing by the door, watching. "Kaelen," Elias said quietly. "Can you do anything for him? A minor mending? A painkiller?"

Kaelen shook his head slowly. His face was pale, a sheen of sweat visible on his forehead despite the cold air of the keep. "My reserves are gone, Elias. I have enough for a shield if something attacks us. If I try to heal a bone or close a wound, I might pass out. Or worse, I might not be able to stop the bleeding."

Elara stepped forward, withdrawing a small vial from her satchel. The liquid inside was clear, pulsing with a faint, internal glow. "Water of life," she said, her voice tight with urgency. "It's not a cure. It's a stabilizer. It will slow the shock and keep his heart steady, but it won't fix the leg. We need to get him out. Now."
