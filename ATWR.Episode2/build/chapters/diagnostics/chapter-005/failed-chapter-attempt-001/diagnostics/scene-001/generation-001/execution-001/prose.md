Kaelen did not have a coin. He had a handful of loose gravel, dislodged from the flagstones by Torin’s recent struggle, and a scrap of dried meat rind that he had been saving for a snack that now felt like a distant, theoretical luxury.

He crouched near the mouth of the corridor, his fingers trembling slightly as he selected a pebble the size of a walnut. It was grey, rough, and perfectly ordinary. It was also the only thing he had left to offer the darkness.

"Ready?" he whispered.

Elias nodded. His eyes were fixed on the far end of the passage, where the shouts of the panicking goblins were growing louder, a chaotic chorus of fear and confusion. The sound was a shield, a blanket of noise that would cover their own movements if they timed it correctly.

"Go," Elias said.

Kaelen stood, his legs stiff from the cold, and walked to the edge of the visible light. He looked up. The ceiling was high, lost in the gloom. He tossed the pebble.

For a heartbeat, the shouting in the center of the keep ceased. The silence was heavy, expectant. Then, the sound of shuffling feet, the clatter of dropped weapons, and the high-pitched yelp of goblins rushing toward the disturbance.

"Now," Elias breathed.

The group moved.

They did not run. Running was a mistake that invited injury and noise. Instead, they flowed. Torin, with Hobb slung over his back, moved first, his body a study in controlled effort. Each step was a negotiation with the pain in his shoulder, a silent plea for endurance that he answered with sheer will. Hobb groaned, a low, rhythmic sound that matched the cadence of Torin’s gait.

Maren followed, her hand on Nella’s elbow. The blacksmith was a bundle of nerves, her eyes wide and unseeing, but she was walking. She was *here*. That was enough for now.

Elias and Elara moved in the center, their boots silent on the wet stone. Kaelen brought up the rear, his heart hammering against his ribs, his mind already calculating the distance to the next turn, the next obstacle, the next breath.

The corridor narrowed again. The air grew thicker, heavier with the scent of old damp and something organic, something that had been here for a long time. The goblins’ shouts faded behind them, replaced by the echo of their own breathing.

They reached the junction.

To the left, the passage opened into a wider space, the upper level of the keep. The air here was different. It was colder, drier, and carried a faint, metallic tang. To the right, the corridor continued downward, deeper into the keep’s bowels.

Elias stopped.

"Which way?" he asked.

Elara stepped forward, her charcoal in hand. She had been sketching the route as they moved, a map of their progress drawn in the dark. She pointed to the left.

"Up," she said. "The goblins are moving away from us. They are investigating the noise. If we go down, we risk running into them. If we go up, we are moving toward the exit, and toward the light."

"Light," Maren said, her voice dry. "We don't have any."

"No," Elias agreed. "But we have eyes. And we have each other."

He looked at Torin. The soldier’s face was pale, his jaw clenched, but his eyes were steady. He had carried Hobb for a hundred yards, and he was still standing. He was still *here*.

"Can you keep going?" Elias asked.

Torin shifted Hobb’s weight, a small, painful adjustment. "I can keep going," he said.

They turned left.

The passage led upward, a steep incline of uneven stone steps. It was a climb that would have been easy for a fresh man. For Torin, it was a test. For Maren, with the axle strapped to her shoulder and the weight of Nella in her arms, it was a struggle.

They climbed.

One step at a time.

The silence was absolute. The only sounds were their breathing, the creak of leather, the soft scrape of stone on stone. The world outside was rain and dark and chaos. The world inside was stone and breath and determination.

They reached the top.

The upper level was a large, circular chamber. It was empty, save for a single, broken table and a few scattered crates. The walls were lined with hooks, most of them empty, but a few still held rusted tools, broken shields, and tattered cloaks.

In the center of the room, a door stood slightly ajar.

It was a heavy, iron-banded door, similar to the one they had passed in the cellar. But this one was different. It was locked. And it was guarded.

A single goblin stood before it. He was larger than the others, his skin a mottled grey-green, his eyes hard and sharp. He held a spear in one hand and a crude club in the other. He was not panicking. He was waiting.

Elias raised a hand, signaling for the group to stop.

They froze.

The goblin looked at them. His eyes were not wide with fear. They were cold, calculating, and utterly devoid of mercy.

He knew they were there.

He had always known.

And he was waiting for them to make a mistake.
