"See that?" Elias whispered, tapping the air near the chains. "One foot, one slip, and the whole thing goes off." He glanced at Kaelen. "Mage or no mage, you don't want to walk through there unless you can make sure we're not making noise before we even touch the stone."

Kaelen nodded, though his expression remained unreadable. He reached into his pack and withdrew a small vial of smokeless powder he had mixed earlier that day. "I can keep us quiet for a bit, but it won't last forever. If they hear us coming through the gate, they'll see us as an easy target."

"Then we don't go through the gate," Elias said, stepping back and scanning the perimeter of the ruined structure. "We look for another way in. Maren, can you tell me what you saw when you checked the outer wall?"

Maren crouched near the base of the crumbling stone, her hammer resting against her shoulder. She had already inspected the broken sections earlier and now nodded without hesitation. "There's a gap on the left side, about twenty feet up. The stones are loose enough that I could probably climb it if I'm careful. But Torin's right—there might be a trap or someone watching from above."

Torin shifted his weight from one foot to the other, keeping his gaze fixed on the dark interior of the keep where faint glimmers of light danced between shadows. "I don't know about traps, but if there's an ambush waiting for us, we'll find out soon enough. We've got no choice but to move forward."

Elara adjusted her satchel and stepped closer to the wall, examining the cracks in the masonry with practiced precision. "This place has seen better days," she murmured. "The goblins must have been using it as a base for some time now. Look at these claw marks—fresh ones, too. They've been here recently." She pulled out her chisel and carefully scraped away a thin layer of dirt from the stone near one of the gaps. Beneath it lay faint traces of oil slicks mixed with ash. "And this... this suggests they're using something flammable inside. Maybe torches, maybe even explosives."

Elias frowned as he studied the evidence Elara had gathered. "Explosives? That's not normal goblin behavior unless they've got someone helping them out." He turned to face the group. "We need to figure out who—or what—they're working with before we make our move. If there's a bigger threat involved, then we might be walking into something far worse than just a few raiders."

Maren grunted in agreement, though she didn't elaborate further. Her focus remained on the task at hand: finding a safe path into the keep without alerting the goblins inside. "Let's try the gap first," she said quietly. "If we can get up there and scout the area, we'll have a better idea of what we're dealing with."

"Here," he said, handing it over to Elias. "Use this if you need to cover our tracks or create a diversion. It won't last long, but it should give us enough time to slip past unnoticed."

Elias took the scroll and tucked it safely into his belt. "Thanks, Kaelen. I'll keep it handy until we decide whether we really need it." With that, he turned back toward the wall where Maren was preparing to ascend.

The climb proved more challenging than anyone anticipated. Torin stayed close behind, offering support whenever needed and watching for any sign of danger lurking in the shadows above them. Once they reached the top of the wall, the group found themselves overlooking a small courtyard filled with stolen goods: sacks of grain, bundles of cloth, and various other items piled haphazardly around a central fire pit.

Inside the keep itself, the air smelled of smoke and damp earth. Through the broken windows, faint outlines of figures could be seen moving about inside, tending to fires or organizing supplies. Elias signaled for silence once more as the group moved cautiously toward the entrance. As they neared the threshold, Elara whispered, "If those are goblins, they're definitely amateurs at this whole setup thing."

"We'll see," Maren replied, her voice barely audible over the sound of wind blowing through the ruins. "Just stay close and follow my lead."
