As they approached the entrance to the keep, Elias signaled for everyone to stop and check their positions before proceeding any further. They paused just outside the main gate, where a crude alarm system hung suspended from chains and pulleys above the doorway. It was obvious that anyone attempting entry would trigger it immediately, alerting goblins inside who were likely preparing to defend their haul or count their spoils. The air here smelled of damp earth, woodsmoke, and the sharp tang of wet wool.

Elias raised a gloved hand toward the mechanism, his fingers twitching slightly—not out of fear, but because he knew exactly how these things worked; they were simple enough to dismantle with a hammer strike or clumsy enough to trigger on any sudden vibration. He looked down at Maren, who was already crouching low beside the muddy gate, her heavy hammer resting against her thigh with the practiced ease of someone who had spent years learning that silence moved faster than steel.

“Keep your eyes up,” Elias said quietly, though his tone carried none of the panic it might have from a younger adventurer. “That thing’s connected to a bell inside. If one of us bumps a chain or steps on a loose stone, they’ll hear it before we’re even within ten paces.” He glanced toward Torin, who stood near the far edge of the clearing, scanning the tree line where shadows shifted like breathing things. Torin gave a single nod, his sword held in a relaxed but ready grip, watching the way the wind carried scent from deeper into the woods—woodsmoke, rotting leaves, and something sharper, metallic, that made Elias’s stomach tighten with recognition.

Kaelen stepped forward, raising one hand palm-outward, a small circle of soft golden light forming between his fingers before fading as he lowered it again. “No need for that,” he murmured, though he didn’t explain why the faint shimmer in the air around them wasn’t attracting attention. He knew better than to waste magic on something so mundane; even a little spell could be useful later if they needed to mask their presence or blur an outline in the dark. The brass gauntlet on his left arm hummed faintly under its sleeve, not with power but with old memory—a relic from before the Blight had taken root somewhere in the world, and it was quiet now, content to sit idle until called upon again.

Elara knelt near the gate’s threshold, pulling a small pouch of loose quartz dust from her satchel. She scattered just enough over the wet earth to catch any faint traces left by passing feet or claws, though she didn’t say what she was looking for yet. Her chisel rested against her hip, unused, but ready; she’d learned long ago that analysis came before action, and even in a world where traps could kill without warning, it was better to know where danger lay than to guess. She paused briefly, watching the way the light filtered through the trees above them, noting how the shadows seemed longer now than they should have been at dusk, then stood up again with a quiet sigh. “There’s something here,” she said finally, not looking at anyone as she spoke. “They’ve been camped here for a while. Fresh tracks lead out toward the northern ridge, but there are older ones—goblin footprints, some of them worn down, others barely visible.”

Maren stood up straighter than Elias had ever seen her before; even though her joints still stiffened with every step she took, and though she winced slightly when she shifted her weight onto her left leg, she didn’t hesitate. She stepped forward again, moving closer to the gate where the chain pulley hung loose and unstable-looking, then reached out to inspect it more closely. “It’s rigged,” she said without looking up. “Someone tied off one end of the rope with a knot that won’t slip unless you pull hard enough to break it. They’re waiting for us to try.” She paused, then turned her head toward Elias. “Can I take the first cut?”

Elias nodded once. “Go ahead, but be careful not to shake anything else when you do.” He knew she meant to test the trigger without making noise; he also knew that if she pulled too hard on the rope, the whole thing would snap loose and fall right onto their heads. Still, she was right about one thing: they couldn’t stay outside forever. The goblins were already inside, clustered around a fire down in the small clearing beyond the gate, and whatever they’d stolen from the village was likely still being guarded or counted by someone who wouldn’t hesitate to throw a spear at the first sign of trouble.

Torin stepped back slightly as Maren moved forward, keeping his distance just enough to stay out of range of any trap she might accidentally trigger, but close enough to catch her if she slipped on the mud. He watched her carefully as she ran her fingers along the rope, feeling for tension or slack in the line, then gave it a gentle tug that made the chain rattle softly against its supports. “Good,” he said quietly when she finished. “Now we know what’s connected to what.”

Elara had already pulled out her notebook and was scribbling something down on a fresh page with a charcoal stick; she didn’t need to ask for permission to take notes, nor did she care if anyone noticed her doing it. She flipped the page over and started again when Elias asked her what she’d found so far. “The tracks,” she said, pointing at the ground where several sets of footprints converged near the gate. “Two humans—one big, one small—and three goblins. The big human’s stride is consistent, about six feet per step, but the small one’s off by a half-foot or so; probably someone trying to keep up with the others.” She looked up then, her expression serious now as she studied the deeper impressions left in the mud. “The goblin tracks show they’ve been moving around here for at least an hour or two, maybe more. They’re not just camped—they’re resting, but they’re also organizing something.”

Kaelen stepped forward again, this time with a small puff of smoke curling out from his mouth as he exhaled slowly. “They’re planning something,” he said simply, though he didn’t elaborate further on what that meant exactly. He knew better than to reveal too much about his own abilities or let others assume he understood everything there was to understand about magic; sometimes the simplest explanation worked best, and even a mage like Kaelen could learn from listening before speaking.

Elias listened carefully as each of them spoke their observations, then nodded again when Maren finished explaining her assessment of the trap. The silence stretched for a moment, heavy with the weight of the decision that followed. Elias took a breath, letting it out slowly to steady his mind. He needed to ensure everyone understood not just what they were facing, but why they were taking the specific steps they intended to take next.

“Alright,” he said at last, his voice cutting through the quiet air. “We move forward quietly, one at a time. Torin leads with me in case we need to deal with any surprises. Kaelen, keep your eyes open for anything unusual—smoke, light, sound—that might indicate they’re using magic or traps beyond simple ropes and bells.” He glanced toward Elara. “You stay behind us and keep track of where everyone is going. Maren, you’re next; go ahead and check the interior perimeter while we wait for Kaelen to clear any obvious hazards.”

Maren nodded once again, then stepped past Elias and Torin into the doorway, her hammer drawn now as she moved with deliberate care to avoid triggering anything else. She paused briefly at the threshold, listening to the faint sounds of laughter and chatter coming from inside, then gave a low grunt that sounded almost like approval. “They think we’re alone,” she murmured, not looking back. “That’s how it stays this way.”

Torin followed behind her, keeping his sword drawn but relaxed, watching the way the shadows stretched across the floor as they moved deeper into the keep. He didn’t need to say anything; he understood that silence was their only ally right now, and that if they wanted to succeed, they’d have to trust each other completely—even if it meant admitting fear sometimes.

Inside, the air smelled of woodsmoke and unwashed bodies, a scent that made Elias’s nose wrinkle slightly as he stepped past Maren into the main hall of the ruined keep. The ceiling above them was cracked in places, with beams leaning precariously over one another like broken teeth; but even so, the space remained large enough to move through freely, and the ground beneath their boots was surprisingly clean for such a place. There were no traps laid out here—not yet—but there were signs of recent activity: overturned crates, scattered tools, and the occasional pile of stolen goods that someone had left behind in haste.

Elara stopped just inside the doorway, her chisel now pressed firmly against her hip as she scanned the room with quick, practiced movements. “They’re setting up something,” she said quietly after a moment, pointing toward a corner where several crude-looking contraptions sat assembled on tables and stools—things that looked like they’d been built from scrap metal and whatever else the goblins could find lying around. “Some kind of signal device, maybe?”

Kaelen stepped forward again, his brass gauntlet glowing faintly now as he raised one hand toward the air above them. He didn’t say anything, just let a small wisp of smoke curl upward from his fingers before fading away again, leaving no trace behind but for the quiet hum in the room that seemed to grow louder with every second they spent inside.
