The road to Pinehollow had been a long trudge through the late autumn woods, the kind of travel that settles deep into the bones and makes a man appreciate a warm hearth more than a fresh sword edge. The air outside the village gates was crisp, carrying the scent of woodsmoke and rotting leaves, but inside the walls of Pinehollow, the mood was somber. The few villagers who gathered on the village square as the party approached were huddled in pairs or small groups, their faces drawn tight with worry they couldn't hide. A few children stood at the edge of the crowd, clutching their parents' skirts, eyes wide and fearful.

The party moved into the center of the town, a cluster of timber-framed houses surrounded by stone walls that looked older than anyone alive remembered. The road was muddy, churned up by cart wheels and heavy boots, with puddles reflecting the grey sky. Maren kept her hammer strapped to her back, watching the perimeter with a practiced eye. Torin walked on Elias's right, his hand resting near the hilt of his sword without being intrusive. Kaelen stayed slightly behind the group, his brass gauntlet gleaming dully in the overcast light, while Elara scanned the crowd and the signs of life around them, her satchel bouncing gently with every step.

They approached the Crooked Stag, a low wooden structure with a thatched roof sagging under the weight of snow and time. A sign above the door depicted a stag with one antler bent at a sharp angle, painted in peeling black paint. Inside, the tavern was warm, smelling of ale, roasting meat, and damp wool. The common room was packed with locals, mostly men nursing tankards or sitting by the hearth, their faces grim.

A man stood near the bar, his hands clasped behind his back, looking like he'd rather be anywhere else in the world than dealing with this particular group of strangers. He wore a rough-spun tunic and a woolen cap that had seen better days, but there was a certain authority about him—perhaps it was the way he held himself, or the quiet confidence of someone who'd spent a lifetime watching his neighbors survive on what little they had to offer.

"Welcome to Pinehollow," he said, his voice steady despite the tension in the room. "I'm Elder Halloway." He gestured toward the bar with a nod of his head. "My name is Halloway."

Elias stepped forward, bowing slightly but keeping his stance open and ready. "Master Halloway," he said. "We are travelers who've heard of your troubles. We're looking for work, or rather, we're looking to help where we can."

Halloway's expression didn't change much, but there was a flicker of something in his eyes—hope mixed with fear. "It is not easy to find good folk willing to come here," he said. "You are the first party we've had in many weeks."

"We're no better than anyone else," Elias replied, keeping his tone even. "But we know what it's like to deal with goblins and other creatures of the dark. We can take care of ourselves, and we'd be glad to help you out if there's something we can do."

Halloway sighed, rubbing his chin thoughtfully. "It is a sad day when a village has to ask for outside help," he said. "But our own people are too afraid to leave the walls. We need someone who knows how to handle this kind of danger."

"We're not afraid," Elias said. "And we've had enough adventures to know that fear doesn't always mean you're in trouble."

Halloway nodded slowly, his eyes darting toward the bar where a few villagers were watching them with interest. "Then perhaps we can talk about what needs doing," he said. "There's something I'd like to show you before we go further into this."

He led the way out of the tavern and into the alleyway behind it, where a notice board was posted outside. The board was covered with notes pinned by nails, some faded from rain and wind. One note caught Elias's eye—a drawing of a goblin, crude but unmistakable, with arrows pointing toward the village outskirts. Another note mentioned missing livestock and grain stores, along with descriptions of the creatures that had taken them.

Halloway stopped in front of the board and pointed to the latest additions. "These are recent," he said. "Goblins have been raiding our farms for weeks now. They take what they need and leave nothing behind."

"We've seen their tracks before," Elias said, gesturing toward the drawings. "We can identify them easily enough."

Halloway nodded again, his expression serious. "That's why we need help. We don't know where they're coming from, or who they work for. But we do know that they're using the old ruins beyond the woods as a base."

Elias exchanged a glance with Kaelen, who gave him a slight nod of agreement. "We'll investigate," he said. "But first, I'd like to know exactly what you need us to do."

Halloway led them back into the tavern, where a table had been cleared for them. He sat down across from Elias and laid out a small leather pouch on the table. "This is all we can offer," he said. "It's not much, but it's enough to cover expenses and provide rewards."

Elias picked up the pouch and opened it carefully. Inside were gold coins, counted precisely by the elder. There were forty-five pieces in total—enough to cover travel costs and supplies for a short job. Elias nodded slowly, considering the amount. It wasn't much, but it was honest work, and the village needed help more than they could say.

"We'll take it," Elias said finally. "But let's make sure we understand what we're dealing with."

Halloway smiled thinly. "Gladly." He picked up a quill from the table and began writing on a scroll of parchment. "Here are the terms: recover Nella and Hobb, secure the farmsteads, and stop any further raids. In return, you receive this sum plus whatever additional reward the village council may decide later."

Elias read through the conditions carefully, nodding as he went along. "Seems fair enough," he said. "But if we find anything else during our investigation—say, a larger band or a different group entirely—we'll need to know before we commit ourselves to anything bigger."

Halloway nodded again. "Of course," he said. "We'd rather not have you get caught in something beyond your pay grade." He finished writing the contract and rolled it up carefully. "Sign here, please," he said, handing Elias a quill.

Elias dipped the pen into the inkwell and signed his name at the bottom of the page. Halloway did the same, sealing the agreement with a final flourish. Then he handed the scroll to Elias, along with the pouch of gold. "We'll leave you to your work," he said. "If there's anything we need, just come back here."

Elias looked at the contract one last time before tucking it away in his satchel. "Thank you," he said. "We won't let you down."

Halloway nodded again, standing up and extending a hand to Elias. "That's all I can ask," he said. "Good luck."

As the party left the tavern, they walked through the streets of Pinehollow, the weight of their new responsibility settling heavily on them. The village was quiet now, the villagers staying indoors or hiding in their homes as if waiting for something bad to happen. The air smelled of fear and uncertainty, but Elias knew that tomorrow would bring answers—and hopefully, a way to put an end to this nightmare.

They made their way toward the edge of town, where the forest began to rise around them like a living wall. Kaelen raised his hand, casting a small circle of light around them as they stepped out onto the path leading into the woods. The lanterns flickered slightly in the gloom, illuminating the muddy trail and the footprints that led deeper into the darkness ahead.

"Ready?" Elias asked, looking at each member of the party. "Let's get to work."
