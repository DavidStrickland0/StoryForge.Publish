The mud here was deep and slick, clinging to boots with the stubbornness of wet clay. Elias kept his hand near the hilt of his sword more out of habit than fear, though his eyes scanned the treeline where shadows seemed to stretch against the dim light better than they should have.

Maren waded through a patch of standing water that had pooled after a recent rain. Her heavy boots squelched with every step, sending up a small cloud of mud that drifted back onto her trousers. She paused at the first sign of violence, stopping dead in the trail to examine a farmhouse porch that hung over nothingness now. The wood was splintered, not from age but from something sharp and deliberate.

"The roof is gone," Maren said, gesturing with a gloved hand toward the structure. "Roof sheared right off. Nothing else standing except the walls."

She walked to the edge of the porch and leaned over the broken sill. The interior was dark, save for the glint of lantern light from her satchel, which she used sparingly to illuminate the damage. "It wasn't just wind," Maren noted, tossing the debris aside. "Wind doesn't punch through walls like that unless it's carrying something heavy enough to tear them apart."
