The path into the woods was not a road in any traditional sense; it was a scar cut through the forest floor where travelers had long ago worn away the leaf litter, only for new roots to tangle and close the gap again. The mud here was deep and slick, clinging to boots with the stubbornness of wet clay. Elias kept his hand near the hilt of his sword more out of habit than fear, though his eyes scanned the treeline where shadows seemed to stretch against the dim light better than they should have.

Maren waded through a patch of standing water that had pooled after a recent rain. Her heavy boots squelched with every step, sending up a small cloud of mud that drifted back onto her trousers. She paused at the first sign of violence, stopping dead in the trail to examine a farmhouse porch that hung over nothingness now. The wood was splintered, not from age but from something sharp and deliberate.

"The roof is gone," Maren said, gesturing with a gloved hand toward the structure. "Roof sheared right off. Nothing else standing except the walls."

She walked to the edge of the porch and leaned over the broken sill. The interior was dark, save for the glint of lantern light from her satchel, which she used sparingly to illuminate the damage. She picked up a piece of roofing material that had fallen from the eaves—a jagged shard of slate mixed with something metallic and strange.

"It wasn't just wind," Maren noted, tossing the debris aside. "Wind doesn't punch through walls like that unless it's carrying something heavy enough to tear them apart." She crouched by a broken window frame where the glass had been shattered inward. Inside the house, there were signs of struggle: overturned furniture, scattered grain sacks, and what looked like hastily packed bundles of wool or leather being dragged toward the door before the final collapse. "They didn't want to wait for us," she said, standing up with a grimace that suggested the smell of wet rot and smoke was doing more than bother her nose. "They wanted in fast."

Torin stepped forward from behind a large oak tree, his hand resting on the crossguard of his sword. He had been walking ahead quietly, scanning the perimeter for movement or sound. Now he moved to flank Maren's position, keeping one eye on the forest line and the other on her movements.

"They're coming back," Torin muttered, his voice low enough that only those close could hear it. "If they took grain last night, they'll be here tonight."

"Not if we act now," Elias said, stepping forward beside Maren. He adjusted the contract scroll in his belt with a careful touch before glancing at Elara. "Elara? Can you tell what kind of damage this is?"

Elara knelt near the broken window, pulling a small notebook from her satchel and using a chisel to gently scrape some residue from the sill into a vial she carried. Her fingers moved methodically, avoiding the sharp edges of the debris while noting the texture and color of whatever remained on the wood.

"There's oil slicked across the floorboards," she said without looking up. "And something else—some kind of soot mixed with ash. Not from a fire, but perhaps from an explosion or a magical effect gone wrong." She paused, holding her breath as if listening to something invisible beneath her feet. "Wait."

She pressed her ear against the floorboard and frowned. "There's movement inside. Someone—or something—is moving around down there. They're not leaving much; they're just packing up what they can take before we get here."

Maren stood back, her hammer resting against her shoulder as she surveyed the house from a distance. "Then we don't have time for this," she said. "We need to find their tracks. If they've taken Nella and Hobb, they've probably left them somewhere nearby."

Torin nodded toward the path leading deeper into the woods. "Tracks lead that way," he said, pointing toward a narrow trail that wound between two trees. "But I don't see footprints. Only claw marks and splinters of wood where they've climbed walls or jumped over fences."

"Claws," Elias repeated softly. He looked at Elara again. "Elara, what do those marks tell us?"

"They belong to creatures with three fingers on their hands and two toes on their feet," she answered immediately, pulling out a sketchpad from her satchel. "And they're using crude rope made from plant fiber and scrap metal." She pointed at a piece of rope hanging from a broken fence post nearby. "This isn't natural growth; it's tied together with knots that look like they were made by someone who didn't know how to tie properly."

Maren walked over and inspected the knot herself, pulling at one end of the rope until it snapped cleanly in half. She looked at Elias with a mix of admiration and frustration.

"See?" she said. "They're amateurs. But they've been doing this long enough to know exactly how to leave no trace except where they want us to find it."

Elias nodded slowly, watching the way Maren's expression shifted between irritation and respect for her judgment. He turned back to Elara. "How deep into the woods do these tracks go?"

"About three hundred paces," Elara replied, checking her notes against the distance markers on a map she carried. "Then they take a steep climb toward the ruins beyond the ridge."

Torin gestured toward the trail again. "If we follow those tracks, we'll reach the keep within an hour or so. But if we run into them along the way..."

"We'll deal with it," Elias said firmly. He paused before continuing, his tone measured but clear. "We agreed to stop if we encounter a larger band or different group than what we signed up for. Remember that promise."

Torin hesitated briefly before nodding again. "Yes, sir. I remember."

Maren smirked slightly at the formality of the exchange, though her eyes remained sharp and alert. "Let's move then," she said, taking a step forward onto the muddy trail. Her boots sank deeply into the earth as she moved, leaving behind a clear impression of her tread pattern.

Elara followed close behind, keeping pace with Elias while occasionally stopping to examine debris or mark coordinates on her map. Torin took point again, scanning the forest line for any sign of motion or sound that might indicate presence beyond mere wind rustling leaves. Kaelen brought up the rear, his brass gauntlet gleaming faintly in the dim light as he cast small circles of illumination around them periodically to ensure visibility without drawing attention from unseen observers.

As they walked, Maren kept a steady rhythm with her steps, occasionally glancing back at Elias or Elara when she noticed either of them lagging behind due to fatigue or injury concerns. Her hands were clean and dry, wrapped in fresh leather that had been prepared since their last journey home; the wounds from the previous adventure had healed completely within the weeks spent resting at the roadside inn. She moved with a deliberate care to avoid aggravating any residual stiffness in her joints, though she made no comment on it unless asked.

"You're right about the goblins," Maren said after some time had passed without incident. "But I think we should hurry before they realize we're coming."

"They haven't realized yet," Elias replied evenly. "And even if they do, we'll be ready for them by then."

Maren didn't argue further, though she kept her hammer drawn and poised near her side as a precaution against sudden ambushes or surprises from the forest ahead. The trail grew steeper as they climbed higher into the hills, where the ground became rocky and uneven underfoot. Roots twisted upward like gnarled fingers reaching desperately for sunlight, catching at boots and forcing them to pause and climb over obstacles one by one.

At the crest of the hill, they found themselves overlooking a small clearing where several figures stood clustered together near a pile of stolen goods—bundles of wool, sacks of grain, crates of metal tools, and other valuables taken from farms nearby. Figures clad in crude armor fashioned from scrap metal sat huddled around a fire, eating roasted meat and drinking from carved wooden cups. Their faces were painted with markings that made them look almost like caricatures of men who had forgotten how to be civilized.

"They're waiting for us," Torin whispered, his voice barely audible over the crackling flames of the fire below. "And they've already set traps along the way."

Elias nodded once, signaling for silence while he signaled Elara and Kaelen to prepare themselves defensively. Maren tightened her grip on her hammer, her jaw set in determination as she prepared to lead the charge forward if necessary. The air smelled of smoke and wet earth, mingling with the scent of fear that lingered in everyone's minds since first arriving at Pinehollow.

"Move quietly," Elias instructed softly. "Don't make noise unless you have to."

They crept forward carefully, using shadows and cover wherever possible while keeping an eye out for any signs of traps or ambushes ahead. The terrain grew increasingly treacherous as they descended into the valley where the ruined keep loomed ominously in the distance. Broken walls stood like skeletal remains of what once had been a sturdy fortress, now reduced to piles of rubble and debris scattered across the landscape.
