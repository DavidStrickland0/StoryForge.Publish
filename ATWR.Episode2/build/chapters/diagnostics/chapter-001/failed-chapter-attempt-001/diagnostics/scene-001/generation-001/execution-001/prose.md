The air inside the Crooked Stag tasted of wet wool, stale ale, and the distinct, sour tang of fear that seemed to cling to Pinehollow like morning frost. Elias Thorne adjusted his leather straps, the bronze plate map tucked securely against his hip, feeling the familiar weight of responsibility settle over him. He was not a savior chosen by destiny; he was a mercenary with a ledger and a sword, but the villagers outside saw something else entirely when they looked at the party standing in the doorway. They saw hope, or at least the only thing that wasn't a goblin.

Maren stood near the bar, her heavy hammer resting on a sturdy oak table. Her hands were bandaged, fresh wounds from the Gate of Silence operation healing but still tender to the touch. She kept one eye on the room’s exits and one on the group of villagers huddled by the hearth. Torin leaned against the wall near the window, his posture relaxed but alert, scanning the road that wound past the tavern toward the treeline. Elara sat in a corner, her satchel open, though she had not yet touched the loose quartz dust inside. She was watching the people with the same critical eye one might use to inspect a bridge before crossing it.

The village elder, a man named Barnaby whose beard was as grey and knotted as old roots, shuffled toward them. He carried a small bag of coins that jingled softly with every step. He looked tired, his eyes red-rimmed from lack of sleep and perhaps too much ale.

"Thank you for coming," Barnaby said, his voice cracking slightly. "We know the tales of your group. We heard you turned back the Blight at the old city. We thought... we thought maybe you might be interested."

"We are interested," Elias said, keeping his tone even and professional. He stepped forward, extending a hand but not allowing himself to shake Barnaby’s; he had seen enough infection in ruined cities to know that germs spread faster than gossip in a small town. "But I want to make sure we understand exactly what is expected."

"We need you to find Nella and Hobb," Barnaby said, gesturing toward the few villagers who had followed him into the main hall. Their faces were pale, streaked with soot from the farm fires. "They went to check on a barn near the eastern ridge. We think they are goblin raiders."

"Goblins," Torin muttered, his hand drifting slightly toward the hilt of his sword. "Small, ugly, and likely to be hungry."

"Organized enough to steal grain and livestock," Elara noted quietly from the corner. She had been listening intently. "And coordinated enough to take two adults and leave only a few scraps behind. If they are just opportunistic thieves, they would have taken everything they could carry without leaving a guard post behind."

"They left tracks," Barnaby said, pointing to a stain on the floor near the door. "Goblin tracks. Small feet, dragging. And... well, there was a dropped arrowhead. Iron, but crude. Not like anything we see in the market."

Maren crossed her arms, her gaze sharpening as she assessed the room. "You say they took Nella and Hobb," she said to Barnaby. "But you also said grain and livestock were stolen. That suggests planning. They went for supplies first, then took people. Why?"

"To repair their equipment," Elias said smoothly, stepping closer to the elder. He knew how these things worked; the village had limited resources, but they had no army. "They need tools to build more traps or weapons. They need livestock to eat and to trade with whatever allies they might have in the hills. It’s not a random raid."

"They are coming back," Barnaby whispered, his eyes widening. "If we don’t stop them, they will come again tomorrow. The farms cannot survive another week."

Elias looked at the elder, then at Maren and Torin. He knew the contract terms needed to be clear. "We can take this job. But I want to set the terms before we leave. We need to know exactly what we are being paid for."

Barnaby hesitated, frowning. "Fifty gold pieces," he said quickly. "That is all we have. That is all we can afford. It is a modest sum, but it is our entire reward."

"Fifty gold pieces," Elias repeated, nodding slowly. "We will accept that for the rescue and the prevention of further raids. But there are conditions."

Maren grunted. "What kind of conditions?"

"We need to secure the scene," Elias said. "We are not just going in blind. We need to understand what we are dealing with before we commit to an assault. If we walk into a trap, you will pay us for our time and effort regardless of the outcome."

Barnaby nodded vigorously. "Of course. Of course. You do not want to lose your lives, do you?"

"Exactly," Elias said. "And if we find that the goblins are just raiders and not part of some larger scheme, we will return with the captives and whatever loot they took. We will bring it back to you. But if there is a bigger threat... if they are connected to something else..."

He paused, looking at Elara, who was already scribbling notes in her small book. "If there is a larger threat, we will tell you. But we need to know what we are walking into first."

Barnaby nodded again, his expression grim. "We understand. We just need you to help us."

Maren stepped forward then, her voice low and firm. "I want to see the farm before we leave," she said. "And I want to talk to anyone who saw what happened. If we don’t know where they went, we are wasting our time."

"We will go now," Elias said. "But first, let me make sure we have the agreement." He turned to Barnaby and extended his hand again. "Fifty gold pieces for rescue and prevention of raids. We return with captives and stolen goods. If we die in the attempt, you pay us nothing, but if we succeed, you pay us fifty. Is that fair?"

Barnaby nodded rapidly. "Fair. Very fair. I will give you the bag now." He reached into his belt pouch and pulled out a small leather satchel. Inside were coins of various sizes and denominations, all jingling softly. "Here is your payment upfront. Fifty gold pieces."

Elias took the bag without touching Barnaby’s hand. He opened it slowly, counting the coins as he did so. One, two, three... fifty. Every single coin was accounted for. No coins missing, no discrepancies. The elder had counted them carefully, or at least he had taken the time to ensure that the payment was accurate.

"Thank you," Elias said, closing the bag and tucking it into his belt pouch. "We will leave now. We will return with Nella and Hobb as soon as possible."

Maren turned toward the door, her expression serious. "I want to see the farm before we go," she said again. "And I want to make sure we don’t walk into a trap."
