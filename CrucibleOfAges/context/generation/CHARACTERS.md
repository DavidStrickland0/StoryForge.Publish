## Caelen

### Established History

- A United States Marine in his first life on Earth.
- Sought status, escape, belonging, and significance; capable of courage and loyalty alongside selfishness and exploitation.
- Lied to his mother about injuries and took credit for Private Davis's tactical call.
- Died shielding a noncombatant mother carrying her dead infant from Sergeant Miller's gunfire. The mother survived.
- He truly died on Earth; his Earth body was destroyed. He remained dead in Nothing (the void), experiencing the passage of time while the acuteness of his memories gradually diminished. Later, his body was reformed from local dust on another world, where he awakened alive.
- He is an awakened person from Earth and perceives a private System. Everyone from Earth eventually awakens. His intentional rescue death triggers automatic Sacrifice assignment. His tutorial choices weigh so strongly toward Dominion that this conflict triggers a rare offer to choose between factions. At death he quickly chooses Sacrifice, without understanding its mechanics. He dislikes what he became on Earth and finds peace only in his final rescue. This weighting reflects hidden faction points, inaccessible during life and weighed only at death.

### Current State

- Alive, collared, and captive in the slave pen at the end of Chapter 4. His declaration of identity is resolve; he has not escaped or obtained legal freedom.
- The returned body has larger, broad-palmed hands and tanned, rough skin. It is locally adapted and physically conditioned but initially unfamiliar.
- Sustained a staff blow to the ribs during capture. Recovery has not been established. Transport restraints injured his wrists; their exact configuration after entering the pen should not be assumed without clarification in prose.
- Cannot understand the local language. Recognizes some situational cues, which do not grant precise knowledge of laws or motives.
- The existing chapters show two private columns containing one and one. Their function under the revised premise is unresolved; they are not visible levels or faction scales. No additional class capability is demonstrated in those chapters.
- Bluffed a guard into lowering his staff and has attracted attention from guards and captives. His standing is provisional.
- Immediate aims are survival, understanding the local rules, and eventual freedom. His latest tactic secured personal position rather than establishing a selfless commitment to the other captives.

### Knowledge from the Creator's Visit

After Caelen chooses Sacrifice at death, the Creator personally visits him to verify his choice. Caelen learns that Earth is the breeding ground of candidates to rule the Creator's universe. He does not learn that only Sacrifice rulers are accepted or that Dominion rulers are destroyed and replaced. The target number of 120,000, the Sacrifice-only acceptance rule, and the destruction and replacement of Dominion rulers are withheld from him and the reader until the end.

The visit does not grant a full understanding of faction advancement or the interface. His later doubts remain possible despite this encounter.

### Faction Choice and Later Doubts

Caelen chose Sacrifice quickly at death. He knew he disliked the person he had become and that his final rescue gave him peace; he did not understand the faction's advancement rules. Remembering or choosing the name does not mean he understands the unexplained interface.

Later he notices that his growth seems connected to sacrifice or genuinely accepted potential sacrifice for others, including defending them with his life and sword, without understanding the hidden progression mechanism. He occasionally questions his choice when this restriction frustrates him. Those doubts coexist with his capacity to care and his continuing selfish impulses; they do not change his faction during life.

### Writing Guidance

His trained habits support observation, endurance, and threat assessment. His experience and judgment have limits independent of any specified age. His extensive military hand-to-hand training is established. Do not invent a service history or command experience merely to explain a convenient capability.

He may mistake hierarchy for stability, compliance for agreement, or usefulness for loyalty. Let those mistakes meet specific people and consequences. His early change should emerge through costly exceptions to self-interest while Dominion methods remain tempting and effective.

Preserve a recognizable voice: practical attention, defensive pride, impatience with helplessness, and dry humor when circumstances permit. Allow fear, embarrassment, grief, tenderness, and dependence to complicate that voice.

## Marcy — Core Companion and Love Interest

### Background

Marcy is a young, non-awakened woman and Caelen's female love interest. She is an adult; do not state her numerical age. Chronic malnutrition has left her small-framed, underweight, and physically underdeveloped for her age. Describe this through health, strength, clothing fit, fatigue, and recovery; do not mistake her youthful appearance for her actual age. Her parents were indentured servants paid wages while being charged for necessities and other expenses in amounts designed to exceed their earnings. The resulting debt could not be paid off. After both parents died, Marcy was sold into slavery to pay their supposed outstanding debt.

### First Meeting and Protector Claim

Caelen meets Marcy in the slave carriage during the opening chapters. Her beauty draws the overseer's attention. Other men in the slave cage harass her or attempt to sexually assault her; Caelen is the only man there who does not join them. His lack of participation is initially rooted largely in his inability to understand the language and recognize what is happening. Do not rewrite that passivity as a fully informed heroic stand.

His different behavior makes Marcy trust him enough to claim him as her protector in desperation. Under slave-pit custom, she claims to have given her life to him in exchange for protection, becoming subordinate to him during her free time. Caelen does not understand the declaration or the obligations it creates.

Because he neither answers the claim nor surrenders her, other slaves force him to fight over her. His extensive military hand-to-hand training helps him prevail. He eventually learns enough language to understand why these confrontations have been happening and what Marcy has claimed.

### Relationship Development

Her initial trust is a desperate judgment about relative safety, not established romantic love or sexual consent. The protector claim does not constitute consent to intimacy. Let romance develop through mutual understanding, choices, and accumulated experience after adult status is established.

Marcy's dependence confronts Caelen with a concrete form of the story's central tension: protecting someone can place him in a position to possess or exploit her. Her claim is a survival action under coercive conditions, not evidence that she freely endorses slavery or wants to surrender all future autonomy.

Her personality, skills, private ambitions, grief, and relationships beyond Caelen remain to be developed. Do not reduce her role to beauty, victimization, gratitude, or motivation for his fights.
