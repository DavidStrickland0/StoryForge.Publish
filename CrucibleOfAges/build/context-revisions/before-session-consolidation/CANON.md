# Canon

## Authority and Scope

The accepted Chapters 1–4 establish the events below. WORLD.md contains authoritative metaphysical mechanics and author-facing setting guidance. NARRATIVE.md holds premise, direction, and current narrative position. CHARACTERS.md separates current character state from history.

Plans describe intended future developments, not completed events. Character beliefs and institutional doctrines are claims until confirmed. When contexts disagree, preserve accepted events and flag any unresolved mechanic rather than inventing an explanation.

## Established History

- Caelen was a United States Marine living his first life on Earth.
- He lied to his mother about injuries and took credit for Private Davis's tactical call.
- Sergeant Miller's gunfire killed Caelen while he shielded a noncombatant mother carrying her dead infant. The mother survived; the infant was already dead.
- Caelen truly died on Earth, with gunshot wounds to chest and thigh destroying his Earth body. He remained dead in Nothing (the void) for a period during which he experienced time and the acuteness of memory diminished. His body was then reformed from local dust, and he awakened alive on another world.
- Caelen is awakened and originates from Earth. Everyone from Earth eventually awakens. His rescue death triggers automatic Sacrifice assignment, but his strongly Dominion-weighted tutorial choices trigger a rare offer to choose between factions. At death he quickly chooses Sacrifice because he dislikes what he became and finds peace in his final rescue. He does not yet understand the choice's implications; the scale reflects hidden faction points, inaccessible during life and weighed only at death.
- The private interface displays two columns separated by a shimmering line. Other people cannot directly see or verify it.
- His returned body is locally adapted, with larger, broad-palmed hands and tanned, rough skin.
- The new world has a pale yellow sky and a dim, hazy sun. His initial sensation of heaviness does not settle whether local gravity is greater than Earth's.
- Local speech is unfamiliar to Caelen, who perceives harsh consonants and rolling vowels.
- Captors assessed him, locked a metal collar around his neck, struck his ribs with a staff, restrained him, and transported him by cart.
- Chapter 4 places him in a stone-walled slave pen containing roughly thirty captives, with guards at the gate and a patrolling staff-bearing guard.
- A masked man in purple carries a ledger and a black-tipped cane, directs a guard, and undermines the scarred captive's apparent standing.
- A guard strikes a distressed young woman. Caelen subsequently bluffs a guard into lowering his staff, seeking to improve his own position.

## Author-Established Faction Background

- Everyone from Earth eventually awakens; only Earth-origin people are awakened.
- Billions of Earth-origin people are spread across trillions of planets. Awakened people are rare, with only a few present on a planet at any time.
- Every planet starts with at least two awakened people, one Sacrifice and one Dominion: its founding Adam and Eve.
- Intentionally dying to save someone qualifies for Sacrifice assignment or an option to choose it.
- Faction change is possible only at death, based on a pattern of intentional choices during life. It is exceptionally rare and known only to a few.
- These are author-known mechanics, not knowledge automatically available to Caelen or local institutions.

- Dominion and Sacrifice points are recorded behind the scenes. Nothing reveals a person's scales during life. Weighing occurs only at death. Hidden points do not grant advancement in an unassigned faction.

- Caelen quickly chooses Sacrifice at death, disliking what he became and finding peace only in his final rescue. He does not yet understand the implications.
- Sacrifice advancement can arise from actual sacrifice or genuine potential sacrifice for others, including defending them with his life and sword. Survival without injury does not automatically disqualify the action.
- Caelen occasionally questions his choice after learning its advancement restrictions. Doubt does not change faction during life.

## Creator's Visit and Ultimate Purpose — Author-Established

- After Caelen chooses Sacrifice, the Creator makes a rare visit to verify the choice.
- Caelen learns that the Creator seeks rulers for his universe and that Earth is their breeding ground.
- The Creator seeks 120,000 rulers who can both sacrifice and rule. This number, the Sacrifice-only acceptance rule, and the destruction and replacement of Dominion rulers are revealed only at the end.
- Author-only truth: only Sacrifice rulers are accepted. Dominion rulers are destroyed and replaced with new candidates.
- The Creator withholds that acceptance restriction and fate from Caelen. The visit does not establish Caelen's final acceptance or full understanding of faction mechanics.
- The judgment stage, destruction mechanism, and replacement process remain undefined.

## End Revelation — Planetary Rulership

The revelation occurs once Caelen becomes ruler of his planet, as one of the few Sacrifice rulers. At that point he learns that the Creator seeks 120,000 rulers capable of both sacrifice and rule, accepts only Sacrifice rulers, and destroys Dominion rulers while putting forth new candidates.

This is the culmination of Caelen's planetary rulership arc, not the earlier liberation of one slave operation or the formation of a small army. Keep these selection truths hidden from Caelen and the reader until he reaches that milestone. Do not treat his early choice or the Creator's verification visit as proof that he has already achieved planetary rulership or passed the final selection.

## Book-One Scope

Book One tracks Caelen's rise from enslaved foreigner to Sacrifice ruler of his planet. Destruction of the local slave operation is an intermediate victory. Planetary rulership and the consequent revelation of the Creator's selection plan form the ending. These are future narrative commitments, not events already completed by Chapter 4.

## Creator Guidance — Future Narrative Commitment

The Creator takes a unique interest in Caelen and supplies seemingly random data in odd ways throughout his journey. This gives him critical clues without intervening to resolve events. Caelen remains responsible for interpretation, decisions, effort, and consequences. The delivery mechanisms and reason for the interest remain unspecified. The prompts do not reveal the final selection truths before planetary rulership.

## Retrospective Recognition of Guidance

Only at the ending, once he becomes ruler of his planet, does Caelen look back and recognize that the odd information and critical clues were supplied by the Creator. Earlier, he can understand and use a clue without knowing its source. His early verification visit does not make him recognize the later prompts as divine guidance.

Prepare this recognition through concrete, memorable clues whose connections become clear in retrospect. Before the ending, preserve Caelen's unawareness; do not give him confirmed knowledge of the Creator's ongoing involvement. The final recognition connects earlier events without erasing his choices, work, sacrifices, or responsibility.

## Caelen's Fighter Progression — Hidden Framework

Caelen is a Fighter. The D&D SRD 3.5 Fighter provides the behind-the-scenes progression framework for his combat capabilities, statistics, proficiencies, skills, feats, and learning prerequisites. This framework does not appear in the fiction as a visible class-level or XP system.

There are no displayed XP gains, XP totals, character levels, or level-up announcements. Caelen does not know that Sacrifice-aligned actions govern his advancement. Do not have him calculate XP, identify his numerical level, or reliably explain the hidden advancement mechanism.

Only Sacrifice-aligned actions advance him, including actual sacrifice or genuine potential sacrifice for others. Combat, kills, victory, practice, and completed objectives do not independently trigger advancement. Practice develops what he is capable of learning; it is not a substitute for the hidden Sacrifice requirement.

When he crosses a hidden advancement threshold, his stats improve and new learning potential opens. Apply the established Fighter progression behind the scenes rather than inventing unrelated class powers. Do not automatically give him a finished technique, complete knowledge, or mastery merely because that potential becomes available.

Learning occurs in the prose through instruction, observation, experimentation, training, mistakes, and use under pressure. New skills and techniques require an earned development sequence. A new capacity may first appear as improved coordination, endurance, understanding, or ability to benefit from instruction, as appropriate to the underlying change.

Keep capability growth distinct from hidden faction scales. Neither improved performance nor any interface reveals where his Dominion and Sacrifice scales lie during life. He may notice patterns or wonder about his growth, but narration must not turn that uncertainty into knowledge of XP or levels.

Exact current statistics, learned techniques, advancement thresholds, and the presentation of stats remain undefined. The two displayed ones are not confirmed XP, levels, or faction-scale readings. Military experience informs behavior without granting unlimited competence.

## Mathematical Conventions

Base-2, base-4, base-8, and base-16 systems are the local norm. Mathematics favors powers of two. Decimal figures in author context describe quantities for reference, not established in-world notation. Local numeral symbols and spoken number names remain undefined.

## Measurement Scale

- Standard sequence: 2, 8, 32, 128, 512, 2048 units.
- Each step is four times the preceding step.
- Pair names two units; octet names eight. The larger step names are pentad (32), heptad (128), ennead (512), and hendecad (2048). These local terms refer to the corresponding binary exponents, not Earth counting-group meanings.
- Do not use double octet as a measurement step.
- Day periods remain 8, 32, and 128 days. Physical base units and conversion ratios remain open.

## Local Anatomy and Numerical Culture

The normal local humanoid hand has three fingers and a thumb, four digits in total. Each foot normally has four toes. This anatomy supports the prevalence of base-four and other powers-of-two counting systems without requiring every culture to use identical notation.

Caelen's hands have five digits apiece, including the thumb, and locals regard them as abnormal. His body is adapted to the planet but retains this visible difference. Reactions can affect how people identify or assess him; five-digit hands do not by themselves prove awakening or grant locals accurate knowledge of his origin. His toe count has not been specified.

## Local Time Periods — Authoritative

An octet of days is the equivalent of a week: eight days.
A pentad of days is the equivalent of a month: thirty-two days, or four octets.
An ennead of days is the equivalent of a year: five hundred twelve days, or sixteen pentads.
A heptad remains one hundred twenty-eight days, equivalent to four pentads; it is not the month equivalent.

These periods do not arise from seasons. Week, month, and year are explanatory comparisons; local speech uses octet, pentad, heptad, and ennead. Do not introduce an Earth-style calendar or state numerical character ages. The measurement scale remains 2, 8, 32, 128, 512, and 2048 with its established names unchanged.
