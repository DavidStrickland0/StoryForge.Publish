# Chapter 4: The Asset

The cart rattled over a cobblestone street that seemed to have been paved by someone with a grudge. Every bump jolted Caelen’s bound wrists, the metal cuffs biting into his skin with a rhythmic, metallic pain. The dust from the road had settled into his pores, coating his tongue with grit that tasted of iron and dry earth. He kept his eyes fixed on the wooden slats above him, refusing to look at the faces of the passersby. Most of them ignored the cart entirely. A few glanced over, their expressions ranging from mild curiosity to detached disinterest. None of them showed fear. They were not looking at a person; they were looking at inventory.

The cart stopped with a squeal of protesting wood. The door was kicked open, and a hand grabbed Caelen by the back of his tunic, hauling him out into the blinding light. He stumbled, his legs shaking from the suspension of his arms, but he caught his balance. He did not fall. He had trained too long to fall on command.

He was shoved into a line. Or rather, he was herded into a pen.

It was a square of packed dirt surrounded by high stone walls, the air thick with the smell of unwashed bodies, old sweat, and the faint, sweet rot of decay. The sun here was harsher, unfiltered by the canopy of the city they had passed through. Shadows were sharp and absolute. There were perhaps thirty slaves in the pen, men and women of various ages and builds. They stood in a rough circle, staring at the ground or at the walls. They did not speak. They did not move. They were statues carved from flesh, waiting for the auction to begin.

Caelen scanned the group. His Marine instincts kicked in, overriding his exhaustion. He assessed the perimeter. Two guards stood at the gate, arms crossed, looking bored. A third patrolled the edge of the pen, a staff in his hand that looked heavy and blunt. Inside, the hierarchy was visible even in the silence. A man in the center, slightly older, with a scar running down his cheek, held his head higher than the others. He was the alpha. Around him, a cluster of younger, more fit individuals stood closer, their postures relaxed but ready. The rest were huddled at the edges, shoulders hunched, eyes downcast.

Caelen recognized the dynamic. It was the same as every squad he had ever led. The strong protected the weak, or rather, the strong used the weak as a buffer. The older man, the alpha, was likely the one who had negotiated better rations or lighter labor in previous transactions. He was a survivor. Caelen felt a flicker of respect, quickly followed by cold calculation. That man was a threat. He would see Caelen as a challenge to his status, a new strongman in the pen who might disrupt the order.

He was tall, dressed in robes of deep purple silk that contrasted violently with the dust and grime of the pen. He wore a mask of polished bone, covering his nose and mouth, leaving only his eyes visible. They were pale, almost white, and they moved with a slow, predatory precision. He did not look at the crowd. He looked at a ledger held in his left hand. In his right hand, he held a cane, the tip of which was capped with a heavy, black stone.

The crowd flinched. It was a collective, involuntary motion, like a herd of deer sensing a lion.

The masked man tilted his head. He spoke a single word.

**1**
