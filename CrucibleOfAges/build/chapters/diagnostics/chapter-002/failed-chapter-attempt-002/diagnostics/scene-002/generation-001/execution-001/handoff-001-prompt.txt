The pattern resolved itself with a click that Caelen felt in his teeth, though he no longer had teeth.

The gray mist didn’t part so much as it stepped aside, making room for geometry. A structure of pale, translucent lines unfolded before him, floating in the suspension of the void. It looked less like a window into heaven and more like the schematic of a machine that had been stripped down to its essential gears. There was no warmth in it. No voice. No choir of angels or the booming thunder of divine judgment. It was a grid. A menu. A dashboard.

Caelen drifted closer, his intent pulling the visual anchor toward his point of focus. He had spent his life in the Marines believing that competence was a form of godhood. If you knew the map, if you calculated the trajectory, if you understood the weight of the bullet and the wind, you were the one who held the power. He had assumed that dying for a stranger would buy him some sort of celestial credit, a golden ticket to a better arrangement, perhaps a direct line to the Creator to explain why he had done something so stupid, so inefficient, so completely against his own self-interest.

Instead, he got a spreadsheet.

The interface hovered in the mist, stable and indifferent. It was composed of two distinct columns, separated by a thin, vertical line that seemed to cut the air with surgical precision. The text was not written in any language he recognized, yet he understood it instantly, the way one understands a speed limit sign or a hazard symbol. The meaning bypassed translation and settled directly into the mind as a functional fact.

Left column: Dominion.
Right column: Sacrifice.

Beneath each heading, a single digit pulsed with a faint, rhythmic light. The first digit pulsed with a faint, rhythmic light.

Caelen stared at the number. He had expected a tally of sins. He had expected a ledger of his cruelties, his manipulations, the times he had used his rank to squeeze favors out of privates, the moments he had kicked a door in to assert dominance, the cold calculus of who could be sacrificed for the mission and who could be left behind. He had assumed that the void would be a courtroom, that the System—if that was what this was—would be the judge.

But there was no judgment here. There was only data.

He focused on the left side, on the word *Dominion*. The system did not offer a definition, but the concept felt heavy in his mind, a weight he recognized. It was the feeling of holding the knife while someone else held the throat. It was the quiet satisfaction of knowing that his survival depended on someone else’s inability to walk away. The number *1* sat there, stark and unadorned. It did not glow with approval. It did not shimmer with condemnation. It simply existed, a record of a fact. He had accumulated one level of this capacity. It was a baseline. A starting point.

He shifted his attention to the right column. *He shifted his attention to the right column, which was labeled Sacrifice..

The word felt different. It was not heavy in the same way. It was hollow, a space where something had been removed. Caelen thought of the mother, the dead infant, the way the bullet had passed through his chest and into the air where her head had been. He had not done it for glory. He had not done it for God. He had done it because the alternative—watching her die, watching the small, crushed body of her child be torn apart by the second burst—had been a violation of the only rule that actually mattered to him, the rule that he was the one who decided who lived and who died, and he had decided that she would live.

The number *1* pulsed there, too.

Caelen tried to touch the interface. He reached out with his mind, expecting a barrier, a warning, a shock of divine energy. But when his intent brushed against the translucent lines, they responded with a subtle shift, highlighting a section of the grid that had been dormant. It was a prompt, or perhaps a cursor. It waited for input.

He realized, with a cold clarity that cut through the lingering fog of his death, that this was a tool.

It was not a reward. It was not a punishment. It was a set of controls.

The interface allowed him to see the mechanics of his own soul’s progression. It showed him the raw materials of his existence, the currency he had been minting without knowing the mint. He had always believed that his instincts were enough. He had always believed that his training, his aggression, his ability to read a room and manipulate the people in it, was the ultimate advantage. He had been right, in a way. His instincts had been efficient. But they had been blind.

He could see the levels now. He could see the paths. And, for the first time in his life, he could see the cost of moving along them.

The interface remained static, waiting. It did not urge him to act. It did not tell him which path was right or wrong. It did not care whether he chose to hoard power or to give it away. It simply displayed the options. The two columns stood side by side in the gray mist, equal in their presentation, equal in their reality.

Caelen felt a strange sensation in his chest, where his heart used to beat. It was not relief. It was not fear. It was the feeling of a soldier who has just been handed a new weapon, one that is far more complex and far more dangerous than anything he had carried before. He had spent his life believing that he was the operator, the one who pulled the triggers. Now he was looking at the blueprint of the machine itself.

He did not reach out to touch the numbers again. He did not try to manipulate the grid or force the system to reveal more. He simply observed. He watched the faint pulse of the lights, the steady rhythm of the data. The interface was a mirror, and for the first time, he could see the reflection clearly.

The void was quiet. The mist swirled slowly around the edges of the geometric structure, indifferent to the presence of the soul within it. Caelen remained suspended in the center, his consciousness anchored to the floating grid. He was no longer fighting the dissolution. He was no longer trying to hold onto the edges of his mind. He was waiting.

He knew, with a certainty that felt as solid as the lines of the interface, that this was only the beginning. The numbers were low. The paths were open. And the choice, for the first time, was his alone to make.

He looked at the two columns, one on the left, one on the right, and felt the weight of the decision settle into his bones. He did not choose. He did not act. He simply held the image in his mind, the dual-path progression, the neutral mechanism of his existence.

The lights continued to pulse. The mist continued to swirl. And Caelen waited, suspended in the gray, with the tool of his own soul floating before him, ready to be used, ready to be directed, ready to change everything he had ever known about power, sacrifice, and the cost of being alive.
