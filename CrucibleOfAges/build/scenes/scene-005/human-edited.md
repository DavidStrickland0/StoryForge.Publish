The scarred man moved with a slow, grinding gait that seemed to drag the air around him. He did not look at the guard who had paused; his eyes were fixed on Caelen as if he could smell the unfamiliar tension in the captive's frame. Scarface stopped near the girl, looming over her without touching her, and extended a hand toward Caelen.

Caelen kept his gaze on the ground between them, refusing to meet those eyes. He understood none of the harsh consonants that rolled into the cell like stones skipping across water, but he saw the man’s posture shift from aggression to something resembling assessment. The guard with the cane remained at attention, a statue of dull obedience, while Scarface leaned in close enough for Caelen to feel the heat radiating off him—a warmth that felt wrong to close to familiar.

The man spoke again. His voice was low, dripping with a rhythm Caelen couldn't parse, yet the cadence suggested authority rather than command with those universal tones you use to sooth a pet or a crying baby. Caelen relaxed at the sound, his muscles screaming in protest from the fresh cut on his back. He wanted to shrink away, to curl into the smallest knot of flesh possible, but the girl’s grip on his arm held him steady. Her nails digging shallow crescents into his skin, an anchor he didn't fight against.

Caelen swallowed hard, the motion painful in a throat that felt too tight. He was naked, exposed, and sore, yet there was a strange clarity in his mind as he watched these figures who ruled this place. They were not monsters; they were simply the gears of a machine he did not understand his actions. Scarface reached out a hand again, palm up. It was an invitation, or perhaps a command to stand, but Caelen didn't move. He stayed low, one knee pressing into the damp earth, keeping his body positioned between the girl and any potential violence.

The silence stretched, filled only by the distant rumble of the cart outside and the ragged breathing of thirty captives huddled in the gloom. Scarface waited, his expression unreadable behind a mask of soot and old scars. Caelen held his breath, waiting to see if the invitation would turn into a blow or something else entirely. He had no plan, only the instinct to protect the small, trembling life beneath him.

With a gesture and a command from Scarface, The Cane moved back to his position behind purple.

After the cane left Scarface stood offering his hand out once again palm up. Both offering and indicating Caelen should stand.   

Beneath him, the girl began to weep. She made no sound, only small, hitching gasps that vibrated through her chest. Her fingers dug into Caelen’s forearm, nails pressing deep enough to draw blood, anchoring him as if she feared he would dissolve into the stone floor. He looked down at her face, pale and streaked with soot, eyes wide and wet.

She was looking for a savior, or perhaps just someone who understood why she was crying. But Caelen saw only the man in purple sitting near the ledger, his cane tapping idly against the floor, completely absorbed in whatever thoughts occupied him. Scarface stood between them now, a wall of scars and silence.

Every eye in the cell focused on him.

Caelen tried to stand, to push himself upright, but the girl’s grip held him down. He realized then that shielding her had not saved her; it had only made him transparent, exposing every vulnerability to the eyes of those who ruled this place.

With a gentle voice with those same tones of soothing, he comforted the girl until her grip relaxed and he was able to stand. The patience of the cell was astounding. No one had moved no one had said anything they just waited and watched. They acted like they had an endless amount of time.  Caelen had to reflect that is nothing else imprisonment taught patience.

Finally picking himself up he helps the girl beneath him up. Once they are both standing, they turn to Scarface only to see that he left already and is back behind the Mr. Purple.

The air inside the stone walls felt heavier than the gravity pressing down, a weight he felt in his aching back as he stood beside the girl. They stood together now, two small figures against the vast, hazy sky visible through the high barred windows of the pen. She was trembling, but the weeping had ceased; her fingers lingered on his bare forearm before pulling away, leaving red marks where her nails had dug in.

Caelen scanned the perimeter for any shift in the guards’ posture or the man in purple’s interest. The silence stretched, thick with unspoken judgments from thirty other captives who watched them like predators assessing a rival pack. His collar clicked softly as he adjusted his stance, ensuring the girl remained partially within his peripheral shield.

Caelen checked the girl’s face again. Her breathing had evened out, though her shoulders still hunched defensively. He shifted his weight slightly, making sure his broad stance did not crowd her but also blocked any direct line of sight from the guards to her small frame. The metal collar around his neck felt cold and unyielding, a constant reminder that he was property, yet he held no desire to claim ownership of this moment or the girl’s fear.
