The floor caught his knees first. Then his shoulder struck the wall, and for a moment all he could do was keep himself from dropping his full weight onto her.

The girl made a small, strangled sound beneath him. Caelen braced his hands against the stone beside her and tried to lift his chest enough to let her breathe. His back was exposed. He knew it was exposed. Knowing did nothing useful about it.

Something hissed through the air.

The cane landed across his shoulder blades.

His arms folded. Pain flashed white behind his eyes, sharp enough to drive every clever thing he might have said out of his head. His cheek scraped the wall. Under him, the girl flinched as though the blow had reached her too.

He sucked in a breath and got almost none of it.

Bad idea. Very bad idea.

The thought arrived with the absurd clarity of a warning delivered after the accident. He could still move. Roll to the side, curl up, make himself small. He had already done that twice today. There was room beside her.

He stayed where he was.

A hand caught his upper arm and hauled. Caelen's knee slid across the wet stone. For one panicked instant he thought they were pulling him clear so the guard could finish what he had started. He dragged his foot back under him, trying to keep some part of himself between the cane and the girl.

The hand tightened.

A voice cut across the cell.

He understood none of it, but the pulling stopped.

Caelen turned his head enough to see the guard looking past him. The cane was raised again, its end dark where it had struck. Across the pen, the man in purple held one hand above his open ledger. His fingers moved once, impatiently.

The guard answered. Whatever he said was short and hard.

The black tip of the other man's cane struck the floor.

Silence followed.

Caelen waited for the next blow. His back had begun to throb in time with his pulse. He couldn't tell whether the warmth spreading toward his ribs was blood or sweat, and he wasn't about to reach around and check.

The grip on his arm loosened. Boots withdrew a step.

Only then did he realize the girl had both hands clenched around the edge of his forearm. Her nails pressed into his skin. He looked down, searching her face for some indication of what he should do next, but she was watching the cane.

"Yeah," he whispered. "Me too."

The man in purple said something else. This time he was looking directly at Caelen.

Behind him, the scarred man shifted away from the wall.

Caelen tried to straighten. His shoulder refused, and he settled back onto one knee, still close enough to cover the girl if the guard changed his mind.

Apparently he had their attention now.
