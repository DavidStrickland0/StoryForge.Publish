The cart lurched forward, its suspension groaning under the weight of Caelen’s bound body. Every vibration traveled through him like a shockwave, amplified by the unfamiliar density of his new frame. Outside the slats of the cart, the world blurred into streaks of pale yellow. The stone wall he had woken beside slid backward, then vanished entirely, replaced by an endless expanse of hazy horizon and the low, dim sun. There were no mountains, no forests—only a flat, dusty expanse that seemed to stretch forever under the oppressive sky. The terrain was soft, yielding to the wheels, suggesting heavy gravity or simply deep, compacted earth. 

Caelen forced himself to stop struggling against the ropes. Resistance only invited more attention, and he had already been beaten into silence once. He lay still, conserving his energy, listening to the creak of the cart’s axle and the distant sound of hooves on gravel. A figure sat opposite him in the cart, legs crossed, eyes closed as if sleeping or ignoring him completely. Caelen watched the man’s profile noting the calluses on his hands, the practical cut of his tunic, and the lack of any insignia that might indicate rank or faction.

He tried to categorize the sensory input. This wasn’t a dream; there was too much weight here, too much pain. Yet the pain was distant now, dulled by the sheer absurdity of his situation. The air smelled of dust, old leather, and something metallic—blood or rust? He couldn’t tell. His mind struggled to categorize the sounds drifting from outside: harsh consonants and rolling vowels that made no sense in any language he knew.

He shifted slightly, testing the range of motion allowed by the cord binding his wrists. The friction left raw, stinging trails on his skin. He felt heavy, anchored down by gravity that felt stronger than anything he remembered from Earth.
