Caelen’s boots cracked against the gravel as he and his squad moved through the outskirts of the town, the air thick with the scent of rain and the distant hum of generators. He kept his head low, eyes scanning the terrain for signs of movement. Sergeant Miller was ahead, leading the group with a sharpness that bordered on reckless. Caelen had seen him in action before—always the first to charge, always the last to retreat. There was a certain pride in that, a kind of brute loyalty to the mission that Caelen envied but never fully understood. He wasn’t sure if it was the Marine code or something deeper, something that had taken root in Miller’s bones long before Caelen had ever set foot in the squad.

The mother appeared without warning, her hand clutching a small, lifeless bundle against her chest. Her eyes were wide, her breath shallow, and Caelen felt a strange pull in his chest. She was a stranger, a civilian, and yet something about her made him pause. The squad moved forward, Miller’s orders sharp in the air, but Caelen’s instincts screamed at him to stop. The mother wasn’t a target, not in the way they were trained to see. She was a mother, a woman who had lost her child, and Caelen had never been one to ignore the weight of a moment like this.

“Caelen, move!” Miller’s voice cut through the haze, and for a moment, Caelen hesitated. The mother’s eyes met his, and in that instant, he saw the fear, the pain, the raw grief that no training could prepare him for. He stepped forward, shielding her from the gunfire that erupted in the distance. The world blurred, the sounds of chaos drowning out everything else. He didn’t think about the bullet that grazed his side, nor the one that took Miller down. He only thought about the mother, about the life she had lost, and the life she had left.

When the silence settled, Caelen found himself alone, the mother cradling her dead infant in her arms, her eyes wide with disbelief. He didn’t know what came next, but in that moment, he made a choice. He stepped forward, placing himself between her and the enemy, a silent promise that he would not let her face the same fate. It was a choice he didn’t fully understand, but something in him knew it was right. He had always wanted to be more than a Marine, to be someone who mattered, and in that instant, he realized that the only way to do that was to give everything—his life, his future, his very soul—to someone else.

The world faded, and with it, the weight of his choices.
