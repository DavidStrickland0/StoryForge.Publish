# Chapter 16: The Last Note

The stream thinned before anyone said it was thinning.

Elias had counted until the numbers lost their meaning. He knew the space between one pulse and the next by the time it took him to breathe. He knew how much of the temple barrier darkened when a strand of corruption reached it, and how long the gold took to clear. Now the dark patch was smaller.

He waited through five more pulses before calling Elara.

"Look at the temple."

She lifted her head from the shattered quartz. Her eyes narrowed. They waited together.

Another strand moved across the valley. The barrier consumed it almost as soon as it touched.

"Less coming through," she said.

"Have the shards moved?"

She checked the stone wedges. The leather thong held each fragment at the angle where they had left it. The chisel was still beside her knee, wrapped in wet cloth to keep its handle cool.

"No."

Kaelen shifted against the oak. "It's farther away."

Elias turned. The mage had been quiet for so long that his voice sounded strange in the clearing.

"What is?"

"The pressure. I don't know how to explain it. Before, it was right here." He touched his forehead. "Now I have to listen for it."

Maren sat with both hands resting palm upward on her knees. The wet cloths Elias had wrapped around them were beginning to dry. She lifted her chin toward the fissure.

"Does that mean we can go?"

Elara shook her head.

"It means something changed. We check what."

Maren let her head settle back against the tree. "I was afraid you'd say that."

Torin rose carefully. For several steps he walked as though his shoulders still carried the weight of the hammer. He collected it from the mud and laid it beside Maren. The handle had darkened where it crossed the groove. One edge was charred.

"You'll need a new haft," he told her.

"I'll need new hands first."

He crouched to examine them. Maren let him loosen the cloth on her right palm, but when he tried to do the same on the left she drew it away.

"That one stays for now."

"It needs cleaning."

"It can need cleaning for another minute."

Elias understood the distinction. They had spent so long doing whatever the next emergency demanded that refusing one small pain felt like choosing something for themselves.

He left Torin to it and returned to the fissure.

The green light no longer reached the top of the groove. It came from deeper down. Elara held a flat stone near the opening without touching the crystal. Fine grit trembled along its edge.

"Still a vibration," she said. "Still an open channel."

"Can you tell if there's less behind it?"

"Not from here."

She took the bronze map when Elias offered it. They set it on the paving well outside the green light, weighting one corner with his scabbard so it would not shift. Water collected in the engraved channels.

Elara traced the route they had followed down from the ridge. Her finger stopped at the mark for the shrine.

"Here. The feed to the temple runs through this branch."

"And the other branches?"

"They join below it. That's why we chose this place."

Elias remembered the council at the watchfort, the work with the chisel on the ridge, the decision to approach through the ditches. None of it had promised that the whole arrangement would remain sound after they broke its key.

"Could another branch have closed?" he asked.

"Yes."

"Leaving some of it trapped."

"Yes."

Elara did not soften either answer. Elias was grateful for that, though it left him looking at a success he could not yet trust.

Kaelen came toward them before Elias could call him. Torin rose halfway, ready to catch him, but the mage stopped at a safe distance from the groove.

"I can try listening farther down."

"Without pulling?"

"Without pulling."

Elias put the map where Kaelen could see it.

"Tell us what you feel. Don't make it fit the marks."

Kaelen studied the bronze, then closed his eyes. His breathing slowed. The gauntlet remained dull except for a faint warmth along one seam.

For a while there was only the hum and Torin working a knot loose from Maren's bandage. A bird called somewhere above the ridge. Elias had forgotten birds could make ordinary sounds here.

Kaelen opened his eyes.

"Something is moving behind the first branch."

Elara put her finger on the map. "Which one?"

"I don't know its name. The one that turns back under us."

She shifted her finger. "This?"

Kaelen frowned, watching the lines. "Maybe. I can hear the stream we're feeding to the temple. There's something behind it, keeping pace."

Elias felt the hair lift along the back of his neck.

Elara put the map aside long enough to check the largest crystal. She held the chisel over it but did not bring the metal into contact.

"Does it follow the note?" she asked Kaelen.

"I don't know."

"I'm going to touch this. Only touch it. Tell me whether the other thing moves with it."

Elias watched her hand, then the mage's face. Kaelen kept both feet planted well away from the groove. Elara lowered the steel until it met the facet with no audible strike.

The hum changed slightly.

Kaelen lifted his head. "It stopped."

"The pressure?"

"The other part. It knows we're here."

Elara drew the chisel away. The note settled back into its earlier pattern. For a few breaths Kaelen said nothing, staring toward the lower ditch.

"Again," he said.

Elias held up a hand. "Wait. What are we proving?"

"Whether it was a coincidence," Elara answered.

"And if it wasn't?"

She looked at the bare bank beyond them. "Then we know before we try to close the key."

Torin moved toward the narrower part of the ditch. He studied the ground before putting either boot near the water. Maren reached for her hammer and began trying to work the stiff fingers of one hand around its handle.

"No one goes below the opening," Elias told them. "If we have to move, we move uphill."

Elara waited until both had found places where they could get away. She touched the shard once more. Kaelen's breath caught.

This time Elias felt a second pulse through the stone under his feet. It came after the hum, with a short, deliberate delay.

Elara lifted the chisel.

The next pulse came without her touching anything.

Elias drew his sword halfway out and stopped. The metal would not make a thing beneath the city keep its distance. He left the blade loose in the sheath, ready to take out when there was something in reach of it.

"We're done testing that," he said.

Kaelen nodded. The young man had shifted his weight back from the fissure. Elias was glad to see that motion. They could all choose a safer position without calling the choice defeat.

"Something like a second stream?"

"Something like listening."

Torin stood. He drew his sword and went to the edge of the clearing, keeping away from the line of corruption. Maren began to rise too, but Elias held up a hand.

"Stay with Elara. She may need you."

Maren looked at her wrapped palms. She reached for the hammer anyway.

The first movement came in the ditch below the shrine. Water gathered against one stone, then climbed over it in a thick black roll. Elias drew his sword. He stayed between the moving water and Elara rather than going down to meet it.

"Torin. Left."

The soldier had already seen it. He moved to a place where the ditch narrowed between two standing stones. Another black roll broke against the lower wall. Something pale rose inside it, bent, and found a purchase on the bank.

An arm. Too long, with fingers that reached into the mud like roots.

Elias could not see a face. He did not need one. He had watched limbs like that repair themselves around a sword wound.

"Don't chase it," he said.

Torin planted his feet at the stones.

"I remember."

The hand closed around the lower edge of the bank. As the body behind it climbed, the first strand of corruption veered away from the temple. It bent toward the ditch.

Elara saw it.

"It's taking the flow."

Kaelen clutched his gauntlet. "It found us."

Elias glanced across the valley. The temple barrier remained bright, but nothing was arriving to feed it. Below the shrine, the shape in the water grew darker.

He had no clean blow that would solve this. The steel at his side was as ordinary as it had been in the courtyard. He could slow the thing. He could not cut out whatever gave it another body each time one failed.

"Can we send it back?" he asked Elara.

"I can change the note."

"We just broke a stone doing that."

"I know."

She put her bandaged hand over the chisel. The crystal fragments hummed unevenly. A thin line of green light ran from the groove toward Kaelen's wrist.

He took a step toward it.

Elias caught his shoulder.

"You said you would listen."

"It will come here if I don't answer."

"It is already coming. Tell us where the pull starts. Don't give it more to pull on."

Kaelen stopped. His face had gone tight with the effort of doing nothing.

In the ditch, Torin struck at the climbing hand. The sword drove it off the bank. Black water sprayed his boots, and he took two quick steps back before it could reach above the leather.

The fingers reappeared. This time there were two hands.

Maren set her hammer aside. She could not swing it well enough to risk a strike beside Elara. Instead she knelt at the scholar's back, pressed her forearms against her shoulders, and anchored her as she had in the first phase.

"Say when," she told her.

Elara looked at Elias. "We don't need a higher note. It followed the open connection. We need the connection pointed where it belongs."

She touched the smallest shard. It had tilted slightly. The stone flake under it was sinking into the mud at the edge of the paving.

Elias knelt beside her. He could see the pressure of her hand moving the leather sling rather than the crystal. If she shifted the whole assembly, the opening could widen again.

"Which side?"

"Under the point. Hold it there."

He put his sword away and used both hands to brace a flat stone beside the shard. His side throbbed. He leaned farther forward anyway, giving Elara room to lift the sunken wedge.

Torin struck again. The blade rang against the ditch stone. Elias heard him curse and did not look away from the crystal.

"Kaelen. Tell her when it changes."

The mage stood above them, one hand gripping the dull brass at his wrist.

"It's turning toward me."

"Tell her when it turns away."

Elara drew the shard up the width of a nail. The vibration sharpened. Maren held her steady, the scholar's coat bunching under her forearms.

"More," Kaelen said.

The stream split. One thread continued toward the ditch. The other reached toward the temple barrier and fell short.

"More," he repeated.

Elara moved the crystal again.

Elias felt the note fit. It happened in his teeth before he saw anything. The roughness in the hum vanished, leaving one low sound through the paving.

The dark stream snapped toward the temple.

Below them, the shape in the ditch sagged. Torin thrust it away from the bank. This time the hand did not return. The black water drew back along the stones, following a narrow trail of corruption toward the open channel.

At the temple, violet light spread across the barrier.

Elias tried to see its edge. It grew wider than any stain since the quartz had broken.

"Hold," he said. "Hold this note."

Kaelen's mouth moved. No sound came out. He put both hands against his ears and stayed where he was.

A deep call rose from the city.

Elias had heard it once before, escaping through the fields with a map against his ribs and Kaelen leaning on his arm. It rolled over the lower ground and seemed to come through the roots of the oak.

The thing in the ruins knew what was happening to its supply.

Torin returned from the ditch, breathing hard. His sword carried pale scraps that dissolved before they reached the ground.

"More of them?" Elias asked.

"Nothing I can see."

"Then watch Kaelen."

Torin moved behind the mage. He did not take hold of him. He stood close enough to catch him if he fell.

Elara settled the replacement wedge. Elias kept the flat stone steady while she retied the leather. Only when the knot held did she take her hands away.

The stream remained pointed toward the temple.

Maren withdrew her forearms from Elara's shoulders. Her bandages had darkened again. She sat heavily, watching the barrier with her mouth open.

"Is it taking it?"

Elias watched the violet edge. It had stopped growing. Gold gathered around it, pressing inward from both sides.

"Yes."

The deep call came again. This time it broke in the middle, leaving a rumble that wandered through the valley and died against the ridge.

Nobody spoke while the gold cleared the last of the dark patch.

Torin went down two steps to inspect the edge where the hands had climbed. Elias watched him closely. The soldier stopped before the water and used the end of his scabbard to turn a pale scrap caught between two stones.

It came apart.

"Nothing holding it together," he said.

"Don't put it in your pack."

Torin looked back at Elias. "I wasn't about to."

"I'm saying it anyway."

He smiled briefly, then nudged the remaining scrap into the water. The current carried it away without a hand rising behind it.

Maren watched the last of it go. "You struck it four times."

"I know."

"Only the last strike held."

"Because the flow changed."

She looked toward the temple. "That's what we tell the fort. No one decides the answer is to send more swords."

Elias nodded. He was still remembering the claw in the courtyard, the horrible moment when a sword buried in corrupted flesh had given its owner no more control than a hand sunk into mud. The things were dangerous for reasons steel could not reach. He wanted the account at the watchfort to retain that fact after someone began retelling the victory.

Elara drew a small mark beneath her sketch of the shards.

"This one slipped," she said. "Not a failure of the whole arrangement. One support sinking."

"Write down the mud," Maren told her. "It will still be there when people come to admire your cleverness."

Elara looked at the flake beneath the smallest shard and gave a tired nod. She added another mark.

Kaelen remained where Torin had caught him. He was watching the ditch rather than the temple. Elias crossed to his side.

"Are you hearing it still?"

"The note?"

"The thing behind it."

Kaelen waited before answering. "No."

"You don't need to search for it."

"I wasn't."

The answer had an edge that Elias accepted. He had asked the same question often enough. It was becoming another way to make Kaelen keep himself connected to what he wanted to escape.

"Sit," he said. "I'll ask Elara about the key. If you notice a change without trying, you tell us."

Kaelen went to the oak. Torin came with him, and the two settled on opposite sides of the roots. The soldier loosened his sword belt so it would not press against his hip when he sat.

Elias went back to the fissure. The repaired wedge was stable. Elara had put the chisel down and held her cut hand open, letting the cold air reach the bandage.

"Can you leave it alone now?" he asked.

"For a while. It doesn't need someone moving it constantly. That was why I wanted the positions written down."

"Then we leave it alone for a while."

They had not planned for a battle in the ditch, and they had no reserve of strength set aside for the end of the work. Resting beside an open channel felt foolish. Refusing to rest until someone's hands shook too much to close it would be worse.

Elias found a stone where he could sit and still see the temple. He kept his sword beside his knee. Maren remained near Elara, within reach of the leather sling if the crystal moved.

They took turns watching. When Torin stood, Elias drank one careful mouthful and shut his eyes long enough to stop seeing dark threads whenever he blinked. When he opened them again, the barrier had cleared another small stain.

The valley had become quieter. The hum was still there, but the heavy answering pulse had ceased. Water ran through the ditch below them, slipping around the stones Torin had defended. It looked like water again. Elias did not ask anyone to drink it.

He watched a small leaf drift past the place where the black hands had climbed. The current carried it to the lower bend, where it caught against a pebble. For a long time the leaf remained there, turning with the ordinary force of the stream.

Nothing came to claim it.

Afterward, the stream narrowed to a line no thicker than a cord.

Kaelen lowered his hands. Torin caught one elbow when the mage's knees bent.

"Still there?" Elias asked him.

Kaelen listened. He shook his head, then corrected himself.

"The channel is. The other thing isn't answering."

Elias rose slowly. The wet paving had left a dark mark across both knees. He crossed to the dead oak and looked down the valley, searching for a fresh movement in the fog.

He could see farther now. The lower ditches lay open, with pale water running through them. Beyond, the city's broken walls stood clear against the slope. The immense shape above the ruins remained partly hidden, but it no longer moved.

Elias could not tell whether it was dead, empty, or waiting. He would not make the distinction from this distance.

"We wait," he said.

Maren made a rough sound that could have been agreement or a laugh.

They waited while Elara cleaned the cut on her hand with the last clean water they could spare. Torin kept watch down the ditch. Kaelen sat against the tree again, his eyes open, his right hand lying flat rather than curled around the gauntlet.

Elias watched each thread reach the temple and disappear. The gaps grew longer. The thin cord became a wispy strand, then a series of separate dark flecks.

No one touched the key.

There was no messenger from the watchfort, and no hidden defense awakening beneath the foundations. The same barrier they had trusted in the first phase continued to consume what the same channel supplied. Eventually it received less than they could see.

Elara crawled to the map. She made three marks on a scrap of paper, recording where the wedges and shards stood.

"I want to try lifting the chisel," she said.

Elias checked Kaelen before answering. The mage had been listening without helping for a long time.

"Do you feel anything growing behind it?"

"No."

"If that changes, say it. Don't stop it yourself."

Kaelen nodded.

Elara lifted the chisel from the largest shard. Its point caught briefly on a rough edge, and everyone stiffened. She freed it without moving the crystal.

The hum persisted for a moment, then lowered.

The dark flecks ceased.

Across the valley, the barrier remained gold.

Elias held up his hand. No one moved. He counted the time by his breathing, letting each breath finish before taking the next. He kept the hand raised long after it began to ache.

When he lowered it, Elara looked at him.

"No surge," he said.

"We haven't closed the groove."

"Then that is next. Slowly."

She set the chisel against the longest shard again. The note returned, faint and rough. There was no answering ribbon of corruption.

Kaelen leaned forward.

"Nothing is coming after it."

Elara took the chisel away. She let the tone die, then moved the largest wedge inward a little. The crystal lowered in its groove. Nothing struck the stone from beneath.

They repeated the test. Each adjustment left a smaller opening. Each time Elara paused, Kaelen listened, Torin watched the ditch, and Elias checked the temple. Maren steadied the leather so it could not drag the smaller shards out of place.

The work was slow enough that Elias began to fear he was using caution to postpone the answer. But he remembered the sound of the first crystal breaking. They had earned the right to finish this carefully.

At last the largest shard settled against the original carved edge. Elara touched it with the chisel. There was a dull tick and no note.

She tried the two smaller pieces.

Neither piece answered.

Kaelen came near enough to kneel beside the map. He extended his left wrist toward the groove and stopped well outside it. The brass stayed dull. He shut his eyes, listened, and opened them again.

"The pressure is gone."

"Not quieter?" Elias asked.

"Gone. The way it was before I heard anything in the city."

Maren looked across the valley. "And the wall?"

The temple barrier still stood, pale and steady. The light no longer rippled with arriving corruption. It held what it had consumed without calling for more.

Elias put his palm on the paving beside the map. He felt cold stone, wet grit, and the sting of a scrape he had not noticed getting.

No pulse came through it.

"Now," he said, scarcely loud enough for the others to hear. "Now we can stop."

Maren covered her face with the back of her wrist. Torin lowered himself onto the paving. He had carried his sword for so long that the empty shape of his grip remained after he put it away.

Elara stayed beside the closed groove. Her chisel lay across her knees. She looked at it as if she had forgotten what ordinary metal was for.

Kaelen laughed once, and the laugh turned into a breath he could not quite control. Elias knelt beside him and put a hand on his back. For a while the young man shook without saying anything. No one asked him to stand.

When he could speak, he looked up at Elias.

"I thought if I let go, it would take all of you."

"You let go," Elias said. "We're here."

Kaelen looked at Maren, then Torin, then Elara. He counted them with his eyes. Elias let him finish.

The relief did not mend Maren's hands or close the wound under Elias's bandage. The city below remained broken. What lay behind the quiet silhouette in the ruins still needed a cautious inspection by people rested enough to survive it.

But the reservoir they had opened no longer pressed against the shrine. The barrier had consumed the released corruption. The creatures in the ditch had collapsed when the stream was drawn away from them, and nothing had risen to replace them.

That was enough to take back to the watchfort. It was more than a promise to bring back someday.

Elara gathered the loose quartz dust from the paving into a fold of cloth. She left the three working shards where they were, still wedged in the closed groove. Her marks on the paper showed their positions. The bronze map would show anyone she brought here how to reach them.

"Nobody pulls these out," she said. "Not to see what they do. Not as souvenirs."

Maren looked at the ruined hammer handle.

"I have my souvenir."

Torin helped her wrap the head so it would not strike the stone when she stood. Elias put the map back inside his clothes. It was heavier with water and colder than before.

Kaelen stood with Torin's hand beneath his elbow. He waited, testing whether his legs would support him. Then he let go.

Elias watched that too.

They did not leave immediately. They walked to the upper bend of the ditch and waited once more, far enough from the shrine that Kaelen could not be lending it unconscious help. The closed groove remained dark. The temple barrier kept its pale outline. The water ran around the stones in an ordinary current.

Elara made another mark on her paper.

"It is holding without us."

Elias looked up the ditch toward the ridge. Water lay in the places where they had stepped coming down. A strip of daylight reached the upper stones.

"Then we tell the watchfort," he said. "We tell them what worked, what broke, and what not to touch."

"And after that?" Maren asked.

He glanced at her hands, at Kaelen's drawn face, and at the chisel Elara was finally putting into her satchel.

"Someone else takes a watch."

They began the climb. Torin went first, testing the slick stones before committing his weight. Maren followed, her hammer secured at her belt. Elara kept the folded diagram inside her coat. Kaelen walked beside Elias, slowly enough that neither of them had to pretend the effort was easy.

At the first turn, Elias looked back.

The shrine was still visible. The three small shards caught a little daylight at the lip of the groove. No green light answered from underneath.

He turned uphill again, keeping his uninjured arm within Kaelen's reach, and left the Gate of Silence behind.
