The stranger paused at the edge of the gate as the mist clung to the cobblestones, concealing a village of black spires that pierced the twilight sky with silent, watchful eyes.

# Chapter 2: The Fog's Green Teeth

The rain hammered against the cobblestones of the ruined market square, a relentless drumming that masked the heavy breathing of Elias Thorne and the figure beside him. The city had been under the shadow of the Blight for three weeks; the air tasted of copper and rot, and the few remaining buildings stood like jagged teeth against the bruised sky.

Elias pulled his hood tighter, the damp wool scratching at his neck. He felt the familiar weight of his leather armor pressing against his shoulders, a burden that usually served to steady him but today felt heavier than before, as if the earth itself was trying to crush him in its grip. His boots crunched on debris, each step a deliberate calculation rather than a rush toward victory.

"Stay close," he murmured, his voice low to avoid drawing attention from the scavengers who still prowled the streets. "The fog rolls in early today."

Beside him, Kaelen moved with a fluid grace that belied his exhaustion. The young man had taken on too much recently, and Elias could see it in the slight tremor of Kaelen's hand when he adjusted the brass gauntlet on his wrist—a relic from an era before the Blight, heavy and ornate but useless against the corruption spreading through the world.

Kaelen didn't answer immediately. He was staring ahead, his eyes darting across the shadowed alleyways. "I can see movement," he whispered finally. "Three figures. They're not human."

Elias stopped, his heart giving a hard thump against his ribs. The rules of the world had been clear enough: if they moved, they would be detected. If they were not human, their intentions could not be assumed to be friendly.

"Keep your eyes forward," Elias said, his voice firm despite the fear that coiled in his stomach. "We do not engage unless we are certain."

The fog thickened, swirling around the legs of the two travelers. It wasn't just weather; it was a symptom of the Blight, a living shroud that obscured vision and distorted sound. Elias could feel the pressure building, a physical weight in his chest that made it hard to breathe. He knew the mechanics of this danger better than anyone: the fog reduced visibility to nearly zero within ten paces, and any attempt to move quickly or make sudden noises would trigger an attack from whatever lurked within.

"Move," Elias commanded softly. "Silently."

They stepped into the mist, their movements slow and deliberate. The fog seemed to part around them for a moment, revealing a narrow passage that led deeper into the ruins of the old temple district. But as soon as they passed through, the darkness swallowed them again, leaving only the faint glow of Kaelen's lantern to cut through the gloom.

"Stay sharp," Elias warned, his hand resting instinctively on the hilt of his sword. The blade was dull from disuse, but it would do better than nothing against a creature that might be trying to claim another soul.

Kaelen nodded, though his eyes remained fixed on the path ahead, scanning for any sign of life. "I hear something," he said, his voice barely audible over the rain. "Not footsteps. Something... dragging."

Elias frowned. The sound was faint, but persistent. It came from behind them, or perhaps from the fog itself. Either way, it suggested that whatever was hunting them had already noticed their presence.

"Back," Elias said, his tone sharp. "We need to regroup and figure out what we're dealing with."

They turned, facing the direction of the sound. The fog pressed against their faces, cold and heavy, making breathing difficult. Elias felt a chill that had nothing to do with the weather; it was the kind of fear that settled in the bones, the kind that made you want to run even when you knew there was no escape.

"Can you see anything?" Elias asked Kaelen, his voice steady despite the turmoil within him.

Kaelen shook his head slowly. "No. Just shapes. They're moving toward us."

The dragging sound grew louder, closer. The air grew colder, and the smell of rot intensified, filling their throats with a cloying sweetness that made Elias's stomach turn. He knew what this meant: the creatures were closing in.

"Ready," Elias said, his hands tightening on his sword hilt. "Hold your ground."

The fog parted once more, revealing two figures emerging from the mist. They were humanoid but wrong—too tall, too pale, their skin stretched taut over bones that seemed to protrude beneath. Their eyes glowed with a sickly green light, and they moved with a jerky, unnatural gait that suggested they were not entirely under their own control. These were not standard zombies; they were **Blight-Touched**, creatures corrupted by the same energy that fueled Kaelen's magic, making them highly resistant to physical damage but vulnerable to specific magical weaknesses.

Elias stepped forward, raising his sword. "Stay back!" he shouted, though the words meant little against the looming threat.

The creatures did not hesitate. They lunged forward, their movements fast and brutal, driven by some instinctual hunger that Elias could only guess at. Kaelen raised a hand, chanting softly as a small puff of smoke erupted from his palm, but it was too weak to stop them. The creatures ignored the smoke, pressing on with a terrifying speed.

Elias parried the first strike, the cold steel biting into his armor as the creature's claw slashed past his guard. He felt the force of the blow, the impact driving him backward, and he knew that this was not just a physical fight; it was a test of survival.

"Kaelen!" Elias shouted over the roar of the rain and the snapping of bone. "Do something!"

Kaelen hesitated, his eyes wide with fear as the creatures closed in on them both. He took a breath, then began to chant again, this time summoning a **Shield** of light that shimmered around him for a moment before fading away. While the spell provided a defensive buffer, it did not stop the physical assault; however, the magical barrier confused the undead, causing their jerky movements to falter as they struggled against the arcane energy.

Elias saw one of them lunge at Kaelen, its green eyes fixed on the young man's throat. He moved with **maximum effort**, driven by adrenaline that pushed his legs beyond their natural limits, driving his sword into the creature's chest with a cry that echoed off the crumbling walls. The blow drove the monster back, but not for long. Another came around him, and Elias felt the wind of its attack ruffle his hair, sending it flying in the direction of Kaelen.

"Get behind me!" Elias roared, parrying another claw that skittered across his pauldron, leaving a trail of sparks where metal met bone. The creatures were resistant to normal steel, so Elias had to rely on his agility and Kaelen's support.

Kaelen stumbled back, clutching his side as he felt the sting of a graze from a claw that managed to slip past the **Shield**. He looked up at Elias with a mixture of awe and terror. "I can't do much more," he whispered, his voice trembling. "They're too fast."

Elias gritted his teeth, his arms burning from the strain of blocking their attacks. He knew he couldn't hold them forever, but for now, he would try to keep them at bay, relying on the confusion caused by Kaelen's magic to create an opening.

"Focus!" he snapped, his eyes never leaving the creatures. "Find a way to slow them down!"

Kaelen closed his eyes and began to chant again, this time with renewed intensity. A faint hum filled the air, and Elias saw the ground beneath them begin to shift, small fissures opening up as if something were trying to pull them under. The **Blight-Touched** creatures hesitated for a moment, their movements faltering as they struggled against the strange phenomenon Kaelen was creating. They were not immune to magical disruption, even if they were tough physically.

"Go!" Elias yelled, pushing Kaelen toward the exit of the narrow passage. "Move now before they recover!"

Together, they ran, the creatures chasing after them with desperate hunger. The rain continued to fall, washing over the ruins and the survivors, but for a moment, there was hope in their flight, a chance that perhaps, if they were quick enough, they could escape this nightmare entirely.

# Chapter 3: The Teeth of the Fog

The narrow passage offered no mercy, its walls closing in like the jaws of a trap. Elias’s lungs burned, the air thick with the coppery stench of the Blight and the sour odor of his own exertion. He could hear Kaelen gasping behind him, the young man’s footsteps faltering as the fatigue from earlier finally caught up to the adrenaline that had carried them through the ambush.

"Keep moving!" Elias shouted, though his voice was ragged. He didn't look back; he couldn’t afford the distraction. The dragging sound had been replaced by a wet, slapping rhythm—the feet of the Blight-Touched hitting the cobblestones with unnatural speed. They were gaining.

The passage opened abruptly into a larger courtyard, overgrown with twisted vines that pulsed with a faint, sickly luminescence. In the center stood the ruins of a fountain, its stone basin cracked and filled with stagnant, black water. Beyond it lay the remains of the temple’s main hall, a dark maw in the fog.

Elias skidded to a halt near the edge of the courtyard, his boots slipping on the wet moss. He spun around, drawing his sword again, though he knew the steel was little more than a toothpick against these things. "Kaelen! The fountain!" he yelled, pointing toward the structure. "Can you create a barrier there? Something solid?"

Kaelen stumbled into view, his face pale and slick with rain and sweat. He clutched his side where the claw had grazed him; a dark stain was already spreading across his tunic. His eyes were wide, fixed on the shadows behind Elias. "I... I don't know," he stammered, his breath coming in short, sharp bursts. "The fog... it’s interfering with the flow. My magic is erratic."

From the mist, three shapes emerged. The Blight-Touched moved with that same jerky, disjointed gait, their elongated limbs swinging wildly. Their green eyes locked onto the pair, glowing brighter as they drew nearer. They didn't rush immediately; they circled, testing the perimeter, driven by a predatory intelligence that was far more terrifying than mindless hunger.

Elias stepped between Kaelen and the creatures, his sword raised in a defensive stance. "Buy us time," he ordered, his voice steady despite the tremor in his hands. "I’ll hold them off."

"Elias, you can’t kill them with that blade!" Kaelen cried out, dropping to one knee as pain flared in his side. He fumbled at his belt, pulling out a small, crystalline vial—a reserve of condensed mana he had been saving for an emergency. "I need a few seconds to stabilize the connection. If I try now, I might blow us both apart."

One of the creatures lunged, its claws scraping against the stone where Elias had stood a moment before. Elias sidestepped, swinging his sword in a wide arc that connected with the creature’s shoulder. The blade bit deep, but instead of severing the limb, it seemed to sink into the corrupted flesh without resistance. The creature didn't even flinch; it simply grabbed the hilt of the sword with its free hand, its grip like iron.

Pain shot up Elias’s arm as the creature twisted, trying to wrench the weapon from his grasp. He gritted his teeth, bracing his legs against the cobblestones, but he was being pulled forward. The other two creatures closed in, flanking him, their movements synchronized in a way that suggested a hive mind or at least a shared instinct.

"Kaelen!" Elias roared, slamming his free hand into the creature’s chest, trying to push it back. The impact felt like hitting wet leather. "Now!"

Kaelen slammed the vial against the ground and shattered it. A pulse of blue light erupted from the shards, washing over the courtyard. For a second, the fog recoiled, burning away in streaks of vapor. The Blight-Touched screeched, a sound like grinding metal, and stumbled back, their green eyes flickering as if blinded.

Elias didn't hesitate. He kicked the creature holding his sword in the knee, hearing a satisfying crack as the joint gave way. He wrenched the blade free, spinning to face the other two. "Run!" he commanded Kaelen, shoving him toward the temple entrance. "Get inside and seal the door if you can! I’ll cover your retreat!"

Kaelen hesitated, looking back at Elias with horror as the creatures began to recover from the magical shockwave. Their bodies twitched, repairing themselves at a visible rate, the corrupted flesh knitting together under the skin. "Elias, no—"

"Go!" Elias shoved him harder this time, sending Kaelen stumbling toward the dark doorway of the temple hall. He turned back to face the nightmare, his sword raised high. The rain hammered down, washing the blood from his armor but doing nothing to cleanse the rot in the air. He knew he couldn't win this fight, but if he could draw their attention long enough for Kaelen to find shelter, maybe—just maybe—they would both survive the night.

The creatures shrieked again and charged, a wave of pale limbs and glowing eyes. Elias met them with a roar, swinging his sword in a desperate arc, buying seconds with every inch of ground he lost. Behind him, Kaelen disappeared into the darkness of the temple, leaving Elias alone in the rain, facing the teeth of the fog.

# Chapter 4: Seal Broken, Shadows Rise

The rain fell harder now, drumming a frantic rhythm against Elias's breastplate that sounded like a thousand tiny hammers striking an anvil of cold steel. His sword was heavy in his hand, the weight amplified by the adrenaline flooding his system and the tremor from the creature's iron grip minutes ago. It had felt like holding a knife through wet cardboard until he had finally snapped it.

The first Blight-Touched didn't die. As Elias stared, horrified, the flesh around the wound where his blade had bitten in retracted with a sickening squelch. The corrupted tissue knit itself together, sealing the hole and regenerating a fresh layer of green, pulsating muscle that seemed to stretch beyond its original boundaries. Then, the creature's head snapped upward, its jaw unhinging slightly wider than before, revealing rows of jagged, needle-like teeth that gleamed in the dim light.

"Resilient," Elias muttered, the word tasting like ash in his mouth. He parried a lashing claw that had swept low, sending shards of stone chipping into the air. The creature's next strike connected with his shoulder plate, driving him back until his boots hit the wet moss hard enough to bruise. Pain bloomed white-hot across his pauldron, but he didn't falter. He couldn  afford it.

The second and third creatures were already upon him, their movements a blur of distorted limbs that defied human anatomy. One extended three arms at once, its hands clutching Elias's throat while the other two slashed for his legs. Elias gasped, choking as his neck met cold, wet fingers. His vision blurred, the edges of the world darkening into shadowy static—the telltale sign of the Blight beginning to seep past his defenses.

He slammed his free hand against the creature's chest again, this time aiming for a knot of black energy swirling in its center. The impact threw the monster backward, but instead of a groan of pain, there was only a wet splash as it hit the water-filled fountain basin. From inside the black water, something else emerged—a writhing mass of viscera and bone that seemed to have no face, only endless maws opening and closing in unison.

Elias realized then that he wasn't fighting three monsters anymore; he was fighting a single hive mind that had shed its form to strike him multiple times at once. The realization hit him with the same force as the creature's claws, cold and paralyzing.

He tried to summon his sword again, but his muscles betrayed him. His arm felt heavy, as if pulled by an invisible weight. A tendril of black slime lashed out from the water, wrapping around his ankle. He could feel it tightening, constricting bone against bone. *No,* he thought, gritting his teeth until they ached. *Not yet.*

Behind him, the heavy wooden doors of the temple hall creaked open. A shadow moved through the rain-slicked courtyard, blocking out the moonlight. It was Kaelen, but not the young man Elias had known moments ago. The boy's face was pale and drawn, his eyes wide with a terror that had somehow curdled into something else—something hard and calculating. He held a staff topped with a glowing crystal, its light struggling against the encroaching fog.

"Elias," Kaelen said, his voice low and steady. "Don't you dare die on my watch."

The creature in the water surged forward, ignoring Elias entirely as it now moved toward Kaelen. The Blight-Touched didn't see people; they saw energy sources. And Kaelen's mana was a beacon.

Elias roared, using every ounce of remaining strength to kick free from the slime binding his ankle. He twisted in mid-air, bringing his sword down hard on the creature's exposed neck. The blade struck true this time, severing a long tendril that retracted into the water with a hiss. But the main mass didn't fall; it dissolved into a cloud of green smoke and reformulated itself into a towering, faceless monstrosity that rose above the fountain, its hands reaching for Kaelen like grasping talons.

"Kaelen!" Elias screamed, throwing himself between the monster's outstretched claw and the boy. The blade bit deep into the creature's flesh, tearing through green, corrupted tissue, but Elias held firm, his body becoming a shield of iron and will. "Your turn!" he gasped, blood dripping from his lip onto the cobblestones.

Kaelen nodded once, a sharp, decisive motion. He raised the staff, not with the hesitation of earlier, but with the focus of someone who had finally accepted that time was running out. The crystal at its tip flared white-hot, casting long, dancing shadows across the courtyard.

"I didn't save you," Kaelen whispered, his voice echoing strangely in the damp air. "I just made sure I'd have to do it again."

With a sound like tearing silk, the staff erupted into a sphere of concentrated light that slammed into the ground between Elias and the Blight-Touched. The shockwave knocked Elias sideways, sending him skidding across the wet stones, but it also seemed to disrupt something fundamental in the air itself. The fog around them began to swirl violently, pulling inward as if drawn by a gravitational well.

The towering monstrosity shrieked—a sound that was no longer human or animal, but the grinding of gears and the tearing of metal—and its form began to destabilize. It let out a final, guttural roar that vibrated through the soles of Elias's boots, sending shivers up his spine. Then, with a sickening crunch, it collapsed into dust, scattering across the courtyard like ash caught in a windstorm.

The other two creatures, which had been circling, froze. Their green eyes dimmed, flickering out one by one until they were just hollow sockets of blackness before falling over, their limbs stiffening in unnatural angles. The immediate threat was gone, replaced by the suffocating silence of the defeated, save for the rain and the ragged breathing of two survivors.

Elias lay on his side, his sword still in his hand, soaking him in blood that smelled of ozone and decay. He watched as Kaelen stood over the smoking remains of what he had called a monster moments before. The boy's face was twisted in effort, sweat dripping from his nose into his mouth.

"You're bleeding," Elias said, his voice hoarse, trying to sit up against the dizziness that threatened to pull him under. "You used mana on yourself."

Kaelen ignored him for a moment, staring at the empty space where the creature had stood. Then he looked down at his hands, which were trembling so violently they could barely hold the staff. He dropped it. It clattered softly into the mud.

"I think," Kaelen said quietly, "I might have broken the seal on my own connection."

Before Elias could process the words, a new sound cut through the quiet—the distant, rhythmic thrumming of something massive moving through the earth beneath them, followed by the crunch of stone under heavy feet that echoed unnaturally close to where they lay. The fog began to thin slightly in the center of the courtyard, revealing not just the ruined temple, but a structure far larger and darker than anything they had seen before, rising out of the mist like a jagged tooth waiting to snap shut.

Elias pushed himself up on his elbows, wincing as pain radiated through his entire body. The adrenaline was fading, replaced by a creeping exhaustion that felt different from the fatigue of running or fighting. It felt heavier, colder.

"Where are we going now?" Elias asked, his voice barely audible. "The fog is clearing..."

Kaelen didn't answer immediately. He picked up his staff again, wiping away blood and sweat with a trembling hand. His eyes were fixed on the emerging structure, wide with a mixture of awe and dread that matched Elias's own internal state.

"We don't go anywhere," Kaelen said finally. "We wait."

"But it's still coming," Elias pointed out, glancing at the massive silhouette rising from the mist. The ground beneath them shook again, a low vibration that rattled teeth in his jaw. "And we're not exactly safe here."

Kaelen looked at him, then down at their scattered belongings—the broken vial, the severed tendrils, the torn armor plating of Elias's shoulder. He nodded slowly.

"Safe doesn't mean much when the Blight is already inside us," he said. "But we're still alive. For now."

He turned toward the temple entrance, his movements stiff and deliberate. Elias followed, limping slightly on one leg, the weight of the day's events settling around them like a second skin. The rain had stopped, leaving the courtyard slick and reflecting the pale, uncanny glow of the emerging tower in its depths. Somewhere in the distance, a new kind of noise began to rise—not the screech of monsters or the crackle of magic, but the slow, deliberate thud of footsteps that seemed to echo from inside the earth itself.

They had won the battle, but as Elias watched Kaelen step into the shadows of the temple doorway, he realized they had only just begun the war.

# Chapter 5: Temple of Ancient Seals

The temple doors creaked shut behind Kaelen, leaving Elias to stand in the courtyard, the remnants of the battle scattered around him. The distant thudding grew louder, and the ground trembled again, sending ripples through the air that seemed to carry a whisper of something ancient and menacing. Elias’s heart pounded in his chest, his breath coming in ragged gasps as he tried to catch his bearings.

He limped toward the temple entrance, the pain in his shoulder and leg a constant reminder of the fight he had just endured. The air inside the temple was thick with the scent of incense and a faint, lingering trace of the Blight. As he stepped inside, the cool stone walls seemed to close in around him, the dim light barely piercing the shadows.

Kaelen was already inside, leaning against one of the pillars, his chest rising and falling with rapid breaths. Blood trickled down his arm, staining the fabric of his shirt. He looked up as Elias entered, his eyes a mix of determination and fear.

"Did you see?" Kaelen asked, his voice strained. "It’s coming."

Elias nodded, his mind racing. "We need to figure out what we're up against. Whatever's out there, it's not just another Blight-Touched. It's something else."

"We don't have much time," Kaelen said, his eyes narrowing as he looked toward the darkened corners of the temple. "We need to find a way to seal it off, or at least buy us some time."

"Seal it off?" Elias echoed, his brow furrowing. "How?"

Kaelen pointed toward a set of stone tablets hanging on the wall, inscribed with intricate runes and symbols. "There might be something here that can help. The temple was built to contain the Blight. There could be ancient magic we can use."

Elias nodded, his mind focused on the immediate task. They needed to find a way to fortify the temple and prepare for whatever was coming. The stone tablets seemed like a good place to start.

They approached the tablets, and Elias could feel a pulse of energy emanating from them, like a heartbeat from the temple itself. He ran his fingers over the runes, tracing the intricate designs etched into the stone. Kaelen stood beside him, his staff held loosely in one hand.

"These runes are ancient," Kaelen said, his voice barely above a whisper. "They predate even the oldest stories of the Blight. They might hold the key to what we need."

Elias nodded, his mind working through the possibilities. "We need to decipher them, and fast. There’s a ritual here, something we can use to reinforce the temple’s defenses."

Kaelen nodded, his eyes flickering to the tablets. "We’ll need to gather the materials from the temple. There are spells and relics hidden within its walls. We have to find them before it’s too late."

Elias felt a cold shiver run down his spine as the distant thudding grew louder, the ground trembling once more. They began to work together, Kaelen focusing on the tablets while Elias scoured the temple for the necessary materials. The air inside grew colder, the shadows creeping closer, as if the temple itself was alive and responding to their efforts.

Time seemed to stretch and compress, each moment both urgent and interminable. The distant thudding grew louder, the ground trembling again, this time stronger than before, sending a wave of panic through Elias.

"Kaelen, we need to hurry," Elias said, his voice strained. "It’s almost here."

Kaelen nodded, his face a mask of concentration. "Almost done. Just a few more moments."

Elias could feel the weight of the impending danger bearing down on them, the air thick with the anticipation of something terrible breaking through. He glanced toward the temple entrance, the distant thudding now a relentless drumbeat, growing louder by the second.

"Kaelen, it’s coming," Elias said, his voice urgent. "We have to finish this now."

Kaelen’s eyes flashed, and he completed the final incantation, the tablets glowing with a fierce light. The air around them vibrated with a sudden surge of power, and a barrier of shimmering light materialized, stretching across the temple entrance.

"Done," Kaelen said, his voice weary but resolute. "It should hold for now."

Elias nodded, his heart pounding. They had managed to fortify the temple, but the danger was far from over. The distant thudding grew louder still, the ground trembling violently. They could hear the distant roar of something massive approaching, its presence a tangible force of destruction.

"We need to prepare for the worst," Elias said, his voice steady. "We need to find a way to confront whatever’s coming."

Kaelen nodded, his eyes dark with determination. "We will. But for now, we need to rest and regroup. We can’t fight at our best if we’re exhausted."

Elias nodded, the exhaustion settling over him like a heavy cloak. The ground trembled again, and the distant thudding grew louder, a relentless drumbeat signaling the approach of something ancient and terrifying.

They huddled together in the temple, the barrier holding back the darkness for now. But the battle was far from over. The temple was their last refuge, and they knew they had to be ready for whatever lay beyond the barrier. The fight was just beginning.

# Chapter 6: The Barrier Drank the Blight

The barrier flickered across the temple doors.

It had begun as a sheet of pale light. Now it was scarcely more than a translucent veil. Several of the runes feeding it had already gone dark. The ones that remained pulsed unevenly, each flare weaker than the last.

Beyond it stood a shape too large for the entrance.

Elias saw fragments whenever lightning flickered through the ruined courtyard: a leg plated in black growth, a shoulder dragging against the temple facade, a cluster of green lights that might have been eyes. Each step shook dust from the ceiling. The barrier shivered in answer.

Kaelen stared at the bending light. Blood had soaked through the cloth at his side and spread down to his belt. His hands shook around the staff, but his expression had gone still.

"How long?" Elias asked.

"I don't know."

"Guess."

The creature pressed one black-plated limb against the barrier. The impact shook grit loose beneath Elias's boots.

Green corruption spread across the pale surface in branching veins.

Elias expected the light to fail.

Instead, the barrier drank the Blight.

The green veins sank into it and vanished. The creature's limb smoked where it touched the light. Black growth peeled from its flesh in long strands and dissolved into the barrier, each strand making the pale radiance brighter. A dead rune beside the doorway flashed white and came alive. Then another awakened above it.

The creature pulled back with a grinding shriek.

The barrier stood stronger than before.

"Not long enough," Kaelen said.

Elias turned from the entrance and studied the sanctuary. Broken benches lay in uneven rows beneath a vaulted ceiling. Rainwater ran down the walls and gathered in shallow channels cut into the floor. At the far end, behind a stone altar, three narrow archways opened into darkness.

They had spent their remaining strength building a wall without understanding what it was. It had looked weak because it had been starved. The moment the Blight touched it, the ancient magic had begun consuming the corruption and using it to restore itself.

That did not make the temple safe. The barrier could feed on the Blight, but Elias had no intention of waiting to learn whether it could consume the creature faster than the creature could tear down the building around it.

"Then we leave."

Kaelen looked at him as though he had spoken in another language. "The temple is containing it."

"The temple contained it before we arrived. Something still broke the first seal."

"If we run and the barrier fails—"

"Then standing here won't make it stronger. The Blight already is."

Another blow rolled through the chamber. The barrier flashed as it stripped more corruption from the creature. A third dormant rune awakened, but a hairline fracture appeared in the stone around it and crept through a carved spiral.

Kaelen's gaze followed it. "I broke the seal in the courtyard. I felt it open through me. If that thing came because of what I did—"

"Then dying beside the door won't repair it."

Elias crossed to him and took hold of his shoulder. Kaelen flinched, but Elias did not release him.

"Listen to me. We came here looking for an answer. We found one. The Blight has roots older than this city, it can turn the dead into weapons, and whatever is outside can command those weapons. We also learned that your magic can hurt it. That's more than anyone outside the fog knows. If we die here, the knowledge dies here too."

Kaelen glanced toward the barrier. The immense silhouette leaned close. Several green lights shifted behind the radiance, arranging themselves around a central darkness.

"Leaving it feels like surrender," he said.

"It is surrender. For tonight."

The admission tasted bitter, but Elias forced himself to say the rest.

"We are outmatched, wounded, and nearly out of supplies. Courage doesn't change arithmetic. We leave, find the others, and become strong enough to choose the terms next time."

For several breaths, only the rain and the strained hum of the barrier answered him.

Then Kaelen nodded.

"There may be another way out," he said. "The water channels."

Elias looked down. Four shallow grooves crossed the floor, carrying rainwater from openings near the ceiling toward the altar. Rather than pooling there, the water vanished beneath it.

"A drain?"

"More than that. These temples were built around springs. Water used in the binding rites had to enter clean and leave without crossing the same ground. There should be a lower passage."

"Should be?"

"Ancient architecture wasn't part of my training."

"I thought ancient everything was part of your training."

Despite himself, Kaelen gave a tired, breathless laugh. "That was the plan."

They moved behind the altar. Its front had been carved with kneeling figures pouring water into an open hand. Elias pushed against the stone, but pain tore through his injured shoulder. His arm weakened at once.

Kaelen wedged the end of his staff into the seam along the floor. Together they leaned against it. Stone scraped over stone. The altar shifted half an inch, then stopped.

The creature struck again.

This time the barrier did not bend. Green vapor boiled from the creature and streamed into the runes. The light brightened until Elias could no longer look directly at it. Cracks sounded from the courtyard as plates of corruption split away from the thing outside.

Kaelen stared past Elias at the doorway. "It isn't resisting the Blight. It's feeding on it."

"Then why was it dying when we arrived?"

"Because nothing had touched it. The seal starved while it waited for something to contain."

This was not simply a fortress built to keep corruption outside. It was a trap built to draw corruption in and consume it.

Elias set both hands against the altar.

"Again."

They pushed. His shoulder screamed. Kaelen's boots slipped on the wet floor, and the wound at his side began to bleed freely. The altar shifted another inch. Beneath it, Elias heard the hollow tumble of loose pebbles.

"There's space under there," he said.

Kaelen lowered the staff and drove it deeper into the seam. "Then help me prove it."

They heaved once more. The altar tilted, balanced for a terrible instant, and toppled onto its side with a crash that echoed through the sanctuary.

Beneath it lay a square shaft and a descending stair.

West, to Greyhaven.

North, if necessary, to the watchfort.

Back to Mira, Bren, Sera, Tomas, and anyone else who had survived.

He and Kaelen disappeared over the hill before the sun rose, carrying a map of the darkness beneath the ruins and a promise neither of them spoke again.

They would return.

Elias woke because someone was arguing about a bowl.

"It has a handle," Maren said. "That is what the handle is for."

"Your palm is bandaged," Torin answered.

"My other palm is also bandaged. Perhaps you should drink it for me."

She caught Elias looking and offered him a tired smile.

"Everyone is improving."

Maren took the bowl by its handle before Torin could answer. She managed one mouthful, then put it down carefully. Neither of them mentioned the small victory.

Elias sat up slowly. The new bandage pulled, but the wound beneath it felt less hot. His boots stood together at the end of the bench. Someone had scraped most of the mud from them.

He looked toward the board where they had left the map.

It was still there.

Elara followed his glance.

"They made a copy of the route last night. The bronze hasn't left the room."

"The watchers?"

"Returned before the change of watch. Another pair went out. The officer said he would tell us when both reports were in."

Elias began to swing his legs down, then stopped.

Elara watched the change without commenting on it. She held out a cup instead. The water was cool and tasted of nothing unusual.

He drank it all.

They ate before anyone came to question them. Bread, broth, and a little cheese were set on the board beside the diagrams. Torin moved the food away from the bronze before Maren could accuse him of protecting a map more carefully than a person.

Kaelen woke while Elias was fastening his boots. The mage looked first at his bare wrist, then at the folded coat beside him. The gauntlet had not moved during the night.

"How long?" he asked.

"Long enough for breakfast," Elias said.

Kaelen sat up. He listened for a moment. His expression changed when the only noises that answered were voices from the yard and Maren breaking a piece of bread badly with the sides of her hands.

"Nothing, apparently."

He took the bread Maren offered him.

"Both watches saw the same thing," he said. "The temple light remained steady. No new fog came out of the channels. Nothing moved from the ruins toward the ridge."

"As instructed."

"And no one touched the key?"

"No one went near it."

She let herself read the rest of the report.
