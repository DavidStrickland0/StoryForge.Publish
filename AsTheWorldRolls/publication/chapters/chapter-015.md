# Chapter 15: The Broken Valve

Kaelen drew his first breath of the second phase, and the brass on his wrist began to warm.

Elias watched the temple barrier across the valley. A dark ribbon lifted from the shrine's fissure and stretched toward the pale wall. Where it touched, violet light spread along the barrier and then faded to gold. The corruption disappeared. The next ribbon followed before the first patch had cleared.

"Slower," Elias said.

Kaelen opened his eyes. "I'm barely pulling."

Elara's chisel trembled against the quartz. Maren tightened her grip on the scholar's shoulders, but the tremor remained. It ran through the crystal, along the steel, and into the stone beneath their boots. Elias felt it in the old wound at his side.

The first phase had given them a rhythm. This was different. Each pulse arrived early, pressing against the last, until the separate beats began to merge.

Elias moved away from the oak far enough to see the line between Kaelen and the shrine. A low bank hid the bottom of the fissure from where he had been standing. He could watch the temple or the opening at the shrine; he could not watch both properly from one place.

"Maren, tell me what the stone does. Torin, his hands. Elara, don't answer unless you need to."

Maren bent closer without loosening her hold on the scholar. "It has moved against the steel. Not out of the groove. Just turned."

"Is it opening farther?"

"I can't tell."

Elias would have preferred any other answer, but she had given him the useful one. He went back to his line of sight. The barrier was brightening unevenly. A dark strand struck low; before that mark had disappeared, another reached higher.

In the courtyard, Kaelen had spent nearly everything to give them a chance to run. Here they had arranged him at a safe distance, assigned people to catch him, and called it a controlled operation. Elias wondered how much those arrangements mattered to someone alone with the pressure behind his eyes.

"Kaelen. Say something."

"What?"

"Anything that isn't about the channel."

The young man stared at him. His right hand had curled over the gauntlet at his left wrist. For a moment Elias thought the request had not reached him at all.

"My boots are full of water," Kaelen said.

"Mine too. Keep noticing that."

Maren turned her head just enough to look at Elias. "What are you doing?"

"Making sure he's still here."

She looked back at Kaelen, then down at Elara. "Your coat is caught under my knee," she told the scholar.

"I know. Leave it."

It was almost an ordinary exchange. Elias used it to hold his own attention on the five people in the clearing instead of on the impossible amount of corruption underneath them.

He had promised Kaelen they would return with help. They had returned with Maren, Torin, and Elara, with a method tested on a ridge, and with enough courage to begin. What they had not returned with was any certainty about how much strain the method could survive.

That was the difference he should have kept in mind when he raised his hand.

Torin saw him looking and gave a brief nod. His free hand was open, ready to take Kaelen's arm, while his sword remained between them and the lower ground. Elias found himself wishing the soldier had a simpler enemy to stand against. A thing with a body and a direction would have let him be useful without watching a friend's face for signs that he was being lost.

"If he stops answering," Elias said, "bring him back from the shrine. Don't wait for me."

Kaelen turned sharply. "I'm answering."

"Yes. I want you to keep answering."

"Taking me away won't close the channel."

"We'll deal with the channel."

The mage shook his head. "You can't promise that."

Elias had no objection to the truth of it. He had promised enough things because saying them was easier than describing what he did not know.

"No," he said. "I can't. But I'm telling Torin to take you out if you can't get out yourself. That is something we can do."

Torin nodded again, this time to Kaelen. The young man looked toward the dark ribbon in the air and did not agree. He did, however, move his hand away from the gauntlet. Elias took the distinction and returned his eyes to the temple.

The next strand left a dark edge that cleared more slowly. Elara moved the chisel a fraction. Maren steadied her before the motion could become a slip.

"No higher," Elias said. "This is as far as we go."

Kaelen's shoulders sank with what might have been relief. The stream continued to arrive too fast.

Torin moved closer to Kaelen. He kept his sword turned away from the mage and reached out with his free hand.

"Can you ease back?"

"I'm trying."

Kaelen's fingers had curled into his palm. The gauntlet's seams showed green. Elias had seen that color in the eyes of things that should have stayed dead.

He raised his hand, closing two fingers. Elara glanced toward him without lifting the chisel.

"Take it down," he said. "Back to the first note."

She eased the pressure. The chisel's vibration lowered, and for a moment the ribbons narrowed. Elias saw the barrier consume the last dark patch. He allowed himself one breath.

Then the quartz clicked.

It was a small sound, almost lost beneath the hum. A white line appeared inside the crystal. Elara's eyes followed it.

"Maren."

Maren caught her wrist as the line split into three.

"Away from it!" Elias shouted.

The stone burst. Maren dragged Elara sideways, pulling both of them off their knees. A fragment struck the dead oak beside Elias. Another glanced from Torin's boot. The chisel fell with a bright ring and slid toward the fissure.

Kaelen folded forward. Torin caught him across the chest and lowered him rather than letting him strike the ground. His sword scraped a line through the mud before he could put it away.

The sound from the shrine stopped.

The dark ribbon did not.

It thickened, spilling from the fissure in uneven folds. Out across the valley, the barrier flared violet. Elias waited for the light to clear as it had before. It remained dark at the point of impact.

"The temple," he said. "Elara, look."

She pushed herself up on one elbow. Blood ran from a shallow cut across the back of her hand. Maren pressed a strip of cloth against it, but Elara pulled away long enough to see.

"Too much at once."

"Close the channel."

"The stone was controlling how it opened. We haven't got the stone."

Elias looked down at the fissure. The quartz had occupied a groove near its upper edge. The groove was still visible, and beside it the chisel lay on a narrow shelf. Below, green light moved in a depth he could not judge.

He reached for the chisel.

"Don't put your hand in there," Elara said.

He stopped. The leather over his knuckles had already tightened with heat. He drew his sword instead, crouched beside the shelf, and hooked the chisel's handle with the tip. On the second attempt he brought it within reach of the wet ground. Maren picked it up through the cloth she had meant for Elara's cut.

The dark patch on the temple barrier widened.

Torin had Kaelen sitting against the oak. The mage's eyes were open, but he seemed unable to fix them on anything.

"Kaelen," Elias said. "Hear me. Can you stop the pull?"

The answer came after a long pause. "There isn't a pull."

"Then what is doing this?"

Kaelen looked toward the green fissure. "We opened it."

Elias had been treating the young man as the one part of the mechanism he could order to stop. The channel beneath the shrine was open whether Kaelen answered him or not.

Maren knelt at the groove. She held her hammer by the head, testing the length of its wooden handle against the gap where the crystal had rested.

"I can cover some of this."

"With the hammer?"

"With the haft. Unless you've brought a door."

She laid the wood across the narrow mouth of the groove. The escaping ribbon bent around it. The hum changed, dipping lower, and the pressure under Elias's teeth eased.

At the temple, a small gold patch opened inside the violet stain.

"There," he said. "Hold that."

Maren planted her boots and leaned into the hammer. Almost immediately, one heel slid in the wet stone.

Torin left Kaelen propped against the tree and came to her side. He took the handle below her hands and braced his shoulder beneath hers. Between them, they kept the wood from rolling out of the groove.

It did not seal the opening. A thinner stream continued toward the temple. Elias watched it arrive, darken the gold, and vanish.

One pulse. Then another.

The barrier was clearing again.

"Enough," Elias said. "Don't press farther."

"Tell the thing underneath us," Maren answered through her teeth.

The hammer shook in their hands. Elara crawled close enough to inspect the exposed ends of the groove, keeping outside the line of the escaping corruption.

"You've narrowed the opening," she said. "But the gap changes every time it moves."

"We noticed," Torin said.

Elias wiped water from his face. They needed the same narrow opening without two people holding it steady. His gaze went to the quartz scattered across the paving. Most of it was dust. Three pieces were large enough to show the original facets.

"What about those?"

Elara followed his hand. "They're broken."

"They still came out of the same stone."

"That doesn't make them a new one."

He had no answer. Across the valley, the last violet stain thinned to a pale edge. The barrier could manage this amount. The problem lay in the hands trembling beside him.

Elias crouched by Kaelen. He kept his voice low.

"Can you sit?"

Kaelen nodded and immediately swayed.

Elias put a hand behind his shoulder. "That's sitting with assistance. Try again."

The mage swallowed. He leaned into the bark, planted both feet, and held himself there. Sweat stood on his upper lip despite the cold water running from his clothes.

"Better. Can you hear the channel?"

"Too clearly."

"You don't have to touch it. Just tell us if it changes."

Kaelen looked at the gauntlet. His right hand crept toward it, then stopped.

"When I pulled, something pulled back."

Elias waited. He could hear Maren shifting her grip, trying to find a place on the wood that had not grown hot.

"Not a voice," Kaelen said. "Not the thing from the forest. I don't know what it was. But I couldn't tell which direction was mine anymore."

"Then you won't pull again."

"If we can't close it—"

"We find another way. You listen. You tell us what you can. That is your part for now."

Kaelen studied him as if the permission required a trick somewhere in it. Elias kept his hand on the young man's shoulder until the tension eased.

"All right," Kaelen said.

Elias stood. His side protested sharply. He pressed the bandage through his shirt, waited for the pain to settle, and crossed to Elara.

She had arranged the three largest shards on a flat stone. One was long and narrow, split cleanly along a facet. The other two still carried part of the curve that had fitted into the groove.

"These edges match," she said.

"Can you put it back together?"

"No. There are pieces missing. And if we ask it to bear the same strain, it will break again."

She turned the long shard. A faint vibration reached Elias through the paving when its point faced the fissure. When she laid it flat, the vibration ceased.

Elara froze. She repeated the movement slowly.

Maren drew a breath. "Whatever that is, do it again."

Elara looked toward Kaelen. He had raised his head.

"I heard it," he said. "A little of the first note."

She brought the shard nearer the groove, keeping it between finger and thumb. The gauntlet warmed faintly. Elias saw a line of pale light move inside the crystal, then flicker out.

"It's answering the channel," Elara said. "I wasn't making a sound."

"Then don't make one yet," Elias told her. "Find out what happens when you take it away."

She withdrew her hand. The note disappeared. The stream remained as narrow as Maren and Torin held it.

Elias looked from the shard to the matching pieces. A broken key could not turn a lock. But they had already turned this one. They only needed to keep it from opening farther.

"Could the pieces hold the note together?"

Elara shook her head at first. Then she looked again.

"Perhaps. If I can find positions where they answer each other. Not where they used to fit. Where they work now."

"How do we keep them there?"

She searched the paving around the fissure. The stone had chipped where the crystal burst, leaving loose flakes along the groove. Elara picked one up and tested its thickness beneath the long shard.

"Wedges. Small ones."

Torin nodded toward the pouch on his belt. "There's cord in there. A leather thong too. You'll have to take it yourself."

Elias unfastened the pouch without disturbing Torin's stance. Inside were a coil of cord, a length of leather, and two folded strips of cloth. He laid everything beside Elara. They would have to make these materials sufficient. He had no spare crystal hidden in his pack, and no messenger waiting beyond the fog.

He checked his own pack anyway. Not for a miraculous answer, but for what he was forgetting. Wet cloth, a little food, the water flask, the map inside his coat. He set the pack under the oak, where Kaelen could reach it, and brought the food back to Maren and Torin.

"Can either of you eat?"

Maren gave him a look.

"Not a meal. A bite."

He broke a piece small enough for her to take without moving her hands. She chewed with her head turned away from the fissure, then shook it when he offered another. Torin accepted one piece and asked Elias to put the rest where Kaelen could find it.

Elias wanted to count that as looking after them. Instead he watched the hammer vibrate against their palms. They needed to stop holding it. Everything else he did was a way of making the delay more bearable.

Elara tested the groove with the chisel, keeping its tip above the green opening. She brought it back to the paving and shook her hand to loosen a cramped finger.

"Stone underneath," she said. "A cut for the original crystal. It isn't a hole we can plug with any object the same size."

Maren pressed the hammer a little harder.

"Then what am I plugging?"

"Part of the opening. Not the whole seal. That's why some still passes it."

Elias knelt at Elara's side. The edge of the hammer lay across the top of the groove, not deep inside it. A thin green gap showed below. The wood shuddered when the pressure rose, and the stream briefly widened each time.

"Can we wedge the hammer here instead?"

"If we drive it down, it might close too much."

"And if we tie it where it is?"

She put two fingers under the leather thong and lifted it toward the carved projection.

"It's already warm. The cord would lie over the mouth. We could use up our hands to tie something that burns through as soon as we step away."

Torin glanced at the narrow opening. "Two people holding a lever isn't much of a long-term plan either."

"I know," she said.

The answer came too sharply. She looked down, turning the long quartz shard between her fingers. Elias had heard the same edge in his own voice whenever someone asked him to fix a danger he was already staring at.

He picked up the wet cloth beside her.

"No one is asking you to rebuild the city. One opening. One change we can test and undo."

Elara looked at the pieces again. "And if the change cannot be undone?"

"Then say so before we try it."

For a moment she sat still. Then she set the cloth flat beside the groove and placed the matching pieces on it. Dust clung to the blood at her knuckles. She wiped it away without widening the cut.

"I need to know whether the note is only in the largest piece," she said to Kaelen. "Or whether they can each answer it."

He listened while she turned the first shard. She moved the second toward it and stopped before it touched.

"That one too," Kaelen said. "But it's different."

"Higher?"

"I don't know. It feels closer."

Elara marked a place on the paving. "Then we won't call it higher yet."

Elias looked toward the temple. The dark stream was still narrow. Gold moved around each stain before the next arrived. He could give them a little time to find out what the words meant.

Torin shifted one boot. His heel had begun to sink into the mud. Elias put a flat stone behind it, and the soldier carefully transferred his weight without letting the hammer rise.

"Thank you."

Maren looked at the first shard. "Would you really take him away if it came to that?"

Elias knew she meant Kaelen. He could see the mage pretending not to listen against the oak.

"Yes."

"Even if it leaves this open?"

"If losing him closed it, we might be having a different argument. We don't know that it would. We just know he'd be gone."

Maren studied him, then turned back to her grip. "Good."

He went to Kaelen and crouched beside him again. The young man had eaten one small piece of bread. He held the rest loosely in his right hand.

"You heard that?"

Kaelen nodded.

"Then don't make us prove it. Tell Elara when the note is wrong. Tell Torin if you can't stay upright. That's how you help us now."

The mage looked at Maren's wrapped palms. "I didn't mean to leave them holding it."

"Nobody thinks you did."

Elias waited until Kaelen looked up, rather than taking the silence as agreement. The young man swallowed the food and shifted against the bark so he could see the shards.

"All right," he said. "I can do that much."

Maren moved one hand away from the hammer. The palm was red.

"How long?" Elias asked her.

"Less than I would like."

He wet a cloth in the drainage ditch and folded it over the wood beneath her hand. She set her palm on it with a hiss, then leaned in again. Torin's jaw had gone rigid.

"I can take your place," Elias told him.

Torin glanced at the bandage under his arm. "You can barely bend."

"I can stand."

"Watch the temple. That's the job only you are doing."

Elias returned to the oak. He hated the sensible answer more than a refusal.

The temple barrier had become a pale gold shape beyond the lower fog. It remained in its own place, across the valley. Nothing had grown outward from it to shelter the shrine. The five of them were still on bare ground beside an open fissure, with corruption moving between them and the defense they were trying to feed.

He memorized the pattern of the light. A narrow dark mark arrived. The gold dimmed around it, gathered, and consumed it. There was a pause before the next mark. If the pause shortened, he would know they were losing control.

Elara laid the first shard into the edge of the groove. She placed a stone flake beneath it and held both with the tip of the chisel.

The note rose.

Kaelen pressed the heel of his hand to his forehead.

"Too sharp."

She moved the flake. The shard tilted outward.

"There," he said. "Lower."

Maren's hammer rolled a fraction under her palms.

"It moved," she warned.

"I saw," Elara said.

Elias watched the dark mark strike the barrier. This time the gold cleared before the next one reached it.

"The flow is slower. Keep the angle."

Elara wedged a second stone flake into place. She took her chisel away by the width of a finger. The shard remained steady.

Torin exhaled. He had not released the hammer, but the strain in his neck eased.

"One piece," Elara said. "I need the other two."

The second shard seemed promising until she lowered it beside the first. Both began to rattle. Green light brightened between them.

"Out," Kaelen said at once.

Elara snatched it away. The rattling stopped. Elias saw the stream thicken, and his hand went up.

"Hold where you are. Nobody makes another change."

They waited through three pulses. Maren and Torin kept the gap narrow. The first crystal stayed in its stone cradle. When the barrier cleared, Elias lowered his hand.

Elara sat back on her heels. The cut on her hand had reopened. Blood had gathered along the shard she was holding.

"Cloth first," he said.

"I'm almost—"

"Cloth."

She surrendered her hand. He wrapped it as tightly as she would permit, making sure she could still close her fingers. The bandage left a small dark crescent where the cut continued to seep.

"I put them too close," she said. "They're interfering."

Elias looked at the pieces. "Can they work farther apart?"

"They might. But I need to stop pretending I know the answer."

She set the second shard down, away from the first, and picked up the third instead. Its broad face caught the pale light from the temple. Elara turned it slowly, watching the fissure rather than the crystal.

"Kaelen, tell me when you hear it."

He leaned forward. Torin looked over his shoulder, but the mage stayed seated against the oak.

"Now."

Elara marked the position with her chisel. She turned the shard a little more.

Kaelen shook his head.

She brought it back to the mark. The note returned. Elias felt it through the wet sole of one boot: two rough tones, then a brief interval in which they fitted together.

"Not enough," Kaelen said. "But it's close."

Maren's arms began to shake visibly.

"Elias. I need to move."

He hurried to her side. Torin held while Elias put his shoulder beneath Maren's elbow, taking a little of her weight. The wound at his side stretched, and for a moment the clearing blurred.

"Lift your right hand," he told her.

She did. He worked a second wet fold of cloth under it. She flexed her fingers, set them on the new pad, and slowly transferred her weight back to the hammer.

Elias stepped away before his knees failed. Maren watched him recover his balance.

"You are not taking my place," she said.

"I heard Torin the first time."

"Then listen the second."

He returned to the temple's line of sight. Elara had fixed the third shard. The first and third stood apart, with an empty space between them for the last piece.

She did not fill it immediately. She tested the remaining crystal above the gap, below it, and turned on its side. Each position changed the note. Twice Kaelen told her to stop. Once Elias raised his hand because the stream reached the barrier faster than the gold could clear.

At last she found an angle that left the corruption moving in separate threads rather than folds. She used the chisel to hold the crystal there while Elias placed a stone flake beneath its lower edge.

The leather thong from Torin's pouch went around the outer shards, anchored to a projection of carved stone. It held them in position; it did not cross the green opening or try to restrain the pressure underneath. Elara pulled it tight and tied it with shaking fingers.

"Do not let go yet," she told Maren and Torin.

They did not.

She placed the chisel against the largest shard. Before touching it, she looked toward Elias. He checked the temple, then Kaelen.

The mage met his gaze. "I'll only listen."

Elias lowered his hand.

Elara tapped the crystal once.

The sound was poor compared with the clear note from the whole stone. It began in two places, wavered, and almost died. Then the smaller shards answered. The vibration spread through the groove and settled into a rough, continuous hum.

At the temple barrier, the dark stream drew thinner.

Maren's shoulders dropped a fraction. "Torin?"

"I feel it."

Elias counted five pulses. Each cleared cleanly. The intervals between them remained even. He counted another five before he dared speak.

"Ease the hammer out. A little."

Torin shifted first, releasing some of the pressure from his shoulder. Maren let the handle rise the width of a fingernail. The hum dipped, but the stream stayed narrow.

They stopped and waited.

The crystal held.

"Again," Elias said.

By the time the hammer came clear of the groove, Maren's eyes were wet with pain. She lowered it into the mud and kept both hands open. Torin backed away from the fissure one careful step at a time, then sat abruptly where he stood.

Elara remained kneeling beside the shards. She did not smile. She watched the leather, the stone wedges, and the dark passage beneath them for anything that might move.

"Is it done?" Torin asked.

"No," Elias said.

Across the valley, the barrier took another narrow strand of corruption and consumed it. The reservoir continued to supply more. They had neither emptied it nor closed the channel.

But Maren and Torin no longer had to hold the gap with their hands, and Kaelen was still himself.

Elias helped Maren down beside the oak. Her wet palm left a mark on his sleeve. Elara kept watch over the replacement key while Torin drank from their water flask. When he offered it to Kaelen, the young man took it without looking toward the fissure.

That small refusal mattered to Elias more than the gold across the valley.

He crouched beside the three of them.

"We keep this rate," he said. "No more widening until we know what it costs. Elara watches the key. Kaelen listens and tells her what changes. I watch the temple. The rest of you get your hands back."

Maren looked at the abandoned hammer.

"And when it breaks again?"

Elias looked at the shards, each sitting at a different angle, all answering the same channel.

"Then we stop before it takes someone with it."

He returned to the oak and settled his back against the wet bark. The first pale gold strand reached the barrier and disappeared. He began counting again.
