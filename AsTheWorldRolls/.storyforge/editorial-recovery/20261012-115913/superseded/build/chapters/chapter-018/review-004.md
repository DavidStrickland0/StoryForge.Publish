# Chapter Review

## Overall Assessment

The chapter contains significant financial logic defects regarding the contract terms and payment structure. It also exhibits a progression defect by re-performing the 'negotiation' work established in Chapter 17 without advancing the specific outcome (the modified contract) that was reserved for later chapters.

## Findings

- [FinancialLogic] The chapter claims Silas offers a choice between two contracts: Option A (standard rate) and Option B (higher fee). However, the text states Elias leaves the second document on the desk while waiting for an answer. This implies he has not yet accepted or rejected the terms, yet the narrative treats the 'choice' as if it were already resolved or irrelevant to his current state of mind.
  Evidence: "Elias stood up. He picked up the main contract, but he left the second document on the desk. 'I'll wait for your answer,' he said." ... "Silas nodded. 'You'll have it by nightfall.'" The text fails to reconcile how Elias can leave a signed or unsigned offer on the table while simultaneously claiming satisfaction and control without addressing the financial commitment required to accept Option B.
- [FinancialLogic] The chapter describes the second document as offering a 'significantly higher fee' that covers equipment, injuries, and debts. Yet, Elias leaves this document on the desk without signing it or confirming acceptance. The narrative then moves to market purchases where Maren buys specific items (iron-bound rope, sulfur paste) using implied funds. There is no reconciliation of how the party possesses sufficient liquid cash for these purchases if they are still negotiating a contract that supposedly covers their expenses.

## Revision Guidance

- Clarify the financial state: Explicitly state whether Elias has accepted Option B or if he is still negotiating. If he hasn't signed, explain how they afford immediate market purchases (e.g., using existing savings, not contract money).
- Advance the negotiation: Instead of re-doing the initial meeting, show the *result* of the previous chapter's promise. Have Silas deliver the finalized modified contract or a rejection letter, forcing Elias to make a final decision.
- Fix the cash flow: Ensure the market purchases in the middle section are funded by established savings or wages, not by the 'higher fee' from an unsigned contract.
- Adjust the pricing and availability of specialized supplies (iron-bound cable, sulfur paste) to reflect their scarcity and high value in a Blight-corrupted world.
- Introduce financial tension or negotiation when purchasing these critical items to emphasize their importance and cost.
- Clarify whether the party has sufficient funds for these purchases or if they are taking on debt/credit.
- Ensure the merchant's inventory levels reflect the scarcity of anti-magical materials.
