# Chapter Review

## Overall Assessment

The chapter contains significant continuity errors regarding the party's location and state after Chapter 17. It also repeats completed narrative work from Chapter 16 without advancing the plot.

## Findings

- [Canon] The chapter places the party in Silas Vane's office and then the market district, contradicting the established fact that they are at the Gilded Tankard tavern. Chapter 17 ends with them inside the tavern; Chapter 16 confirms they are resting there.
  Evidence: Silas led them through a narrow alleyway... into a separate building that looked more like a small counting house than a merchant's stall.
- [Canon] The chapter depicts Torin and Maren buying supplies in the market, but Chapter 16 establishes they are resting at the shrine location after stabilizing the barrier. They have not left to shop.
  Evidence: Torin moved through the crowd... Maren walked beside him... They turned and walked back toward the tavern.
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter repeats the 'mosaic frequency' breakthrough from Chapter 16 as if it were a new discovery, rather than acknowledging it as an established fact.
  Evidence: "Math is reliable," Elara said... People are not. But Torin's logistics and Maren's... practicality... are reliable in their own way.
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter resolves the caravan contract negotiation (Option A vs Option B) which was explicitly reserved for later chapters in the arc targets.
  Evidence: "Very well. I will speak to the alchemists. If they agree to a handover at the Watchfort gate, then we will sign this modified contract."
- [NarrativeDirection] The chapter ends with the party leaving the tavern to go on the road, contradicting the arc target which requires them to finalize terms and prepare before departing.
  Evidence: "They turned and walked back toward the tavern... They had the supplies. They had the plan. Now they just needed to make it work."
- [FinancialLogic] The party purchases high-cost specialized equipment (iron-bound cable, sulfur paste, heavy greaves) without any established source of funds or payment method, contradicting their status as impoverished adventurers who previously struggled to afford basic necessities.
  Evidence: Torin gathered the bundles, checking the weight of each one. He had secured the iron-bound rope, the sulfur paste, the heavy greaves, and a crate of high-grade flint. It was enough to keep them alive... Maren purchased two hundred feet of the iron-bound. And the matching shackles. The ones with the double-lock mechanism.
- [FinancialLogic] The party accepts a contract offering 'daily wages' and 'lodging and meals covered' but immediately spends their entire available cash on supplies before receiving any payment, leaving them with zero buffer for immediate expenses like food or transport.
  Evidence: Silas slid a heavy leather folio across the desk... Daily wages, lodging and meals covered, and a bonus for delivering the crate intact to the Watchfort. ... Torin gathered the bundles... He had secured the iron-bound rope, the sulfur paste, the heavy greaves, and a crate of high-grade flint.
- [FinancialLogic] The purchase of 'high-grade flint' is financially implausible given the party's established poverty; such specialized magical-grade materials would cost significantly more than standard supplies, yet no funds are mentioned.
  Evidence: Torin gathered the bundles... He had secured the iron-bound rope, the sulfur paste, the heavy greaves, and a crate of high-grade flint.
- [FinancialLogic] The party negotiates a contract with 'standard rates' and a 'bonus' but then immediately departs to purchase expensive supplies without confirming if the contract terms include an advance or credit line, creating a cash flow gap.
  Evidence: 'Standard rates, standard provisions. You protect the crate, I pay you.' ... Torin moved through the crowd... He was not here to haggle over the price of an apple... he was here to secure the logistical skeleton of their upcoming escort.

## Engagement Findings

- The chapter resolves the caravan contract negotiation (Option A vs Option B) which was explicitly reserved for later chapters in the arc targets.
- The chapter ends with the party leaving the tavern to go on the road, contradicting the arc target which requires them to finalize terms and prepare before departing.

## Revision Guidance

- Keep the party at the Gilded Tankard tavern. Do not move them to Silas's office or the market.
- Acknowledge the 'mosaic frequency' breakthrough from Chapter 16 as established fact, not a new discovery.
- Delay resolving the contract negotiation; have them agree to meet Silas tomorrow morning.
- End the chapter with the party resting or discussing strategy inside the tavern.
- Establish a clear source of funds for the market purchases (e.g., selling old gear, using a small advance from Silas, or having hidden savings).
- Clarify if the contract payment is an immediate lump sum or deferred, and adjust the spending accordingly.
- Reduce the cost or quantity of specialized items to match the party's known financial constraints, or explicitly state they are borrowing against future earnings.
