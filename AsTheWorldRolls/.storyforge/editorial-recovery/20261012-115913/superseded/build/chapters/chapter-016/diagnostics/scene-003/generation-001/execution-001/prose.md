It was no longer a roar that demanded attention, but a presence that acknowledged their survival.

Elias leaned heavily against the rough stone of the drainage ditch wall, his bandaged side throbbing with a dull, insistent ache. The heat radiating from the fissure had receded to a gentle warmth, the kind that suggested a hearth fire rather than an inferno. The distributed circuit of quartz shards Elara had arranged within the stone mouth of the fissure was working as intended, filtering the chaotic energy into a manageable stream that the barrier could digest without strain.

"The mechanism holds," Elias said, his voice rough from disuse but steady. He didn't turn to look at Maren or Elara; he kept his eyes on the shimmering curtain of light. "The shards are acting as baffles, creating internal resistance. Kaelen is guiding it, and we're not overloading the seal."

"Before, it felt like we were holding back a flood with our bare hands. Now... it feels like watching water flow through a garden hose. Controlled. Deliberate."

Elara stood near Kaelen, who was still seated cross-legged in the center of the clearing, his eyes closed, his brass gauntlet glowing faintly as he modulated the resonance. She had just returned to her satchel, gathering the leather strips and steel wedges she had used to align the shards. A few larger pieces of quartz remained in her hand, their jagged edges catching the violet light.

"He's doing better than I expected," Elara said softly, her eyes on Kaelen. "The circuit corrects itself when he adjusts his focus. He doesn't have to force it anymore; he just has to listen."

"Good," Elias replied, turning his head slightly toward them. The pain in his side was a constant reminder of the cost they had paid for this moment of stability, but the sight of the stable barrier made him feel lighter, as if the weight pressing on his chest had finally lifted. "We need to keep it running until we get confirmation from the Watchfort. If we stop now, the pressure will build up behind the seal again."

Maren nodded, her expression hardening with resolve. She stood up slowly, brushing dust from her cloak. "Then we hold the line. If they don't come back soon, we'll be ready to act when they do."

"We're not waiting indefinitely," Elias corrected gently. "We need to maintain this for a few more hours, then we can assess our options. The city's foundations are absorbing the energy; if we keep feeding it at this rate, we might be able to weaken the Blight enough to create an opening for evacuation or further action."

Elara stepped forward, holding out a small piece of quartz to Maren. "Take this," she said, her voice low but firm. "It's smaller than the others, but it can act as a secondary regulator if Kaelen needs to shift his focus elsewhere. I've already calibrated it to match the circuit."

Maren took the shard with both hands, examining it carefully before tucking it into her belt alongside her hammer. "Thanks," she said, offering a tired smile. "I'll keep an eye on it. If anything starts acting up, I'll know before you do."

"No need to worry about that," Elias said, standing up and brushing the dust from his armor. He looked at the barrier one last time, noting how the violet light seemed to stabilize around the quartz circuit. "We've got a while yet. Let's let Kaelen rest for a moment. He's been pushing hard."

Kaelen opened his eyes then, blinking as if emerging from a deep sleep. His face was pale, but there was no sign of panic or confusion in his gaze. "It's quiet," he said quietly, his voice still raspy. "The flow is... smooth. Like water in a riverbed."

"That's because you're guiding it now," Elias replied, moving to stand beside him. "You're not fighting the current anymore; you're shaping it."

Kaelen nodded slowly, then leaned back against Torin's shoulder. The soldier had been standing guard near the fissure, his sword still drawn but relaxed in its grip. Now he shifted slightly, offering Kaelen support without crowding him. "He's got a good head on his shoulders," Torin said, his voice low and serious. "I've seen him hold that line longer than anyone else would."

"He's not alone," Elara added, kneeling beside them. "We're all part of the circuit now. Maren's hammer helps stabilize the physical input; Elias monitors the barrier; I manage the quartz geometry. Kaelen is just the conduit, but he's doing more than that—he's integrating our efforts into a single system."

Elias nodded, looking around at his companions. They were exhausted, their clothes damp with sweat and fog, their faces streaked with dirt and soot. But there was a sense of unity among them now, a shared understanding of what they had accomplished and what still lay ahead. They were no longer just survivors; they were architects of a fragile peace, holding together the edges of a world that wanted to tear itself apart.

"We're stable," Elias said, his voice carrying a note of quiet pride. "For now."

"For now," Maren echoed, her gaze returning to the barrier. She closed her eyes for a moment, letting the warmth of the valley seep into her bones. The fog was less oppressive now, drifting lazily around them rather than pressing down like a shroud. Even the air tasted cleaner, less metallic and rotting.

Elias sat back down on a nearby stone ledge, leaning against the wall with his arms crossed over his chest. He watched the glow of the barrier, watching the way the violet light seemed to pulse in time with Kaelen's breathing. It was a strange sensation, witnessing magic that felt almost mundane now—a controlled flow of energy rather than a violent eruption. But there was still danger lurking beneath the surface; they knew that the city's foundations were only absorbing so much, and the Blight was vast, deep, and hungry.

"We need to think about what comes next," he said finally, breaking the silence. "If we hold this for a few more hours, we can weaken the seal enough to create an opening. But if they don't arrive soon, we'll have to decide whether to push harder or retreat."

Maren looked at him, her eyes serious. "Retreat? To where?"

"To the Watchfort," Elias said simply. "To regroup and wait for reinforcements. Or we continue here and risk everything on a single play."

Elara stood up then, gathering the remaining quartz shards and placing them in her satchel. "We don't need to decide yet," she said. "The system is stable. We can hold this position until we know what's happening. If they're coming, we'll be ready. If they're not, we'll have weakened the Blight enough to make a move."

"Agreed," Elias said, nodding at her. "We wait. We monitor. And when the time comes, we act."

They sat in silence for a while longer, listening to the low hum of the barrier and the gentle drift of the fog. The valley felt peaceful now, almost serene, but they both knew that this peace was temporary. The Blight was still there, still flowing through the fissure, still waiting for its moment to strike. But for now, they had won. They had bought themselves time, and in a world where time was often the only currency left, that was enough.
