It was no longer a roar that demanded attention, but a presence that acknowledged their survival.

Elias leaned heavily against the rough stone of the drainage ditch wall, his bandaged side throbbing with a dull, insistent ache. The heat radiating from the fissure had receded to a gentle warmth, the kind that suggested a hearth fire rather than an inferno. He looked at the barrier, watching the violet glow pulse gently against the grey fog of the valley. It was stable. The distributed circuit of quartz shards Elara had arranged within the stone mouth of the fissure was working as intended, filtering the chaotic energy into a manageable stream that the barrier could digest without strain.

"The mechanism holds," Elias said, his voice rough from disuse but steady. He didn't turn to look at Maren or Elara; he kept his eyes on the shimmering curtain of light. "The shards are acting as baffles, creating internal resistance. Kaelen is guiding it, and we're not overloading the seal."

Maren nodded slowly, her hands resting on her knees. She had been sitting against the wall for several minutes, watching the flow, her earlier trembling replaced by a deep, weary calm. The heat that had scorched her skin moments ago was now just ambient warmth, no longer a threat. "It feels different," she murmured, her gaze drifting from Elias to the barrier. "Before, it felt like we were holding back a flood with our bare hands. Now... it feels like watching water flow through a garden hose. Controlled. Deliberate."

Elara stood near Kaelen, who was still seated cross-legged in the center of the clearing, his eyes closed, his brass gauntlet glowing faintly as he modulated the resonance. She had just returned to her satchel, gathering the leather strips and steel wedges she had used to align the shards. A few larger pieces of quartz remained in her hand, their jagged edges catching the violet light.

"He's doing better than I expected," Elara said softly, her eyes on Kaelen. "The circuit corrects itself when he adjusts his focus. He doesn't have to force it anymore; he just has to listen."

"Good," Elias replied, turning his head slightly toward them. The pain in his side was a constant reminder of the cost they had paid for this moment of stability, but the sight of the stable barrier made him feel lighter, as if the weight pressing on his chest had finally lifted. "We need to keep it running until we get confirmation from the Watchfort. If we stop now, the pressure will build up behind the seal again."

"Confirmation?" Maren asked, shifting her gaze toward him. She was still wearing her cloak, which had been dampened by the fog and the earlier heat. "We're still cut off? The signal didn't make it through?"

Elias shook his head, a faint frown crossing his brow. "The surge disrupted the transmission channel. We don't know if they received our warning or if they've already sent a response team. Until we see smoke from their towers or hear their horn calls, we're on our own."

Maren nodded, her expression hardening with resolve. She stood up slowly, brushing dust from her cloak. "Then we hold the line. If they don't come back soon, we'll be ready to act when they do."

"We're not waiting indefinitely," Elias corrected gently. "We need to maintain this for a few more hours, then we can assess our options. Elara stepped forward, holding out a small piece of quartz to Maren. "Take this," she said, her voice low but firm. "It's smaller than the others, but it can act as a secondary regulator if Kaelen needs to shift his focus elsewhere. I've already calibrated it to match the circuit."

Maren took the shard with both hands, examining it carefully before tucking it into her belt alongside her hammer. "Thanks," she said, offering a tired smile. "I'll keep an eye on it. If anything starts acting up, I'll know before you do."

"No need to worry about that," Elias said, standing up and brushing the dust from his armor. He looked at the barrier one last time, noting how the violet light seemed to stabilize around the quartz circuit. "We've got a while yet. Let's let Kaelen rest for a moment. He's been pushing hard."

"That's because you're guiding it now," Elias replied, moving to stand beside him. "You're not fighting the current anymore; you're shaping it."

"For now," Maren echoed, her gaze returning to the barrier. She closed her eyes for a moment, letting the warmth of the valley seep into her bones. They sat in silence for a while longer, listening to the low hum of the barrier and the gentle drift of the fog. The valley felt peaceful now, almost serene, but they both knew that this peace was temporary. The Blight was still there, still flowing through the fissure, still waiting for its moment to strike. But for now, they had won. They had bought themselves time, and in a world where time was often the only currency left, that was enough.
