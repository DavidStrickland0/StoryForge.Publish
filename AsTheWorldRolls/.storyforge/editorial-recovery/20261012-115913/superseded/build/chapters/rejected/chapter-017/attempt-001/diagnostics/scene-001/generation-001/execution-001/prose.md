The heavy oak door of the Gilded Tankard groaned in protest, a sound that seemed to echo through the quiet valley more than the bustling noise of the tavern itself. Inside, the air was thick with the scent of roasted meat, brewing ale, and unwashed bodies—the perfume of civilization after weeks of open-air camping and survivalist grit.

Torin pushed the door open, his hand braced against the wood to steady it against the wind that still drifted through the valley pass. The party filed in, their boots leaving damp prints on the polished floorboards before being greeted by the immediate warmth of a hearth fire. Elias stepped up first, scanning the room with practiced eyes. It was a large hall, divided into three long tables where patrons sat drinking and talking in hushed tones. A few adventurers leaned against the bar, tossing coins to the bartender who seemed more interested in polishing his ale tankard than serving drinks.

The tavern keeper—a broad-shouldered man with hair like steel wool and a beard that could double as a rope—stood behind the counter, wiping down a glass with a rag that looked suspiciously used for other purposes. He raised an eyebrow as the party entered, his gaze lingering on their battered armor, torn cloaks, and the general aura of people who had just been fighting literal monsters in the open air.

"Welcome," the keeper said, his voice carrying easily over the low murmur of conversation. "You've got a long journey ahead of you, haven't you?"

Elias nodded, keeping his posture relaxed but alert. "Just getting settled before we move on to the road."

The keeper's eyes narrowed slightly, then he gestured toward a corner table near the hearth. "Right this way," he said, his tone suggesting that further questions would be met with silence. "And don't bother asking for the usual fare. We're short on fresh provisions tonight."

Torin glanced at Elias as they moved through the crowd. The tavern was full enough to feel crowded, but not so packed that movement was impossible. There were tables scattered along the walls and in the center of the hall, each occupied by travelers who seemed wary of outsiders but unwilling to turn them away outright.

As they approached the corner table, Elias could see why the keeper had chosen it—it offered a view of the hearth, where flames licked at logs that had been split into manageable pieces earlier. The warmth radiating from the fire was welcome after the chill of the valley air, and the sound of crackling wood provided a comforting backdrop to their arrival.

"Sit," the keeper said, gesturing toward the table with a sweep of his hand. "And don't worry about your boots. We've got a mop in the back if you leave tracks."

Elias smiled faintly. "We'll be careful."

They took their seats, and Torin immediately reached for the tankard placed before him by a passing server. The ale was dark and thick, with a frothy head that suggested it had been freshly brewed. Elias raised his glass in a silent toast before taking a sip. It tasted of hops and barley, slightly bitter but smooth enough to swallow without complaint.

"The water's good," Maren said after a moment, glancing at the pitcher on the table. "Better than what we'd get from any well in these parts."

Elias nodded. "Good ale helps clear the head after a long day."

The keeper watched them for a moment, then turned back to the bar. "You're new around here," he said casually, as if reading minds was part of his job description.

"New to the road," Elias corrected gently. "But we've been through worse than this valley before now."

The keeper's expression softened slightly. "You're not from these parts then?"

"No," Elias admitted. "We're heading eastward, toward the Dragon Road."

The keeper nodded slowly, his eyes drifting back to the fire. "Dragon Road... that'll be a long haul if you're going all the way there."

"We know," Elias replied. "That's why we need supplies before we hit the pass."

The keeper chuckled softly, then turned away without waiting for further response. His movements were efficient, as if he'd been anticipating their needs before they even asked. A moment later, a young boy—no older than ten—entered carrying a tray of bread rolls and cheese. He set them down at each place setting with a flourish that suggested he was trying to impress the adults in the room.

"Here you go," the boy said, bowing slightly before retreating into the back of the tavern. "The master says don't eat too much or you'll throw up on your journey."

Maren laughed quietly, shaking her head as she accepted the tray. "He's got a point. Last time we ate at a place like this, I ended up feeling like I'd swallowed a bag of stones."

Elias watched the boy leave, then turned his attention to the others. "We should check on Elara and Kaelen," he said quietly. "They've been resting in the corner since we arrived."

Torin nodded. "Let me go get them."

He stood up, leaving Elias with the others while he crossed the room to where Kaelen and Elara sat huddled together near a window overlooking the valley. The two of them looked exhausted but relieved, their faces illuminated by the soft glow of the hearth fire filtering through the glass panes.

"Here you are," Torin said as he approached, holding out his hand toward Kaelen's gauntlet. "You're awake now?"

Kaelen blinked, then nodded slowly. "Yes... I was just tuning again."

Elara smiled weakly. "The resonance is stable now. The barrier is feeding the Blight at a controlled rate, and we've got enough time to rest before moving on."

Torin sat down beside them, careful not to disturb their formation. "Good. Then maybe we can take a proper break tonight."

Elias watched the exchange from his seat, noting how naturally Torin had stepped in to care for his companions. It wasn't lost on him that this was exactly the kind of camaraderie they needed to survive whatever came next. The party was coming together, piece by piece, through shared hardship and mutual reliance.

When Torin returned, he found Elias still seated at the table, watching the fire dance across the room. "They're resting," Torin said quietly. "Kaelen's been asleep for maybe an hour now."

Elias nodded. "Good. Let them sleep. We'll take our turn when they wake up."

The keeper returned with another pitcher of ale, setting it down on the table before turning back to the bar. "If you need more food or drink, just call out," he said. "We're not closing early tonight."

"Thank you," Elias replied politely. "We'll take what we need when we need it."

The keeper nodded once, then disappeared behind the counter. The room settled into a comfortable rhythm of low conversation and clinking tankards, the kind of atmosphere that made even the most weary traveler feel like they belonged somewhere for the first time in weeks.

Maren leaned forward to pick up her bread roll, breaking it apart with careful precision before taking a bite. "This is actually decent," she said between mouthfuls. "I wasn't expecting much after what we've been through."

Elara smiled faintly, reaching for her own piece of cheese. "It's just food," she said. "But knowing you can eat something without worrying about whether you'll choke on it makes all the difference."

Torin nodded in agreement. "And knowing we have a roof over our heads for once is worth more than most treasure hoards."

Elias listened quietly, letting the warmth of the fire and the sound of the tavern settle around them like a protective shield. For now, they were safe. They had food, shelter, and companionship. Tomorrow would bring challenges again, but tonight was about rest—about recovering from the exhaustion that had been building up since they'd first entered the valley.

The keeper returned with a fresh batch of ale, setting it down on the bar before turning back to his work. "You're doing well," he said casually, as if commenting on the weather. "Not many groups make it through this pass without losing someone along the way."

"We're not going to lose anyone," Elias replied firmly. "That's a promise we made to each other."

The keeper paused in his wiping of a glass, then gave a curt nod. "Agreed," he said. "But don't forget—" He gestured toward the door. "—the hospitality code still applies. We're not letting you stay forever for free."

"We understand," Elias said evenly. "We'll settle our account when we leave."

The keeper nodded once more, then turned back to his work. The party settled into a comfortable rhythm of eating and drinking, the tension of the day finally giving way to a sense of relief that felt almost surreal after weeks of near-constant danger.

As Maren finished her roll, she leaned back in her chair and let out a long sigh. "I think we've earned this," she said softly. "After everything we've been through..."

"We have," Elias agreed, raising his tankard in a silent toast to the night ahead. "To rest."

"To rest," Torin echoed, clinking his glass against Elias's.

The sound of their voices blending with the ambient noise of the tavern created a momentary sense of unity—a reminder that they were still people, not just survivors. Outside, the wind continued to blow across the valley, but inside, warmth and safety prevailed.
