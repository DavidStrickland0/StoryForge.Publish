Elara pushed her journal across the table, the leather cover worn smooth from constant handling. The parchment beneath was spread out on the rough wood, marked with Elias’s charcoal sketch of the road northward. It wasn’t a perfect map; there were no borders or compass roses, just a series of squiggly lines representing terrain and dotted connections where they’d heard rumors of safe passages.

“Look here,” Elara said, pointing to a cluster of ink smudges near the valley pass. “The Blight isn’t random. It follows a cycle.”

Elias leaned forward, his eyes tracing the diagram. He hadn’t expected her to have already synthesized the scattered reports they’d gathered over the past few days into such a coherent pattern. The tavern noise seemed to fade as he studied the symbols she’d drawn—circles of varying sizes connected by lines that spiraled outward from a central point labeled *Valley Core*.

“Cyclical,” he repeated, testing the word in his mouth. “Like tides or seasons.”

“Exactly,” Elara confirmed, tapping a finger against the page. “The Blight draws strength from the valley’s ley lines. Those lines pulse with residual magic—what we called ‘energy’ earlier. When the moon is full, the ley lines amplify. That’s when the creatures are most active. But there’s a lull between cycles.”

She traced a spiral on her diagram, showing how the Blight activity peaked at specific intervals before receding into relative dormancy. “If we time our departure correctly, we can cross the pass during the low point. And if we need to rest overnight, we can choose locations that minimize exposure.”

Elias nodded slowly, committing the information to memory. It was useful intelligence, practical and actionable. But he also sensed Elara’s hesitation; she wasn’t just sharing data—she was proposing a strategy that required trusting her analysis completely.

“Where does this leave us?” he asked, his voice steady despite the weight of the implication. “If we wait for the lull, how long do we stay in the city?”

Elara didn’t answer immediately. Instead, she flipped through her journal, pulling out a separate sheet filled with calculations and observations about moon phases and magical resonance frequencies. She tapped a particular section marked *Next Peak Activity: 4 Days*. Then another one labeled *Low Point Window: 2–3 Days*.

“Two to three days,” she said finally. “We could leave tomorrow if we want to push through before the next surge. Or we wait, secure our supplies, and depart when the activity dips. It’s a trade-off.”

Maren interjected from across the table, her arms crossed over her chest. “Waiting means eating less, moving slower, and giving Silas Vane more time to double-cross us if he ever feels like it.”

Elias glanced at her sharply. “He signed the contract,” he reminded her. “And he’s not known for betrayal.”

Maren rolled her eyes, though there was a hint of concern in her tone. “Nothing’s foolproof, Elias. We need to weigh the risks carefully.”

Kaelen, who had been silent throughout the discussion, finally spoke up. “What about the creature near the gates?” he asked. “Is it part of the cycle too?”

Elara nodded. “Likely. The Blight-Touched are drawn to disturbances in the ley lines. If we can avoid triggering anything major while we’re in the city, we should be fine. But if someone tries to provoke a reaction—like attacking a patrol or setting off traps—we might wake the entire valley.”

She paused, then added, “That’s why we need to keep our movements low-key tonight. No flashy displays of power, no unnecessary confrontations. Just quiet preparation.”

Elias considered her words, then looked at Elias’s sketch again. The route was simple enough to memorize, but the timing was the real challenge. They couldn’t afford to linger in the city indefinitely, yet they also couldn’t rush blindly into danger. It was a delicate balance.

“Alright,” he said finally. “We’ll stick with Elara’s plan. Two to three days in the city, then we move out during the low point.” He turned to Maren. “And if Silas tries anything, we deal with it directly. No second-guessing contracts or playing games.”

Maren nodded, her expression softening slightly. “Agreed. But let’s make sure we’re ready for whatever comes next.”
