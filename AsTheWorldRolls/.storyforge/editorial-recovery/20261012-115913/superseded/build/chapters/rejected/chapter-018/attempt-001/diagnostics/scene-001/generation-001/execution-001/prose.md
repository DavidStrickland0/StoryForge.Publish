Silas Vane leaned back against the edge of Elias’s table, his posture relaxed but his eyes sharp behind a veil of practiced charm. The merchant had spent the better part of an hour outlining the terms for their journey to the Temple Springs: supplies delivered at the riverbank, a guide provided by Silas himself, and a discount on healing potions in exchange for exclusive rights to sell any excess “pure water” recovered during their escort.

Elias had listened without interruption, taking careful notes in his leather-bound ledger before setting it aside with a quiet sigh. The terms were generous on the surface—better than most merchants offered—but there was one clause that sat heavily between them: Silas insisted they agree to carry an additional crate of enchanted herbs from his own stockpile, claiming it would “balance” their load and reduce transport costs.

“Silas,” Elias began gently, tapping a finger against the table where the rolled parchment lay flat. “We’re willing to take on extra weight if it keeps us moving faster or safer—but this particular crate doesn’t fit our current plan.”

Silas blinked, feigning surprise as he crossed his arms over his chest. “Extra work? For free?” He raised an eyebrow, letting the joke hang in the air like dust motes dancing in candlelight. “You Adventurers are strange creatures, Thorne. Always picking and choosing which burdens you’ll bear.”

Elias didn’t smile back. “It’s not about burden—it’s about focus. We’ve already agreed to escort your primary cargo: the sealed crate of pure water from Temple Springs. Adding another item now complicates everything without giving us any real benefit.” He paused, looking directly at Silas. “If you need someone else to haul those herbs, we can point you toward the Guild’s standard contractors. They’ll handle it for a fixed rate.”

Silas chuckled softly, shaking his head as if amused rather than offended. “Ah, there it is—the same stubbornness that got you kicked out of three different guilds in the last decade.” He leaned forward again, resting his elbows on the table with a conspiratorial grin. “But let me tell you something, Thorne: those herbs aren’t just ‘extra.’ They’re critical to stabilizing the magical resonance around the water crate. Without them, your job becomes infinitely harder—and much more dangerous.”

Maren shifted slightly beside Kaelen, her hand still resting lightly on his shoulder as she watched the exchange with narrowed eyes. She hadn’t spoken since the rival adventurers left, but her presence was a silent reminder that they were a team—not just two individuals negotiating alone. Elara sat quietly across from them, flipping through her journal while occasionally glancing up to observe Silas’s expressions. Torin stood outside near the entrance, keeping watch as always.

Elias nodded slowly, acknowledging Maren’s unspoken support before turning back to Silas. “We’ll consider your offer seriously,” he said evenly. “But right now, we’re focused on securing the primary contract terms first. Once those are finalized, we can discuss whether adding extra cargo makes sense.”

Silas smiled again, this time with genuine warmth that didn’t quite reach his eyes. “Fair enough,” he conceded gracefully. “Let’s get down to business then.” He unrolled the parchment tied with leather cord and slid it across the table toward Elias. “Here’s the full agreement: escort fee based on distance traveled, payment split three ways—half upfront upon signing, half delivered at midpoint of journey, final balance paid only after we successfully reach Temple Springs.”

Elias picked up the scroll carefully, scanning each line before nodding once. “Upfront payment is standard,” he noted aloud. “Midpoint delivery works fine too—but hold on to that last portion until we confirm everything’s safe at destination. That way, you don’t lose money if something goes wrong near the end.”

Silas laughed heartily, leaning back in his chair with a satisfied sigh. “You’ve got me there, Thorne,” he admitted openly. “Agreed. Final balance only after we reach Temple Springs—that’s fair game.” He reached into his coat pocket and pulled out a small satchel containing coins wrapped in silk cloth. “Here’s your upfront payment: two hundred gold pieces, plus ten silver for expenses incurred so far.”

Elias took the bundle without hesitation, counting them quickly before placing them inside his pouch. Two hundred gold pieces represented nearly three months’ worth of wages for a typical adventurer group traveling alone—a significant sum considering they’d spent weeks dealing with the Blight’s aftermath in the Watchfort region. Still, he kept his tone professional rather than celebratory.

“Thank you,” Elias said formally, slipping the coins into his belt pouch before rolling up the parchment and tucking it away safely. “We’ll sign this contract once we’ve confirmed all details are correct.” He glanced at Maren briefly, who gave him a reassuring nod before turning her attention back to Elara’s journal spread open on the table.

Silas chuckled softly, rising from his chair with a flourish that nearly knocked over his ale flagon. “That’s how it should be done,” he said cheerfully. “No rushing, no pressure—just clear agreements between honest folks.” He paused dramatically, raising an eyebrow playfully. “Though I must warn you: once we start traveling together, things might get a little… interesting.”

Elias smirked faintly despite himself. “Interesting is good,” he replied evenly. “As long as it stays within reason.” He stood up slowly, offering Silas a hand to help him roll up the parchment properly before sealing it with wax and string. “Let’s meet again tomorrow morning at your campsite near the riverbank,” Elias said finally. “We’ll review everything one more time before setting off.”

Silas bowed slightly, accepting thanks graciously before gesturing toward his wagon parked outside the tavern entrance. “Anytime,” he replied warmly. “Consider this just the beginning of our partnership, Thorne. Tomorrow morning, we’ll meet at my campsite near the riverbank and finalize terms regarding your purchase order.” He paused briefly, raising an eyebrow playfully. “Though don’t expect cheap rates—we charge premium prices for quality goods after all!”

Elias chuckled softly despite himself before heading back toward his booth where Maren waited patiently alongside Elara, who sat quietly reading through one of her journals while occasionally exchanging amused glances with Kaelen. Torin meanwhile was already preparing to head outside toward the entrance once more.

The noise of the Gilded Tankard swelled again around them—a chaotic symphony of clinking tankards, shouting patrons, and the occasional thud of a stool being dragged across the floorboards. Inside their corner booth, however, the atmosphere remained calm and focused, insulated by the heavy wood and the sheer density of the crowd pressing against its walls.

Elias leaned back in his chair, rubbing a thumbnail against the side of his mouth as he surveyed the sea of faces around them. “We need to know what’s on that caravan,” he said, his voice low enough not to carry but loud enough for those at their table to hear. “The rumors say it’s high-value goods from the Temple Springs.”

Elara didn’t look up from her journal, though she paused with her stylus hovering over a page of notes. Her dark eyes remained fixed on the ink-stained parchment as she flipped through a section titled *Regional Trade Routes & Risk Assessments*. “The Temple Springs,” she murmured, more to herself than to the group. “That’s where the Pure Water comes from.”

Kaelen frowned slightly, shifting his gaze between Elara and Elias. “Pure Water?” he asked, his tone questioning. “I’ve heard of the springs in the eastern valleys, but I don’t think anyone calls them ‘Pure’ unless they mean something special.”

Elara finally looked up, closing her journal with a snap that drew an amused glance from Maren across the table. “It’s not just clean water,” she explained, gesturing to a diagram sketched in the margins of her book. “The Temple Springs produce a water infused with stabilizing magical properties. It’s used to heal wounds, cleanse ailments, and even reinforce certain wards. The Blight-Touched entities are drawn to it like moths to flame.” She paused, tracing a finger along the illustration of a caravan route overlaid with symbols for danger zones. “If that crate is missing or compromised, not only will we lose its value—but whatever creature is hunting us next might be even more dangerous than before.”

Maren nodded slowly, her hand resting lightly on Kaelen’s shoulder in a gesture that spoke volumes without needing words. She hadn’t said anything since resolving the drunk patron incident earlier that day, but her presence felt like an anchor. If anyone had tried to corner Elias or Elara with questions about their tactics or motives, Maren would have intercepted them with a glare sharp enough to cut stone. Now, she simply listened, occasionally interjecting with observations about the tavern patrons or pointing out familiar faces who’d been seen earlier in the day.

The tavern’s information network was far from official, but it was thorough. Rumors traveled faster than messages in this town, and for a coin or two, someone would sell you half-truths wrapped in speculation. Elias had already heard snippets here and there: whispers of a caravan carrying “miracle water,” sightings of shadowy figures loitering near the city gates, and even a few claims that Blight-Touched creatures had been spotted prowling the outskirts. None of it added up to anything useful until now.

“Who’s selling this stuff?” Elias asked, leaning forward slightly to catch the attention of the barmaid who happened to wander by with a tray of empty mugs. “Anyone? We could ask around.”
