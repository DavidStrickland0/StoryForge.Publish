Maren watched Vane disappear into the shadows of the back room, his silhouette swallowed by the dim light before he vanished from sight. The tension at their table had eased, replaced by a quiet, comfortable stillness that felt more like earned rest than mere absence of danger. Elias leaned back in his chair, rubbing his temples with a thumb that was slightly dampened by sweat and ale.

“Silas is… intense,” Elara murmured, though she didn’t sound worried so much as impressed. “But I think he respects us now. That’s rare.”

Kaelen stirred, blinking against the flickering hearthlight as consciousness returned to him in stages. He sat up slowly, wincing slightly at the ache in his neck and shoulders. The brass gauntlet on his wrist remained dark, its cold weight a constant reminder of what he’d lost—and regained—in the past few days.

“Good,” Maren said immediately, reaching across the table to offer him one of her water skins. “Drink. You’re dehydrated.”

Kaelen took the skin without hesitation, drinking deeply until his throat cleared and his hands stopped trembling. The liquid was cool, refreshing, and tasted faintly of mineral springs rather than the stale brew they’d been sipping at their table. He set it down on the wood with a soft clink, then looked up at Maren, meeting her eyes with a small, genuine smile.

“Thanks,” he said quietly. “You’ve been too busy protecting me from everything lately. I appreciate it.”

Maren shrugged modestly, though there was warmth in her expression. “Someone has to keep you from saying anything stupid when people start asking questions about where we’re going or what we did before this.”

Elara snorted softly, reaching for another tankard of ale. “Stupid? You didn’t even say anything wrong yesterday. Just… bluntly honest.”

“Bluntly honest is a form of lying in some places,” Kaelen replied, taking another sip. “And I wasn’t sure how much we could trust Silas Vane yet.”

Elias raised an eyebrow from across the table, though he didn’t interrupt. He’d been listening to their exchange with quiet interest, noting the way Maren had naturally stepped into a role she hadn’t claimed for herself until now: protector, advocate, social shield.

“Let’s hope your honesty pays off tomorrow,” Elias said finally, leaning forward slightly. “We need to know exactly what Vane wants from us—and whether he’ll honor his word when we cross that bridge.”

Kaelen nodded slowly, setting the water skin aside. “I’m ready,” he said, his voice steady despite the fatigue in his eyes. “But I don’t want to do anything reckless again.”

“No one’s asking for recklessness,” Maren replied, giving him a reassuring pat on the shoulder before pulling her satchel closer. “Just careful competence. And maybe a little patience.”

Elara finished her ale and stood up, stretching her arms overhead with a yawn that nearly knocked over her chair. “If anyone needs coffee or tea after we leave here, I’ll be awake,” she said, heading toward the bar to refill her own cup. “But if you two need to talk about anything else first, feel free.”

Maren watched her go, then turned her attention back to Kaelen and Elias. There was still work to do before dawn, but for now, they could rest in peace. The road ahead would be long and difficult, but they were stronger together than apart.
