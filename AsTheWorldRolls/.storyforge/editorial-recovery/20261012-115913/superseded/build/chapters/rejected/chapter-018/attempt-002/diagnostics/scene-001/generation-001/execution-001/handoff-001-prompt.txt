The tavern noise seemed to recede, replaced by the scratch of a quill and the low, gravelly murmur of negotiation. Silas Vane had not actually left the table, though he had positioned himself with the casual ease of a man who owned the room. He sat on the edge of the adjacent booth, legs crossed, his silver-threaded robes catching the candlelight. In front of him lay a sheaf of parchment, thick and heavy, bound with a strip of green leather.

Elias stared at the document. It was a contract. Specifically, it was a *very* specific contract.

“Let’s look at this line by line,” Elias said, his voice flat. He tapped the parchment with a fingernail, not quite touching it. “You call this a ‘comprehensive escort and intelligence package.’ That’s a lot of adjectives for what amounts to us walking behind a wagon.”

Silas smiled, a expression that suggested he was enjoying the friction. “And yet, the wagon contains goods that could heal a battalion, Elias. The ‘intelligence’ portion is where the value lies. You have the sword; I have the map. Together, we are a force of nature.”

Maren, who had been leaning back in her chair with her arms folded, uncrossed them. She leaned forward, resting her elbows on the table, her eyes fixed on Silas with the intensity of a hawk spotting a field mouse. “You’re trying to add a second merchant group,” she stated. It wasn’t a question.

Silas raised an eyebrow. “I’m attempting to optimize the route, Maren. If we can secure the protection of a second caravan traveling the same corridor, we can split the cost of the ‘premium’ escort services. It’s good business.”

“It’s bad business if we get killed,” Maren countered, her voice devoid of inflection. “Two caravans means double the targets. Double the chances of a Blight-Touched entity deciding our little procession is a buffet. And you want to put that in the contract? As a ‘liability mitigation clause’?”

Elias looked at the clause in question. It was buried in the fine print, written in a script so small it looked like a swarm of angry ants. *Party agrees to maintain escort integrity for all associated commercial interests within a fifty-yard radius...*

“Fifty yards,” Elias repeated, feeling a headache beginning to throb behind his eyes. “Vane, if we are escorting *your* caravan, we are not also babysitting some other merchant’s oxen. That’s not an escort; that’s a circus.”

Silas waved a hand dismissively. “It’s a standard industry practice for high-value goods. You’re being overly literal. It’s a ‘zone of influence.’”

“It’s a trap,” Maren said.

Elias turned to Silas, his expression hardening. “Here is what happens. We sign for the primary caravan escort only. The payment structure is as we discussed: fifty percent upfront, fifty percent upon safe delivery. The ‘zone of influence’ clause is struck. If another merchant wants protection, they can hire their own guards. They can even hire you, if you feel so inclined, but you don’t get to attach their liability to our neck.”

Silas’s smile faltered, just for a second, before he smoothed it back into place. He took a sip of his ale, though he hadn’t touched the liquid. “You’re difficult, Thorne. I respect it. But I’m a man of contracts. I can’t just strike a clause. It sets a precedent.”

“Then let’s set a different precedent,” Elias said. He reached into his coat and withdrew a small pouch of coins. He didn’t place them on the table; he simply held them up, letting the metal clink softly in the silence that had fallen around their booth. “This is a deposit. Not for the contract, but for the map. You said you had detailed route intelligence. That intelligence is valuable. I’m buying it. Separately. If you want to sell us the escort, we’ll negotiate that based on the primary caravan only. If you want to sell the map, you take the coin.”

Silas’s eyes flickered to the pouch, then back to Elias’s face. The calculation was visible, rapid and precise, behind his merchant’s mask. He was weighing the immediate liquidity of the coin against the long-term potential of a difficult client.

“And if I refuse?” Silas asked, his voice dropping an octave.

“Then you keep your map, and I keep my coin. And you’ll have to find another way to sell that ‘pure’ water if you don’t have a reliable escort. Word travels fast in this city, Vane. Especially about merchants who try to tie adventurers down with fine print while they’re still bleeding from the last job.”

Maren gave a low chuckle, the sound dry and sharp. “He’s bluffing,” she said to Silas, though her hand rested casually on the hilt of her hammer. “But he’s not wrong about the fine print. It’s ugly. And I hate ugly things.”

Silas stared at Elias for a long moment. The tavern noise seemed to swell again, the clatter of plates and the roar of conversation pressing in, but the air between the three of them remained suspended, brittle and sharp.

Finally, Silas reached out and tapped the parchment with a single finger. “The map,” he said. “You want the map.”

“I want the route,” Elias corrected. “And I want it to be accurate. No ‘zone of influence’ nonsense. Just the path, the dangers, and the landmarks.”

Silas picked up the sheaf of parchment. He didn’t strike the clause; he didn’t need to. He simply tore the page containing the ‘associated commercial interests’ section from the rest of the document. The sound of the tearing paper was loud in the quiet booth. He slid the remaining pages, the ones detailing the primary escort and the map’s location, across the table.

“Take the map,” Silas said, his smile returning, though it was thinner now, more cautious. “I’ll have the primary escort contract ready for review tomorrow morning. But Elias?”

Elias took the map, his fingers brushing against the parchment. It was heavy, high-quality vellum. “Yes?”

“If that map leads us into a Blight-Touched nest, I’m billing you for the cleanup.”

Elias looked at Maren, who raised an eyebrow, then back at Silas. “Deal,” Elias said.

He took the map. The transaction was complete, clean, and devoid of the messy entanglements Silas had tried to weave in. The party had secured the intelligence they needed without surrendering their autonomy or their sanity. It was a small victory, but in the world of merchants and adventurers, small victories were the only ones that lasted.

Silas stood up, brushing a speck of dust from his sleeve. He looked at the group, his gaze lingering on Kaelen, who had been silent throughout, studying the table’s grain as if it held secrets.

“You’re an interesting group,” Silas said, his tone light, almost friendly. “Don’t let the Blight get you before I can collect my other half.”

With a nod to Maren and a sharp glance at Elias, he turned and disappeared into the crowd, his silver robes swirling around him like a phantom.

Elias sat back, exhaling a breath he didn’t realize he had been holding. He unfolded the map on the table. It was detailed, marked with red lines and symbols that Elara, sitting quietly beside him, was already leaning over to inspect.

“Good work,” Maren said, her voice soft. She didn’t smile, but the tension in her shoulders had eased. “He wanted to bleed us. You stopped him.”

Elias looked at the map, then at his companions. The threat was still out there, the Blight still prowling the edges of the city, but for now, they had a path. And they had the time to study it.

The chapter ended with the silence of the tavern, the weight of the map in Elias’s hands, and the quiet understanding that they had just bought themselves a chance to survive.
