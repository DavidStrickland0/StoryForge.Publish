Silas Vane leaned over the bar, his elbows resting on the polished oak, eyes narrowing as he studied Elias with the scrutiny of a merchant weighing coin against wares. The tavern had settled into its evening rhythm—clinking tankards, low chatter, the crackle of the hearth—but the air around their table remained tense. Vane’s fingers traced the rim of his ale mug, not drinking, just measuring the moment.

“Thorne,” he said, voice smooth but edged with caution. “I’ve heard enough tales about your kind from the roadmen who pass through here. You don’t ask questions. You don’t bargain. You just take what you need and leave before the next meal.”

Elias kept his expression neutral, tankard in hand though he hadn’t taken a sip. He’d already paid his share, tipped the keeper for the table, and accepted the fine for spilling ale on the floor earlier—a small price to keep from being turned away outright. But Vane was probing, testing whether they were worth the trouble of dealing with.

“We’re not here to take,” Elias replied evenly. “We’re heading east. Toward the Dragon Road.”

Vane’s eyes flicked toward him, then back to Elias’s face. “Dragon Road,” he repeated, almost tasting the phrase. “That’s dangerous ground now. The Blight’s spreading faster than anyone’s admitting. You think you can walk that path without getting lost or eaten?”

“We know the risks,” Elias said. “That’s why we’re prepared.”

Vane laughed softly, a sound that didn’t quite reach his eyes. “Careful what you say to men like me, boy. I don’t deal in bravado. I deal in supply lines, contracts, and people who pay their debts before they vanish into the fog.” He leaned forward slightly. “I have a crate sealed tight. Inside is something from the temple springs—pure water, bottled for travelers. Rare thing these days. But if you’re going east, you’ll need it more than most.”

Maren shifted slightly on her stool, her hand resting near her hammer handle—not threatening, just ready. Kaelen slept in the corner, Elara beside him, both unaware of the shift in tone that had taken hold of the table. Torin sat nearby, silent as a statue, watching Vane’s movements like a guard dog assessing a stranger at the gate.

Elias set his tankard down with a soft thud. “And if we refuse?”

Vane smiled thinly. “Then you walk out that door and find your water rationed at half strength. Or worse—you don’t find it at all.” He paused, letting the threat hang in the air. “But I’m not trying to drive you off. Far from it. You’ve got grit. That’s something I respect. So tell me—what do you want?”

Elias considered his options. They needed supplies for the road ahead, yes—but pushing too hard could cost them more than they’d gain. Still, Vane wasn’t offering charity; he was offering partnership. The question was whether Elias could steer this conversation into something mutually beneficial without compromising their autonomy or falling into a trap.

“We’re not looking to buy,” Elias said finally. “We’re looking for allies.”

Vane raised an eyebrow. “Allies? With us?” He chuckled, though there was no warmth in it. “Kid, we’re both sides of the same coin. You need passage, supplies, maybe even protection. I need someone strong enough to carry my cargo through hostile territory. And if you’re smart, you’ll agree to terms before you meet the first bandit.”

Elias leaned back slightly, letting the silence stretch just long enough for Vane to fill it. “We’ll discuss terms when we reach your camp,” he said. “In the morning.”

Vane’s expression softened, just a fraction. “Good answer,” he murmured. “You’ve got spine. Most folks would have taken whatever I offered and bolted. Or tried to steal my crate on the way out.” He stood slowly, straightening his coat. “I’ll send word ahead. My riders will guide you when you’re ready. And if you behave yourself… maybe you’ll get a discount.”

Elias nodded. “Fair enough.”

Vane turned and walked away, disappearing behind the bar to speak with the keeper. The tension at their table eased slightly, though it hadn’t fully left. Elias glanced at Maren, who was already reaching into her satchel for water skins she’d packed earlier. She caught his eye and gave a small nod—she understood. They weren’t just surviving anymore; they were building alliances, one cautious step at a time.

Kaelen stirred in the corner, murmuring something unintelligible in his sleep. Elara didn’t wake until he shifted again, then she gently shook him awake with a finger to his shoulder. “Time’s slipping,” she whispered, her voice barely audible over the tavern’s hum.

Elias watched Vane move toward the back room, likely arranging transport or discussing further logistics with the keeper. He could feel the weight of the night settling around them—the kind that comes after a hard day’s work, when the world feels both safer and stranger than before. They’d earned this rest, but they also had responsibilities to uphold tomorrow. The road ahead wouldn’t be easy, but for now, they were together, breathing in sync, ready to face whatever came next.
