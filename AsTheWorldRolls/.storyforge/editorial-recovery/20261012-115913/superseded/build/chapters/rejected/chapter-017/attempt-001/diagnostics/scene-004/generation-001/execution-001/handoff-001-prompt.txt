The tavern keeper returned, wiping his hands on a rag that had seen better days. He stepped up to the corner table where Elias sat with Kaelen and Maren, his presence casting a long shadow across the hearth’s glow.

“Gentlemen,” he said, his tone carrying the weight of officialdom even as he leaned casually against the bar. “I’ve spoken with my superior regarding your request for lodging.”

Elias raised an eyebrow, keeping his attention on the keeper rather than the table where Kaelen was finishing his water. “Of course you have,” he said smoothly. “We were just discussing how best to utilize our time before bedfall tomorrow.”

The keeper’s smile didn’t quite reach his eyes. “Your request has been approved, yes, but there are conditions attached. The room is in the upper wing, as per regulation 4, section two. You will find it locked until you present your lodging fee and sign the hospitality contract.” He paused, letting the silence stretch just long enough to make the point clear. “And that contract requires a deposit of gold coins equal to three nights’ stay plus an additional sum for what we call ‘extraordinary circumstances.’”

Elias leaned forward slightly, his posture relaxed but alert. “Extraordinary circumstances?” he asked, feigning ignorance despite knowing exactly what was being implied.

The keeper nodded, tapping a finger against the bar counter. “Yes, extraordinary. You’ve been seen near the shrine fissure, yes? That means you’re dealing with something we don’t want to name in public. And if you’re dealing with something like that, then your stay here will require extra scrutiny.” He glanced at Maren, who had already stood up and was moving toward Elara’s position at the bar. “Which brings me to the second condition: we need proof that you’re not carrying contraband or magical artifacts that could destabilize the tavern’s reputation.”

Maren didn’t hesitate. She stepped forward, holding her satchel close to her chest. “We’re just travelers looking for a place to rest,” she said calmly. “And if you have any concerns about what we carry, I’m happy to show them.”

The keeper’s eyes flicked briefly toward Kaelen before settling on Elias again. “That’s all very well, but the contract must be signed first. And the deposit paid in full. We don’t take risks with our establishment.” He pulled a parchment from his sleeve and held it out like a shield. “Sign here, then pay.”

Elias took the parchment, studying its contents for a moment before nodding to Elara. “Elara,” he said quietly. “Can you handle this?”

Elara stepped forward, her expression serious as she reached into her satchel and produced a small pouch of coins. She counted them carefully before placing them on the table. “This is enough for three nights plus an extra sum,” she said. “Is that what you’re asking for?”

The keeper nodded, taking the coins without looking at them. “That’s exactly what we need. And now, please sign.” He pressed a quill into Elias’s hand and pointed to a blank line near the bottom of the page. “Sign here, then we’ll show you to your room.”

Elias hesitated for only a fraction of a second before signing the contract. The ink seemed to dry instantly, sealing their agreement with finality. He handed the parchment back to the keeper, who folded it carefully and placed it in his pocket.

“Thank you,” Elias said politely. “And thank you for your hospitality.”

The keeper smiled again, though this time it reached his eyes more genuinely. “We’re glad to have guests like you. And now, if you’ll excuse me, I’ll show you to your room.” He turned and gestured toward the stairs leading up to the upper wing. “This way.”

As they followed him upstairs, Elias couldn’t help but notice the way Maren had subtly positioned herself between them and any potential threats in the tavern below. She wasn’t just guarding Kaelen anymore; she was protecting the entire party from whatever dangers might lurk within these walls. It was a testament to her growth as both a warrior and a friend.

Once inside their room, which turned out to be surprisingly comfortable despite its modest furnishings, Elias closed the door behind them. The key clicked into place with a satisfying sound that seemed to seal away any outside noise.

“This is fine,” he said, letting out a breath he hadn’t realized he’d been holding. “Better than we expected.”

Maren nodded, setting her satchel down on the bed before sitting on the edge of it. “Yeah,” she agreed. “Much better than what I imagined.”

Kaelen stretched his arms overhead, yawning widely as he did so. “I think I’m ready for sleep now,” he said softly. “Thanks again for everything today.”

Elias smiled faintly at him. “You’re welcome,” he replied. “And remember, we’re all in this together. No matter what comes next.”
