Torin stepped out into the warm afternoon air, leaving the clamor of the Gilded Tankard behind him. He found a patch of sun-drenched cobblestones near the entrance where he could lean against the wall and watch the dust motes dance in the light. The tavern’s noise was a familiar comfort now—clattering tankards, raucous laughter, the clink of cutlery against ceramic plates—but inside, the atmosphere still hummed with residual tension from the rival adventurers.

Inside, Elias sat back in his booth, wiping gravy from his chin with the back of a gloved hand. Maren remained seated beside him, her posture relaxed but alert, eyes scanning the room without focusing on any single point. Elara was deep in her journal, though she occasionally glanced up to exchange a glance with Kaelen, who sat quietly across from Elias, his brass gauntlet dark and still as it had been since the shrine.

“Alright,” Elias said softly, leaning forward slightly. “Torin’s out. We’re alone for a few minutes.” He turned his gaze toward the window, watching the dust swirl outside. “We need information about the caravan route. Specifically, what’s in that sealed crate Silas mentioned.”

Elara closed her journal with a soft snap. She had been scanning the room as well, though she kept her attention on Elias rather than the other patrons. “The tavern keeps,” she said, “have access to certain rumors through their regulars and the local watch. But we’ll need coin to pay for that kind of intel.”

Elias nodded, pulling a small pouch from his belt and counting out three copper pieces. “Three coppers should be enough to buy a whisper or two. If they want more, we can negotiate later.” He slid the coins across the table toward Elara. “Get us something about the caravan, especially anything about the sealed crate.”

Elara studied the coins briefly before pocketing them. She stood up and moved toward the bar area, where she had a contact—a nervous-looking young man with ink-stained fingers and a perpetually anxious expression. He nodded when she approached, his eyes darting around the room as if expecting trouble.

“Tell us about the caravan,” Elara said quietly, keeping her voice low enough not to attract attention from nearby patrons. “What’s in that crate? Why is it sealed?”

The young man shifted uncomfortably, glancing at the barkeep before lowering his voice. “It’s pure water,” he whispered back. “From the temple springs. The monks say it’s holy, but everyone knows it’s worth a fortune. People will kill for it.” He paused, then added, “And there are… things following it. Things that don’t belong here. They’ve been watching us since we left the watchfort.”

Elias leaned forward slightly, his expression unreadable. “Who sent them?” he asked.

The young man hesitated, then shook his head. “I don’t know. Maybe the Blight itself. Or maybe someone who wants to use it for something else.” He glanced at the door, as if expecting an intruder. “Don’t trust the caravan masters. They’re hiding something from you.”

Maren stood up silently and moved closer to Elara, placing a hand on her shoulder without breaking eye contact with the barkeep. Her presence was subtle but unmistakable—a quiet assertion that they were not alone, that someone would watch their backs if needed. The young man flinched slightly at her proximity, then quickly composed himself.

“Thank you,” Elara said softly after the exchange. She pocketed the coins and returned to the booth. “They’re tracking it. And they’re dangerous.”

Elias nodded, his jaw tightening. “We’ll need to be ready when we meet Silas Vane tomorrow. If the caravan’s cargo is that valuable, someone else will want it too.” He looked at Maren, who gave him a small nod of agreement. “What do you think?”

Maren crossed her arms over her chest, her expression thoughtful. “It’s a trap,” she said finally. “Someone wants to steal the water, and they’ve been watching us since we left the watchfort. If Silas knows about this, he’ll either give it to us—or try to take it himself.”

Elias nodded again. “We’ll need to decide what to do when we meet him tomorrow. Do we trust him? Do we go into his camp alone?” He turned to Elara. “Elara, can you tell me more about the temple springs? Where are they located?”

Elara pulled out her journal and flipped through a few pages. “They’re northeast of here,” she said, pointing to a rough sketch on the map. “Near the old stone arches. The monks guard them fiercely. If someone’s targeting the water, they’ve already broken into the monastery.”

Elias frowned. “That means the Blight is closer than we thought.” He looked at Kaelen, who was watching the barkeep intently. “Kaelen, can you feel anything from out there? Any signs of Blight nearby?”

Kaelen shook his head slowly. “Not directly,” he said. “But the air feels… heavy. Like something’s moving through the land.” He paused, then added, “Maybe we should bring Torin back in. He could keep an eye on things while we plan our next move.”

Maren nodded silently, stepping toward the door to call him back inside. “He’s right,” she said as she opened it. “We need all hands on deck.”

Torin stepped back into the room just as Maren reached for the handle. He looked around briefly before joining the group at the booth. “What’s going on?” he asked, his tone casual but alert.

Elias summarized the situation quickly, explaining about the sealed crate and the threats following them. Torin listened carefully, his expression darkening as he absorbed the details.

“Alright,” he said finally. “If someone’s been watching us since we left the watchfort, then we need to be ready for anything. Let’s get our gear packed and head out tomorrow morning.” He glanced at Elara. “Did you get anything else from the barkeep?”

Elara nodded, pulling her journal back into her satchel. “Just that the caravan masters are hiding something from us,” she said. “We’ll need to decide what to do when we meet Silas Vane tomorrow.”

Elias stood up, gathering his cloak and weapons. “Let’s wrap this up,” he said. “Torin, take point on the way back to the riverbank. We’ll need to move early tomorrow morning if we’re going to catch the caravan before anyone else does.”

Maren followed close behind him, her hand resting lightly on Kaelen’s shoulder as they left the booth area. Outside, the sun was beginning to dip lower in the sky, casting long shadows across the cobblestones. The tavern’s noise faded into the distance, replaced by the soft sounds of evening settling over the city.
