"Let’s not take it personally," Elias advised calmly, though he didn't remove his own weight from the table. "People will always talk when they don’t understand something—or worse, when they’re jealous."

Maren grunted noncommittally but didn’t respond otherwise. Instead, she focused her gaze on the stew before her, pushing a chunk of bread through the gravy with deliberate slowness. The act was mundane, but it served as an anchor, keeping her from rising to defend their honor in a way that would escalate tensions unnecessarily.

Elias turned his attention back to Silas Vane, who stood near their table, leaning casually against the counter despite the noise and chaos of the tavern around them. The merchant’s eyes were sharp, scanning the room as if counting potential threats or opportunities. He had introduced himself earlier, offering a catalog of supplies that ranged from mundane rations to enchanted potions and finely crafted weapons. His tone had been professional, but there was an undercurrent of caution—a merchant testing the water before diving in.

"Silas," Elias said, his voice steady despite the ambient din. "You’ve offered us your wares, and I appreciate the gesture. But before we discuss trade, there are conditions attached that feel… excessive."

Vane raised an eyebrow, stepping closer to their table with a confident stride. He wore leather armor stained with ink and travel grime, a satchel slung over one shoulder that bulged with rolled scrolls and pouches of coin. His smile was polite but not warm, the kind worn by men who dealt in high-stakes ventures where reputation could be as valuable as gold.

"Conditions?" Vane repeated, his voice carrying just enough volume to be heard over the clatter of tankards and laughter from neighboring booths. "I’m a merchant, Elias. I provide goods. You provide labor. That’s how the world works."

"Not quite," Elias corrected gently. "You’re offering contracts that shift all liability onto my companions if anything goes wrong. If a caravan member gets injured, or if supplies spoil during transport, your terms say we bear full responsibility. Even if the delay was caused by weather, bandits, or magical anomalies beyond our control."

Vane chuckled, leaning back against the counter with a dismissive wave of his hand. "Standard practice, Elias. We don’t want adventurers running off half-cocked and blaming us when things go south. It’s better to have clear terms than muddy ones later."

"Clear terms," Elias repeated slowly, letting the words hang in the air. "But these aren’t just about protecting your interests. They’re about forcing us to absorb every risk involved in our work—including risks we can’t reasonably foresee or prevent. That’s not fair."

"It is if you know what you’re doing," Vane replied, his voice dropping slightly as he stepped closer, lowering his pitch but increasing the intensity of his stare. "You think a little injury or a delayed shipment is bad? Try losing a whole caravan because the leader made one wrong turn. Or worse—losing lives because someone didn’t follow protocol."

Elias held his ground, meeting Vane’s gaze without flinching. "We understand risks, Silas. But that doesn’t mean we should sign away our rights to negotiate them. We’re not here to be cannon fodder for your profit margins."

Vane’s expression hardened, his smile vanishing like smoke in a draft. "You’re either brave or foolish," he said bluntly. "Or maybe both. Either way, you’ll learn quickly that some deals aren’t meant to be renegotiated."

Elias leaned forward slightly, resting his elbows on the table. "Then let’s talk about what *is* negotiable. We can agree to indemnify you for damages caused by our gross negligence or intentional misconduct. But natural hazards, magical interference, and external threats? Those are shared risks. If anything happens because of factors outside our control, we deserve fair compensation—not total liability."

Vane studied him for a long moment, his eyes narrowing as if weighing the man’s resolve against the cost of refusal. Finally, he sighed sharply and straightened up, brushing imaginary dust from his sleeve. "Fine," he said reluctantly. "I’ll adjust the terms. But don’t expect me to lower my standards just because you’re stubborn."

"That was the point," Elias replied with a faint smile. "You offered us supplies and services, which means we need flexibility too. If we can’t negotiate fair terms, then perhaps this partnership won’t work for either of us."

Vane nodded once, his expression softening back into something resembling politeness. "Agreed. I’ll revise the contract tomorrow morning at first light. Until then, enjoy your meal and keep your ears open. The roads are full of talkers these days."

Before Elias could respond, Maren spoke up quietly from across the table. "They’re spreading rumors about us again," she said flatly, her voice cutting through the noise like a knife. "Something about incompetence and lack of skill."

Elias glanced at her, then back to Vane. "Maren’s right," he admitted. "Someone’s been whispering about how we couldn’t handle our own magic or move beyond basic adventuring tasks."

Vane smirked faintly. "Rumor mill in this city is louder than a dragon’s roar. You’ll hear worse before you leave town. Just ignore it. Most folks are too busy worrying about their own problems to care much about others’ reputations."

"Ignore it?" Maren muttered, her tone sharp enough to draw attention from nearby patrons. "They’re saying we’re reckless amateurs who got lucky once and now think they’re heroes."

Elias raised a hand to calm her, though his own jaw tightened slightly. "Let them talk," he said firmly. "Our work speaks for itself. Once we prove our competence, the rumors will fade like morning mist."

"That’s easy to say when you’re not sitting here eating stew while someone tells stories about your failures," Maren countered, picking up her bread knife and slicing another piece of crusty loaf with practiced ease. "But fine. Let them talk. We’ll let our actions speak louder than their words ever could."

Vane watched the exchange for a moment before turning back to his catalog, flipping through pages filled with prices, descriptions, and illustrations of enchanted items. "If you need anything else," he said casually, "just ask. I’m happy to help however I can within reason."

"Thank you, Silas," Elias replied sincerely. "Your willingness to negotiate means a lot to us. Once we finalize the terms, we’ll be ready to move forward with your services."

Vane nodded again, his demeanor relaxed yet alert. "Good. Now eat your stew before it gets cold. And remember—reputation is built on results, not words."

As Vane stepped away, leaving the table to attend to other patrons, Elias turned back to Maren and Kaelen. The rival group had already dispersed into smaller clusters, their whispers dying down as attention shifted elsewhere in the tavern. But the damage was done: a few people now believed they were incompetent adventurers who hadn’t earned their place yet.

Kaelen looked up from his bowl of stew, his expression thoughtful rather than defensive. "It’s interesting," he murmured softly. "People assume incompetence because they don’t understand what we’ve been through. They see us as outsiders, strangers who don’t fit into their narrow view of success."

Elias nodded slowly, taking a sip of ale and washing down the bitterness with a swallow of warmth. "Exactly," he said. "And once we prove ourselves, those assumptions will crumble like dry parchment in rain."

Maren pushed her empty bowl aside, leaning back against the booth seat with renewed energy. "Let them talk," she said firmly. "We’ll let our actions speak louder than their words ever could."

Elias smiled faintly at her determination, feeling a sense of pride swell within him. They were far from perfect, but they were honest, capable, and willing to stand firm even when the world tried to push them aside. And that was enough—for now.
