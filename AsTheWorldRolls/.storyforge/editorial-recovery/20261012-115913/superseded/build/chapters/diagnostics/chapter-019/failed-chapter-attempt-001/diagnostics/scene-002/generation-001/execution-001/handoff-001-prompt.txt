The dawn light was failing fast, swallowed by a thickening cloud of grey mist that seemed to press against the edges of vision. Torin’s hands were a blur as he worked the iron-bound rope, his knuckles white as he lashed the crate tighter to the wagon frame. Maren stood like a sentinel at the rear of the line, her spear tip dipped in sulfur paste, ready to puncture any creature that dared approach Silas’s wagons.

“Hold,” Elias said, his voice cutting through the din of wind and rattling wheels. He didn't shout; he didn't need to. The others had heard him. They had all felt the shift in pressure.

The third wave was coming. It wasn’t just a horde; it was a funneling mass of shadowy forms, drawn by something invisible but undeniable. The siphon effect was pulling them toward the center of the convoy, where the crate sat on its trailer. Elias could see the creatures swirling in the air before they struck, their movement synchronized to a rhythm that felt less like nature and more like a machine winding down to release a bolt.

“They’re drawn to the water,” Torin called out, not looking up from his work. “They’re not just hunting us; they’re hunting the crate.”

“Then we move it,” Elias said, already stepping toward the wagon’s driver seat. He grabbed the reins, but before he could pull, a hiss of spores erupted from the mist behind them. The first creature lunged, its form shifting between solid and smoke, aiming for the space between Maren’s spear point and the wagon wheel.

Maren didn’t flinch. She thrust forward with practiced precision, the sulfur-coated tip of her spear striking the creature’s shoulder. It shrieked—a sound like tearing metal—and dissolved into a cloud of black spores before it could reach her. The smell was foul, burning at the back of the throat, but she kept her stance, waiting for the next one.

Elias drew his sword in one smooth motion, stepping forward to intercept the second creature. It swung with unnatural speed, its form elongating as it moved, trying to bypass him entirely. Elias parried, the blade ringing against a tendril of shadow. He didn’t try to hold it back; he drove the flat of his blade into its chest and slashed downward, severing the connection to whatever was pulling it forward. The creature collapsed, dissolving into spores that hissed as they touched the road.

“Torin!” Elias shouted over the roar of the wind. “Get the crate away from the wagon! We need to move it upwind.”

Torin hesitated only a fraction of a second before he cut the lashings on the crate’s side release and grabbed the handle of the trailer. He pulled with all his strength, fighting against the momentum of the creatures closing in. The crate rattled as it shifted, its seal glowing faintly under the dim light.

“Go!” Elias yelled again, pushing off the creature he’d just felled. He spun, his sword arcing through the air to meet another attacker. Kaelen joined him instantly, his shield raising a wall of steel that blocked two more charges. Together they formed a defensive line, their movements coordinated and efficient. They weren’t fighting alone anymore; they were a unit, honed by necessity and trust.

Elara stepped forward from the rear, her staff glowing with a soft blue light as she began to chant. She wasn’t casting a spell in the traditional sense; she was weaving a ward around the crate, a magical barrier that would dampen the siphon effect just enough to give them time to reposition.

“Hold!” Elara commanded, her voice steady despite the chaos around them. “Keep them off the crate! I’ll stabilize the seal for thirty seconds!”

The creatures swarmed, their forms twisting and writhing as they tried to find a way past the ward. Elias blocked two more with his sword, while Kaelen held firm against a group trying to climb over his shield. Torin was already halfway across the road, hauling the crate toward the front of the caravan, where Silas’s men had erected a secondary line of defense using canvas awnings and reinforced wheels.

The siphon effect intensified, pulling at Elias’s skin with invisible fingers. He gritted his teeth, pushing through the discomfort as he kept his blade moving, deflecting blow after blow. “Keep them back!” Elias shouted, parrying a third charge and driving his blade into the creature’s core again. “Torin, move faster! Elara, hold the ward!”

Torin grunted, muscles straining as he dragged the crate up the incline toward the safer zone. The iron-bound rope held firm, its metal core resisting even the greatest tension. Maren covered the rear, her spear a blur of motion as she kept the creatures at bay, her focus unwavering.

Elara’s ward shimmered around the crate, glowing brighter with each passing second. She was pouring every ounce of her will into maintaining the barrier, sweat dripping from her brow despite the cold wind. The creatures pressed harder, their forms stretching and twisting as they tried to break through, but the ward held—barely.

Elias caught his breath for a moment, wiping blood from the corner of his mouth. He wasn’t tired; he was focused, every instinct screaming at him to keep pushing forward. The siphon effect was building, but so was their resolve. They had prepared for this moment, and now they were proving that preparation mattered more than luck.

“Almost there!” Torin called out, his voice strained but determined. He hauled the crate onto the higher ground near Silas’s wagons, where the creatures couldn’t quite reach it anymore. Silas himself stepped forward, ordering his men to reinforce the perimeter with additional canvas and stakes.

Then, with a final surge of effort, the creatures surged forward again, only to be repelled by the combined might of the party’s defenses. The siphon effect began to fade as they moved away from the source of the pull, and the creatures slowed, retreating back into the mist.

Elias sheathed his sword, exhaling a long breath he hadn’t realized he’d been holding. He looked at Elara, who was catching her breath, her face pale but resolute. “We bought some time,” she said quietly, brushing spores from her cloak. “But we’re not out of this yet.”
