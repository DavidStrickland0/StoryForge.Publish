The map on the wagon bed was still spread out, a patchwork of charcoal lines and faded ink that Elara had spent the last half-hour scrutinizing with a magnifying glass she’d borrowed from Silas’s satchel. Elias stood beside her now, his hands tucked into the pockets of his leather jerkin, watching the way light caught the parchment dust motes dancing above the surface.

Silas Vane leaned against the side of his wagon, arms crossed and gaze drifting toward the horizon where the sky was beginning to bruise purple with twilight. His merchant’s smile had softened into something more contemplative, less performative. He hadn’t made a single move since Elias proposed they keep the crate sealed until further notice; he’d simply nodded once when Torin distributed the remaining supplies onto the wagon bed.

Kaelen was already checking the gauntlet one last time before mounting up, his fingers tracing the brass surface with practiced ease. The gauntlet’s inner glow had dimmed to a faint ember-pulse, barely visible now that the immediate crisis had passed. But there was still something else he couldn’t ignore—the weight of it in his palm, the way it felt less like a tool and more like a burden he’d been entrusted with before he was ready.

Maren had found herself standing near Torin again, though this time they weren’t arguing about perimeter shifts or surge patterns. She was watching him pack his sword into its scabbard with that same methodical care he showed everything else. “You ever think about what happens when we run out of monsters to fight?” she asked quietly, not looking at him as if expecting an answer she might not want given.

Torin paused mid-motion, one hand still on the hilt. He didn’t turn around immediately; he just let her question hang in the air between them for a moment longer than necessary. “I think about that all the time,” he admitted finally, voice low enough that only Maren could hear it over the distant sound of Silas’s wagon wheels turning on cobblestones. “But until then, we’re moving forward.”

Elias turned from the map then, his expression thoughtful as he approached where Kaelen stood by the wagon. The gauntlet was still dark now, its surface cool to the touch and unremarkable except for the faint scar patterns etched into the metal that seemed almost decorative at this point. “You ready?” Elias asked, gesturing toward the stirrup waiting on his horse’s saddle.

Kaelen nodded once, pulling himself up onto the saddle with a smooth motion that belied the exhaustion still clinging to him from yesterday’s ordeal. The gauntlet remained inert at his wrist, its brass surface dull and unremarkable except for those faint scars that now looked like nothing more than decorative flourishes rather than evidence of recent magical activity. “Ready enough,” he said simply, settling himself into the saddle with a posture that suggested readiness despite fatigue.

Maren mounted behind him, her movements careful but confident as she adjusted herself into place beside him. Torin followed shortly after, though he lingered for a moment near Elias’s horse as if waiting for permission to join them rather than asserting his own presence. Silas Vane remained leaning against the wagon side until everyone else had mounted, then gave a single nod before turning back toward his own preparations.

Elias exchanged a glance with Maren over Kaelen’s shoulder. “We’ve got enough,” he said quietly, gesturing toward the crate now marked with Elara’s charcoal warning sigil that seemed almost comical against the backdrop of impending danger. “The crate stays sealed until we hit the next checkpoint. Then we decide what to do next.”

“Agreed,” Maren replied without hesitation, though her tone carried a note of something deeper than mere agreement—a recognition that they’d reached some kind of consensus about how to handle the situation ahead. “And if things get messy?” she added with a half-smile that didn’t quite reach her eyes.

“Then we’ll handle it together,” Elias said simply, turning his horse toward the road ahead. “That’s what parties are for.”

The road stretched out before them, winding through valleys and over ridges that promised danger as well as discovery. There would be storms to dodge, Blight surges to weather, and rivals to outmaneuver along the way—but for now, they had supplies, a plan, and each other. That was enough to keep moving forward, at least until the next challenge arose.

As they started walking toward their horses, Elias paused briefly near the wagon where Elara was finishing her work on the crate. “How’s that sigil looking?” he asked, glancing down at the charcoal marking now etched onto the wood surface. It had been drawn with deliberate care, forming a spiral pattern that seemed almost alive as it caught the fading light.

“It’ll do,” Elara said with a shrug, wiping her hands on a cloth before tucking it into her satchel. “Just enough to warn anyone who comes looking without giving away exactly what’s inside. And if they need something worse than a warning, we can deal with them later.”

“Later,” Maren echoed, stepping up beside Elias as he climbed onto his horse. “For now, let’s get moving before the storm hits early tomorrow.”

The group mounted their horses and began the march toward the horizon, leaving behind the market square and its noisy commerce for the quiet solitude of the road ahead. Behind them, Silas Vane watched them go with a satisfied smirk before turning back to his wagon and checking one last time that everything was properly loaded before setting off after them.

The road stretched ahead of them, winding through valleys and over ridges that promised danger as well as discovery. There would be storms to dodge, Blight surges to weather, and rivals to outmaneuver along the way—but for now, they had supplies, a plan, and each other. That was enough to keep moving forward, at least until the next challenge arose.
