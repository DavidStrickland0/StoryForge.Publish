The second phase did not begin with a shout or a command, but with a silent, terrifying shift in Kaelen’s posture.

Elias stood at the edge of the clearing, his eyes locked on the young man ten paces away. He had spoken the word *Ready*, and now he was watching for the first sign that the mechanism was failing. The plan had been simple: listen, channel, feed. But the barrier’s reaction to the initial flow had been immediate and violent.

The violet light of the seal did not merely grow; it expanded, pulsing outward like a heartbeat in slow motion. The air around the shrine grew heavy, pressing against their ears with a low-frequency thrum that vibrated in Elias’s teeth. The ground shook again, but this time the rhythm was erratic, stuttering as if the earth itself were struggling to keep pace with the surge rising from below.

Kaelen stood still, his eyes closed, his hands clasped behind his back. But Elias could see the change in the air around him. The fog that had been retreating moments ago was beginning to curl inward, drawn toward Kaelen’s skin like moths to a flame. It wasn’t pulling him away anymore; it was clinging to him, coating his shoulders and hair in a thin, green film.

But it was the gauntlet that told Elias what he needed to know before he even saw the physical effects on Kaelen himself.

The brass device had gone from cold and inert to burning hot within seconds. It glowed with a dull, angry red light that seeped into the wood of his trousers where it touched his thigh. The heat radiated outward, warming the damp air around him, but it was not comfort—it was stress. Metal under pressure, expanding, threatening to crack.

Torin had shifted position instinctively, stepping closer to Kaelen with his sword still drawn. His hand rested near the hilt, ready to intervene if Kaelen lost control. He did not speak, but his body language screamed warning. The soldier understood that the balance was tipping. The channel was too wide. The valve was overheating.

Elias watched the barrier again. The violet light had deepened to a bruised purple, almost black at the edges where the Blight was being consumed. But the consumption rate had spiked. The stream of green smoke pouring from the shrine’s fissure had thickened, rising faster than before, and the air around Kaelen was beginning to shimmer with visible distortion.

Elias took a step forward, his boots sinking into the mud. “Kaelen,” he said, his voice low and steady. “Hold it back.”

The young man opened his eyes. They were no longer dark or vacant; they were glassy, fixed on something only he could see. Sweat beaded on his forehead, rolling down his temples to vanish in the collar of his shirt. His breathing was shallow, rapid, and controlled by sheer willpower alone.

“I’m trying,” Kaelen whispered, his voice strained. “It’s… it’s pulling harder.”

Elias looked at Torin, then back at Kaelen. The heat from the gauntlet was radiating outward, warming the damp air around them. It was a dangerous sign. If Kaelen pushed too hard, if he let the channel widen beyond his control, the feedback loop could shatter the seal entirely and release everything they had been trying to contain.

“Elara,” Elias said without turning. “Check the chisel angle. We need to dampen the resonance.”

Elara stepped forward from behind Maren, her chisel still pressed against the quartz stone. She didn’t speak; she simply adjusted her grip, shifting her weight slightly as if sensing the change in vibration before it could be heard. Her eyes narrowed as she watched the barrier’s pulse.

Maren moved to stand beside Elara, her hands hovering near the chisel but not touching yet. “How much?” she asked, her voice quiet but urgent.

Elias looked at Kaelen again. The young man’s lips were trembling slightly, his jaw clenched tight enough that the muscle beneath his skin stood out in sharp definition. He was holding back a tide, and the cost was showing on his face.

“More than we thought,” Elias said. “If he pushes another inch, the seal breaks.”

Elara nodded once, sharply. She moved the chisel slightly, changing the angle by a fraction of a degree. The vibration in the air shifted instantly, becoming less intense. The fog around Kaelen slowed its retreat, then began to recede more deliberately, no longer clinging with desperation but flowing under controlled conditions.

The heat from the gauntlet cooled slightly, though it remained warm to the touch. Kaelen exhaled sharply, a sound of relief mixed with exhaustion. His shoulders dropped an inch, and the tension in his face eased.

Elias watched the barrier again. The violet light pulsed steadily now, no longer surging wildly but maintaining a consistent rhythm. The stream of Blight continued to flow, thinner than before but still substantial enough to keep the seal growing stronger.

Torin relaxed slightly, lowering his sword just an inch. He didn’t speak, but his stance was no longer rigid with anticipation of disaster. The immediate danger had passed—for now.

Elias turned back to Kaelen, who was staring at the ground as if afraid to look up. “You did well,” Elias said quietly. “But remember: this isn’t a contest of will. It’s a machine. And machines break when you push them too hard.”

Kaelen nodded slowly. “I know.”

Elias looked at Elara and Maren. They were already adjusting their positions, fine-tuning the resonance to match the new flow rate. The barrier was stable, but fragile. One wrong move, one moment of hesitation, and everything could unravel.

He turned back to the horizon, watching the city below. The fog had lifted further now, revealing the spires of the temple in the distance, glowing faintly with an inner light that seemed to pulse in time with the barrier’s heartbeat.

They were still feeding the seal. They were still buying time. But the cost was rising, and they couldn’t afford to keep paying it at this rate.

Elias looked at Kaelen again. “We need to find a way to reduce the load without stopping the flow.”

Kaelen nodded. “I’ll try to focus harder. But if I push too hard…”

“You won’t,” Elias said firmly. “Not while we’re together. Not while we have each other.”

Torin stepped up beside Kaelen, placing a hand on his shoulder. A silent reassurance. A reminder that he wasn’t alone in this fight.

Elias looked at the barrier one last time. It was strong now, but only because they had paid the price to make it so. And the price wasn’t going to end here.

The second phase had begun. And it was far from over.
