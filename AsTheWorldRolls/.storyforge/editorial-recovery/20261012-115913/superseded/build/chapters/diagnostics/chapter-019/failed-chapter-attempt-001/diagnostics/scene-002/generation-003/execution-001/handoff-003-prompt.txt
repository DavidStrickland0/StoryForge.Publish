Torin's voice cut through the rising hum of static, sharp as a flint strike. "The crate is unstable."

Elias barely heard him over the roar in his ears. The air had tasted like ozone for hours now, but since dawn, it had thickened into a heavy, electric pressure that made the hair on his arms stand up and the metal on his belt feel uncomfortably hot. He didn't shout back; he moved instead, stepping away from the wagon line to gain a clearer vantage of the approaching shadow.

The road ahead was no longer just dark. It was a void, a churning wall of unnatural fog that seemed to breathe with its own slow rhythm. Within that darkness, shapes were coalescing, drawn forward not by sight or sound, but by the terrifying pull of something deeper in the world's fabric.

"Siphon!" Elias warned, his voice cracking slightly against the wind. He grabbed Torin's arm before the man could rush back to the wagon alone. "Don't let it get loose! Keep the tension even on the rope!"

Torin grimaced, sweat already beading on his brow despite the morning chill. His fingers were raw from wrestling with the iron-bound cable, but he didn't slacken his grip. Instead, he adjusted the loops around the crate's corners, ensuring the load was distributed perfectly against the strange gravity that seemed to be pressing down on it. "I know what I'm doing," Torin muttered, though his eyes darted nervously toward the darkening sky. "Just... keep your blade ready."

Elara had already taken position beside Elias, her stylus hovering over a small leather-bound notebook she'd brought with them—a habit of hers that no one seemed to understand until now, when the math began screaming in their ears. She wasn't writing notes; she was calculating trajectories. "Three seconds," she whispered, not looking up from her page. "The surge will hit in three seconds. If we don't move the crate before then, they'll tear through the rope and drag it away with them."

Elias nodded once. "On three?"

"On two!" Elara snapped. "Move it! Now!"

They were still standing too close to the wagon's center when the third wave struck—not as a surprise attack, but as a tidal rush of spore-filled shadows that surged forward with unnatural speed. They weren't just moving faster; they were *pulled* toward the crate, their movements coordinated like dancers responding to a single conductor.

"Left flank!" Elias shouted, swinging his broadsword in a wide arc that cleaved through two of the shadow creatures before they could latch onto the wagon's hull. The steel bit deep into the blight-thing's form, and instead of blood, thick black ichor exploded outward, staining his armor and searing his skin with a brief, hot sting.

Torin roared in response, charging forward with his greatsword raised high. His blade was heavier than any normal weapon, forged to cleave through steel beams and stone walls alike, but even it seemed to struggle against the density of the Blight-Touched mass. As he swung down, a creature intercepted his path, wrapping its spore-fingers around his leg with terrifying strength. Torin gritted his teeth, using his free hand to pry at the grip while slashing downward in a brutal horizontal sweep that split the creature in half.

Maren held firm at the rear, her spear tipped with a sharpened iron point designed specifically for piercing magical constructs. She didn't need to shout; she simply pointed toward a cluster of creatures trying to slip past Silas's men, and one by one they were impaled, their bodies dissolving into harmless mist before they could cause more harm.

But the sheer number of them was overwhelming. The Blight-Touched weren't individual beasts anymore—they had become part of the siphon effect itself, a single organism made of flesh and shadow that responded to the presence of pure water like moths to flame. They surged forward with relentless ferocity, ignoring pain, fear, and death until their target was reached.

"Stabilize!" Elias ordered, grabbing one end of the rope while Torin held the other. "Now!"

Together they pulled hard on the iron-bound cable, which wrapped tightly around the crate's base and the wagon's frame. With a groan of protesting metal, the crate shifted slightly, moving away from the wagon just enough to reduce the direct pull of the siphon. It wasn't perfect—the pressure still felt like a physical weight pressing against their shoulders—but it was enough to slow the advance.

Elara stepped forward, raising her wand and weaving a quick stabilization spell around the crate. A faint blue glow emanated from the glowing runes on the crate's lid, creating a temporary barrier of force that repelled the spores trying to breach the seal. The effect wasn't perfect—some creatures managed to squeeze through anyway—but it gave them crucial seconds to regroup and reposition.

"You're doing well," Elias said quietly, glancing at Elara as she worked. "But this isn't going to last."

"I know," she replied, her voice steady despite the chaos around them. "That's why we need to keep moving. Once the crate is stable enough, we'll relocate it to a safer position. We have time for that if we act fast."

Elias nodded grimly. They were winning—for now—but the siphon effect was already beginning to wear on them. The air crackled with energy, and the static charge in their clothes made sparks jump from finger to finger whenever they brushed against each other. If they didn't find a way to break the cycle soon, the entire caravan would be consumed before anyone could react.

Silas Vane watched silently from his wagon, his face masked by a hood that shielded him from view. He wasn't idle; he was organizing his men to reinforce the perimeter and prepare for whatever came next. If they couldn't hold the line, there might not be enough time to retreat before the Blight consumed them all.

And then, just as suddenly as it had begun, the third wave receded. The creatures that had been surging forward froze mid-step, their spore-forms flickering like dying candles in a draft. For a brief moment, silence fell over the road again—but it was only temporary.

Elias exhaled sharply, lowering his blade. "Okay," he said, wiping ichor from his arm. "Let's try this again."
