The heavy iron-bound cage wheels grated against the cobblestones as Torin directed the lead wagon away from the Gilded Tankard's front entrance. The crate, a monolithic box of treated oak and brass fittings, sat secured in the center of the carriage, its surface scarred by faint, phosphorescent runes that pulsed with a rhythmic, barely perceptible light. It was the most precious cargo on the road to the Watchfort, and it was already humming with tension.

Silas Vane clambered onto the running board of the wagon, his knees trembling slightly as he adjusted his heavy leather satchel. He had spent all night negotiating the route and securing permits from the city watch, but now that they were moving, the weight of the mission pressed on him like a physical burden.

"Easy there," Silas muttered, gripping the reins of the lead mare until his knuckles turned white. "We're not running for our lives yet."

Torin gave the horses a light tap with his whip, and they moved into a steady trot. The road ahead wound away from the city walls, cutting through fields where the air was thick with the scent of ripe grain and the distant sound of roosters. Maren walked alongside the wagon, her staff tapping rhythmically against the dirt. She didn't look back at Silas; she was focused on the formation, checking the spacing between the escort wagons that followed the lead vehicle.

"The crate is leaking," Maren called out quietly, her voice cutting through the ambient noise of the horses' hooves and the creaking of wood. "Not water. Energy."

Elias walked beside her, scanning the horizon. The sky was clear, but the air felt charged, static making the hair on his arms stand up. He had seen that look before—a prelude to a shift in atmospheric pressure or magical resonance.

"Leaking energy," Elias corrected, keeping his voice low. "It's siphoning off the ambient instability."

Maren nodded, her expression grim but composed. "Elara said it acts as a beacon during the lunar cycle. We're approaching the transition window. The third-quarter moon is cresting the eastern ridge in twenty minutes. That's when the river tide drops and the seal weakens."

"Then we need to be ready," Elias said, checking his sword hilt. "If the siphon peaks, the creatures won't be wandering in; they'll be pulled from the seal itself."

Silas Vane leaned over the side of the wagon, peering at the crate with a mixture of awe and terror that made him look uncharacteristically young. "It's beautiful," he whispered, as if afraid to break its spell. "And terrible."

"Don't touch it," Maren snapped without looking up. "You'll disrupt the resonance field."

"I wasn't going—" Silas started, then stopped short, eyes widening as a low hum vibrated through the wagon floor. The crate's runes flared briefly, casting long, pale shadows across the road. The horses shied, their nostrils flaring, and Torin quickly applied the brakes, bringing them to a halt before they had traveled another fifty paces from the tavern.

"It's rising," Torin said, his usual calm demeanor replaced by sharp focus. He reached into his pack and pulled out a coil of thick hemp rope, though it was unlikely to be enough. "The energy spike is coming faster than Elara predicted."

Elara emerged from the back of the second wagon, her journal tucked under one arm. She looked up at the crate, her dark eyes narrowing as she analyzed the fluctuating light. "The seal is destabilizing," she said, her voice tight. "The siphon effect is accelerating. The pressure inside the seal is forcing a reaction."

"Then we reinforce it," Elias said, stepping forward to inspect the crate's bindings. He saw that the brass fittings were straining against the internal pressure. "Torin, Maren, prepare the iron cable. Now."

Maren moved instantly, snatching the heavy coil from Torin and beginning to wrap it around the crate's base. Her hands worked with practiced efficiency, tightening each loop until the metal wire was taut. Silas scrambled back, clutching his satchel as if it were a lifeline.

"What do I do?" he cried out, his voice cracking. "I can't fix this! I don't know what to say!"

"You're the merchant," Elias said calmly, turning to face him. "Your job is to keep us on schedule and make sure no one follows us. If the Watchfort sends a courier or a patrol after us, Silas must handle that diplomatically."

Silas blinked, confusion washing over his face. "Handle what? There's no time for speeches!"

"There will be complications," Elias said, his tone firm but not unkind. "That's why you're here. But first, we deal with the immediate problem."

A low growl rippled through the air, vibrating in their chests. The crate's light shifted from a soft pulse to a harsh, strobing glow. Shadows detached themselves from the road ahead, coalescing into forms that twisted and writhed like smoke given shape by a storm. They were Blight-Touched, drawn by the siphon, eager to consume the unstable energy inside the crate.

"Form up!" Elias shouted, drawing his sword. "Torin, hold the left flank. Maren, right side. Elara, keep us calm with your wards or spells."

Maren raised her staff, a small shield of blue light flickering into existence around them. Torin took a defensive stance, his broadsword drawn and low to the ground. Silas backed away again, muttering something about "unfair odds" before hiding behind Elias's shoulder.

The first creature lunged, its form jagged and translucent, eyes burning with hungry green fire. It moved faster than anything else they had faced, reacting to the siphon rather than brute strength. Maren intercepted it with a sweeping strike from her staff, the impact knocking the thing back with a sickening crunch of bone.

"Fire!" Torin yelled, unleashing a burst of flame that scorched the creature's lower half before it could regenerate.

Elias parried a claw swipe, the force of the blow rattling his arm. "It's not just attacking us," he shouted over the din. "It's trying to get to the crate!"

The second creature lunged for the lead wagon, its claws slashing at the wood. The crate shuddered violently, and a spray of liquid water escaped from a crack in the side, hissing as it touched the dry ground.

"Elara!" Elias called out. "Reinforce the seal! Now!"

Elara raised both hands, her fingers twitching as she channeled energy from her journal's diagrams. A web of golden light erupted from her palms, wrapping around the crate's exterior. It was a temporary ward, designed to redirect the siphon back into the seal rather than letting it leak outward. The creature recoiled as if burned by an inferno, its form dissolving into wisps of smoke before reforming.

"Hold!" Maren shouted, driving her staff into the ground and anchoring the wagon against the surge. "Don't let it breach the seal!"

The battle was chaotic, but the party moved with a unity born of necessity. Torin held the creatures at bay, using his shield to deflect claws while delivering heavy strikes. Elias focused on the immediate threats, his sword flashing in arcs that severed limbs and forced enemies back. Maren coordinated their movements, calling out positions and timing adjustments as the tide of battle shifted.

Silas peeked around Elias's leg, his face pale but determined. "I'll… I'll call for help," he stammered. "Someone must send reinforcements!"

"There's no time for help!" Elias snapped, though his tone softened instantly when Silas flinched. "We handle this ourselves. You focus on the perimeter and make sure no one else joins us."

Silas nodded quickly, stepping back and pulling out a small flare from his satchel. He ignited it, holding it high above the wagon to signal any nearby patrols or caravans. The flare burned with an eerie purple hue, casting long, distorted shadows across the road.

"Good," Elias said, seeing the creature's form destabilize under Elara's ward. "Now, push it back!"

Maren charged forward, her staff glowing brighter as she unleashed a wave of kinetic force that knocked the creatures aside. Torin followed up with a sweeping strike, cleaving through the air where one had been moments before. The remaining creature hesitated, its form flickering as if unsure whether to continue pursuing them or retreat toward the weakening seal.

"Retreat!" Elara shouted. "The siphon is peaking! It's pulling them toward the crate instead of us!"

The creatures turned, their gaze locked on the glowing runes of the crate. They didn't attack; they hovered in mid-air, drawn by the instability like moths to a flame. The energy inside the crate was surging, creating a vortex of raw power that threatened to consume everything nearby.

"The seal is failing!" Elara cried out, her voice strained as she worked harder than ever before. "We need to move it now!"

"Move it?" Torin asked, his eyes wide with shock. "That crate won't budge without a crane or a dozen men!"

"No crane," Elias said, grabbing hold of the crate's handle. "No time. We push it ourselves."
