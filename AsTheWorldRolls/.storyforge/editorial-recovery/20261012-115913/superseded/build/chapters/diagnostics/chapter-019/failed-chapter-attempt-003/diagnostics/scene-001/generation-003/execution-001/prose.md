The road out of the city was a ribbon of dust that quickly swallowed the footprints behind them. The wagon, pulled by four sturdy oxen, lumbered into the lead position, flanked by Elias’s small party and Silas Vane riding pillion on his own horse at the rear, clutching his ledger case to his chest with white-knuckled grip.

Torin rode beside the wagon, his eyes constantly scanning the horizon where the dust plumes of other caravans had faded into the haze of the valley. He checked the tension on the iron-bound cables securing the crates in the wagon bed again. They groaned softly under the strain, a sound that Maren seemed to hear without looking down from her saddle.

"Maren," Elias called out, riding up alongside her so they could speak low enough not to carry over the creaking of the wagon wheels. "Torin is double-checking the rigging. He’s right; if we’re going to exploit that dead zone you calculated, the structural integrity needs to be absolute."

Maren nodded, a single motion of her head as she looked ahead at the road winding toward the foothills. The landscape was already beginning to shift; the gentle ebb of the valley floor gave way to the jagged teeth of the mountain range looming on the western horizon. "Torin knows his business," she said flatly. "And my calculations on the surge window are based on the seal’s instability, not just celestial mechanics. We have forty minutes before the pressure builds."

Elias glanced at Silas, who was still behind them, staring nervously at the empty road as if expecting a shadow to detach itself from the trees and lunge for the water crate. "Silas," Elias said, turning his horse toward the merchant’s retreating figure. "Keep your distance until we’re in open ground. If anything comes, you want to be able to run, not hide behind the wagon."

Silas paled visibly at the mention of 'anything' and tightened his grip on the saddle horn. "Understood," he muttered. "I’ll stay back. I don’t like it out here, but… if there’s trouble, I can send a runner back for help."

"There is no help coming from the city for forty miles," Elias said gently. "You’re with us now. That’s all that matters."

Silas swallowed hard, nodded mutely, and pulled his horse back to let them pass. Behind them, the wagon trundled forward, carrying the weight of their shared purpose. The crates were sealed, the water inside humming with a faint, rhythmic vibration that Elias could feel in the soles of his boots through the connection of the road and the ground. It was a hum that made him wonder how many others in the valley had heard it, or perhaps only felt it as a headache before they realized what it meant.

They rode for an hour, the sun climbing higher into a sky that seemed unusually clear. The wind picked up, kicking up dust that coated their boots and settled into the crevices of their armor. Maren adjusted her goggles, squinting against the glare. "The seal’s instability is peaking," she said, her voice cutting through the din of the road. "Forty minutes to go."

Elias checked his watch, noting the time with a nod. "Then we move into position. Torin, keep an eye on the rigging. Maren, prepare the sulfur paste. Elara and Kaelen, stay close to the crate. We’re going to test the dead zone now."

Torin’s eyes narrowed as he looked at the crate behind them. The crates were wrapped in lead-lined canvas, marked with warning symbols that no one could read without proper training, but everyone knew what they meant: *Blight-Touched Resonance Hazard*. "The cable tension is holding," Torin said, his voice steady. "But I’m keeping the slack tight. If anything tries to latch onto the rigging, I’ll cut it before it snaps."

"Good," Maren replied, her gaze fixed on the road ahead. "Mistakes happen when you’re rushing. We don’t rush until we have to."

As if summoned by their words, a shadow detached itself from the treeline on the left side of the road—a Blight-Touched creature, its form shifting between solid and gaseous as it drifted toward them with unnatural speed. It let out a low growl that sounded like stones grinding together beneath water, and then it lunged for the crate.

Elias drew his sword in one smooth motion, the blade flashing silver in the sunlight. "Maren!" he shouted. "Now!"

The creature slammed into the crate, its claws scrabbling against the lead lining with a sound like tearing metal. Elias parried its attack, the force of its blow sending him sliding backward across the road as his boots dug into the dirt. The crate groaned, the seal vibrating violently under the pressure.

"Use the paste!" Maren shouted, drawing a flask of thick yellow liquid from her saddlebag and throwing it at the creature’s side. The sulfur paste splattered against its skin, hissing as it reacted with the Blight residue. The creature recoiled, snarling in pain as the chemical burn threatened to disrupt its cohesion.

"Keep it off!" Elias yelled, stepping forward to shield the crate with his body while Kaelen raised a small barrier spell that shimmered into existence above the lead lining. "It’s drawn to the resonance! Don’t let it touch the seal!"

Torin slashed at the creature’s limbs with his scythe-like blade, severing tendrils that latched onto the rigging before they could snap the cables. "The tension is holding!" he shouted over the din of battle. "But if it gets close enough to the crate, it’ll destabilize the seal!"

Elias felt the hum in his boots intensify, the vibration growing stronger as the creature tried to break through the barriers around it. He could see the cracks forming in the lead lining where the creature’s claws had scored deep gouges. "It’s working!" he called out to Maren. "The dead zone is activating! Hold it off for just a few more seconds!"

Maren threw another flask of sulfur paste, the creature hissing as the chemical burn disrupted its form. "It’s going to hold!" she shouted. "But we need to move now!"

Elias nodded, drawing his sword again and parrying another blow from the creature’s claws. The seal was humming louder now, the vibration so strong it made his teeth ache. He could feel the water inside the crate shifting, the magical properties being siphoned into the creature as it fed on the instability.

"Go!" he shouted. "Now!"

He pushed the creature off with a burst of strength that sent it skidding back toward the treeline, its form dissolving rapidly as the sulfur paste continued to burn its skin. The crate was safe for now, but they knew they had only bought them a few minutes before the creature returned, stronger and hungrier than before.

"Let’s move!" Maren shouted, leading the way toward a small hill where they could set up a temporary observation point. "We’ll wait until the surge peaks and then strike!"

They scrambled up the hill, leaving the road behind them as the creature continued to circle back, its form growing more solid with each passing second. Elias could see the seal vibrating, the cracks spreading wider as the creature tried to break through.

"We’re doing it," Maren said, her voice steady despite the chaos around them. "The dead zone is working."

Elias nodded, wiping sweat from his brow as he looked back at the road where the creature was now circling them again, its form more solid and dangerous than before. "Then we hold off until the surge peaks," he said. "Then we strike."

They waited in silence, the only sound being the hum of the seal and the growling of the creature as it circled them. Elias could feel the tension in his muscles, the vibration of the seal making him want to retreat into himself and hide. But they had a plan, they had each other, and they had the trust that came from weeks of shared hardship and triumphs.

"Ready," Maren said, her voice calm despite the danger around them. "When it gets close enough, we strike."

Elias nodded, drawing his sword again as the creature began to lunge toward them once more. The hum in the air grew louder, the seal vibrating so hard it felt like it was about to break apart entirely.

"Go!" he shouted, charging forward to intercept the creature with his blade.
