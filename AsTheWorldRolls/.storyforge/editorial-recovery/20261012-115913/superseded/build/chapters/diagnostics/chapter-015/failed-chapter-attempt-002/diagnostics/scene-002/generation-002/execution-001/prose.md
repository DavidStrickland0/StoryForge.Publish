Elara lowered the chisel, her breath fogging in the air around her eyes. The quartz stone had returned to its dull, translucent state, no longer glowing with the dangerous violet fire of the previous moment. The hum beneath their feet had settled into a rhythm that felt less like a heartbeat and more like a clockwork gear turning steadily in time.

Maren stepped back from Elara’s shoulder, her hands dropping from where she had been bracing the younger mage's frame. Her own face was pale, sweat beading on her forehead despite the chill of the valley, but her eyes were sharp with relief. She glanced at Kaelen, then at Torin, before looking up to Elias.

"Angle adjusted," Elara said, her voice steady but soft. "Vibration reduced by roughly two hundred percent. The stone is singing a lower note now. It’s... quieter."

"Easier to hold?" Elias asked, his gaze never leaving the barrier. He watched where the violet light met the fog. The black ink of the Blight was still flowing, but it moved with a deliberate slowness now, like oil spilling from a cracked bottle rather than water gushing from a burst pipe.

"The stone is listening to us," Elara corrected. "It’s not just absorbing. It’s filtering. We’re feeding it only what it can digest without overloading the seal." She looked at Kaelen, offering him a small, tired smile. "The surge was dangerous. If we had kept pushing that hard, the resonance would have shattered the quartz, and the Blight would have flooded back in full force."

Kaelen nodded, his hands shaking slightly as they gripped the brass gauntlet. The heat had receded from his skin, leaving him feeling cold again, but the internal pressure was gone. He could breathe. He could think. He could see Elias clearly without the world tilting around him.

"I can feel it," Kaelen said, his voice rough. "It’s still there, pulling at my wrist. But... it’s not trying to break me anymore. It’s waiting."

"Waiting for what?" Torin asked, though he had moved closer to place a firm hand on Kaelen's back again. The soldier’s posture was relaxed now, the tension that had knotted his shoulders loosening as the immediate crisis receded.

"For the next phase," Elias said quietly. He turned from the barrier and faced them fully. "We stabilize this flow. We keep it at this level for a few minutes. That will give the barrier enough time to build its strength back up. Then, we can push the frequency higher again."

He walked over to the edge of the clearing, where the stone walls of the shrine met the open sky. The ground beneath them was still damp from the water they had waded through earlier, but it was no longer slick with the oily residue of the Blight. The air smelled faintly of ozone and wet stone, a sterile scent that replaced the rotting earth.

"We need to make sure Kaelen doesn't push too hard," Elias continued, turning back to them. "Not because he's weak. Because the machine needs precision. If we try to pull more than it can handle, the entire system fails."

Kaelen looked down at his wrist. The brass gauntlet was warm now, not burning hot, but possessing a steady, comforting heat like a cup of tea on a winter morning. He flexed his fingers, feeling the subtle vibrations travel up his arm and settle in his bones. It was a strange sensation, one he had never experienced before: the feeling of being anchored, held in place by something stronger than fear or pain.

"I know what to do," Kaelen said. "I can hear it."

"Good," Maren said. She looked at Elara. "Keep the angle steady. Don't let her waver. If the pitch changes, even slightly, we lose control."

Elara nodded again. She kept her hands on the chisel and the stone, feeling the subtle shift in resonance as they held the new frequency. It was a delicate balance, one that required all of them to work together. Maren provided the force, Elara provided the precision, Kaelen provided the connection, and Elias provided the oversight.

Torin stood beside Kaelen, his hand resting lightly on the young man's shoulder. He didn't say anything, but his presence was a silent promise: *I'm here. You don't have to fight alone.*

Elias watched the barrier, watching the way the violet light pulsed in time with their own breath. It was beautiful, in a terrifying way. The Blight was being consumed, not by force, but by harmony. They were turning the enemy's own power against it, using the very essence of the corruption to fuel the shield that would protect them all.

"How long do we need to hold this?" Maren asked.

"Five minutes," Elias said. "Maybe six. Then we'll raise the pitch again, just a little. Not enough to break us, but enough to make the barrier stronger."

He looked at Kaelen. The young man was watching him intently, his eyes wide and focused. There was still fear there, but it was no longer paralyzing. It was part of the equation now, a variable they could account for.

"I'll keep an eye on you," Elias said softly. "If you feel yourself slipping, tell me immediately. Don't try to push through it."

Kaelen nodded. "I won't."

They held the position. The silence returned, but it was no longer heavy or oppressive. It was a silence of concentration, of shared purpose. The wind died down completely, leaving only the sound of their breathing and the faint hum of the quartz stone.

Elara adjusted her grip slightly, just a fraction of an inch, to maintain the perfect angle. Maren kept her weight distributed evenly, ready to step in if Elara faltered. Torin watched Kaelen's face, watching for any sign of distress or strain. Elias kept his eyes on the barrier, watching the way the violet light seemed to grow brighter with every passing second.

The world felt different now. The fog was still there, but it no longer threatened to swallow them whole. The path ahead was clearer. They had bought time, and they had used it well.

They were ready for the next phase. And this time, they would not be pushed beyond their limits. They would walk forward together, step by careful step, toward the end of the Blight.
