The morning air was crisp, carrying the scent of damp earth and woodsmoke that seemed to cling to the cobblestones of the market district. The Gilded Tankard stood behind them, a warm and chaotic memory already fading into the cool clarity of the road ahead. Elias adjusted the strap of his sword belt, feeling the familiar weight settle against his hip. It was a grounding sensation, a promise of readiness that contrasted sharply with the uncertainty of the path stretching out before them.

The caravan was already in motion, a slow, grinding procession of heavy wagons that moved with the reluctant dignity of living creatures. Silas Vane sat in the lead wagon, his posture rigid, his eyes scanning the perimeter with the sharp, acquisitive gaze of a man watching his own inventory. He did not look at the party. He did not need to. They were part of the machinery now, gears designed to keep the machine moving.

Elias fell into step beside the lead wagon, his boots finding the rhythm of the wheels on the uneven stone. To his left, Torin walked with a purposeful stride, his hands clasped behind his back, his eyes fixed on the rigging of the wagons ahead. He was counting something, Elias knew. Not the wheels, but the tension in the axles, the angle of the horses’ shoulders, the precise distance between the iron-bound cables that wrapped around the crates.

Maren walked slightly ahead of them, her silhouette cutting a sharp line against the pale morning sky. She moved with a fluid, predatory grace, her gaze sweeping the rooftops and alleyways as if inspecting them for hidden threats. She did not look at the road; she looked at the spaces between the objects on it.

Behind them, Elara and Kaelen walked in tandem, their conversation a low murmur that was cut off by the distance. Elias could hear the occasional rustle of parchment, the scratch of a stylus. They were the brain and the shield of the group, and their proximity spoke to a trust that had been forged in the quiet hours of the previous night.

The road out of the city was a narrow ribbon of packed dirt that wound through the tree line. The buildings receded quickly, replaced by the dense, green wall of the forest that bordered the valley. The air grew thicker here, heavy with the scent of pine needles and the distant, cold breath of the river.

Elias kept his eyes on the tree line. The Blight-Touched did not walk in the open, not when they were hunting. They waited in the shadows, in the hollows of trees, in the deep pools of water that lay hidden beneath the roots. The siphon effect Elara had described was a theoretical framework, a mathematical certainty that Elias had to trust with his life. But as they moved deeper into the forest, the reality of the situation began to press in. The light changed, filtering through the canopy in long, golden shafts that made the air look solid.

Torin slowed his pace, his eyes narrowing. He pointed to a specific spot on the ground, where the dirt was slightly darker than the rest.

"Moisture," Torin said, his voice low and steady. "But not rain."

Elias crouched down, inspecting the patch of earth. It was damp, but not wet. It felt cold, colder than the surrounding soil. He touched it with the tip of his finger, and a faint, electric tingle shot up his arm. It was a subtle thing, easily missed, but it was there.

"The seal," Elias murmured, his mind racing. "It’s leaking."

Torin nodded, his expression grave. "The pressure is building. We are close to the first surge."

Maren stopped walking. She did not turn around; she simply stood still, her hand resting on the hilt of her dagger. Her head was tilted slightly, listening to something that Elias could not hear.

"Silence," she said softly. "The birds have stopped."

Elias looked up. The forest was indeed quiet. The usual chatter of the morning had vanished, replaced by a heavy, expectant stillness. The air felt tight, charged with a potential energy that made the hairs on his arms stand on end.

Elara emerged from behind the wagons, her face pale but her eyes bright with focus. She held up her journal, its pages fluttering slightly in the breeze.

"The timing," she said, her voice tight. "We are within the window. The seal is weakening. We need to reinforce it now, or we will be drawn into the siphon."

Elias straightened, his mind snapping into focus. The plan was clear. They had to create a dead zone, a pocket of stability that would push the Blight-Touched back. It required precision, it required time, and it required them to work together, each person doing their part without hesitation.

He turned to Torin. "The cables. Are they ready?"

Torin nodded. "Tension is set. We can hold."

He turned to Maren. "Keep your eyes on the shadows. If anything moves, you kill it before it touches the crate."

Maren gave a single, sharp nod. "I will."

He turned to Elara. "The ritual. Can you do it in time?"

Elara’s jaw tightened. "I can."

Elias took a deep breath, the cool air filling his lungs. The forest watched them, silent and waiting. The Blight was close, drawn by the instability of the seal, pulled by the siphon effect that Elara had so precisely described. They were standing on the edge of a precipice, and the only way to survive was to step forward, not back.

"Let’s move," Elias said.
