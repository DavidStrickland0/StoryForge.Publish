The air inside the Gilded Tankard tasted different than the valley outside—thick with smoke, spilled ale, and the heavy perfume of unwashed bodies pressing shoulder-to-shoulder against rough-hewn tables. It was a place where survival was measured in coins and courage, but Elias had learned quickly that in some corners of the world, courage was not enough; you needed to know exactly when to speak, when to listen, and how to navigate the invisible lines drawn by custom and hierarchy.

He stepped across the threshold with Maren and Kaelen close behind him, their boots making no sound on the worn flagstones despite the noise of a hundred conversations overlapping like a storm. The interior was dimly lit by oil lamps that flickered against the smoke rising from the hearth, casting long shadows across faces that ranged from weary to wild-eyed. A few patrons sat slumped over their tankards, laughing too loudly or arguing about distant wars they might have fought in years past. Others huddled near the fire, nursing wounds or simply trying to stay warm enough to keep moving.

Elias scanned the room with a practiced eye, noting which tables were occupied by locals who seemed comfortable here and which were reserved for strangers—often those who had failed to prove themselves worthy of the tavern’s hospitality code. He spotted Silas Vane near the front counter, surrounded by crates marked *Silas Vane Merchants* in faded ink. The merchant looked up as they approached, his expression unreadable behind a beard that seemed to have seen decades of travel.

"Thorne," he said without rising, his voice calm but firm enough to carry over the din. "I see you've found your way back to civilization."

"We're here for passage through the pass," Elias replied, keeping his tone neutral despite the tension in his shoulders. "And we'd like to discuss terms before committing to any escort arrangement."

Vane chuckled softly, pouring himself a drink from one of the many mugs stacked on the bar. "Terms? Ah, yes. Terms are everything, aren't they? You know how I operate. Fairness is important, but so is protection. If things go south—and they often do in these parts—I need someone who understands that liability matters more than profit."

Elias nodded slowly. He knew where this was going. "We understand," he said evenly. "But we also believe that a partnership should be built on mutual respect, not fear."

Vane's eyes narrowed slightly, though whether it was amusement or threat remained unclear. "Respect is earned, Thorne. Not given. And if you're looking for sympathy from a man who's seen half the continent burn down around him, you've come to the wrong place."

Before Elias could respond, a commotion erupted near the entrance of the tavern. A group of soldiers had stumbled in, drunk and disheveled, shouting about "fair prices" and "unfair taxes." One of them, a burly man with a scar running down his cheek, spotted Kaelen standing nearby and immediately made eye contact.

"Well, look at this," the soldier boomed, pointing directly at the mage. "What do we have here? A little wizard boy with glowing eyes and no idea what he's doing."

Kaelen stiffened instantly, his hand instinctively reaching for the chisel tucked into his belt. Elias raised a finger to stop him before Kaelen could move.

"Leave him alone," Elias said firmly, stepping forward just enough to block the soldier's view of Kaelen without drawing attention to himself. "He's not your concern."

The soldier laughed, swaying as he took a step toward them. "Oh, I'll tell you about him. He's got that look in his eye like he's waiting for the world to end while everyone else is just trying to get by. What kind of magic are you casting, kid? Is it real, or did you learn it from some old book?"

Kaelen opened his mouth to respond, but Elias held up a hand again. "We're not here to argue about philosophy," he said quietly. "We're here to pass through safely. If we can't get that done without incident, then maybe this isn't the right place for us."

The soldier paused mid-step, surprised by the calmness of Elias's voice. For a moment, it seemed like he might press further, but then another patron stepped in between them—a woman with sharp features and an air of authority that commanded immediate attention.

"That's enough," she said sharply, her gaze locking onto the soldier. "If you don't want service here, leave before I have someone escort you out."

The soldier hesitated, glancing around nervously before muttering something unintelligible under his breath and stumbling back toward the door. The other patrons watched silently, some nodding approvingly while others exchanged amused glances. It was clear that the tavern had its own rules, its own hierarchy of behavior—and Elias knew exactly how to play it.

"Good," Vane said smoothly once the disturbance settled. "Now, about those terms."

Elias turned back to him, offering a small nod of thanks to the woman who had intervened before returning his focus to the merchant. "We're willing to negotiate," he said evenly. "But we need something reasonable. We've already taken risks for you; now we expect fair compensation."

Vane smirked faintly, leaning back against the bar. "Fair compensation is relative, Thorne. Depends on what kind of risk you're taking and how much trouble I'm willing to tolerate before calling in backup. Let's say five hundred gold pieces upfront, plus a cut of whatever we find along the way. Sound reasonable?"

Elias raised an eyebrow. "Five hundred? That's steep for someone who hasn't even earned our trust yet."

Vane shrugged casually. "It is if you think I'm paying for nothing. We've lost men before because adventurers thought they could outsmart fate. You'll get your share, but only if we survive together. And that means following my lead at all times."

Elias studied him carefully, weighing his options. They needed this contract badly; without escort income, their resources would dwindle faster than expected. But accepting unfair terms would set a dangerous precedent—one that could cost them dearly later. He glanced briefly at Maren and Kaelen, both watching silently from behind him. They were professionals too, capable of navigating difficult situations when given clear direction.

"Let me think about it," Elias said finally. "We'll need time to review your proposal properly."

Vane nodded dismissively. "Take all the time you want. But remember: opportunities don't wait forever. Once we're gone, someone else will fill our spot—and they won't be as forgiving as I am."

With that, he turned away, disappearing into the crowd of patrons who seemed equally indifferent to their conversation. Elias exhaled slowly, feeling the weight of responsibility settle heavily on his shoulders once more. There was work to do—not just securing a contract, but proving themselves trustworthy enough to earn respect in a world where trust was currency far more valuable than gold.

"Let's find somewhere quieter," he said quietly to Maren and Kaelen. "We need to discuss strategy away from the noise."
