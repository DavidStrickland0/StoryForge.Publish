Elias leaned forward slightly, resting his elbows on the scarred oak table where three tankards of mead sat cooling. The smoke in the alcove seemed thicker here, curling around Elias’s hair as he watched Silas Vane polish a silver goblet with a rag that had seen better decades. “So,” Elias began, his voice cutting through the tavern din without raising its volume. “It sounds like we’ve found common ground on most points.”

Vane stopped polishing and tucked the cloth into his belt, his movements precise despite the grime of travel. He set the goblet down with a soft *clink*. “Common ground is good,” he said, his voice rough but measured. “Though I’d not call our positions quite aligned yet. Five hundred gold upfront was the price for my protection. Three hundred gets me… well, it gets you a hearing.”

Elias nodded slowly. “We can work with that. And if we clear the rumors without incident, and deliver on time, the rest is yours.” He met Vane’s eyes squarely. “But let’s make one thing clear: we’re not mercenaries for hire. We’re partners in this venture. If you want interference handled quietly, we’ll do it. But if you expect us to fight a war because someone said a rumor about your crate, we’ll have words.”

Vane smirked, a thin curl of amusement escaping his lips. “Rumors? You think I care about what some drunkards in an inn say?” He leaned back against the tapestry, crossing his arms. “My reputation’s built on steel and strategy, not gossip. If my rivals start whispering nonsense to make you hesitate, I’ll silence them before they leave town.”

“Good,” Elias replied, keeping his tone neutral but firm. “Because hesitation isn’t an option. The caravan leaves at dawn. We need your word that the route we’re given is clear, and that you’ve taken steps to ensure our safety beyond just ‘I’ll send some thugs after them if they bother me.’”

Vane’s expression darkened slightly, but only for a moment. “You want guarantees?” he asked, his voice dropping. “Fine. I’ll have my scouts shadowing the caravan ahead of us. If anyone tries anything, they find themselves facing my men first. And if you’re worried about interference…” He gestured vaguely toward the main hall where a group of locals were arguing over dice near the hearth. “My network’s already working on those rumors. By morning, the inn will be full of people who’ve heard nothing but praise for Silas Vane’s latest deal.”

Elias raised an eyebrow. “That sounds… thorough.”

“It’s necessary,” Vane replied without hesitation. “I don’t take risks lightly. And I don’t leave loose ends.” He paused, then added, “But let’s be clear: if you go off-script and cause trouble that isn’t directly tied to the crate or the route, that’s on you. My protection doesn’t cover your choices.”

Elias nodded again, satisfied with the clarity. “Understood. We’ll stick to the plan unless safety is compromised. In that case, we act—but only in ways that don’t undermine your interests.”

It’s worth far more than anyone admits aloud. Whoever controls that supply chain has leverage.”

Elias felt a spark of curiosity ignite. “And you think we’re going to find some trouble along the way because of that?”

Vane nodded. “Rival companies are sniffing around already. Some might try to sabotage your delivery, others might try to steal the crate for themselves. That’s why I’m funding this—not just out of greed, but because I need that water in my hands before anyone else does.” He glanced at Elias with a mix of respect and wariness. “You’re going to need me as much as I’ll need you.”

Elara, who had been silently listening from the shadows of the alcove, stepped forward now, her voice calm but firm. “We don’t want trouble either,” she said, addressing Vane directly. “We just want to get this done safely and efficiently. If we stick to your plan, there’s no reason for us to face anything worse than what we’ve already encountered.”

Vane studied her closely before nodding again. “Good answer,” he muttered. “Keep that in mind if you hear something that sounds too much like trouble.” He turned back to Elias. “Meet me at the entrance tomorrow morning. We’ll finalize everything then, including payment terms and any additional details about the route.”

Elias stood up, gathering his cloak as Vane did the same. “Agreed,” he said, extending a hand toward the merchant. “Thank you for coming down to talk to us personally. Most merchants would’ve just sent an agent or demanded we sign a contract without reading it.”

Vane shook his hand firmly, his grip strong and steady. “Don’t thank me yet,” he replied with a wry smile. “You’ll owe me if you make this go sideways.” He walked toward the main hall, pausing at the threshold to look back. “And remember—this isn’t over until we deliver that crate.”

As Vane disappeared into the crowd of patrons and servers alike, Elias turned to Elara with a relieved expression. “He’s got guts,” he said, watching Vane disappear among the tables. “Not everyone’s willing to face rumors head-on instead of running away.”

“He has his reasons,” Elara replied quietly, her voice tinged with both admiration and caution. “And so do we.” She followed Elias back into the bustling tavern, her movements brisk but deliberate. “Now that we’ve got a verbal agreement, let’s head home. We can rest tonight and start preparing for tomorrow.”

Elias smiled faintly as they walked out of the alcove together. The noise of the tavern was still loud, but now it sounded different—less threatening, more like background hum. They had secured their next contract, gained access to information about the caravan’s route, and earned a measure of respect from someone who knew exactly how dangerous this job could be.

As they stepped into the open area where other adventurers were heading toward the kitchens or their own quarters, Elias glanced down at Kaelen, who was standing silently nearby, watching the flow of people with quiet intensity. “Come on,” he said gently, taking Kaelen’s arm and guiding him forward. “Let’s get some rest. Tomorrow’s going to be another big day.”

Kaelen nodded, letting Elias lead him away from the main hall. The tension had eased slightly, though it lingered in his posture. He didn’t say anything about Vane’s warnings or the potential dangers ahead; he simply followed Elias toward the stairs that led upstairs, where rooms were rented out to weary travelers like themselves.

Behind them, the tavern continued its lively rhythm, patrons laughing over dice games and tankards of ale, oblivious to the serious negotiations that had just taken place in the shadowed corner. But Elias didn’t mind. He wasn’t looking for applause or fanfare; he was content with the knowledge that they’d faced down a merchant who knew exactly what kind of trouble lay ahead—and still chosen to partner with them.

That was progress enough for now.
