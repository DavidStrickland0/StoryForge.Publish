Elias kept his eyes on Vane as the merchant paced the small circle of their booth, his boots clicking rhythmically against the flagstones. The noise of the tavern had settled into a low hum again, a comfortable backdrop to the business being conducted at the table. It wasn’t just the contract that needed signing; it was the agreement itself—the terms, the conditions, and the understanding that both sides were entering with eyes open.

“Silas,” Elias began, his voice cutting through the ambient din without rising in volume. “The terms we discussed earlier are sound. But there’s a detail I’d like to clarify before we finalize everything.”

Vane stopped pacing and leaned against the booth again, crossing one arm over his chest while holding a flagon of ale in the other hand. He didn’t drink; instead, he gestured with the bottle toward Elias as if it were already part of the argument. “You’ve got spirit, Thorne,” Silas said, his voice carrying easily over the din without rising to a shout. “I like that. Most folks would have bolted at rumors like rats at the smell of smoke.”

“Rumors are free,” Elias replied evenly. “They don’t carry much weight unless someone believes them.” He turned his gaze back to Vane. “But if you’re offering supplies, maps, potions—those things have value. Let’s talk business.”

Silas chuckled, a low rumble that seemed to vibrate through the wood of the table. “Business is what we do best,” he said smoothly. “Though I must warn you: these aren’t ordinary items. You’ll find my stock carefully curated for those who know how to use it properly.” He reached into his coat and produced a rolled parchment tied with leather cord. “Here’s a sample of my current offerings. Careful reading is required before signing any contracts, eh?”

Elias took the scroll from Vane’s hand, glancing at the map inside while keeping an eye on the man himself. The paper smelled faintly of ink and old parchment, but there was something else beneath that scent—a metallic tang of oil and iron that made him pause momentarily before unfolding it fully. When he did, he found the usual array of goods: potions vial by vial in neat rows, weapons polished to a dull shine, bundles of dried herbs sealed in waxed cloth. But tucked among them were several rolled maps marked with red lines tracing possible caravan routes through the valley.

“Interesting,” Elias murmured, folding the scroll carefully before setting it aside on the table. “You offer both supplies and intelligence?”

Silas nodded approvingly. “That’s my specialty. Information is currency in this trade. The world moves fast, and those who stay ahead of the curve are the ones who survive.” His smile widened slightly, revealing teeth that looked far too white compared to most people he’d met on the road. “I’d say we’re already off to a good start, wouldn’t you agree?”

Elias didn’t answer immediately. Instead, he studied Vane more closely, noting the way his eyes darted toward the doorway where two figures stood exchanging whispers with each other. Those were members of the rival adventurer group mentioned earlier, spreading rumors about their incompetence despite having never met them personally. Their voices carried faintly across the room, loud enough to confirm what everyone else already suspected: trouble was coming from outside sources as well as within.

Maren cleared her throat softly behind him, drawing Elias’s attention back to the immediate situation. “We should hear these rumors out,” she said quietly, though her tone wasn’t accusatory or defensive—it was pragmatic. “If we ignore them now, they’ll keep spreading until someone takes action.”

Elias nodded slowly. “You’re right,” he admitted. “Let’s hear what they say.” He stood up then, pulling his chair back with a scrape of wood against stone that cut sharply through the ambient noise. “Come along,” he said to Silas, gesturing toward the entrance where the two rivals stood arguing amongst themselves near the door.

Vane followed obediently, though his expression remained unreadable as they approached the group. The first speaker was a tall woman with braided hair and goggles pushed up against her forehead, wearing armor that looked like it had been scavenged from various battles past. Her companion was shorter, clad in heavy robes embroidered with symbols that Elias couldn’t quite make out at a glance. Both held themselves with confidence bordering on arrogance, their voices rising above the tavern’s chatter as they turned to face the party.

“Thorne!” the woman called out, her voice bright enough to carry halfway down the block. “We heard you’ve been causing nothing but trouble lately! How does one group of incompetent adventurers manage to survive long enough to spread rumors like wildfire?” She laughed heartily at her own joke, earning a few nervous chuckles from nearby tables while others rolled their eyes or muttered under their breaths.

Elias raised an eyebrow, waiting for the punchline that hadn’t come yet. Instead, he stepped forward, placing himself squarely between the rivals and Silas. “Careful with your words,” he said calmly. “Unless you have proof of our incompetence, those are just opinions wrapped in insults.”

The woman crossed her arms defiantly, stepping closer to the booth where Elias stood. “Oh, we’ve got plenty of proof!” she declared, raising her voice so everyone could hear. “Last time I saw your team around here, they were trying to negotiate with a merchant who wouldn’t even let them inside his shop! That’s incompetence right there!”

Silas interjected smoothly, leaning against the table again as if he hadn’t moved at all during the exchange. “Perhaps she means something else entirely,” he suggested gently. “Merchants often refuse entry to untested adventurers until they prove themselves capable. It’s standard procedure.”

The rival woman blinked, caught off guard by Silas’s intervention. Her companion nodded nervously behind her, lowering their voice but continuing to argue amongst themselves regardless. “Still!” the woman insisted stubbornly. “And then there was that incident with the drunk patron! You managed to calm him down without getting hurt—but only because you didn’t actually try to fight him in the first place. That’s not bravery; it’s cowardice!”

Maren stepped forward then, her expression cool and composed despite the provocation. She crossed her arms over her chest, mirroring the posture of the rival woman but projecting authority rather than defensiveness. “Bravery isn’t about fighting when you can avoid it,” she said clearly, enunciating every word so that even those sitting at distant tables could understand. “It’s about knowing when to hold your ground and when to walk away. My friend here demonstrated exactly that skill.”

Silas smiled warmly, nodding his approval at Maren’s retort before turning his attention back to the rivals. “You seem to miss the point entirely,” he noted mildly. “Adventurers come in all shapes and sizes, each with their own strengths and weaknesses. Some rely on swords and shields; others prefer wits over weapons. Neither approach is inherently superior unless circumstances dictate otherwise.”

The rival woman opened her mouth to respond, only for Silas to cut her off before she could speak again. “Let me finish,” he continued smoothly. “If your team truly believes our group lacks skill, perhaps you’d care to demonstrate why? Show us where we went wrong?” He gestured broadly toward the tavern interior, inviting them to engage in whatever manner they chose.

The woman hesitated for a moment, clearly torn between pride and caution. Finally, she shrugged dismissively and stepped back into the crowd without answering directly. “Fine,” she muttered bitterly. “We’ll let you have your way for now. But mark our words: once we finish gathering information from those fools at the Watchfort, we’ll be back to settle this properly.”

With that final threat unspoken aloud, the pair turned and walked away toward the opposite end of the tavern, disappearing into the throng of patrons without further comment. Silas watched them go with a satisfied smirk before returning his attention to Elias. “See?” he said cheerfully. “No harm done at all. We’ve handled ourselves well enough for anyone’s liking.”

Elias nodded slowly, watching as Maren relaxed slightly after the confrontation ended. Her shoulders lowered visibly, releasing tension she’d carried throughout the encounter without realizing it herself. He glanced around briefly, taking note of how much quieter things had become in the area where the rivals had stood moments earlier. Patrons seemed genuinely interested in Silas’s story now, listening intently as he regaled them with tales of past adventures gone wrong and right alike.

“Agreed,” Elias said finally, clapping a hand lightly on Maren’s shoulder. “Well handled overall.” He turned back to Silas then, offering him a genuine smile despite lingering fatigue from earlier events. “Thank you for helping us navigate this mess today.”

Silas bowed slightly, accepting thanks graciously before gesturing toward his wagon parked outside the tavern entrance. “Anytime,” he replied warmly. “Consider this just the beginning of our partnership, Thorne.
