The heavy iron gates of the city groaned shut behind them, sealing out the chaotic hum of the market and the lingering scent of roasted meat and stale ale. They were alone on the road now, save for the lowing of the draft animals pulling Silas Vane’s wagon and the two smaller carts that formed the escort.

Silas Vane walked alongside Elias, his hands clasped tightly behind his back as if trying to physically hold them together. He kept glancing over his shoulder, eyes darting between the wagon wheels and the road ahead. “The timing is critical,” he muttered, more to himself than to the party. “If we fall an hour behind schedule, the siphon effect will be at its peak. If we arrive early, the seal might not hold. We need to be perfect.”

Elias walked slightly ahead, keeping pace with Torin who was inspecting the iron-bound rope securing the wagon’s harnesses. He watched Silas out of the corner of his eye, noting the merchant’s nervous energy. “Don’t worry about it,” Elias said calmly. “Elara’s calculations are precise. We’re moving at a steady clip.”

Silas nodded quickly but didn’t relax. “Precise is good. Precise is necessary. But… what if something goes wrong? What if the creatures appear sooner than expected?”

“They won’t,” Maren said from the other cart, her voice cutting through Silas’s anxiety with the same dry efficiency she’d used at the market stall. She sat cross-legged atop a crate of sulfur paste, leaning forward slightly as she adjusted the bridle on one of the horses. “The Blight-Touched are drawn to instability. We’re creating stability ahead of us. By the time they catch up, we’ll already be in position.”

Torin straightened up from inspecting the harnesses and joined Elias’s side. He nodded toward the road. “Maren’s right. The rope is holding. The tension points are reinforced. If anything tries to latch on, it’ll take a direct hit first. We’ve got seconds, not minutes.”

Elias glanced back at Silas. “We’re ready for whatever comes next,” he said. “But don’t forget—this isn’t just about surviving the creatures. It’s about getting the crate intact to the Watchfort. That’s the real challenge.”

Silas nodded again, though his eyes remained fixed on the road. “Right. Right. Of course.”

The road stretched out before them, a ribbon of packed earth flanked by fields of golden wheat that swayed gently in the breeze. The sun was high, casting long shadows across the landscape. The air felt lighter now that they’d left the city behind; there was no longer the weight of the tavern’s noise or the pressure of the rival adventurers’ whispers. But beneath that lightness lay a tension Silas couldn’t shake.

As they approached the first ridge beyond the valley, the temperature dropped noticeably. The wind picked up, carrying with it a faint, unnatural chill that made Elias shiver despite the warmth of the sun. Torin raised an eyebrow, glancing at Maren. “You feel that?” he asked quietly.

Maren nodded once, her expression unreadable. “The seal’s holding,” she said. “But something’s shifting.”

Elias stopped walking and looked around. The wheat fields seemed to ripple in a way that didn’t match the wind’s direction. Shadows stretched unnaturally long across the ground, even though the sun was still high. And then he saw it—a faint shimmer in the air, like heat haze but colder, darker.

“Elara?” Elias called out over the wind. “Did you calculate for this?”

Elara emerged from Silas’s wagon, her journal tucked under one arm. She frowned slightly as she scanned the horizon. “I did account for lunar cycles and tidal patterns,” she said. “But…” She paused, tapping a finger against her temple. “The resonance frequency is higher than predicted.”

Silas stepped forward eagerly. “What does that mean?”

“It means the seal’s instability is being amplified by external factors,” Elara explained, her voice steady despite the urgency in her tone. “Something else is feeding into the siphon effect. Something we didn’t anticipate.”

Maren dismounted and walked to Elias’s side. “The creatures are coming faster than expected,” she said, her tone calm but urgent. “We need to get the party into formation now.”

Elias nodded and turned to Silas. “Get everyone ready. We’re deploying the dead zone protocol.”

Silas swallowed hard and nodded again. “Yes. Yes, of course.”

The rest of the party moved quickly, taking their positions around the caravan. Torin checked the iron-bound rope one last time, ensuring it was taut and secure. Maren positioned herself near the wagon’s wheels, ready to reinforce the tension points if needed. Kaelen stood guard at the rear, his hand resting lightly on the hilt of his sword.

Elara began chanting a low, rhythmic incantation as she raised her hands toward the shimmering air above them. The resonance frequency shifted slightly, and the shimmering heat haze cleared momentarily, revealing a patch of calm sky where none should have existed.

The Blight-Touched appeared at the edge of the field—a cluster of shadowy figures that moved with unnatural grace, their forms flickering like smoke in a draft. They paused for a moment, drawn toward the caravan by the scent of the Pure Water, then stopped abruptly as Elara’s spell took effect.

The dead zone held. The creatures recoiled, their forms destabilizing as they approached the boundary of stability. Elias watched closely, noting how quickly they were repelled. “Perfect timing,” he said to no one in particular. “Elara nailed it.”

Maren nodded approvingly. “She’s good. Better than expected.”

Silas let out a breath he hadn’t realized he’d been holding. “We’re… we’re safe,” he whispered, though his voice trembled slightly.

Elias looked at him. “Not yet. We’re safe until the creatures decide to change their minds.”

He turned back to Elara. “Keep the zone stable. Torin, make sure the rope doesn’t snap under tension. Maren, keep watch on the rear. Kaelen, stay ready.”

They moved as one unit, each person aware of their role and trusting the others to fulfill theirs. The tension in Silas’s posture lessened slightly, though he didn’t fully relax until the last creature had been driven back into the shadows.

By the time they reached the next ridge, the sun was beginning its descent toward the horizon. The road ahead wound through a narrow pass where the trees closed in tightly, blocking out most of the sky. The air grew cooler still, and the smell of sulfur from Maren’s paste mingled with the earthy scent of pine needles.

As they pushed forward, Elias couldn’t help but feel a sense of relief mixed with unease. They’d faced the first surge successfully, but this was only the beginning. The Blight-Touched were persistent, and the siphon effect would likely intensify as they got closer to the Watchfort. But for now, they had the plan, the supplies, and each other.

And that was enough.
