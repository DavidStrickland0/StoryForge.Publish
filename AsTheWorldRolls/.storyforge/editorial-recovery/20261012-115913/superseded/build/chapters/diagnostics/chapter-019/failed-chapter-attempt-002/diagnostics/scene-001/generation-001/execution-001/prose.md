Torin pushed open the heavy oak doors of the Gilded Tankard with a grunt that seemed to carry for several feet through the thick wood, the sound swallowed instantly by the roar of the tavern before he stepped out into the morning air. The heat hit him like a physical blow, smelling of dust, manure, and the metallic tang of river water rising from the cobbles.

Silas Vane’s wagon sat parked near the edge of the market district, its canvas cover drawn tight against the glare. It was an unassuming vehicle compared to the grand caravans Elias had seen in capital cities, but it spoke of efficiency. A single sturdy ox, mottled with patches of white and grey, stood tied to the front, chewing slowly on a wad of hay. The wagon itself was a fortress of iron reinforcements and reinforced timber, designed not for comfort, but for carrying heavy loads across rough terrain.

“Right then,” Silas said, his voice cutting through the din of the market as he guided Elias toward the rear of the wagon. He held open the flap with a leather strap that looked thick enough to stop a blowpipe bolt. “Load in your gear. I’ll have the crate ready for you once we’ve secured the perimeter.”

Torin moved quickly, checking the harness lines and ensuring the ox’s weight was distributed correctly before Silas could finish speaking. He grabbed Elias’s satchel and the heavy bundle of supplies—iron-bound rope, sulfur paste, purified flint—from where they rested against the wagon wheel. “I’ll help with the crate,” Torin said without waiting for permission. “Maren will check the perimeter.”

Elias nodded, stepping up onto the wagon platform with a careful movement to avoid jarring his side. The interior was surprisingly spacious despite its compact exterior. A small sleeping pallet had been cleared in the corner, and a sturdy lantern hung from a hook near the ceiling, casting a warm glow that illuminated stacks of dried rations and water skins. Silas had clearly anticipated this journey would be uncomfortable; he’d even tucked a coil of rope along one side for securing cargo.

Torin began loading the crate onto the wagon bed with practiced efficiency. It was a sealed wooden box, no larger than a coffin, but heavy enough to make his shoulders ache as he lifted it. The crate’s surface was smooth and unmarked, save for a single inscription carved into one corner: *Temple Springs – Pure Water*. Beneath the words, a simple symbol—a circle bisected by a vertical line—glowed faintly with an inner light that seemed to pulse in time with the ox’s breathing.

“Careful,” Silas warned, watching Torin’s movements closely. “That crate is not just water. It’s… something else entirely.”

Torin paused mid-lift, glancing at Silas over his shoulder. “We know that,” he replied calmly, setting the crate down on the wagon bed with a thud that vibrated through the wood. “It’s why we’ve got extra rope and antiseptic ready.”

Silas nodded slowly, his expression unreadable. He turned to Elias, who was now standing beside him, watching as Torin secured the crate with the iron-bound cable Maren had insisted on. The merchant leaned against the wagon’s side, crossing his arms over his chest. “You’ve got a good team,” he said quietly, his voice dropping the performative cheerfulness of earlier. “Not many adventurers know how to work together like this.”

Elias crossed his arms, looking at Torin as he tightened the final knot on the crate’s binding. “We learned quickly,” Elias said. “But we’re not done yet.”

Silas smiled faintly, shaking his head. “That’s the way of it,” he replied. “No one stays comfortable for long when there’s work to be done.”

Torin finished securing the crate and stepped back, wiping sweat from his brow with the back of his hand. “Ready whenever you are,” he said, though his tone suggested he’d prefer staying behind while Silas handled any potential threats.

Maren emerged from the tavern just then, her presence marking a shift in the atmosphere. She walked with purpose, her eyes scanning the surrounding area for signs of trouble before she approached the wagon. “Perimeter clear,” she announced, her voice carrying easily over the distant noise of the market. “No Blight-Touched within sight range. But I’ve marked three likely patrol routes we’ll need to monitor.”

She handed Elias a rolled map that had been pinned to her belt earlier in the day. It was detailed, showing the caravan’s route with annotations for potential ambush points, natural barriers, and safe resting spots. “I’ve plotted our progress based on Silas’s timing,” she said, pointing to specific markers along the path. “We’ll leave early tomorrow morning, before dawn. That gives us a head start and puts us ahead of most other caravans heading north.”

Elias took the map, studying it carefully. “Good,” he said, nodding at her work. “Let’s get this moving then.”

Silas nodded again, reaching into his coat to retrieve a small pouch of coins. He tossed them to Torin, who caught them with a practiced flick of his wrist. “For your trouble last night,” Silas said cheerfully. “And for keeping the tavern alive after that little incident with the drunk patron.”

Torin grinned, pocketing the coins without hesitation. “Appreciate it,” he said, though his expression remained serious as he looked at Elias and Maren. “We’ll make sure everyone gets paid fairly when we reach the Watchfort.”

Elias watched as Silas climbed into the driver’s seat of the wagon, adjusting the reins with practiced ease. The ox gave a low grunt, shifting its weight slightly before settling down again. Torin stepped back to let Silas settle in, then turned to Elias and Maren, offering them a hand up onto the wagon platform.

“Come along,” he said, his voice warm despite the fatigue etched into his features. “We’ve got a long day ahead of us.”

Maren followed him up after a moment’s hesitation, her gaze lingering on the crate one last time before she settled beside Elias and Torin. The wagon creaked softly as Silas began to move it forward, the wheels rolling smoothly over the cobblestones with surprising ease. As they pulled away from the tavern, the market district fell behind them, replaced by the quiet hum of the road ahead.

Elias leaned back against the wagon’s side, watching the world pass by through the gap between the canvas cover and the wagon frame. The journey had begun—not with fanfare or ceremony, but with careful preparation and a shared understanding that they were all in this together. The Blight-Touched still hunted them, but so did Silas Vane’s knowledge of the road, Torin’s logistical acumen, Maren’s tactical awareness, and Elara’s precise calculations.

As the wagon picked up speed, Elias felt a strange sense of calm settle over him. They were ready.
