Elias stood at the threshold of the alcove, his hand resting lightly on the heavy tapestry that separated them from the tavern’s main hall. The noise was a different texture now—the clatter of tankards and raucous laughter didn't feel like an assault anymore, but like the ambient hum of a functioning ecosystem. He glanced back at Maren and Kaelen, who had followed him to the door. They were still seated, watching him with that mixture of pride and nervous expectation that only comes when you've just survived a negotiation.

Silas Vane adjusted his belt pouch, the movement causing the silver coins inside to clink softly against one another. The merchant's eyes were narrowed, assessing the space Elias had reclaimed, but there was no hostility in them anymore. Just business, sharp and clean.

"You're right about the crate," Silas said, his voice carrying easily over the din without shouting. "And you're right about the rivals." He gestured toward the main hall with a sweep of his hand that felt less like an invitation and more like a dismissal of anyone foolish enough to get in the way. "They'll try to undercut me tomorrow morning. They think I'm desperate because my caravan is stuck on the high pass."

Elias nodded, keeping his tone neutral. "We understand. Our priority is securing a safe passage through the pass, regardless of who carries the cargo or how much they charge for it."

"And our priority," Silas countered smoothly, stepping closer to the tapestry, "is that my cargo arrives intact. If your rivals steal my 'pure' water before we even reach the pass, I lose everything. So let's make this simple: you take the job as a caravan escort. We split the haul profits three ways—me, you, and the guild contacts I'll be calling in once we're clear of the pass."

"Split profits," Elias repeated, testing the weight of the word. It wasn't an ideal arrangement for them, but it was acceptable. They weren't here to run a merchant route; they were adventurers hired for protection. The haul split was a bonus, not the core of their contract. But Silas Vane knew that if his rivals tried to steal the crate, Elias would intervene, and if Elias intervened, he'd want a share of the spoils. It was a fair equation.

"How much upfront?" Elias asked, keeping his eyes on the merchant's face rather than scanning the room for exits or threats. "We need to cover immediate expenses before we reach the pass."

Silas smiled, a thin expression that didn't quite reach his eyes. "Three hundred gold. We'll talk about the rest when you get back from the pass. If you do well—if you keep my cargo safe—I'll pay you more than three hundred. If you screw up..." He let the threat hang in the air, unspoken but clear.

"Three hundred gold," Elias confirmed. "We accept."

"And one condition," Silas added quickly, before Elias could object. "You don't try to negotiate with my guild contacts or local investors while we're on the road. That's handled by me. You just... escort the cargo and protect us from bandits and rivals. Deal?"

"Deal," Elias said. He turned back toward Maren and Kaelen, who were still seated at the table. They had been listening intently to every word, their expressions shifting from relief to renewed focus as the terms solidified.

Maren stood up first, brushing dust from her tunic. She gave Silas a curt nod. "Three hundred gold upfront," she said. "We'll be ready to leave at dawn."

"And you'll handle any interference before we even get out of this tavern," Silas reminded her, pointing toward the door with a flourish that suggested he'd already imagined the scene where rivals would try to interrupt them.

"We'll handle it," Elias said calmly. "But we're not taking on more than we can carry. If someone threatens to steal your cargo and you insist on handling it yourself, we'll wait here."

Silas laughed, a dry sound that cut through the tavern noise. "Don't worry about my rivals. I've got friends in high places who know how to handle troublemakers. You just focus on what you do best: protecting me and my cargo."

"Understood," Elias said. He turned back toward Maren and Kaelen, then gestured with his head toward the tavern door. "Come on. Let's get some drinks before we go home."

Maren smiled, though it was still guarded. "I could use a drink," she admitted, her voice low enough that only Elias heard it. "After that... I'm pretty sure Silas knows more about my family than he should."

Elias chuckled softly. "He does. But so do we. And now he knows we're not stupid enough to let him play us against ourselves."

Kaelen stood up slowly, his movements still cautious but steady. He looked at Silas Vane, who was already walking toward the bar area, calling out orders to his waiters. The merchant paused, turning to face them one last time.

"See you at dawn," Silas said, his tone light but professional. "And remember—we're splitting profits three ways. That's not a bad deal for anyone."

"We'll see you then," Elias replied. He turned back toward Maren and Kaelen, who were already moving toward the tavern door. The noise of the main hall seemed less overwhelming now that they had secured their terms. It was just background music to their next adventure.

As they stepped back into the bustling tavern, the air felt different—lighter, even. They weren't leaving as strangers anymore; they were part of a transaction, a partnership built on mutual benefit and clear boundaries. Silas Vane knew who they were now, and they knew what he wanted from them. That knowledge alone was enough to make the road ahead feel less uncertain.

Maren took Elias's arm, guiding him toward the bar where they could get a drink before heading home. "Three hundred gold," she said quietly, testing the weight of it in her hand. "That'll cover our lodging and supplies for the pass."

"And leave us with enough to save up for next time," Elias added. He glanced at Kaelen, who was already walking toward the bar, looking less tense than before. The young mage seemed to be settling into his role within the party again, his magic now a tool rather than a burden.

"Good work today," Elias said softly, keeping his voice low enough that only Maren could hear it. "You handled Silas well."

Maren shrugged, though her smile was genuine this time. "I just told him what he needed to know. He took it."

"He also realized we're not pushovers," Kaelen added from behind them. His voice was steady now, no longer trembling with anxiety. "And that's half the battle."

Elias nodded, feeling a sense of accomplishment wash over him. They had secured a contract, gained access to valuable information about the caravan route and the sealed crate, and established their reputation as difficult but competent partners. That was progress enough for today.
