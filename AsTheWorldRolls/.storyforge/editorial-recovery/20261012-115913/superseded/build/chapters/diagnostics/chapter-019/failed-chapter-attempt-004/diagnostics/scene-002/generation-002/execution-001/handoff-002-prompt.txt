The words left Elias’s mouth with a weight that seemed to displace the silence around them.

There was no pause for hesitation. The party did not exchange glances of reassurance or double-check their gear. They simply moved, a fluid expansion of intent into action. His hands moved with a brutal efficiency, tightening the tension until the metal hummed, a low, vibrating note that cut through the stillness of the forest. He did not look up. He did not need to. He knew exactly where Maren would be standing, and he knew exactly where the creatures would emerge.

Maren melted into the shadow of the nearest oak, her body aligning with the dark until she was less a person and more a part of the forest’s gloom. She raised her crossbow, the bolt already loaded, and adjusted the angle of her wrist. Her eyes were fixed on the tree line ahead, where the light was beginning to warp, the air shimmering like heat haze over a summer road. She did not breathe. She waited.

Elara stepped into the center of the clearing, her journal clutched to her chest. She did not look at the warping air. She looked at the ground, specifically at the patch of earth Elias had identified moments before. She began to chant, not in words, but in a rhythmic, percussive cadence that matched the beating of her own heart. Her stylus moved across the parchment, scratching lines that seemed to glow faintly in the dim light. The air around her grew heavy, charged with a static electricity that raised the hairs on Elias’s arms.

Elias watched them. He watched the knot tighten, the shadow hold, the line scratch. He felt a strange, cold clarity settle over his mind. This was not a fight. It was a mechanism. And mechanisms did not care about fear, or reputation, or the rumors spreading in the taverns of the city. They only cared about the input, the process, and the output.
