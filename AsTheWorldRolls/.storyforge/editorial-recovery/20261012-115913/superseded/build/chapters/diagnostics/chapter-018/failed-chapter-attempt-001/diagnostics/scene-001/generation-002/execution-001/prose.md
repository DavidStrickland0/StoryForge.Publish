The noise of the Gilded Tankard was a living thing, a cacophony of clinking tankards, raucous laughter, and the low murmur of scores of conversations that seemed to rise in volume whenever anyone tried to speak privately. Torin had already slipped away toward the door, eager for fresh air or perhaps just distance from the lingering tension of the rival adventurers’ departure. Inside the corner booth, the atmosphere was quieter, insulated by the heavy wood and the sheer density of the crowd pressing against its walls.

Elias leaned back in his chair, rubbing a thumbnail against the side of his mouth as he surveyed the sea of faces around them. “We need to know what’s on that caravan,” he said, his voice low enough not to carry but loud enough for those at their table to hear. “The rumors say it’s high-value goods from the Temple Springs.”

Elara didn’t look up from her journal, though she paused with her stylus hovering over a page of notes. Her dark eyes remained fixed on the ink-stained parchment as she flipped through a section titled *Regional Trade Routes & Risk Assessments*. “The Temple Springs,” she murmured, more to herself than to the group. “That’s where the Pure Water comes from.”

Kaelen frowned slightly, shifting his gaze between Elara and Elias. “Pure Water?” he asked, his tone questioning. “I’ve heard of the springs in the eastern valleys, but I don’t think anyone calls them ‘Pure’ unless they mean something special.”

Elara finally looked up, closing her journal with a snap that drew an amused glance from Maren across the table. “It’s not just clean water,” she explained, gesturing to a diagram sketched in the margins of her book. “The Temple Springs produce a water infused with stabilizing magical properties. It’s used to heal wounds, cleanse ailments, and even reinforce certain wards. The Blight-Touched entities are drawn to it like moths to flame.” She paused, tracing a finger along the illustration of a caravan route overlaid with symbols for danger zones. “If that crate is missing or compromised, not only will we lose its value—but whatever creature is hunting us next might be even more dangerous than before.”

Maren nodded slowly, her hand resting lightly on Kaelen’s shoulder in a gesture that spoke volumes without needing words. She hadn’t said anything since the rival adventurers left, but her presence was a quiet reassurance. If anyone had tried to corner Elias or Elara with questions about their tactics or motives, Maren would have intercepted them with a glare sharp enough to cut stone. Now, she simply listened, occasionally interjecting with observations about the tavern patrons or pointing out familiar faces who’d been seen earlier in the day.

The tavern’s information network was far from official, but it was thorough. Rumors traveled faster than messages in this town, and for a coin or two, someone would sell you half-truths wrapped in speculation. Elias had already heard snippets here and there: whispers of a caravan carrying “miracle water,” sightings of shadowy figures loitering near the city gates, and even a few claims that Blight-Touched creatures had been spotted prowling the outskirts. None of it added up to anything useful until now.

“Who’s selling this stuff?” Elias asked, leaning forward slightly to catch the attention of the barmaid who happened to wander by with a tray of empty mugs. “Anyone? We could ask around.”

The barmaid glanced at him over her shoulder, her expression neutral but uninterested. “You’ll find it faster if you know where the rumors start,” she said flatly before turning back to refill another customer’s glass.

Elias sighed, standing up and walking toward a group of merchants clustered near the hearth. They were discussing something intently, their voices low but animated enough that Elias could pick out key phrases: *caravan*, *Temple Springs*, *sealed crate*. He tapped one of them on the shoulder. “Mind if we join you?” he asked politely.

The man looked up, his eyes narrowing as he recognized Elias’s face from earlier interactions. He gestured for him to sit down while the other merchants glanced curiously at him. “You’re that adventurer,” one of them said, smirking slightly. “Heard you caused quite a stir this morning with your little stunt against those rivals.”

Elias kept his expression neutral, nodding once. “We were just trying to keep things peaceful,” he replied calmly. “Is there something you can tell us about the caravan?”

The man leaned in conspiratorially, lowering his voice as if speaking in a crowded room made him paranoid by default. “That’s what everyone’s talking about,” he whispered. “Sealed crate from the Temple Springs. Supposedly holds ‘pure’ water worth a king’s ransom. But word is, there’s a problem.”

Elias waited for him to continue. The merchant hesitated briefly before deciding to elaborate further. “There was a creature seen near the gates,” he said quickly. “Some say it’s Blight-Touched. Others think it’s just a trick of the light or a stray beast gone rogue. Doesn’t matter how you look at it—the caravan is heading straight for trouble.”

Elias nodded slowly, jotting down notes in his own book. “What about the water itself? Is there anything special about it?”

The man shrugged. “It’s supposed to be sacred,” he said with a grin that didn’t reach his eyes. “Healers use it for miracles. Fighters use it to recover faster after battles. Even nobles drink it to stay young forever.” He paused, then added with a wink, “Though I wouldn’t recommend drinking it if you’re planning on surviving long enough to see the effects take hold.”

Elara chuckled softly from across the room where she remained seated with Kaelen. “Sounds like they’ve got more than just water in mind,” she muttered under her breath, though loud enough for Elias to overhear.

Maren’s gaze drifted toward the group near the hearth, her expression unreadable but clearly protective of Elias and Elara. If anyone had tried to corner them with unwanted questions or demands, she would have handled it herself. Instead, she simply listened, occasionally nodding at points that aligned with her own opinions about the dangers ahead.

Kaelen watched Elara’s journal intently as she flipped through pages filled with diagrams, calculations, and annotations in neat handwriting. He leaned closer, studying the sketches of magical circuits and resonance frequencies. “Do you think we can replicate that kind of effect?” he asked quietly, his voice barely audible over the din of the tavern.

Elara glanced up at him, her dark eyes reflecting the flickering candlelight. “Maybe,” she said thoughtfully. “But it’s not just about mimicking magic—it’s about understanding the principles behind it. Once you grasp those foundations, you can adapt them to whatever situation arises.”

Elias returned to their table after speaking with the merchants, his mind already racing through possibilities. “We’ve got enough intel now,” he said as he took a seat again. “The caravan is carrying something valuable, and someone’s hunting it down. We need to make sure we’re ready when they arrive.”

Maren nodded, her hand still resting lightly on Kaelen’s shoulder. “And if things get messy?” she asked, her tone measured but serious.

Elias smiled faintly, glancing at her. “Then we’ll handle it together,” he said simply. “That’s what parties are for.”

The conversation lulled briefly as they finished their meals, leaving only the ambient noise of the tavern to fill the space around them. Then, just as Elias was reaching for his ale again, the door swung open once more and another figure entered—the same rival adventurer group they’d seen earlier outside the gates. This time, however, they weren’t alone; they were accompanied by a merchant named Silas Vane, who wore robes embroidered with silver thread and carried a staff topped with a glowing crystal.

Silas Vane approached their table with a confident stride, his smile sharp but not unkind. “Elias Thorne,” he greeted them warmly. “I trust you’ve had a good day?”

Elias nodded politely. “It’s been fine,” he replied. “Though we’re still waiting to hear from the Watchfort.”

Vane chuckled softly. “That’s hardly my concern,” he said smoothly. “But I do know that your reputation precedes you, even here in the Gilded Tankard.”

Maren raised an eyebrow suspiciously. “And what exactly does that mean?”

“It means you’re well-known throughout the city,” Vane explained cheerfully. “Which is why I thought it would be wise to introduce myself personally before anyone else gets the chance.” He gestured toward Silas, who stepped forward with a bow so deep it nearly knocked him onto his face.

“I am Silas Vane,” the merchant announced grandly. “Trader of rare goods and expert negotiator of fair deals. Perhaps we might discuss terms?”

Elias frowned slightly but kept his tone neutral. “Terms for what?” he asked cautiously.

Vane grinned again, this time with a hint of mischief in his eyes. “For everything,” he said expansively. “From supplies to services to information—and maybe even a little extra coin thrown into the pot.”
