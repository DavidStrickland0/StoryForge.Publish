Silas Vane’s wagon sat parked near the edge of the market district, its canvas cover drawn tight against the glare. It was an unassuming vehicle compared to the grand caravans Elias had seen in capital cities, but it spoke of efficiency. A single sturdy ox, mottled with patches of white and grey, stood tied to the front, chewing slowly on a wad of hay. The wagon itself was a fortress of iron reinforcements and reinforced timber, designed not for comfort, but for carrying heavy loads across rough terrain.

“Right then,” Silas said, his voice cutting through the din of the market as he guided Elias toward the rear of the wagon. “Careful,” Silas warned, watching Torin’s movements closely. “That crate is not just water. It’s… something else entirely.”

Elias crossed his arms, looking at Torin as he tightened the final knot on the crate’s binding. “We learned quickly,” Elias said. “But we’re not done yet.”

Silas smiled faintly, shaking his head. “That’s the way of it,” he replied. “No one stays comfortable for long when there’s work to be done.”
