The silence that followed the operation was not empty; it was heavy, solidified by the absence of hums, roars, and the metallic tang of ozone. The valley floor, bathed now in the pale, cool light of the setting sun, felt unnervingly ordinary. A bird called out from the treeline, its song sharp and clear against the sudden quiet.

Elias exhaled slowly, his shoulders dropping as if a physical weight had been lifted. He watched Kaelen, who was leaning heavily against the rough bark of a dead oak for support. The young mage's brass gauntlet sat at his side, completely inert. It no longer pulled on the air, nor did it hum with a low-frequency vibration that made Elias's teeth ache. It was just cold metal and leather, a piece of equipment as mundane as a shield or a sword.

"You're quiet," Kaelen murmured, not looking at him. His voice lacked its usual confidence, carrying the rasp of someone who had run on adrenaline until the tank ran dry. "Is it... is it really over?"

Elias walked over and placed a hand on Kaelen's shoulder, feeling the stiffness in the muscles beneath. "It is over here," he said softly. "The Blight is gone from this spot."

Kaelen nodded slowly, pushing himself upright with an effort that made his face grimace. "Feels like I walked through it instead of holding it back." He rubbed his temples, a gesture of discomfort. "For hours there... and then nothing. Like flipping a switch off."

Maren stepped up beside them, her hammer resting on her shoulder. She had already checked the perimeter; she was confident that no Blight-Touched remained in the immediate vicinity. The threat of sudden attacks had dissolved as quickly as the fog. "The barrier is knitting shut," she said, her tone matter-of-fact. "It's just a scar now. Like a fresh wound on stone."

Elara knelt by the fissure one last time, using a magnifying lens to inspect the collapsed conduit. The green glow was gone from the drainage ditches; only the dark, still water remained, reflecting the twilight sky. She gathered her satchel, packing away the remaining quartz shards with care. "The resonance patterns are stabilizing," she announced, standing up and brushing dust from her knees. "I'm cataloging the frequency shift for the Guild's records. We have a new data point on how much energy the Blight can store before it saturates the field."

Torin checked his sword, running a hand along the hilt as if to ensure it was still grounded in reality. "We're moving out," he said, looking toward the ridge where they had planned their next waypoint. "The wind is picking up. If we stay too long in this valley, we'll freeze before we can reach the watchfort."

Elias nodded. "Yes. Let's go. Kaelen, you need to rest before we move another mile."

"I'm fine," Kaelen insisted, though he stumbled slightly as he took a step forward, his leg shaking from exhaustion. "Just... drained. That's all."

"You held the line until it broke," Elias said firmly but gently. "Drained is an understatement. You are not a machine; you are flesh and blood. Rest is required." He paused, looking at the group. "Maren, Torin, help him walk us out. Elara, pack up your samples. We leave in ten minutes."

The party moved carefully away from the shrine, stepping over the cooling stone where the fissure had been. The ground was firm now, devoid of the oily slickness that had coated their boots earlier. The oppressive weight of the Blight's presence had lifted, replaced by the crisp, clean air of a mountain valley at dusk.

As they walked along the ridge line, the city lights began to flicker on in the distance, their warm glow cutting through the encroaching darkness. They were safe. The immediate danger was neutralized not by force, but by understanding and adaptation. The Blight had been fed until it ran out of food, its reservoir drained into the system where it could be dispersed harmlessly. It was a victory born of patience rather than power, a stark reminder that sometimes the best way to fight a monster was to starve it.

Elias glanced back at Kaelen, who was struggling to keep up with Torin's steady stride. The young mage had accepted the cost of his contribution without complaint, but Elias knew there would be lingering effects. The mental strain of regulating such a massive flow wouldn't vanish overnight. He hoped that rest and time would ease the ache in Kaelen's mind and body before they faced any new challenges.

"We've done enough here," Maren said quietly, breaking the silence as they paused to look out over the valley one last time. The fog had not returned; the stars were already beginning to pierce the twilight sky, pinpricks of white light against indigo. "No more Blight for a long while."

"Unless it's regenerated," Torin noted dryly, though his tone lacked any real malice. "Or unless someone else decides to mess with the shrine again."

Elias smiled faintly. "Good point. But tonight, we rest. Tomorrow, we move on. The road ahead is open."

Kaelen nodded, leaning heavily on Torin's arm for a moment before finding his own balance. "Open," he repeated, tasting the word. "That feels... strange. Like walking on glass that isn't broken anymore."

Elara adjusted her pack, securing the last of her tools inside. "Strange is good," she said with a small smile. "It means we're moving forward. And if anyone tries to walk on that glass again, we'll be ready."

The companions turned away from the shrine and began the descent toward their campsite, their footsteps echoing softly against the stone path. The night was settling in, but it was a peaceful night, the kind that follows danger passed rather than the dread of it approaching. They had survived the operation, emerged changed but intact, and now they would recover before facing whatever lay beyond the valley floor.
