The silence in the corner booth felt different now—not the heavy, expectant quiet of waiting, but the settled calm of a storm passing. Elias folded his hands over the parchment Silas Vane had brought earlier, the rolled map tied with leather cord sitting prominently atop the table like a contract of sorts. Outside, the Gilded Tankard roared on, but here, amidst the steam from their cooling stew and the flickering candlelight, the air was still enough to hear the scrape of boot heels on the floorboards when someone approached.

Silas Vane stepped out from the crowd near the entrance once more, this time accompanied by a second merchant. This one was smaller, dressed in robes of faded indigo with patches of moss-stained wool at the hem, carrying a satchel slung over a shoulder that looked far too thin for the weight it bore. He didn’t speak immediately; instead, he offered them both a curt nod before glancing toward the window where Torin stood guard. His eyes narrowed slightly as they passed him, but he made no move to intervene—Silas had given him a warning glance earlier, and the smaller man understood the boundaries of their engagement.

“Thorne,” Silas said smoothly, his voice carrying just enough distance to be heard without rising above the tavern’s din. “I trust you’ve had time to consider our arrangement.”

Elias didn’t look up immediately. He studied the parchment again, tracing a line with his fingernail that outlined the route to the mountain pass. The ink was fresh, still wet in places where Vane had pressed too hard during their initial drafting. “We have,” he said finally, looking up. His expression was neutral, but there was a faint tightness around his mouth that suggested he was holding back something—perhaps frustration, perhaps just caution.

“Good,” Silas replied with a small smile. “Then perhaps we can move forward.” He leaned against the booth’s side, crossing one arm over his chest while gesturing vaguely toward the table. “Though I must say, your team is… unique.” He paused, letting the word hang in the air before elaborating. “Most adventurers would have accepted my terms without question. But you? You negotiated for clarity, demanded transparency, and refused to sign anything until every clause was fully explained.”

Her hand rested lightly on Kaelen’s shoulder again, though he wasn’t looking at her this time—he was staring at the map, his brow furrowed as if trying to decode some hidden message in the red ink. “It’s not about demanding anything,” she said quietly, her voice carrying enough over the din to be heard without shouting. “It’s about making sure everyone knows what they’re agreeing to. If we don’t understand the terms, how can we say we’ve agreed to them?”

Silas chuckled softly, shaking his head as if amused by her logic rather than annoyed. “Fair point,” he admitted with a shrug that seemed almost casual for someone whose entire career revolved around high-stakes trades. “But here’s where things get interesting.” He gestured toward the second merchant—the smaller, indigo-robed figure who had finally found the courage to step forward and join them at their table. “This is Thaddeus Vane, my junior partner. And while you might think we’re offering a single deal, let me assure you: our partnership offers multiple options.”

The smaller man blinked, caught off guard by Silas’s introduction. He looked between the two of them, then back to Elias, before finally clearing his throat. “Hello,” he said awkwardly, extending a hand that trembled slightly as he did so. “I’m Thaddeus Vane. We’re… well, we represent another group—yes, there are always several groups—but our focus is different.” He paused, then added more clearly. “We specialize in escorting caravans carrying non-magical goods: salt, grain, textiles, timber.”

Elias tilted his head slightly, studying Thaddeus’s face for signs of deception or nervousness. There were none visible, but the way he spoke suggested he was trying to downplay something important. “And what exactly do you mean by ‘non-magical’?” Elias asked cautiously. “Because if it’s anything magical, we’ll want to know about it.”

Thaddeus blinked again, clearly unprepared for such a direct question. He glanced nervously at Silas before answering. “Nothing like Temple Springs water,” he muttered defensively. “Just plain old goods. Salt from the coastal mines, grain from the central valleys, textiles from the weavers’ guilds.”

Maren snorted under her breath, which was enough to draw a sharp look from Silas. He didn’t miss it entirely—he merely smiled politely while continuing to gesture toward Thaddeus. “Exactly,” he said smoothly. “Our primary caravan is special, yes, but so too are these secondary routes. And we’d like our partners to know that we’re serious about protecting all caravans equally.”

Thaddeus opened his mouth to respond, only for Silas to cut him off with a raised hand. “Let me answer that,” he said calmly. “The reason we’re offering this arrangement is because we value transparency and fairness above all else. If anyone knows how dangerous the world can be, it’s us—and we know that sometimes one caravan isn’t enough to handle everything.” He gestured toward Thaddeus again. “Our junior partner here handles logistics for smaller routes, but he works closely with our main team whenever resources allow.”

Maren crossed her arms over her chest, mirroring the posture Silas had used moments earlier. “So what are you asking of us?” she asked directly. “Are you expecting us to protect both caravans simultaneously? Or just one?”

Silas smiled warmly, though there was a hint of amusement in his eyes that suggested he knew exactly how this conversation would unfold. “Just one,” he said simply. “Your focus remains on the Temple Springs cargo. Our secondary partners will handle everything else.” He leaned in closer, lowering his voice as if sharing a secret with them alone. “Think of it this way: when we split our efforts, everyone benefits. Your team gets to concentrate on the most valuable load, while ours ensures nothing else goes unnoticed.”

Elias frowned slightly, considering Silas’s words carefully. “It sounds like you’re trying to divide responsibility,” he said cautiously. “But if you expect us to protect only one caravan, then we’ll need to make sure we’re prepared for that specific threat.”

Silas nodded approvingly. “Precisely,” he confirmed with a gesture that seemed almost paternal in its tone. “That’s why I brought Thaddeus along—to show you exactly what kind of protection your team can expect from us.” He gestured toward the smaller merchant again, who had now stopped trembling and was watching them both intently. “He’ll accompany you as backup whenever needed, but he won’t interfere with your primary mission.”

Maren studied Thaddeus’s face for several moments before nodding slowly. “Alright,” she said finally. “But if anything happens to our caravan while you’re handling the other routes, we’ll need compensation.”

Thaddeus nodded eagerly, though his voice lacked conviction. “Of course,” he assured her. “We’ll take care of everything.”

Silas chuckled softly, shaking his head as if amused by Thaddeus’s eagerness. “See?” he said to Elias and Maren. “This is how we work. Everyone gets a fair share, and no one gets left behind.” He turned his attention back to the parchment on their table, pointing at a specific clause with his free hand. “Here’s where things get complicated,” he noted mildly. “The contract specifies that we’re responsible for any losses incurred during transport. But since your team is handling the primary caravan, you’ll need additional coverage for anything unusual.”

Elias picked up a quill from the table, rolling it between his fingers as he considered Silas’s words. “What kind of coverage are you proposing?” he asked evenly.
