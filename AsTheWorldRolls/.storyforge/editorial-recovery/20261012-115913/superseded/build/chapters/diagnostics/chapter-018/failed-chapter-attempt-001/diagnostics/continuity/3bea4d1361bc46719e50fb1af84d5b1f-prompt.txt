Maren leaned back against the rough concrete of the drainage ditch wall, her breathing slow and even. Her face was flushed, not from fever or exertion, but from the sheer relief of having stopped holding the line alone. The brass gauntlet on Kaelen’s wrist had gone dark again after the initial surge, then re-ignited with a faint, rhythmic pulse that matched the hum of the barrier. It no longer screamed for control; it simply existed, a quiet engine powering the violet curtain that now stretched from horizon to horizon without flickering.

Elara sat cross-legged on the grass, carefully sorting the remaining quartz shards into her satchel. She had salvaged only the largest fragments, the smaller dust having been swept aside by the wind moments ago. Her hands moved with a gentle precision, wiping the sharp edges of the crystal with a strip of leather before placing them away.

"You know," she said, her voice low and tired but carrying easily over the hum, "I never thought I’d be able to arrange broken glass into something that sings."

"It’s not glass anymore," Elias replied, watching the barrier. The violet light was steady now, a solid wall of energy that seemed to hold the valley in place with its own weight. "It’s a circuit. You’ve built a dam out of crystal shards and willpower."

"Willpower doesn’t conduct electricity," Elara shot back with a small, weary smile that didn’t quite reach her eyes. She looked up at him, her dark eyes reflecting the violet glow of the barrier above them. "But maybe it conducts something else. Maybe it’s fear, or hope, or just… stubbornness."

Elias caught the edge of her gaze and softened. "Maybe it’s all three," he said quietly. He shifted his weight, wincing slightly as his side throbbed beneath the bandage. The wound was old now, a scar from the watchfort days that had reopened during the resonance surge, but it wasn’t bleeding. It was just a reminder of how close they’d come to losing everything when they first tried to tap the seal.

Torin stood near the edge of the clearing, his sword still drawn but lowered to a rest position against the stone beside him. He hadn’t spoken much since the flow stabilized, content to watch the perimeter and make sure nothing else tried to slip through the fog. His eyes scanned the trees on the ridge, the distant rooftops of the city, the way the light bent around them like heat waves off pavement.

"The barrier’s holding," Torin said finally, his voice rough but calm. "No more flickering."

"No more screaming," Maren added, closing her eyes for a moment and letting her head roll back against the wall. "It’s quiet now. For the first time since we arrived, it’s quiet."

Elias nodded. "We’ve bought ourselves some time. That’s all this was ever meant to do. Feed the beast until it grows tired, then let the city foundations absorb what’s left."

"That assumes the foundations are still intact," Elara murmured, though she didn’t sound worried. She was already reaching into her satchel for a small vial of oil and a fresh rag to wipe down one of the larger shards. "If the reservoir beneath us has collapsed, or if the magic that holds the city together has gone dry…"

Elias shook his head. "We don’t know yet. We need confirmation from the Watchfort before we move on. But for now, we’re safe. The seal is weak, yes, but it’s not broken."

Maren chuckled softly, a dry, humorless sound that carried over the hum of the barrier. "Safe," she repeated, testing the word. "I’m starting to forget what that feels like. I haven’t felt safe since we left the watchfort. Maybe ever."

Elias turned to look at her properly this time. She was leaning back so far against the wall that her knees were almost touching the ground behind her. Her hair was matted with sweat and dust, her clothes torn in places, but her posture was relaxed for the first time since they’d arrived at the shrine. There was no coiled tension in her shoulders, no readiness to spring into action. She looked like a woman who had just finished a marathon and was finally allowed to sit down.

"You did good," Elias said simply. "You held the line when we needed you most."

Maren didn’t answer immediately. She closed her eyes again, letting her head fall back against the cool stone behind her. "I’m not proud of it," she said eventually. "But I’ll take credit for it if anyone else asks. And if someone tries to tell me I was wrong, they can ask me why I didn’t run away."

Elias smiled faintly. "You wouldn’t have run," he said. "You always stood your ground."

Maren opened one eye and gave him a sidelong look. "And what about the rest of you? Did any of you stand anywhere?"

They all laughed, a low, tired sound that broke the tension in the air like a breaking wave. It wasn’t much, but it was something. A moment of shared humanity, a reminder that they were still people even if they’d just spent hours manipulating the raw magic of a collapsing city.

Elara finished wiping down the last shard and capped the vial with a twist of metal. She stood up slowly, brushing the grass from her knees before handing Elias the rag she’d used to clean the crystal. "Here," she said. "For your side. Or at least, for something else later."

Elias took it, nodding. He didn’t need cleaning supplies right now—he’d already cleaned his wound earlier—but he accepted the gesture anyway. It was a small thing, but it mattered. In the aftermath of something like this, gestures carried more weight than words.

Torin sheathed his sword with a soft click and leaned against the stone beside him, crossing his arms over his chest. "If we’re waiting for confirmation," he said, "then we might as well take a moment to rest."

"We can’t stay here forever," Elias reminded him gently. "The heat will fade eventually. Once it does, we’ll need to find shelter before the night comes."

"And where exactly is that?" Maren asked, though she sounded less concerned than curious. "Are we camping out here? Or do we march back to the watchfort?"

"We go wherever the road tells us," Elias said. "But first, we rest. Just for an hour. Let our bodies recover from what they’ve done today."

Maren nodded slowly, closing her eyes again. "Fine. But if anyone tries to talk about strategy or planning while I’m resting, I’ll ignore them."

Elias turned back toward the barrier, watching it pulse with its steady violet rhythm. The hum was softer now, almost peaceful, like a lullaby sung by someone who knew exactly what they were doing. It wasn’t perfect—the system was still fragile, still dependent on Kaelen’s focus and Elara’s careful tuning—but it was stable. That was enough for now.

The fog had begun to thin along the edges of the clearing, revealing patches of sunlight that had been hidden behind the clouds earlier in the day. The green light from the shrine fissure had dimmed further, fading into a deep indigo that seemed to absorb more than it emitted. The barrier was doing its job: feeding the Blight until it ran out of fuel or until they found a way to shut it down completely.

For now, they just waited. They rested. And for the first time in weeks, they breathed without fear.
