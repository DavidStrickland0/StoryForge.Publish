The heavy oak doors of the Gilded Tankard groaned shut behind them, sealing out the chaotic symphony of clinking tankards and raucous laughter that had defined the afternoon. Inside the corner booth, however, the air was thick with a different kind of tension—the quiet, sharp focus of men doing business while danger loomed just outside the city walls.

Silas Vane did not retreat to the bar or vanish into the tavern’s shadows as Elias expected him to after rejecting the escort clause. Instead, he leaned casually against the back of their booth chair, one hand resting on the empty flagon of ale, his smile widening in a way that suggested he found their refusal amusing rather than alarming.

“Ah,” Silas said, his voice smooth as oil poured over hot iron. “So we’re done with the escort package.” He tapped the table lightly with two fingers. “A bold move for those so young and inexperienced. Though I suppose it speaks to your confidence that you trust your own wits enough to walk into a lion’s den without an entourage.”

Elias folded his hands over his tankard, keeping his tone even. “We don’t need protection we can pay for. If there’s trouble, we’ll handle it ourselves. That’s how companies work here in the city—pay for what you use, keep control of your own affairs.”

“Mmm,” Silas hummed noncommittally, leaning forward slightly to rest his elbows on the table. The movement brought him closer, and Elias could smell faint traces of lavender soap mixed with the metallic scent of old coin from the merchant’s coat. “That’s noble-sounding. Very… idealistic.” He paused, letting the word hang in the air before adding dryly, “But I must admit, it does make my job considerably more interesting. Most clients beg for every extra service we offer. You’re actually turning down free security because you don’t want us following your lead.”

Maren watched Silas’s expression closely from across the table, her fingers tracing invisible patterns on the wood of Elias’s tankard. She didn’t interrupt, though her posture shifted imperceptibly as if bracing for a sudden shift in conversation. Her presence remained a silent anchor, ready to intervene if things turned unpleasant.

Kaelen sat with his gauntlet still cold at his wrist, studying Silas with the same intense curiosity he’d shown earlier when examining Elara’s journal diagrams. His gaze was analytical rather than hostile, noting every nuance of posture and micro-expression. “And what exactly is your final offer?” he asked quietly, breaking the spell of the conversation. “If we’re walking away from protection, does that mean you’ll just sell us supplies at full price now?”

Silas’s smile didn’t falter; if anything, it deepened. He reached into his coat pocket and produced a small leather-bound book, flipping through its pages with practiced ease before setting it down on the table. “Precisely. Full prices for premium goods, yes. But I’ve also included a few… incentives.” He tapped the cover of the book lightly. “Consider this my personal touch. A gesture of goodwill toward those who know how to bargain like adults.”

Elias glanced at the book briefly before turning his attention back to Silas. “What kind of incentives?” he asked, keeping his voice neutral but attentive. “Are we talking discount on potions? Bonus credits for early payment? Or perhaps something more unusual given your reputation as a master negotiator?”

Silas laughed softly, the sound rich and resonant in the quiet booth. “Oh, my dear Elias Thorne,” he said, using their names with an intimacy that seemed odd considering they’d only met hours ago. “You have a sharp mind for business logic, I’ll give you that. But let me tell you something about commerce in this city: sometimes the best deals aren’t found in ledgers or contracts—they’re found in relationships built on mutual respect and shared interests.” He paused dramatically, leaning back slightly as if sharing a profound secret. “And trust me, there’s nothing worth sharing quite like information about Blight-Touched entities patrolling the outskirts.”

Kaelen tilted his head slightly, his expression unreadable but clearly processing the implication. “Information?” he repeated slowly. “About what exactly? Creatures stalking the caravan route? Locations of nesting grounds? Or perhaps warnings about which villages have been abandoned due to recent attacks?”

Silas’s smile faltered for just a fraction of a second before returning full force. “All of the above,” he admitted openly. “And much more besides. My agents are everywhere, watching everything, reporting anything that might affect trade routes or market prices. For a modest fee—well, slightly less than full price—you get access to reports compiled from multiple sources, cross-referenced with local legends and recent sightings.” He tapped the book again. “Think of it as insurance against surprises along the road. A little extra knowledge never hurt anyone who knows how to use it properly.”

Maren shifted slightly in her seat, her hand moving subtly closer to Kaelen’s shoulder without drawing attention. She hadn’t said a word since their meal arrived, but her posture suggested she was listening intently to Silas’s offer. “And what exactly do these reports cost?” she asked finally, her voice low and measured. “Is it per report? Monthly subscription? Or does everything come bundled into your standard package?”

Silas grinned broadly now, the kind of grin that suggested he genuinely enjoyed negotiating deals even more than closing them. “Let’s talk about value,” he replied smoothly. “For five hundred gold pieces upfront—a sum that covers all future reports until you reach the mountain pass—you get unlimited access to my intelligence network. That includes real-time updates on creature movements, changes in weather patterns affecting travel conditions, and even rumors about rival companies plotting against your convoy.” He paused for effect, watching each face carefully as he listed his offerings. “No hidden fees. No surprise charges. Just honest trade between two parties who understand the importance of staying ahead of the curve.”

Elias considered this offer thoughtfully, weighing its merits against potential drawbacks. Five hundred gold pieces wasn’t an insignificant sum—it represented nearly a third of their current coin supply—but it might buy them valuable information that could save their lives down the road. “So you’re offering us continuous updates until we cross into the mountains?” he clarified, making sure they were on the same page before committing to any agreement.

“Exactly,” Silas confirmed with a nod. “Until your party enters the mountain pass, yes. And beyond that point, depending on what happens next, additional fees may apply. But for now, consider it an investment in safety and peace of mind. Think of it as hiring a ghost advisor who follows you all along your journey without ever needing sleep or food.”

Kaelen nodded slowly, his gauntlet still inert at his wrist but his mind clearly working through the implications. “Five hundred gold pieces,” he repeated quietly, testing the number mentally. “For unlimited updates until we reach the mountains. And beyond that, additional fees based on usage?”

“Correct,” Silas affirmed with a satisfied smirk. “Though I must warn you: once we start moving into hostile territory, those reports will become increasingly valuable. Each piece of intel could mean the difference between life and death. So consider it not just an expense but potentially a lifeline.” He leaned forward again, his voice dropping slightly as if sharing a crucial secret. “And remember, Elias Thorne: trust is earned over time—not handed out freely like candy. But I do think you two have shown some promise already today.”

Elias exchanged a glance with Maren, who gave him an almost imperceptible nod of approval. They both understood the gravity of Silas’s offer—it wasn’t just about buying information; it was about building a partnership that could sustain them through whatever challenges lay ahead. “Alright,” Elias said finally, his voice steady despite lingering fatigue from earlier events. “Let’s talk numbers.”

Silas’s grin widened again as he produced a fresh parchment and quill from his coat pocket. “Excellent choice,” he praised warmly. “I always knew you’d be the one to walk away with our company’s most valuable client list.” He set up the writing materials carefully, ensuring everything was positioned perfectly for signing before gesturing toward Elias. “Go ahead, then. Sign where I indicate here.”

Elias picked up the quill hesitantly, examining the nib before pressing it lightly against the parchment. As he signed his name, he couldn’t help but notice how Silas’s demeanor shifted subtly—the merchant became almost theatrical in his delight at having secured another deal, yet there was something genuine underlying the performance. It wasn’t just about profit; Silas genuinely believed in the value of sharing information with those who needed it most.

Maren signed her name beside Elias’s, her handwriting neat and precise. Kaelen followed suit with a brief flourish that matched his usual efficiency. By the time they finished, Silas was already reaching for additional scrolls containing detailed maps marked with red lines tracing possible caravan routes through the valley. “Here you go,” he announced cheerfully, sliding the bundles toward them. “These contain everything we discussed earlier: potion recipes, weapon specifications, herbal remedies, and updated intelligence reports covering the next week’s journey.”

Elias picked up one of the scrolls briefly before setting it aside on the table. The paper smelled faintly of ink and old parchment, but beneath that scent lingered something else—a metallic tang of oil and iron that made him pause momentarily before unfolding it fully.
