Elara's fingers hovered over the chisel, the steel tip hovering an inch above the quartz. The hum in Kaelen’s chest was a physical weight now, pressing against his ribs, making it hard to draw a full breath. He wasn't just holding back a tide; he was straining against a dam that was being flooded.

Elias watched the barrier. The violet light had stopped flickering and settled into a steady, rhythmic pulse. It looked less like a shield and more like a living membrane, stretching and contracting with the intake of the Blight. But it was a fragile equilibrium. If they pushed too hard, the feedback loop would shatter the seal. If they pulled back too soon, the surge would overwhelm them both.

"Elara," Elias said, his voice barely audible over the low thrum. "The angle. It's too steep."

Elara didn't blink. Her eyes were fixed on the line of contact between the steel and the crystal. She saw what he saw: the vibration was becoming erratic, a wavering note that threatened to climb out of control. The quartz stone was glowing with an intensity that made the air around it shimmer.

"I know," she whispered. "I'm compensating for the pressure. If I lower the angle now, the resonance will drop below threshold and the flow stops."

"Then we don't lower the angle," Maren said, stepping in beside Elara. She placed her hammer on the ground between them, not to strike, but as a counterweight. "We change the frequency. We match the intake to the output."

Kaelen felt his knees tremble. It wasn't fear anymore; it was the physical toll of maintaining that level of suppression. His vision swam at the edges, tinted with the green ghost-light of the Blight even as the fog retreated from him. He heard a sharp intake of breath from Torin behind him, but he didn't turn. Turning would break the focus.

"Steady," Maren whispered again, her hand sliding over Elara's shoulder to steady her arm. "Don't fight the vibration. Ride it."
