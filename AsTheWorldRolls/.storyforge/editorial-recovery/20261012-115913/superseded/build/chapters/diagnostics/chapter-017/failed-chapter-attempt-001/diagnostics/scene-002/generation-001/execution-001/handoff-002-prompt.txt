Silas Vane’s shadow fell across the scarred wood of the tavern table as he leaned in, his fingers drumming a rhythmic, impatient pattern against the surface. The noise of the Gilded Tankard—the clatter of tankards, the low murmur of bets on the arena match out front—was a constant backdrop, but Vane’s voice cut through it with the precision of a drawn blade.

“Five hundred gold pieces upfront,” he said, his tone leaving no room for misinterpretation. “Plus a ten percent cut from whatever we haul back. That’s my floor. Anything less, and I walk.”

Elias didn’t flinch. He kept his hands resting on the table, his posture relaxed but alert, his eyes fixed on Vane’s face rather than the scattered coins that lay between them like an accusation. “Mr. Vane,” Elias began, his voice even. “I understand you’re a man of business. But I also know that in this town, reputation is worth more than gold if you want to keep it.”

Vane smirked, leaning back against the table’s edge with a casual arrogance that said he knew exactly how much power he held. “Reputation gets you into rooms,” he said. “Gold keeps you alive. And my reputation? It’s built on contracts signed in blood and ink. You want a job, Elias Thorne? You take my terms.”

Maren shifted slightly behind Elias, her eyes darting toward Kaelen, who stood just out of view near the bar, watching the exchange with wide, fearful eyes. She caught his gaze for a second, giving him a silent nod that conveyed *stay calm*, before turning her attention back to Vane’s retreating figure as he prepared to leave.

Elias didn’t rise to meet him immediately. Instead, he took a slow breath, steadying himself against the pressure of the moment. “Mr. Vane,” he said again, his voice low but firm. “I’m not asking for charity. I’m offering a partnership. We’ve got skills you can’t buy with gold: navigation through the Blight’s distortion, magical containment techniques, and the ability to read the terrain like a map drawn in our own minds.”

Vane scoffed, shaking his head as he adjusted his glove. “Skills are overrated these days,” he muttered. “I need someone who can follow orders, not someone who thinks they’re better than me. Five hundred gold now. That’s the deal.”

Elias stood, smoothing out the folds in his coat before stepping toward Vane. “We’ll come back tomorrow with a counteroffer,” he said simply. “But first, let me ask you this: What exactly are we hauling?”

Vane hesitated, his eyes narrowing slightly as if he’d forgotten to mention that detail. He glanced around the tavern, catching the wary glances of nearby patrons before leaning in again. “A sealed crate,” he said quietly. “From the High Court. It’s got a sigil on it—*pure water*, they call it. I don’t know what’s inside, but I do know that if we get back without damage, the reward alone will clear our debts for a decade.”

“Pure water?” Elias repeated, his brow furrowing as he tried to reconcile the term with anything he’d heard of in his studies. “Is that… literally just water? Or is it something else?”

“Something else,” Vane admitted, his voice dropping even lower. “It’s not meant for drinking. It’s meant for rituals. And if we mess it up, the High Court won’t be happy about it.”

Elias nodded slowly, his mind already racing through possibilities. If the crate contained something supernatural, then the stakes were higher than just securing a contract. But if Vane was right and it was indeed a ritual substance, then they needed to handle it with care. “Then we’ll need to know how to transport it safely,” he said. “Can you give us more details?”

Vane hesitated again, clearly reluctant to share information with outsiders. After a moment, he sighed and nodded. “It’s fragile,” he said. “Don’t drop it. Don’t let it get wet. And don’t even think about opening it unless we’re under direct supervision from the High Court’s own agents.”

“Understood,” Elias replied, his tone respectful but firm. “We’ll make sure it stays safe. But we also need to know what happens if something goes wrong. What’s your contingency plan?”

Vane shrugged, a gesture that said *I don’t care enough to plan*. “If it breaks, we fix it,” he said. “If it leaks, we clean it up. If it explodes, well…” He trailed off, shaking his head as if the thought itself was too absurd to entertain.

Elias didn’t press further. Instead, he stepped back and gestured toward the quieter area of the tavern where they could speak privately. “Let’s talk strategy,” he said. “We’ll come back tomorrow with a better offer. But first, let me ask you: Who’s on our team?”

Vane smirked again, his confidence returning as he looked around the tavern. “Me and my crew,” he said. “Three of us. We’ve got experience with high-value cargo. And we’ve got connections in the right places.”

“Connections are good,” Elias agreed. “But I think we can do better than that. Let’s say we form a joint venture. You provide the crate, we provide the escort and protection, and we split the profits evenly. No upfront payment required. Just a share of what we earn.”

Vane frowned, his expression hardening as he considered the proposal. “That’s… ambitious,” he said finally. “But maybe it works. Let’s say we give you until noon tomorrow to think about it. Then we meet again and settle the terms.”

“Agreed,” Elias said, shaking Vane’s hand firmly before stepping back. “We’ll be ready.”

As they moved toward the quieter section of the tavern, Maren caught up with them, her expression still tense but relieved that Vane hadn’t forced them into a corner yet. “He’s not wrong about the crate,” she said quietly as they settled at a small table tucked away from the noise. “Pure water doesn’t sound like much, but if it’s ritual-grade stuff, then it could be worth a fortune.”

“Or it could be cursed,” Kaelen added, his voice still shaky as he sat down beside them. “I’ve heard stories about things that go bad when they’re mishandled. We need to be careful.”

Elias nodded, pouring himself a glass of ale before setting it aside with a sigh. “Careful is the right word,” he said. “But we also need to trust our instincts. If something feels off, we act on it. And if Vane’s crew tries anything shady, we shut them down.”

Maren nodded, her expression serious as she reached for her own tankard. “We’ll do our best,” she said. “But I hope we don’t have to fight anyone else today. Just the usual monsters and traps.”

“Monsters and traps,” Kaelen repeated, his voice still trembling slightly. “That’s… that’s what we’re supposed to deal with, isn’t it?”

“Yes,” Elias said gently. “But sometimes the worst threats are people who want to cut us down before we even leave town.”

They sat in silence for a moment, each lost in their own thoughts as they considered the implications of Vane’s proposal. The weight of responsibility settled heavily on Elias’s shoulders once more, but he didn’t let it show. Instead, he leaned forward and placed a reassuring hand on Kaelen’s shoulder. “We’ve got this,” he said softly. “Just keep your wits about you, and we’ll make it through.”

Maren nodded, taking a sip of her ale before setting the glass down with a soft clink. “Let’s go home,” she said finally. “We’ll come back tomorrow with a better offer.”

“Agreed,” Elias said, standing up and offering his hand to help Kaelen rise. “Let’s get some rest. We’ll be ready when we return.”
