The moment the word *Ready* left Elias’s lips, the air in the clearing changed. It did not shift with the sudden, violent snap of a breaking seal, but with the slow, heavy exhale of a lung filling for the first time in an age. The vibration from the quartz stone, previously a steady drone, deepened. It dropped below the range of sound and into the range of bone, a frequency that Elias felt in the marrow of his shins and the ache of his side.

Kaelen opened his eyes.

They were no longer the pale, washed-out blue of a young man who had been fighting for his life. They were dark, dilated, the irises bleeding into the black of the pupil until only a thin ring of light remained. He did not look at Elias, nor at Torin, nor at the shrine. He looked *through* them, his gaze fixed on a point in the middle distance that did not exist in the physical world.

"Pull," Kaelen whispered.

The word was not a command. It was a request, fragile and terrified.

Torin’s hand tightened on the hilt of his sword, his knuckles whitening. He was the anchor, the brake, the one who would strike if the line snapped. But he did not move. He waited.

Kaelen inhaled.

The reaction was immediate and visceral. The thin stream of Blight that had been flowing from the shrine toward the barrier did not thicken; it *surged*. The heavy smoke, which had drifted with the lazy, sickly scent of copper and rot, suddenly coiled with purpose. It rushed out of the fissure in the stone, a dense, viscous rope of darkness that lashed across the clearing like a living thing.

Elias felt the pull before he saw it. It was a vacuum, a sudden drop in pressure that made his ears pop and his vision blur. The fog, which had been retreating from Kaelen in a respectful, fearful arc, now surged back in. It did not attack Kaelen; it flowed *toward* him, a river of gray mist seeking a drain.

Kaelen stood at the center of this confluence, his body rigid. His brass gauntlet, which had been cold and inert for hours, began to hum. Not the erratic, dangerous flicker of the forest, but a low, resonant thrum that matched the deepening drone of the quartz stone. The runes etched into the brass glowed with a faint, sickly green light, pulsing in time with Kaelen’s breath.

"Steady," Elias murmured, his voice sounding thin in the rushing air. He watched the barrier.

The violet hue of the containment field was no longer static. It was rippling, the light bending and stretching as it absorbed the increased volume of corruption. The barrier was not just a wall; it was a mouth, and it was hungry. The stream of Blight hit the edge of the light and vanished, swallowed without a ripple, without a sound. But the barrier itself was changing. The violet deepened, becoming a bruised, almost black purple, and the light within it began to swirl in complex, spiraling patterns.

Elias turned his attention back to his companions. The physical toll of the first phase, which had been masked by adrenaline and the sheer magnitude of the event, was now coming home to roost.

Maren was the first to falter.

She had been bracing Elara’s shoulders, her hands gripping the girl’s jacket with a strength that belied her own exhaustion. But as the ground shook with a new, deeper rhythm, Maren’s knees buckled. She didn’t fall, but she swayed, her face drained of color. Her hands, which had been steady enough to hold a chisel against a vibrating stone, were now trembling violently.

"Elara," Maren gasped, her voice rough. "I can't hold her. The vibration..."

Elara, still pressed against the quartz stone, looked up. Her face was pale, her eyes wide with a mixture of concentration and fear. The chisel in her hand was blurred, the low-frequency vibration traveling up the shaft and into her bones. She couldn't let go. If she released the pressure, the resonance would shift, and the seal could shatter or, worse, snap shut again.

"I'm okay," Elara said, but her voice was tight, a wire pulled too taut.

Elias moved quickly, closing the distance between them. He didn't offer a hand; he didn't have the energy for gentle gestures. He stepped behind Maren and placed his hands on her back, supporting her weight. He felt the heat of her body through his leather armor, the rapid, shallow rise and fall of her breathing.

"Lean on me," Elias said, his voice low and rough. "Breathe."

Maren nodded, leaning back into him. It was a small, practical act of survival, but in the chaos of the Blight’s surge, it felt like an anchor. She closed her eyes, focusing on the solid pressure of Elias’s hands, the familiar scent of old leather and sweat, grounding herself in the present moment rather than the terrifying future.

Elias looked up at Kaelen.

The young man was still standing at the center of the clearing, but he was no longer entirely there. His body was a conduit, a bridge between the Blight and the barrier. He was pale, his skin taking on the same sickly hue as the fog. His lips were moving, not in words, but in a silent, desperate count.

*One. Two. Three.*

Elias could see the strain in Kaelen’s shoulders, the way his neck muscles bunched and released with each cycle of the pull. The gauntlet on his wrist was glowing brighter, the green light spilling over his hand, casting long, distorted shadows on the wet ground.

"Kaelen," Elias said.

Kaelen didn’t respond. He couldn’t. His mind was elsewhere, in the deep, dark place where the Blight lived.

Elias turned to Torin. The swordsman was standing exactly where he had been, his sword drawn, his eyes fixed on Kaelen with an intensity that bordered on obsession. He was watching for the sign, the micro-expression, the slight tremor that would tell him Kaelen was losing control.

"How long?" Torin asked, his voice flat.

Elias didn't know. He didn't have a clock, and the Blight did not follow the laws of time. It followed the laws of hunger.

"I don't know," Elias said. "But the barrier is holding. For now."

Torin nodded, but his eyes did not leave Kaelen. He was the safety, the fail-safe. If Kaelen broke, Torin would be there. If Kaelen consumed the Blight, Torin would be there. If Kaelen consumed *them*, Torin would still be there.

The ground shook again, a deep, resonant thrum that vibrated through the soles of their boots. The quartz stone was glowing now, a soft, pulsing green that matched the light on Kaelen’s gauntlet. The stream of Blight was thicker, darker, flowing with a steady, relentless force.

Elias looked at the barrier. It was no longer a flat sheet of light. It was a sphere, a vast, encompassing dome that had expanded to cover the entire clearing, the shrine, and the surrounding trees. The light was swirling, digesting, breaking down the corruption into something harmless, something empty.

It was working.

But the cost was rising.

Maren’s breathing had become shallow, her hands still trembling on Elara’s shoulders. Elara’s face was a mask of concentration, her eyes unblinking, her jaw clenched. Kaelen was a statue, a conduit, a bridge.

And Elias was tired.

The wound on his side, which had been a dull ache, was now a sharp, burning pain. The cold water in his clothes was seeping into his bones, making it hard to keep his muscles taut. His mind was racing, calculating, analyzing, but his body was failing.

He looked at his companions. They were tired, wet, and afraid. But they were ready.

They were ready to do what had to be done.

The second phase was in full swing. The Blight was flowing. The barrier was consuming. And Kaelen was pulling.

Elias closed his eyes for a moment, focusing on the rhythm of the vibration, the sound of the Blight, the weight of his friends. He was the observer, the monitor, the one who would signal the stop if it was needed.

But for now, they held.

The clearing was silent, save for the hum of the stone, the roar of the Blight, and the steady, rhythmic pulse of the barrier. They were in the eye of the storm, in the heart of the Blight, and they were holding on.

The second phase had begun.
