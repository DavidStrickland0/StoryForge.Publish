The dark layer at the bottom of the dome did not just flow; it poured. It rushed toward the earth with a hungry, liquid violence, seeping into the soil, the roots, the very bedrock of the forest. The ground beneath their feet gave a low, groaning rumble, as if the earth itself were swallowing a stone.

Elias watched the barrier. The churning vortices slowed, the frantic spinning settling into a steady, downward pull. The sickly green light that had been pulsing with agitated energy dimmed, replaced by a deeper, darker shade. It was no longer a trap filling up; it was a drain emptying.

But the drain required a pump. And the pump was Kaelen.

The young man stood rigid in the center of the clearing, his body a conduit for the force that was now rushing past him and into the ground. His skin had taken on a translucent, sickly hue, the veins beneath visible as dark, branching rivers. His eyes, previously wide with the terror of the surge, were now dark and dilated, the pupils swallowing the iris whole. He was not looking at them. He was not looking at the barrier. He was looking down, his gaze fixed on the soil, his head tilted slightly as if listening to a sound only he could hear.

Torin stepped forward, his sword still drawn, but his grip had loosened from the white-knuckled tension of the crisis. He moved to Kaelen’s side, not to touch him—Elias had seen what happened when they touched during the surge—but to stand close enough that he could intervene instantly if the boy’s body began to spasm or convulse.

"Kaelen," Torin said, his voice low and steady. "Can you hear me?"

There was no answer. Kaelen’s lips moved, but no sound came out. Instead, a low hum vibrated from his chest, a resonance that matched the deep thrum of the ground beneath them.

Elias watched the boy with a mixture of awe and dread. In the first phase, Kaelen had been a valve, a passive channel that allowed the Blight to flow through him with minimal resistance. He had been listening. Now, he was pulling. The distinction was subtle, but the cost was immense. The energy was no longer just passing through; it was being drawn out of the boy’s own reserves to facilitate the transfer.

"He’s holding," Torin murmured, more to himself than to Elias. But his eyes were sharp, scanning Kaelen’s posture for any sign of failure. "He’s stable. For now."

"For now," Elias echoed. He looked at the barrier. The dark layer was gone. The dome was clear, shimmering with a faint, residual glow. The Blight was in the ground, moving west, following the channels that the map had shown. But the source—the massive entity, the true heart of the corruption—was still active. The barrier had drained the immediate reservoir, but the well had not dried up.

And Kaelen was still the pump.

If they stopped the flow now, the seal would reset. The channel would close. The Blight that had already been pulled into the system would be trapped, potentially causing a backup that could shatter the seal from the inside. But if they kept the flow going, Kaelen would continue to drain himself.

"How much time?" Elias asked.

Torin looked at Kaelen, then at the stone. "The stone is still resonating. The seal is stable. But Kaelen..." He gestured to the boy’s trembling hands. "He’s losing the fight against the cold. The magic is eating him from the inside out."

Elias looked at Kaelen. The boy’s skin was beginning to crack, tiny fissures appearing along his forearms, leaking a faint, greenish mist. It was not blood. It was essence. The magic that fueled the Blight was the same magic that fueled Kaelen, and in the act of purging the Blight, Kaelen was sacrificing his own vitality to maintain the connection.

"He can hold for a short time," Torin said. "Maybe a minute. Maybe two. But he cannot hold for an hour. He cannot hold for days."

Elias looked at the barrier, then at the map in his mind. The channels went west, toward the forest, toward the keeper’s domain. The Blight was moving. It was no longer a static threat in the temple; it was a moving tide. And they had only just begun to drain it.

"The source is still active," Elias said. "If we stop, it will try to push back. If we keep going, Kaelen dies."

"Then we find a third option," Torin said.

Elias looked at Kaelen. The boy’s head had dropped slightly, his chin touching his chest. His eyes were closed. He was no longer pulling. He was fading.

"No," Torin said, his voice hard. "He’s not fading. He’s sleeping. The magic is putting him to sleep to protect his mind. It’s a mercy."

Elias looked at the barrier. It was silent. The air was still. The Blight was gone from the dome, but it was in the earth. And the earth was shaking.

"We have to move the flow," Elias said. "We can’t keep pulling through Kaelen. We have to let the barrier do the work."

Torin looked at him. "How?"

Elias looked at the quartz stone. The chisel was still pressed against it, vibrating with a low, heavy pulse. The resonance was still there, but it was weakening.

"We have to change the frequency," Elias said. "We have to make the barrier push instead of pull."

Torin looked at Kaelen. The boy was still, his chest rising and falling in slow, shallow breaths. He was alive. He was sleeping. And the barrier was stable.

For now.

Elias looked at the ground. The Blight was moving. It was heading west. And the forest was waiting.

"We move," Elias said. "Now."
