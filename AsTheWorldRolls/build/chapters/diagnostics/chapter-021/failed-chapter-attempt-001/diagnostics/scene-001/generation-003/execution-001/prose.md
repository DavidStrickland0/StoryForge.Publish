The path back to the tavern was no longer steep, though the forest floor remained treacherous. Roots that had seemed innocuous in the moonless dark now curled like grasping fingers beneath the boots of Elias and Kaelen. The air smelled of wet loam and pine sap, a stark contrast to the copper and rot that had hung over the city for weeks. Even here, at the edge of the trees where the Blight’s influence should have been strongest, the fog remained lifted, revealing a pale, overcast sky.

Elias adjusted the grip on his chisel, keeping it low in his hand. He carried the quartz stone he had retrieved from the outcrop. It felt cold and dead against his palm, inert after the brief hum of resonance that had filled the ridge. The successful demonstration was settled history; Kaelen had stabilized the frequency, acting as a bridge rather than a victim. But success did not erase the mechanics of their survival, particularly when it came to Maren.

As they moved through the tree line and back onto the main track toward the way station, Elias noticed Maren faltering. She was walking beside Elara, who had been holding her lantern steady. Maren reached for a loose clod of dirt along the trail edge, intending to clear it from a boot print, but her hand jerked before she could grasp the stone. The small object skittered across the mossy ground, landing near a fallen branch.

Maren stopped immediately, watching the dirt stop rolling in the wind. Her breath came out too fast, though the night was cool. She pressed both hands to her stomach, her knuckles white against the pale leather of her tunic. The tremor had returned with a vengeance, a sudden spasm that rippled through her arm and into her chest.

"It's nothing," she said, but her voice lacked conviction. "Just the exertion. I'll be fine."

"Torin is watching Kaelen," Elias said quietly, keeping his eyes on the path ahead. He didn't turn around to see her face. It was not about pity; it was about logistics. "But you are not ready for the next step."

"We need the chisel work," Elara interrupted gently, though she kept her lantern low enough to cast only a pool of light on the ground between them. She did not look at Maren with disdain, nor did she offer empty reassurance. Her tone was factual, like a surveyor marking a boundary line. "Maren, you know your limits. The Guild requires certification for health inspections because we need to be sure who can handle which tools and why."

"I know," Maren replied, her voice rising in pitch. She shook her head, trying to force the tremor out of her grip, but it only seemed to make the shaking worse. "It is just the fatigue. The salve is working on the outside layers, but... I am not strong enough for the chisel yet."

Elias nodded as if she had spoken a law of physics. "Good. Then you are done with that task until your hands are steady again."

He turned slightly to face them. The lantern light caught the shadow of his leather armor, showing the scars and wear of his travels more clearly than he expected. He held the quartz stone loosely between two fingers, letting it hang at his side.

"The ridge test confirmed Kaelen's ability to act as a sensor," Elias said, his voice steady despite the ache in his own side. "He can regulate the resonance. He can hold the frequency without collapsing. That was the goal."

"It is done," Elara confirmed. "But the next phase is different. We have to use the chisel on the actual seal at the Gate of Silence tomorrow, or whatever we find there when we get to the western way station. If the tremors prevent Maren from doing the calibration, then someone else must."

"And who is that?" Torin asked from behind them. His voice was low, barely audible over the rustle of wind through the trees. He had been walking silently beside Kaelen, keeping a hand near the young man’s shoulder in case he needed to intervene again. "I am not a wizard."

"You do not need to be," Elara said. She paused, glancing at Maren, then back at Torin. Her expression was thoughtful, but she did not offer an explanation for the change in protocol unless it was asked for. "Elara has been chiseling stone since she was ten years old. I have watched her work on the foundations of the temple shrines and the drainage seals. Her hands are steady. She understands the weight of the hammer, the angle of the chisel, and the rhythm required to cut without breaking."

Maren stepped back from Elara’s side, clutching her tunic with a grimace that did not reach her eyes. She nodded once. "If she must do it, then so be it. I am tired. The salve is not enough to keep my hands steady for the work required."

Elias nodded again. There was no anger in his gesture, only acknowledgment of a fact they all shared. "Then we will talk about how that works when we get back to the tavern. For now, let us move faster."

The group continued walking. The dynamic had shifted without fanfare or argument. Maren had accepted the limitation on her own abilities and stepped back from the chisel role. Elara had taken it up with a quiet competence that did not require praise. Torin watched Kaelen more closely, his presence a silent reassurance rather than a burden. And Elias carried the quartz stone as if it were a weight he could feel in his bones, knowing that its silence now meant only that they were ready to use it again when the time came.

As they approached the light of the tavern, the air grew warmer and heavier with the scent of cooking oil and woodsmoke. The Pick and Axe was open, as usual for a frontier establishment. Patrons sat at tables inside, speaking in low voices about trade routes and market prices. The common room was clean, with polished wood benches and a hearth that crackled with a steady fire. It was not a place of luxury, but it was safe, and it was where they were meant to be.

Torin guided Kaelen toward the far corner of the room, away from the noise and the clatter of pots on the stove. Maren moved slowly behind them, heading for the back stairs that led to the sleeping quarters. Elara followed Elias into the main seating area, where the proprietor, Aldric, waited with a bottle of water and a stack of parchment sheets.

Elias took his seat at one of the long wooden tables, placing the quartz stone on the surface with a soft thud. The light from the lantern flickered across its flat face, catching no reflection. He did not speak to Elara right away; instead, he watched as she sat down opposite him and folded her arms across her chest.

"You are tired," she said. It was not a question. Her tone was neutral, but the way she spoke of his exhaustion betrayed a quiet concern that she tried to mask behind professional detachment. "Torin has been carrying Kaelen for hours."

"Kaelen is fine," Elias replied. "He can regulate the resonance now. That was the only thing that mattered."

Elara nodded once. "Yes. The test proved it." She looked toward Torin, who was already seated at a table with Maren and Kaelen, helping them settle into the tavern’s quiet corners. "But we cannot assume everything will be fine when we reach the next node. The barrier needs to consume the Blight source. That requires precise timing. If anyone is shaking or unstable, the seal could burst prematurely."

"And if it does not burst?" Elias asked.

"Then the flow slows," Elara said. "And the Blight pressure builds. We cannot let that happen." She paused for a moment, then leaned forward slightly. "Torin knows how to cut wood and stone better than most people. He has hands steady enough for basic tasks. But he is not a wizard. He does not know the frequencies. That is why I must take over."

Elias nodded again. "That makes sense. You have been doing this work since you were ten years old. You are better suited than anyone else I know."

"Thank you," Elara said, but she did not smile. She did not look pleased at the compliment. Her tone remained neutral, as if acknowledging a fact rather than receiving an award. "But we must also consider the cost of this. If Maren cannot use the chisel, then someone else must handle it. And if that someone is me, then I must be certain of my own stability."

"Of course," Elias said. "You are more qualified than anyone else."

Elara looked at him and nodded once more. Then she stood up and walked toward the stairs where Maren was heading. She stopped beside Torin’s table, where Kaelen sat with his head lowered, staring at the floor as if it were a puzzle he could not solve.

"Kaelen?" Elara said softly.
