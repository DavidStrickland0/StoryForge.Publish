The warmth of the tavern common room felt like a heavy blanket draped over their shoulders, contrasting sharply with the cold, inert quartz stone resting on the table between them. Elias sat with his back against the wall, watching Elara inspect the chisel she had taken from the pack. It was a simple tool of iron and wood, unadorned except for the care taken in its maintenance.

"Maren is right," Elias said, his voice low enough not to carry over the crackle of the hearth fire. "We can't risk using her hands tonight. The tremors are active, and I don't want to push her into something that might make them worse."

Torin, who had been holding Maren's left arm with a firm but gentle grip to keep it steady, nodded. "She admitted it herself. Better we know now than find out in the middle of the operation tomorrow."

Elara set the chisel down on the table and picked up a small piece of scrap wood she kept for testing her own dexterity. She moved with a quiet efficiency that belied the chaos often associated with the frontier life. "I can do it," she said, tapping the tip of her knife against the edge of the chisel. "My hands have held these since I was ten. The stone doesn't care about your history, Elias. It cares about rhythm and pressure."

Elias nodded slowly, accepting the practicality of the situation. "Then we proceed with you on the chisel. Kaelen, can you still hear the hum without the gauntlet glowing?"

Kaelen sat in the corner of the room, his eyes half-closed as he listened to the silence around him. The brass gauntlet on his wrist was dark and cool, yet a faint vibration seemed to travel up his arm and into his shoulders. He didn't need to see the barrier to know its state. "It's there," he murmured, his voice rough from exhaustion. "Not loud, but it's there. Like a heartbeat in my own chest."

"Good," Elias said. "That means you're still the sensor we need. Elara, demonstrate what you think the frequency should be. Tap the stone once. Just once."

Elara picked up the chisel and placed the tip against the quartz resting on the table. She didn't strike it with force; instead, she applied a light, rhythmic pressure, tapping it in a slow, steady cadence. The sound was barely audible—a soft, high-pitched ringing that seemed to hang in the air before fading away.

Kaelen's eyes opened slightly as he focused on the vibration. "It matches," he said. "The barrier is hungry for this specific frequency. It responds to it."

"That's what we need to confirm," Elias replied. "Elara, increase the pressure slightly. See if you can sustain the tone longer."

Elara adjusted her grip, applying more force to the chisel while maintaining the same rhythm. The sound grew louder, a clear, sustained note that seemed to resonate with the very walls of the tavern. Torin leaned forward, his hand hovering near Kaelen's shoulder in case he needed to intervene.

"It holds," Elara said, her voice steady despite the effort required to maintain the pressure. "It doesn't flicker like before. It's stable."

Elias watched the quartz stone intently, observing how the light seemed to shift slightly when struck with the correct frequency. "That's the key. We don't need brute force or a hammer to break the seal. We just need to keep this tone going until the barrier can consume the Blight on its own."

The group fell silent for a moment, letting the sound of the chisel against the stone fill the room. It was a strange kind of magic—one that relied on precision and patience rather than raw power. For the first time since leaving the forest, the air in the tavern felt less oppressive, as if the tension had been released from their shoulders.

Torin finally relaxed his grip on Maren's arm and stepped back. "You did good, Elara," he said, giving her a rare smile. "I'm glad we didn't stick with Maren after all."

Elara smiled back, though there was no arrogance in it. "She's got other talents. Healing, especially. Just not this kind of work tonight."

Maren shifted uncomfortably in her chair, wincing slightly as the movement sent a ripple through her left wrist. "I'll rest it," she said quietly. "Torin helped me get here, but I don't need his help much longer."

"Don't push yourself," Torin replied gently. "We'll manage without you doing that kind of work tonight. Just focus on keeping your strength up for the next day."

Elias stood up and walked over to the table where the quartz stone lay. He picked it up and held it in his palm, feeling its cold weight. "If Elara can sustain this tone, we're ready to move forward with the plan. We just need to make sure we don't lose our footing before dawn."

He glanced at Kaelen, who had closed his eyes again, now listening intently to the faint hum that seemed to emanate from within him. "You're still holding it, aren't you?"

Kaelen nodded without opening his eyes. "Just a little. It's not going anywhere tonight."

"Good," Elias said. "Then we rest here until morning. We'll review the route one more time and make sure everyone knows their role. Then we head out to the Gate of Silence before first light."

The group settled into the tavern's common room, ordering drinks and food while discussing what they'd learned on the ridge. The conversation flowed naturally now, filled with jokes about Elara's mechanical skill, Torin's protective instincts, and Maren's honest admission of weakness. It wasn't perfect, but it was real—and that was enough for tonight.

As the food arrived, the tavern's common room buzzed with activity, though the group remained focused on their conversation. The warmth of the fire and the soft hum of the chisel against the quartz stone provided a strange kind of comfort. They were no longer just surviving; they were preparing to fight back.

"Let's eat," Elias said, gesturing toward the table. "Then we'll rest."

Maren took a bite of her stew, chewing slowly as Torin watched her with concern. She forced a small smile and nodded. "I'm fine," she said. "Just need to keep moving my wrist gently."

Torin relaxed slightly, though he kept his hand near her arm in case she needed support. "That's all I ask," he replied. "Let me know if you need anything else."

Elara wiped the table clean with a rag she had brought along, her movements precise and deliberate. "We'll be ready by dawn," she said. "Just make sure we don't lose our footing before then."

Elias nodded, satisfied that everyone was on the same page. "Then let's rest. Tomorrow, we go to the Gate of Silence."

The group finished their meal in silence, the weight of what they had accomplished settling over them like a heavy cloak. They were no longer just survivors; they were ready to fight back. And as the tavern emptied around them, leaving only the faint hum of the quartz stone and the warmth of the fire, Elias knew that this was the beginning of something new.
