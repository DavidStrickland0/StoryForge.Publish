The path from the ridge outcrop down toward the Pick and Axe was not long, but it was steep enough to make every step count for Maren. The forest floor, usually slick with pine needles and damp loam, offered little purchase as she tried to maintain her usual rhythm. Her left hand clutched at the leather strap of her healer’s bag, knuckles whitening around the vial of amber salve she had packed hours ago.

She could feel the residual stiffness in her wrist, a cold iron grip that refused to loosen despite the passage of time since the last flare-up. It wasn’t painful—not anymore—but it was undeniable. When she tried to extend her fingers to check the pulse of a small stream they passed earlier, the joints locked, and the tremor returned with a sudden, violent jerk that forced her to drop her hand.

“Let’s not walk too fast,” Elias said, his voice low and even. He walked beside her, one hand resting lightly on the hilt of his blade, though he offered no assistance unless she asked for it. “We’ve got distance to cover before we reach the tavern.”

Maren nodded silently. She didn’t want to rely on them. She was a healer; she knew how to treat wounds, stabilize patients, and mend broken bones. But this tremor wasn’t a wound—it was something deeper, something that lived in her marrow and refused to leave even when the fever had broken. It made her feel like a child pretending to be an adult, like someone trying to hold together a house of cards with their bare hands.

They moved through the trees, the light from the tavern growing brighter as they approached the tree line. The smell of woodsmoke drifted up through the underbrush, mingling with the scent of wet earth and pine resin. Torin walked ahead, scanning the treeline for any sign of movement or disturbance. Elara followed close behind Elias and Maren, her eyes fixed on their surroundings. Kaelen brought up the rear, his gauntlet dark and silent, but his presence still felt like a weight in the air.

They reached the edge of the tavern’s clearing just as night began to settle fully over the forest. The building itself was modest—a single-story structure with wide doors that opened into a common room filled with patrons speaking in low voices about trade routes and market prices. Inside, the hearth crackled steadily, casting dancing shadows across polished wood benches and tables cluttered with empty tankards.

Torin led them inside, keeping an eye on the entrance as if expecting trouble from someone outside rather than within. Aldric, the proprietor, greeted them from behind the bar where he was polishing a glass. His expression remained neutral, professional, but there was no warmth in his greeting. He wasn’t known for friendliness, especially not to strangers who hadn’t paid their dues yet.

“Aldric,” Elias called out quietly. “We’re back.”

The man looked up, wiping his hands on a rag before turning fully toward them. His eyes scanned the group, lingering briefly on Maren’s face and then settling on her trembling hand as she shifted her grip on her bag strap.

“Back from the ridge?” Aldric asked, his tone flat but not unkind. “And did you find what you were looking for?”

Maren didn’t answer immediately. She stared at her own hands, watching them twitch slightly as they moved toward her belt pouch where she kept a few tools and supplies. Then she stopped herself, realizing that if she touched anything too delicate now, she might break it before even using it.

“I can’t do the chisel work,” she said finally, her voice steady despite the tightness in her chest. “Not like this.”

The room seemed to go quiet for a moment—not because everyone had stopped talking, but because the way Maren spoke made everyone else pause mid-sentence to listen. Torin glanced at her with concern, while Elias exchanged a look with Kaelen, who stood quietly near the door.

Elara stepped forward then, placing herself between Maren and the rest of the group. She wasn’t afraid of being seen as less capable; she simply knew that honesty was better than pretending.

“You’re right,” Elara said softly, her tone respectful but firm. “I’ll take the chisel.”

Maren blinked at her, surprised by the sudden shift in focus. “You?”

“I’ve been sharpening rocks since I was ten years old,” Elara replied, a small smile playing on her lips. “And yes, my hands are steady enough for this kind of thing. You should rest your wrist until you’re sure you can use it again.”

Maren hesitated. She wanted to argue, to insist that she could still do the work somehow, but something in Elara’s tone silenced any protest before it formed. There was no pity in her words, only clarity and acceptance.

Elias nodded slowly, then reached into his belt and pulled out a small iron case he carried everywhere. Inside were various tools: chisels, hammers, quartz stones, brushes, and other implements used for testing resonance frequencies. He handed one of the chisels to Elara without hesitation.

“Thank you,” she murmured, taking the tool carefully and examining it closely before setting it aside on a nearby table.

Torin moved closer to Maren now, offering her his arm as support. “We’ll figure something out,” he said quietly. “Just take your time.”

Maren nodded again, accepting his help without protest. She wasn’t proud anymore; she was honest about her limitations, and that felt lighter somehow. For once, she didn’t feel like a failure for needing help.

Kaelen stepped forward then, looking at the group with a mix of relief and uncertainty. “I can still sense the barrier’s hum,” he said quietly. “Even without the gauntlet glowing.”

“Good enough,” Elias replied. “You’ll act as sensor tonight. We’ll test the method tomorrow morning when we’re rested.”

Maren watched Kaelen nod, then looked back at Elara, who was already inspecting the chisel with practiced ease. There was no shame in admitting she couldn’t do something anymore; there was only pride in knowing someone else could step up and fill the gap without hesitation.

The group settled into the tavern’s common room, ordering drinks and food while discussing what they’d learned on the ridge. The conversation flowed naturally now, filled with jokes about Elara’s mechanical skill, Torin’s protective instincts, and Maren’s honest admission of weakness. It wasn’t perfect, but it was real—and that was enough for tonight.
