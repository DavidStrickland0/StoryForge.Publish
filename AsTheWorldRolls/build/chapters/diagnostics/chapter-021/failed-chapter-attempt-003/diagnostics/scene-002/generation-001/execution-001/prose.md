Elara’s blade settled against the quartz face with a soft, rhythmic tap, the sound carrying through the tavern air like a distant heartbeat. The stone beneath her fingers hummed—a low, resonant vibration that seemed to travel up her arms and settle in the marrow of her bones. She didn’t force the movement; instead, she listened to the feedback loop between her breath and the tool’s edge, adjusting her pressure until the quartz responded rather than resisted.

“Steady,” Elias murmured from across the table, his eyes fixed on Elara’s hands. “You’re listening for the right note. Don’t fight it.”

Elara nodded, her jaw tightening slightly as she maintained her grip. The chisel vibrated against the stone, not with chaotic impact but with a precise, controlled resonance. Kaelen sat beside Elias, his brass gauntlet resting limp on the table, its runes dull and inert. Yet he remained alert, his head tilted as if straining to hear something only he could perceive.

“Can you feel it?” Torin asked from the other side of the circle, leaning forward in his chair. “The hum under your skin? That’s the seal waking up.”

Kaelen nodded slowly, his voice rough but clear. “It’s like… a thread pulling through fog. I can guide it if Elara hits the right frequency.”

Elias raised his brow, watching both of them carefully. “That’s all you need to know. You’re not trying to pull the whole thing open at once. You’re just giving it space to breathe. The barrier will eat what comes through.”

Maren winced as her left arm twitched involuntarily, the stiffness in her shoulder flaring up with a sharp ache. She shifted slightly, wincing as the tremors returned in a wave of involuntary spasms. Her fingers curled into her palm, gripping the edge of the table too tightly.

“I know,” she said quietly, forcing herself to remain upright despite the pain radiating through her shoulder. “I just… I can’t do this anymore. Not with these hands.”

Elara paused mid-tap, lowering the chisel slightly as the quartz’s hum faded. She looked at Maren, then back at the stone. Her expression softened, though she kept her focus on the task at hand.

“It’s okay,” Elara said gently. “You’re not needed here right now. Rest is part of the plan.”

Maren blinked, startled by the sudden shift in tone. She looked down at her hands, then back up at Elias and Torin, who both gave her nods of support without saying a word. The group was silent for a moment, the only sound being the faint hum of Elara’s chisel as she resumed its steady rhythm against the quartz.

Kaelen leaned forward again, his eyes fixed on the stone. “I can hear it,” he said, his voice barely above a whisper. “It’s… like a song I’ve heard before. But deeper than that. It’s calling me.”

Elias nodded slowly, his expression unreadable but his tone calm. “Good. Keep listening. Don’t try to control it yet. Just let the resonance guide you. The barrier needs your help, not your strength.”

Torin crossed his arms and watched Kaelen with a mixture of concern and cautious approval. “You’re doing better than I thought,” he said quietly. “But remember—this isn’t about winning. It’s about survival. If things go wrong, we need to know you can stop yourself before you lose control.”

Kaelen didn’t answer immediately. He watched the quartz pulse with a faint glow, his own gauntlet resting motionless on the table. For a moment, he closed his eyes, letting the sensation of the resonance wash over him without resistance. When he opened them again, there was less panic in his gaze and more clarity.

“I can stop it,” he said finally. “I just need to… let go. That’s the part I’m still learning.”

Elias nodded slowly, a small nod that carried more weight than words could convey. “Then we’ll take it one step at a time. The dawn operation is hours away. Use this time to rest, eat, and prepare your minds for what comes next.”

Maren exhaled through her nose, the tension in her shoulders easing slightly as she accepted the role of observer rather than participant. She reached into her pack and pulled out a small vial of amber salve, uncorking it with trembling fingers before setting it aside. “I’ll keep watch,” she said softly. “If anyone needs me, I’m right here.”

Elara resumed her work, the chisel tapping against the quartz in a steady rhythm that seemed to sync with the quiet pulse of the tavern itself. The group sat around the table, each person absorbed in their own thoughts yet united by the shared weight of what lay ahead. Outside, the night air still carried traces of wet loam and pine sap, but inside the tavern, something had changed—something small but significant.

The resonance test wasn’t just a success; it was a confirmation that their plan could work. The barrier didn’t need brute force or magical overload—it needed precision, timing, and trust in each other’s abilities. Elara held the chisel steady as her breath synced with the quartz’s hum, while Kaelen listened to the resonance thread pulling through him like a faint melody.

Torin watched them both, his expression unreadable but his posture relaxed enough to suggest he felt safe for the moment. Elias kept one eye on Elara’s hands and another on Kaelen’s gauntlet, ready to intervene if either of them lost their focus.

Maren sat quietly, her trembling hands resting in her lap as she monitored the group without interfering. She wasn’t useless; she was part of something larger, and that realization seemed to settle over her like a warm blanket despite the ache in her shoulder.

The tavern lights flickered overhead, casting long shadows across the tables where patrons laughed and argued over games of dice and cards. But none of them noticed the subtle shift happening within this small group—the way the air itself seemed to vibrate with potential energy, the quiet understanding that passed between them without words, and the unspoken promise that they would face whatever came next together.

Outside, the black spires of the village loomed against the gray sky, silent witnesses to the work being done inside. The barrier was stable, but the entity in the spires remained hungry, patient, and far from defeated. And yet, for now, the group had something they hadn’t had before: a working plan, confirmed tools, and a renewed sense of purpose.

It wasn’t a battle anymore; it was a conversation between man and magic, between fear and faith, between what had been lost and what could still be saved.

The night stretched ahead of them, full of quiet moments and uncertain paths. But as Elara continued her work, tapping the quartz with practiced precision, the group found themselves moving forward—not because they had all their answers, but because they knew how to ask the right questions together.
