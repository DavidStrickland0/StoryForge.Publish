Elias adjusted his leather armor, the scratch of fabric against steel the only sound in the clearing save for the distant hoot of an owl. He held the quartz stone loosely in one hand, its surface cool and dead to the touch now that the resonance had faded from their work on the outcrop. The stone felt like a simple piece of glass compared to the humming void they had just navigated, but Elias knew it was still dangerous.

"We rest," he said again, though his voice carried less weight than before. The declaration hung heavy in the damp air, settling over them like a blanket. "We eat. And then we go."

No one argued. No one hesitated. They simply nodded, one by one, and began the slow walk back toward the light of the tavern, leaving the ridge outcrop and the silent stone behind them in the dark.

The path wound down through the forest, the trees looming overhead like sentinels that had seen too much. The air here was different from the courtyard; it smelled of wet loam and pine sap, a clean scent that did nothing to wash away the lingering taste of copper and rot from their memory. Maren walked with her head slightly bowed, her eyes fixed on the ground. Her left hand, which had trembled only moments before, remained stiff at her side, betraying the exhaustion that had seized her muscles after holding it steady for the duration of the test.

"Torin," Elias said quietly as they emerged from the tree line and caught sight of the tavern's lantern glow through the branches. "Make sure Kaelen eats."

"I will," Torin replied, his hand resting on the hilt of his blade. He glanced at Kaelen, who was walking a few paces behind them, his brass gauntlet dark and silent against his wrist. The young man looked pale, his breathing shallow but even. "He needs to keep his strength up."

Kaelen offered a faint smile, a ghost of a gesture that didn't quite reach his eyes. "I'm fine," he muttered, mostly to himself. "Just... listening to the wind."

"The wind doesn't taste like rot," Elias said, keeping his tone neutral. He walked beside Maren, watching her profile as she moved with a deliberate care around the uneven terrain. "Maren, can you handle the chisel tomorrow?"

Maren's shoulders tensed. She stopped walking for a moment, turning to face him. The lantern light caught the sharp line of her jaw and the slight stiffness in her neck. Her eyes were clear, but there was a fatigue in them that went deeper than physical exhaustion.

"I..." she started, then paused. She looked down at her left hand again, flexing her fingers slowly. They moved with a jerky precision that was painful to watch. "The tremor is gone," she said, her voice steady despite the effort it took to keep it so. "But the stiffness... if I have to hold the chisel for more than a few minutes, my hand will lock up before I can make the cut."

Elias nodded, accepting the limitation without question. He had seen it happen during their last rest stop; her hand had gone rigid after only thirty seconds of holding the tool steady. It wasn't fear or lack of skill. It was the toll of channeling that much energy through a body already worn down by the journey and the magic.

"Then Elara takes the chisel," Elias said, glancing toward the healer who walked slightly ahead. "She has the steady hands for it."

Maren looked at Elara, who had been walking silently in the rear, her expression unreadable in the dim light. The younger woman was a specialist in stone and metal, trained since childhood to work with precision that few others possessed. She understood the mechanics of resonance better than anyone else in their group, aside from Elias and Kaelen.

"I can do it," Elara said when Maren looked at her. Her voice was low but firm, lacking any uncertainty. "I've been chiseling stone since I was ten years old. My hands are built for the work."

Maren gave a single, sharp nod. "Then we will discuss the method of operation before dawn," she said, turning back to Elias. "If you need me to assist with anything else..."

"You'll be needed to monitor Kaelen," Elias replied. "He's not exactly stable yet."

Kaelen had been walking in silence, his gaze fixed on the forest floor. He stopped when they reached the tavern gate, leaning against the rough-hewn wood for support. His breathing was shallow, almost labored, and he looked like a man standing at the edge of something invisible and vast.

"I'm not listening to anything," Kaelen said, his voice hoarse from disuse. "I just... I feel tired."

"You're a cauterized wound," Maren said gently, stepping forward to check his pulse. Her fingers brushed his wrist, feeling the erratic throb of his heart beneath the skin. "You need rest and salve. Not heroics."

"I'm fine," Kaelen insisted, though he didn't pull away from her touch. "Just... listening to the wind."

"The wind doesn't taste like rot," Elias repeated, echoing his earlier words with a slight twist of humor that failed to fully lighten the mood.

Kaelen let out a short laugh that sounded more like a cough. "Maybe not this time. But I think it does when we're near the spires."

Elias nodded, shifting his weight from one boot to the other. The leather armor creaked softly as he moved. "We'll deal with the spires later," he said. "For now, we rest and recover. If you try to pull Blight again before dawn, I'll have Torin knock you out."

Kaelen nodded slowly, a faint grimace crossing his face. "Fair enough."

They entered the tavern together, the warm light of the hearth greeting them like an old friend. The common room was clean and polished, with wood floors that reflected the glow of the fire and shelves lined with jars of dried herbs and bottles of ale. Patrons sat at tables in the corner, whispering among themselves about the news from the frontier, but none of them were strangers to the group. They knew who they were, what they had done, and why they mattered.

Aldric stood behind the bar, wiping down a glass with a rag that looked as old as the tavern itself. He gave them a nod of acknowledgment, his expression serious but not unfriendly. "Welcome back," he said. "You look like you've seen a ghost."

"We have," Elias replied, stepping up to the bar. "But we're still standing."

"Good," Aldric said, pouring water into Maren's mug without asking. "Drink it all."

Maren took the cup and drank deeply, her stiff hand holding the ceramic firmly despite its weight. She set the mug down on the counter with a quiet clink. "Thank you."

Torin ordered ale for himself and Kaelen, while Elara sat at a table near the hearth, pulling out a small satchel from her pack. Inside were tools: chisels, hammers, calipers, and a roll of parchment marked with geometric patterns that looked more like diagrams than art.

"I'll start prepping the quartz," Elara said, her voice calm and focused. "We need to test the frequency again before dawn."

"We don't need to test it again," Elias said, though he knew she meant the ridge outcrop. He had seen what happened there; Kaelen had stabilized the resonance into an amber hum that vibrated through the stone without shattering it. That was enough proof. What they needed now was preparation for the actual operation at the shrine.

"We need to confirm the method," Elara corrected, her tone firm but not argumentative. "If Maren's hand is stiff, I'll take the chisel. But if we haven't tested the frequency in a controlled environment first..."

She trailed off, looking at Elias. He nodded once, understanding what she meant. They didn't need to prove anything to each other; they just needed to be sure of the mechanics before risking everything on a single strike.

Elias sat down at the bar, his back against the wood. The stone in his hand felt heavier than it should have been now that he wasn't holding it during a moment of crisis. He looked at Kaelen, who was sitting quietly across from him, staring into his ale like it held some secret meaning.

"You're listening to something," Elias said softly. "Tell me what you hear."

Kaelen shook his head slowly. "Nothing," he said. "Just... silence."

"That's not right," Elias replied. "You can't just listen to silence unless there's nothing else for you to hear."

"There is nothing else," Kaelen insisted, though his voice lacked conviction. "The barrier isn't hungry anymore. Not like it was before."

Elias studied him for a moment, then nodded. "Then we'll rest and recover until dawn," he said. "Tomorrow, we feed the barrier properly. And if you try to pull Blight again without permission, Torin will have to tie your wrists to the chair."

Kaelen chuckled softly, the sound faint but genuine. "I'd rather not risk that."

"No one wants to risk it," Elias said, raising his mug in a toast. "But we'll be ready when dawn comes."
