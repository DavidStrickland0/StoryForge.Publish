The air that hit their faces as they crossed the threshold was different. Inside the watchfort, the atmosphere had been heavy, stagnant, a closed loop of ozone and static that clung to the back of the throat. Out here, it was thin and sharp, smelling of damp pine needles and the wet rot of soil turned over by the Blight’s unseen fingers.

Elias Thorne stepped out first, his boots crunching on gravel that seemed too clean for a forest floor this deep in the season. He held the map tight against his chest, the parchment stiff and cold. The ink lines he had drawn were precise, confident, but the terrain itself offered no such certainty. The road they had followed to reach the watchfort—the slurry of mud and debris that had once been a path—lay dead ahead, obscured by a wall of dense undergrowth that had surged forward like a living tide.

"East is blocked," Elias said, his voice low, barely rising above the rustle of leaves in the wind. He didn't look back at them; he was already scanning the tree line for any sign of movement that shouldn't be there. "The roots have swallowed the cut. We can't cross the road."

Kaelen nodded, his eyes fixed on the path where the mud had once been. The dark veins in his skin pulsed faintly beneath the surface, a reminder of how little time they had before the land decided to reclaim them. He could feel the pull of the Blight, a subtle vibration in the soles of his boots, like standing next to a humming engine. It was gone now, mostly, but the memory of it remained in the air, a ghost in the machine.

"Then we go through," Torin said, stepping up beside Elias. His hand rested on the pommel of his sword, not drawn, but ready. "We find the drainage channels Elara mentioned. She’s right; the forest is built on old waterways. We just need to find the ones that haven't been clogged."

"Right," Elara said, stepping forward with her chisel and a small pouch of quartz in hand. She moved with a scholar’s precision, scanning the ground for signs of erosion or exposed stone. "The channels are there, but they're choked with debris. We'll need to clear them carefully. If we disturb too much earth at once, the land might shift again."

Maren was already moving ahead of Torin, her hands glowing faintly with the residual warmth from the salve she had applied earlier. She didn't speak; she simply walked, her movements deliberate and economical. Her skin still bore the marks of the last battle—the bruising, the cuts—but they were healing, slow and steady. She kept a hand near Kaelen’s shoulder, not touching him yet, just hovering in case he stumbled or needed support.

Kaelen followed Maren, his legs shaking slightly with every step. The journey from the watchfort had been short, but it felt like a marathon. Every footfall was an effort, every breath a reminder of how depleted he was. He could feel the weight of his gauntlet on his wrist, the metal warm and regulated now, but still heavy. It was a burden he didn't want to be, but he couldn't afford to let it go—not yet.

They moved into the tree line, the darkness thickening around them as the sun dipped below the horizon. The trees here were ancient, their bark twisted and gnarled, their branches interlocking overhead to form a canopy that blocked out most of the stars. The light from the hearth behind them faded quickly, leaving them in the dim glow of the moon and the faint violet pulse that still emanated from Kaelen’s chest.

"Keep your eyes open," Torin muttered, his voice tense. "We're not alone."

"I know," Elias replied, though he didn't look back at him. He was focused on the map, comparing the terrain they were traversing to the lines drawn on the parchment. The discrepancy was growing larger by the minute; the forest wasn't matching the map as it should have. "The scale is off by a mile. We're moving faster than the land allows us to."

"Then we move slower," Kaelen said, his voice weak but steady. He stopped, leaning against a tree trunk for support. His chest heaved with the effort of breathing. "We don't push through. We listen."

The group paused, forming a loose line as they walked. Elara was at the front, scanning the ground for signs of movement or disturbance. Maren was beside Kaelen, her hand hovering near his shoulder again, ready to offer support if needed. Torin and Elias brought up the rear, their weapons drawn and ready.

The forest here was alive in a way that made the group uneasy. The trees seemed to shift subtly as they passed, their branches curling inward or swaying in a wind that couldn't be felt. It wasn't just the environment; it was the land itself reacting to their presence, testing them, waiting to see if they would break under the pressure.

"We're close," Elara whispered, her voice barely audible over the rustle of leaves. "There's a stone-lined ditch ahead. It's dry, but it's wide enough for us to cross."

They pressed on, the path narrowing as the trees closed in around them. The air grew colder, and the silence was heavier, pressing down on them like a physical weight. Kaelen could feel the pull of the Blight growing stronger, a subtle vibration in his bones that made it hard to focus. He could hear it now, the distant hum of something vast and ancient stirring beneath the earth, waiting for an opportunity to break free.

"We're going to need to move fast," Elias said, his voice tight. "The land is waking up."

"Then we move together," Kaelen replied, his grip on Maren's hand tightening slightly. "We don't fight it. We move with it."

They stepped forward, the forest swallowing them whole as they crossed the threshold from the safety of the watchfort into the unknown. The road ahead was uncertain, but for now, they had the time and the strength to take it.
