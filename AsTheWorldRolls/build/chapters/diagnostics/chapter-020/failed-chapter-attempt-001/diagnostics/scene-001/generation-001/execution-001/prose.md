The water pitcher sat on the table, its surface beaded with condensation in the cool air of the common room. Elias took another sip, the liquid cold and clean against his throat. It was a small mercy, this glass of water, but after weeks of tasting ash and the metallic tang of the Blight, it felt like drinking from a spring.

Torin leaned back in his chair, the wood creaking under his weight. He stretched his arms above his head, a long, slow motion that popped several vertebrae. He looked at Maren, who was meticulously wiping a smudge of mud from her sleeve with a cloth she had produced from her tunic. Her hands were steady, professional, but her eyes were tired.

Kaelen sat in the corner of the table, his posture hunched. He stared at the table’s grain, his eyes unfocused. The brass gauntlet on his wrist was silent, cold, and inert. He did not blink often. He did not speak. He simply existed in the space between the other three, a quiet monument to the effort it had taken to get them here.

Aldric returned from the back, carrying a tray of plates. The smell of stew and roasted root vegetables drifted forward, a warm, savory comfort that cut through the lingering scent of damp wool and old stone. He placed the food before them with a heavy thud.

"Eat," he said, his voice flat. "Then we talk."

Elias picked up his spoon. The stew was thick, hearty, and hot. He ate in silence, the flavors grounding him. Torin ate with the enthusiasm of a man who had not seen a hot meal in days. Maren ate slowly, her eyes scanning the room, taking in the faces of the other patrons. They were watching them. Not with fear, but with a mix of curiosity and caution. They were adventurers, after all. And adventurers brought news.

When the plates were empty, Aldric cleared them away. He returned with a small inkwell and a quill. He placed the liability waiver on the table, the paper crisp and white against the dark wood.

"Sign here," he said, pointing to the line at the bottom. "Here for the dates. And here for the acknowledgment of the escort contract."

Elias took the quill. His hand was steady. He signed his name, the ink flowing smoothly. He passed the quill to Torin, who signed with a flourish. Maren signed with a precise, looping hand. Kaelen did not move.

Elias looked at Kaelen. The young man was staring at a point on the table, his lips moving slightly, as if he were counting something.

"Kaelen," Elias said softly. "Sign the paper."

Kaelen blinked. His eyes focused, briefly, on the quill. He reached out, his fingers trembling slightly, and took the quill. He signed his name, the letters shaky but legible. He placed the quill down and leaned back, his breathing shallow.

Aldric took the waiver, checked the signatures, and tucked it into his ledger. "Good," he said. "Now, about the caravan."

He pulled a map from his pocket, spreading it on the table. It was a simple sketch of the western road, marked with a few key points: the Pick and Axe, a series of river crossings, and a large circle labeled "Capital."

"The caravan leaves at dawn," Aldric said. "It’s a merchant convoy, heavily laden. Iron, timber, grain. The capital is short on supplies, and the Blight has disrupted the eastern trade routes. This is the only way in."

He looked at them. "I need a security detail. Five people. You’re here. I know what you can do. I know what you’ve survived. I don’t want to ask, but I’m asking."

Elias looked at the map. The road was long, and the terrain was rough. But it was also a chance to move, to see the world beyond the watchfort, to find out if the rest of the world was still turning.

"We’re in," Elias said.

Aldric nodded. "Good. You’ll need supplies. I have a list of what’s in the storeroom. Take what you need. But be fair. There are other travelers who need to eat, too."

He turned and walked away, leaving them alone with the map and the weight of the decision they had just made.

Torin rolled his shoulders. "Well," he said. "That was less boring than I expected."

Maren picked up the map, tracing the route with her finger. "The river crossings are the danger," she said. "The water is low, but it’s fast. And the Blight… it’s not just in the city. It’s in the water, too."

Elias looked at the map. The lines were thin, delicate. They looked like veins.

"We need to test our gear," Elias said. "Specifically, the resonance method. We haven’t used it in the field since the hydraulic throat, and that was… difficult."

He looked at Maren. "You know the quartz stone. You know the chisel. We need to make sure we can activate it quickly, cleanly. No mistakes."

Maren nodded. She pulled a small pouch from her tunic. Inside was the quartz stone, cold and inert. Next to it was the chisel, its edge sharp and gleaming.

"I’ll set up a test," she said. "We need a clear surface. Something hard. Stone or metal."

She looked around the room. The tables were wood. The floor was stone, but it was uneven, cracked. Not a good surface.

"The counter," she said. "The stone counter behind the bar. It’s smooth. Flat."

She stood up, her movements precise. She took the quartz stone and the chisel, and walked to the bar. Aldric was wiping down the counter, his movements rhythmic, practiced.

He looked up as she approached. "Yes?"

"I need to use your counter," she said. "For a moment."

Aldric looked at the quartz stone in her hand. His eyes narrowed. He knew what it was. He knew what they had done.

"Go ahead," he said. "But be careful. If you scratch the stone, you’re paying for it."

Maren placed the quartz stone on the counter. It sat there, cold and still. She picked up the chisel. She held it in her right hand, the way she had been taught. The way Elias had taught her.

She began to tap the stone.

The first tap was soft. A small click. The quartz stone did not hum. It did not vibrate. It sat there, inert.

Maren frowned. She tried again. A harder tap. A sharper click. Still nothing.

She tried a third time. Her hand trembled. The chisel slipped. It hit the stone at the wrong angle, and a small chip flew off, landing on the floor with a tiny clatter.

Maren stopped. Her hand was shaking. She looked at the chisel, then at the quartz stone. She set the chisel down.

"I can’t," she said. Her voice was quiet. "My hands… they won’t steady."

Elias walked over. He looked at the counter, at the quartz stone, at Maren’s trembling hand. He understood. The stress of the last few weeks, the lack of sleep, the physical toll of the Blight, it had all taken its toll. Her hands were no longer steady enough for the precise work required.

"It’s okay," he said. "We need someone else."

He looked at Torin. Torin shook his head. His hands were strong, but they were not precise. He was a warrior, not a craftsman.

He looked at Kaelen. Kaelen was still staring at the table, his eyes unfocused. He was not in a state to perform any kind of precise work.

Elias looked around the room. The other patrons were watching. They were traders, miners, adventurers. They were practical people. They knew what they could do.

He needed a third person. Someone with a steady hand. Someone who could learn the resonance method quickly.

He looked at the bar. Aldric was still wiping down the counter. His hands were steady. He was focused. He was precise.

But Aldric was the proprietor. He was not part of the party. He was not part of the solution.

Elias looked at Maren. She was watching him, her face pale. She knew what was happening. She knew that she had failed.

"I’m sorry," she whispered.

Elias put a hand on her shoulder. "Don’t be. We’ll find a way."

He looked at the map on the table. The route to the capital. The caravan. The danger.

He needed to solve this problem before dawn. He needed to find someone who could do what Maren could not.

He looked around the room again. The patrons. The traders. The miners.

One of them had to have a steady hand. One of them had to be willing to help.

Elias walked back to the table. He picked up the map. He traced the route with his finger.

"We need to move," he said. "We need to be ready."

He looked at Maren. "Rest. You’ve done enough."
