The food arrived before the silence did.

Two clay bowls of thick stew, ladled from a pot that seemed to have been simmering since the dawn. The steam rose in gentle, aromatic plumes, carrying the scent of root vegetables, cured meat, and herbs Elias couldn’t name. Beside each bowl sat a heel of dark bread, dense and crusty, and a small clay cup of something amber and sharp.

Elias pushed his glass aside. The water had served its purpose, flushing the taste of travel dust and iron from his mouth. Now, his hand moved to the bread. He tore a piece, the crust flaking slightly, and dipped it into the stew. The heat of the broth warmed the inside of his fingers, a pleasant, grounding sensation that contrasted with the lingering chill of the forest air clinging to his skin.

Around him, the common room of the Pick and Axe hummed with a low, steady rhythm. It was the sound of normalcy, a stark and welcome departure from the jagged, screaming silences of the Blight’s domain. Men in leather aprons argued over dice rolls. A woman with a child on her hip laughed at something a broad-shouldered miner had whispered. The noise was not chaotic; it was a tapestry of overlapping voices, clinking pottery, and the scratch of quills on parchment, weaving together into a fabric of safe, mundane existence.

Torin ate with the mechanical efficiency of a man who had learned that hunger was a distraction best eliminated quickly. He did not speak, but his eyes remained open, scanning the room, cataloging exits, assessing the patrons. Kaelen did not touch his food.

Aldric approached the table again. He held a sheaf of papers, his ledger tucked under his arm. His expression was neutral, the bureaucratic mask firmly in place, though there was a slight softening of the lines around his eyes that had not been present during the initial negotiation.
