The air outside was not merely cold; it was heavy, laden with the scent of wet rot and ozone that clung to the back of the throat like a second skin. They stepped from the warmth of the watchfort’s stone walls into the forest’s embrace, and the difference was immediate and jarring. Inside, the barrier had hummed a steady, if uneasy, lullaby. Out here, the silence was not peaceful; it was waiting.

Kaelen walked beside Maren, his footsteps light but deliberate. He tried to ignore the way his own body felt like a borrowed vessel—legs that trembled whenever he stopped, skin that prickled with phantom sensations of cold fire. Every step required concentration, a conscious effort to keep his mind anchored in the present rather than drifting toward the pull of the Blight or the dark memories that seemed to lie just beneath the surface of his thoughts.

Maren kept one hand on Kaelen’s arm, her touch firm but gentle. She moved with a practiced economy, her eyes scanning the undergrowth for any sign of disturbance. The salve on her hands had soothed the raw burns from holding him down, but the exhaustion etched into her features remained. She had given everything to stabilize him, and now she carried the weight of that sacrifice in every stride.

Elias followed a half-step behind them, clutching his map tightly against his chest. The parchment was damp from the forest’s mist, the ink running slightly at the edges. He kept glancing up at the canopy, where shadows seemed to shift independently of any wind. His breath came in short bursts, though he tried to appear composed. The ridge they sought was only a mile away, but the terrain ahead looked less like a path and more like a barrier itself.

Torin took point, sword drawn and ready, though he made no attempt to move too quickly. He kept his head low, scanning the perimeter for any sign of movement—any hint that the forest might be shifting again. The trees here were tall and ancient, their bark twisted and scarred by decades of storms and magical interference. Their branches interlaced overhead, forming a canopy so thick that only slivers of moonlight filtered through, casting long, jagged shadows across the trail.

Elara brought up the rear, her satchel slung over one shoulder as she studied the ground. Her chisel hung at her hip, unused so far, but she kept her fingers lightly curled around the handle, ready to pry away debris if needed. She had been quiet since they left the watchfort, her gaze fixed on the forest floor as if looking for clues in the mud and leaves.

As they moved forward, the path began to narrow. The trees seemed to lean inward, their roots treading over one another like grasping fingers trying to close a wound. The trail had once been a rough track worn by travelers and beasts, but now it was obscured by undergrowth that looked almost deliberate in its obstruction.

Elias stopped abruptly, raising a hand to halt the group. “This isn’t right,” he muttered, unfolding his map again despite the dampness. “The ridge is supposed to be straight ahead, but the path has turned south.”

Kaelen paused, his heart skipping as he caught the shift in Elias’s voice. The older man’s tone was flat, almost resigned, but there was an undercurrent of urgency beneath it. Kaelen looked around, trying to make sense of what he saw. The trees here seemed to bend toward each other, their trunks leaning at odd angles as if they had grown together over time rather than standing in rows.

Maren stepped forward, her eyes narrowing as she scanned the area. “Is this… intentional?” she asked softly.

Elias shook his head, pointing to a patch of moss that seemed unnaturally bright green against the surrounding gray. “No moss grows in this light unless something is feeding it. Something’s been here. Or something *is* here.”

Torin stepped forward, his sword still drawn. “Then we go around,” he said decisively. “We don’t wait for the forest to let us pass.”

Elias hesitated. “If we force our way through, we risk triggering whatever caused this blockage. We need a better plan.”

Kaelen felt the pull in his chest again, a faint vibration that echoed Maren’s earlier sensation when she had touched him. It was subtle, almost imperceptible, but it told him something was wrong. He glanced at Elias, then at Torin, and finally at Maren, whose eyes were fixed on the forest’s edge.

“We don’t force our way,” Kaelen said quietly. “We listen.”

Elias nodded slowly. “Then show us how.”

Kaelen closed his eyes for a moment, focusing on the resonance that had stabilized the barrier earlier that night. It was faint now, barely more than a whisper beneath the forest’s oppressive silence, but it was there. He could feel the land’s pulse through his boots, a rhythmic thrumming that seemed to match his own heartbeat.

He opened his eyes and gestured for the group to follow him. “Walk with me,” he said softly. “Feel the ground beneath your feet.”

They moved forward again, this time slower, more deliberately. Kaelen closed his eyes once more, letting the resonance guide them. As they walked, the forest seemed to respond, the trees parting just enough to let them pass without striking against each other. The path ahead opened up slightly, revealing a narrow passage that wound through the undergrowth like a vein in flesh.

Torin stayed alert, his sword ready but relaxed enough to suggest trust in their progress. Elara brought up the rear, watching for any sign of trouble from behind.

The journey felt surreal, as if they were walking through a dream where the rules of reality had been twisted beyond recognition. The forest seemed alive, breathing around them, reacting to their presence with an intelligence that went beyond mere instinct.

As they neared the center of the blockage, Kaelen stopped again, turning to face Elias and Torin. “We’re going through,” he said firmly. “But we go gently.”

Elias studied the map one more time before nodding. “Agreed.”

Torin lowered his sword slightly, though his stance remained cautious. “Just don’t get us killed trying to be polite.”
