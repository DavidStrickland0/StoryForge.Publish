The air outside the watchfort tasted different than the stale, recycled oxygen of the stone room. It was thinner, colder, and carried the metallic tang of ozone that always lingered when the Blight’s seal leaked. Elias Thorne stepped across the threshold first, his boots crunching on frost-dusted earth before he paused to look back at the pulsing barrier.

“Hold,” he said, his voice low against the sudden wind. “The ridge is closer than the map suggests. Look at that.”

Torin moved to stand behind Maren, his hand resting near the hilt of his sword, though there was no enemy in sight. The forest edge loomed beyond him—a wall of twisted silhouettes where the trees seemed to lean inward, as if they were listening for a command that would never come. But Elias wasn’t pointing at the trees. He was pointing at the ground beneath their feet.

“See that line?” he gestured with a gloved hand toward a patch of dark soil where the frost had failed to settle properly. “That’s not frost. That’s the land remembering a different shape.”

Kaelen stepped forward, his legs shaking only slightly now that he was moving. The cold radiated from him in waves, making the air around his boots frost over before he even touched it. He didn’t need to speak; Maren could feel the tension in his posture, the way his eyes scanned the treeline with a predator’s patience.

“Show me,” she said softly, her voice barely rising above the wind.

Elias nodded, pulling a rolled parchment from his belt. The map was no longer just paper; it was a living document, its ink shifting as he traced a route that didn’t match the actual terrain. “The slurry road ends here. Beyond this point, the earth has turned to mud again, but there’s a dry channel—ancient drainage cut into the bedrock before the Blight woke. We need to follow it.”

Kaelen held out his gauntlet, the amber glow dimming to a soft pulse. “I can feel the resonance of that channel. It’s quiet beneath this noise.”

“Quiet?” Elias frowned, squinting at the map. “The forest is screaming right now. The trees are… moving, aren’t they? Look.”

Torin had indeed been right. As they stepped into the clearing, the ground beneath them shuddered, and a low groan echoed through the trees like a distant voice. One of the saplings nearby had bent almost horizontally, its roots visibly shifting in the frozen soil before snapping back upright with a sound like a breaking bone.

“Sentience,” Elias muttered, glancing at his map again. “Or something worse.”

Maren stepped beside Kaelen, her fingers brushing his arm as she walked. Her hands were still red and raw, but she refused to let go of him. “We follow the channel,” she said, her voice steady despite the tremor in her own legs. “Kaelen can feel it.”

“Can he?” Elias asked, not looking at her. He was watching the treeline, where shadows seemed to detach themselves from the trunks and drift toward them like smoke. “Because if that thing starts shifting again, we’ll be walking straight into its stomach.”

“Then we walk faster,” Torin replied, his sword drawn now, though the point of it remained sheathed. He wasn’t afraid; he was waiting for a cue. The forest had given him one.

They moved forward, their footsteps leaving faint impressions in the frost that vanished as soon as they left them behind. The channel Elias described was indeed dry—a narrow trench carved into the bedrock, lined with black stones that seemed to absorb the light rather than reflect it. But it wasn’t empty. As they approached it, the air grew heavy with a low hum, the same frequency that had pulsed from Kaelen’s gauntlet earlier.

Kaelen stopped, his eyes narrowing as he placed a hand on the stone wall of the channel. The frost around his fingers melted instantly, not from heat but from the sheer force of the resonance bleeding into the ground. “It’s awake,” he said, his voice rough from disuse. “And it’s hungry.”

Elias cursed under his breath, pulling the map closer to his chest. “Hungry for what?”

“For us,” Kaelen answered, his grip tightening on the stone. “For any sign of life. For any pulse that could be confused with our own.”

Maren stepped between them, her hands glowing faintly as she activated a second layer of warmth. “We don’t give it anything to eat,” she said, her voice sharp. “We keep moving.”

The forest responded by shifting again. This time, the trees didn’t just groan; they *moved*, their branches twisting and snapping with unnatural speed, creating a wall of foliage that blocked their path forward. The channel had been covered, not by debris but by living wood that seemed to grow in real time, as if the forest itself was trying to push them out.

“Clear it,” Elias ordered, though his voice lacked conviction. “Maren, use your warmth. Torin, hack at the roots.”

Torin nodded, stepping forward with his sword drawn. But before he could swing, Kaelen raised a hand, and the resonance in his gauntlet flared brighter. The frost on the ground began to melt again, not into water but into a thick, viscous liquid that pooled around their boots.

“It’s not just cold,” Kaelen said, his voice strained. “It’s… it’s trying to pull us back. To keep us in the dark.”

Maren gasped as her hands began to tremble again, the warmth fading faster than she could regenerate it. “Kaelen, stop! You’re overloading yourself!”

“I’m not stopping,” he replied, his voice steady despite the strain on his face. “I’m showing them we’re not afraid. We’re moving forward.”

The trees responded by pressing closer, their branches curling inward like fingers trying to grasp at something that wasn’t there. The ground beneath them shook, and the frost cracked with a sound like thunder.

Elias stepped back, his map slipping from his hand as he grabbed Torin’s arm. “We need to go around!”

“Where?” Maren shouted, her voice cracking under the pressure of the forest’s resistance. “There’s no way around! It’s all connected!”

Kaelen closed his eyes, letting the resonance flow through him again. The frost on the ground began to recede, revealing a dark, wet surface beneath that smelled of decay and old blood. “It’s not trying to stop us,” he said, his voice quiet but firm. “It’s trying to tell us something.”

“Tell us what?” Elias demanded, though he was already backing away from the edge of the channel.

“That we’re not alone,” Kaelen answered, opening his eyes again. “And that we’re not the first ones to walk this path.”

The forest seemed to pause for a moment, the trees still and silent as if listening to something far beyond their reach. Then, with a sudden rush, the foliage shifted aside, revealing a narrow passage that led deeper into the woods. The air within it was warmer, almost too warm, and carried the scent of pine needles and damp earth.

“Go,” Kaelen said, his voice barely audible over the wind. “Before it changes its mind again.”

They moved forward, their footsteps leaving faint impressions in the frost that vanished as soon as they left them behind. The channel stretched out before them, a dark ribbon winding through the frozen landscape, leading deeper into the unknown.
