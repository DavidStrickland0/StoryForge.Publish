The spongy path ahead dissolved under their boots, not crumbling into mud but shifting laterally as if the ground itself were breathing. The corridor of light Elias had been using as a map anchor twisted, the edges of the trees knitting together to form a wall on their left and opening up a dark fissure on their right.

"You stop," Elias commanded, his voice cutting through the low hum of the forest's discomfort. He didn't wait for confirmation; he stepped forward, placing himself between Kaelen and the shifting gap, using his own weight as an anchor against the slide. "Torin, hold the line. Elara, scan."

"Scanning what?" Elara asked, though her hands were already moving. She pulled the quartz stone free from her tunic, holding it aloft with two fingers. The stone pulsed with a soft, rhythmic blue light, matching the beat of Kaelen's chest despite his injury. "The forest is rewriting its geometry again. I can't distinguish 'safe' from 'dead' anymore."

"Safe means where the trees are dense," Elias said, watching the ground beneath him as two massive roots crossed paths like giant fingers snapping together. "Dead means where they thin out and let us fall into the dark."

Torin didn't argue. He dropped his pack straps, freeing his hands to grip a thick vine that had just begun to sway toward them. The vine was alive, pulsing with the same violet haze that clung to the air, but Torin's boots were planted firmly, digging into the slurry until he found purchase. "I'm not letting anything move us out of this line without me knowing about it."

The forest didn't care about their knowledge. It cared about intent. As soon as Torin stepped aside to let Elias pass, the roots surged forward, trying to reclaim the space Torin had just vacated. Elias grabbed a handful of moss-covered bark and pulled, the vine resisting with surprising strength. He wasn't fighting an enemy; he was fighting the terrain's desire to be somewhere else entirely.

"Kaelen," Elias said, not looking back. "Your gauntlet."

Kaelen looked down at his left arm. The metal band around his wrist had heated up, radiating a sharp, unpleasant heat that seeped through his leather glove. Inside the casing, the forest's energy swirled in response to his presence, creating a feedback loop of pain and information. His skin felt tight, stretched over bone as if he were a puppet made of dry parchment. The dark veins under his arms had faded slightly, but the gauntlet glowed brighter now, illuminating his face with an eerie, sickly light.

"It's trying to tell me something," Kaelen murmured, his voice rough. He flexed his fingers, and a spike of agony shot up his arm, forcing him to grit his teeth. "It says... the gap is closing."

"Then we move through it before it closes," Elias said, yanking the vine free from its entanglement. He didn't stop there; he kept pulling, dragging himself forward while Torin held the line and Elara watched the stone glow. "But we don't go straight. We go diagonal. Toward the ridge."

Elara nodded, her eyes fixed on the quartz. The blue light was fading, replaced by a flickering violet that matched the forest's mood. "The resonance is unstable," she warned. "If we move too fast, we trigger another shift. If we move too slow, we lose the path entirely."

"Then we move with precision," Elias said. He released the vine and stepped over a root that had just snapped back into place like a spring. The ground shifted beneath him, but he didn't slip; he planted his boots carefully, finding the new footing before it vanished. "Torin, watch your step. Elara, keep telling us where the safe spots are. Maren, watch Kaelen."

Maren stepped forward, her hands hovering over Kaelen's shoulders. She could feel the heat of the gauntlet radiating through his clothes, and she could also feel the tremor in his limbs as he struggled to maintain balance. "He's shaking," she whispered, more to herself than to anyone else.

"He's fighting the feedback," Elias corrected. "Don't let him freeze. Keep him moving."

"Understood," Maren said, her voice steady despite the tension in her chest. She reached out and gripped Kaelen's arm, using her own strength to counteract the pull of the gauntlet and the forest's pressure. "Hold on to me, Kaelen. Don't let go unless I tell you to."

Kaelen nodded, his eyes closed for a moment as he recalibrated his balance. He could feel Maren's warmth seeping into his bones, a small but vital counterweight to the cold that had taken over most of the forest. He opened his eyes again, focusing on Elias and Torin as they navigated the shifting path ahead.

"Ready," Elias said. "Let's go."

The group moved forward, their steps synchronized with the forest's rhythm. The ground shifted beneath them, but they adjusted in time, finding purchase before it vanished. Elara led the way with her quartz stone glowing brightly, illuminating the path ahead while also warning them of any shifts that might come. Torin held the rear, watching for any signs of movement that might indicate a threat or a trap. Maren kept Kaelen grounded, using her warmth and strength to keep him from collapsing under the pressure of the gauntlet and the forest's energy.

As they moved deeper into the forest, the trees seemed to part slightly ahead of them, creating a corridor of light that guided them toward the ridge. The air grew warmer, but it was a false warmth, a trick of the forest's magic that made them feel safe only to drop them back into the cold darkness when they weren't paying attention.

"Watch your step," Elara warned, her voice tense. "The path is narrowing."

They moved faster now, their movements precise and deliberate. The forest was testing them, trying to throw them off balance, but they were too focused on each other to make mistakes. They didn't need to know the forest's secrets; they just needed to trust each other and keep moving forward.
