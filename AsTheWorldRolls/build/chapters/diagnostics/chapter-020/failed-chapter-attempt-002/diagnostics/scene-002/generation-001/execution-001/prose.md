Elara stepped forward, her chisel raised like a tuning fork against the air itself. She didn't strike it with force; she pressed the cold quartz flat against the spongy root that had just erupted from the mud beneath them. The stone didn't glow brightly—there was no dramatic flash of light to signal magic at work—but the hue shifted. It pulsed with a faint, rhythmic violet, matching the hum of Kaelen’s gauntlet but slower, deeper.

“Left,” she whispered, her voice barely rising above the sound of their breathing. “The resonance is stronger on that side. The forest is trying to show us where it wants us to go.”

Torin moved immediately, stepping over a thick, living root that had just shifted position as if testing his weight. His boots sank slightly into the slurry, but he didn’t slip. He was used to this terrain, even if the mechanics were alien. “I’ll clear the path,” he said, grabbing a loose vine hanging from the canopy above. The vine recoiled when he touched it, snapping back with surprising speed. Torin didn't flinch. He just kept moving, using his strength to physically push aside encroaching growth that tried to block their way.

Kaelen stumbled beside Maren, his legs trembling under the strain. His gauntlet was hot now—not painful in a sharp way, but uncomfortable, like wearing gloves made of iron after a long day at the forge. The forest’s energy reacted to him, and it showed in the low hum that vibrated through his bones. Every step cost him something, whether it was strength or clarity. But he didn’t stop. He couldn't.

Maren supported him with one arm while her other hand hovered near his back, ready to adjust if he slipped. “We’re almost there,” she said softly, more for herself than for anyone else. “Just a bit further.”

Elias kept pace at the rear, scanning the shadows ahead of them. The map in his hands was useless here—the shifting forest rendered any static representation meaningless—but he still held it anyway, like an anchor against disorientation. He nodded once when Elara signaled the turn. “Keep going,” he muttered. “Don’t let us fall behind.”

They moved forward together, a single file of four souls walking through a place that refused to stay still. The trees leaned inward, closing ranks as if forming a cathedral archway around them. Roots twisted upward from the ground, weaving into branches above, creating corridors of green and gray that seemed alive with intent. There was no malice in it, just precision—a living machine guiding them toward something they didn’t fully understand yet.

Elara adjusted her grip on the chisel, her eyes fixed on the faint glow of her quartz stone. “The path widens ahead,” she called out. “But be careful—the ground shifts faster near the ridge.”

Torin grunted, stepping over a patch of moss that rippled like water beneath their feet. “Then we move quicker.”

Kaelen nodded, though his expression remained unreadable. He focused on each step, counting them mentally to keep from losing track. The forest was not hostile—it was simply different, operating on rules they hadn’t yet learned. And as long as they followed its rhythm, they could survive.

The air grew heavier the closer they approached the ridge. It wasn’t just humidity or pressure; it felt like standing near a storm front that had already passed, leaving behind only the echo of its violence. Kaelen’s skin felt tight again, the dark veins receding slightly but still visible beneath his pale flesh. He pushed through the sensation, forcing himself to keep moving despite the fatigue clawing at his limbs.

Maren noticed the change in him first. Her brow furrowed as she tightened her grip on his arm. “You’re slipping,” she said quietly. “Let me take more of your weight.”

“I’m fine,” Kaelen replied, though his voice lacked conviction. He leaned into her support anyway, letting her share the burden without complaint. “Just… focus on the path.”

Elara stepped ahead again, her chisel glowing brighter now as she tested the resonance of the next section. The stone pulsed faster, almost erratically, before settling into a steady rhythm once more. “This way,” she called back, pointing toward a narrow gap between two massive trunks that seemed to have grown together naturally—or perhaps unnaturally.

Torin moved ahead of her, clearing debris and vines from the path. His movements were economical, precise. He knew how to move through this kind of terrain without wasting energy or making unnecessary noise. That was the difference between him and someone who had spent most of their life in cities built on stone and mortar. Here, silence meant survival.

Elias stayed behind, watching the shadows for signs of movement that wasn’t part of the forest itself. The ridge was close now—close enough to see the jagged outline of rock peeking through the canopy like teeth bared against the sky. But even as they approached it, the forest seemed to shift again, rearranging itself subtly to guide them toward the opening.

Kaelen felt the pull of the ridge before he saw it. The hum in his gauntlet grew louder, almost unbearable. It wasn’t pain anymore—it was anticipation. The forest knew what lay beyond that point, and it wanted them to know too. But Elara’s voice cut through the tension. “Almost there,” she said again, her tone steady despite the exhaustion creeping into her movements.

Torin paused mid-step, scanning the opening ahead. “Clear path,” he called back. “Just a few more steps.”

Maren adjusted Kaelen’s weight once more, making sure he didn’t falter as they crossed the threshold into the open space beyond. The ridge was higher than expected, jagged cliffs rising sharply on either side of them. Beyond it lay… something else. Something unknown.

They stood there for a moment, taking stock of their surroundings. The forest had brought them here, guided them through its shifting corridors and dangerous paths. Now they faced whatever waited beyond the ridge. Whatever came next, they would face it together.
