Hard to tell the difference when you can't see the end of it."

He resisted, focusing on the effort of walking, but the hum in his arm grew louder, almost deafening.

"Elias," he said softly, his voice barely audible. "Don't touch it."

"Why not?" Elias asked, turning to face him. "It's just a root structure. It won't hurt us if we're careful."

They moved forward together, stepping carefully over roots that snapped into place beneath their feet, avoiding branches that brushed against their skin with a lightness that felt almost sentient. The forest seemed to breathe around them, exhaling pulses of violet light that danced across the ground like fireflies caught in a storm.

At the bottom of the ravine, the trees grew tall and slender, their leaves shimmering with a faint inner glow that seemed to pulse in time with the hum of Kaelen's gauntlet.

Elara stepped forward, her chisel raised once more. "We use my stone as a guide. I'll find the frequency that matches the ravine's natural rhythm. If we sync with it, we can pass safely."

"And if it doesn't work?" Torin asked, stepping forward to hold the edge of the ravine.

"Then we try again," Elara said, her voice steady despite the tension in the air. "We're not here to fail. We're here to survive, and that means adapting when things don't go as planned."

Kaelen nodded, feeling the hum in his arm settle into a steady beat that matched the rhythm of their footsteps. He looked at Maren, who was still supporting him, and gave her a small smile. "We'll make it," he said softly. "Together."
