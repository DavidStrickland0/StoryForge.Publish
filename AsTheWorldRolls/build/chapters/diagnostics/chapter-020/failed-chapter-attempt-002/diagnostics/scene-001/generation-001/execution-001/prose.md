The heavy iron-bound doors groaned as Torin pulled them open, the sound swallowed instantly by the dense, living wall of trees that rose like stone pillars on either side of the path. The air inside the watchfort had been stale, recycled through magic and hearth-fire, but this air was different—cool, damp, and thick with the scent of wet earth and rotting wood. It tasted of decay, yet it was alive in a way that made Elias’s teeth ache.

"Formation," Torin barked, his voice cutting through the oppressive quiet. He didn't wait for an answer; he simply moved to the rear of the group, sword hand low, eyes scanning the shadows where the tree trunks merged into one another. "Elias and I cover the rear. Elara, point with Kaelen. Maren, stay close."

Kaelen stepped out first, his boots sinking slightly into the mossy root system that formed the path. His legs were still unsteady, a faint tremor running up from the soles of his feet, but he walked with a deliberate posture that suggested control. The dark veins beneath his translucent skin pulsed in time with the violet hum of the barrier behind them—a rhythm that seemed to sync with the forest itself.

Elara moved to Kaelen’s left, her quartz chisel glowing faintly in her grip. She didn’t speak immediately, just holding the stone up to the air as if listening for a frequency. The forest responded with a low, rhythmic thrumming of branches—a sensation more felt than heard, like a giant heart beating beneath their feet.

"Moss is shifting," Elara murmured, her voice barely audible over the rustling leaves. "Roots are retracting near the left bank."

"Then we go right," Elias said, stepping out beside her. He clutched his iron pot tightly, the heat radiating from it a stark contrast to the chill of the night. "Keep moving. Don't let the ground decide our route."

The path ahead was narrow, hemmed in by trees that seemed to lean inward as if trying to pinch them off. The canopy above was so thick it blocked most of the moonlight, leaving them in a twilight gloom where shadows stretched and curled like fingers. Torin’s sword clicked softly against his boot as he paced back and forth behind Elara and Kaelen, keeping watch over their flanks.

Maren walked between Elias and Kaelen, her hands still wrapped in the clay pot’s residue, the salve keeping the cold at bay for now. She kept a hand on Kaelen’s arm, not because she needed to support him physically—he was managing—but because she wanted to feel the warmth of his skin. Even through the thin layer of flesh and the strange, glowing veins beneath it, she could sense the depletion in his magic reserves. It felt like walking beside someone who had been running on empty for days.

"How are you holding it?" she asked quietly, not looking at him directly but focusing on the ground ahead.

Kaelen nodded once, a sharp gesture that betrayed his discomfort. "Fine."

"Your pulse is racing," Elias muttered from the rear. "You're bleeding off more than you should be."

"I said fine," Kaelen snapped, though his voice lacked its usual edge. It was thin, strained, as if he were trying to force words through a dry throat. "I can walk. I can fight. I don't need you to carry me like a wounded hound."

"You're not carrying weight," Maren said gently. "You're bleeding out energy. The forest is feeding on it."

"It’s not feeding," Kaelen corrected, stopping mid-step and turning to face her. His eyes were still too bright, the violet light swirling in their depths like a storm trapped behind glass. "It’s... testing us. If I break, they move faster."

Elias stepped forward, his voice low and practical. "That’s exactly what we’re afraid of. You’re not breaking, you’re just running on fumes. We can’t afford to lose control again."

Torin grunted from the rear, his hand tightening on his sword hilt. "We move now. The slurry is getting thicker. If we don't cross within the hour, the road will be gone."

Elara raised her quartz stone again, the faint glow of its surface dimming as the forest’s hum grew louder. The trees were closing in—not physically collapsing, but shifting their positions ever so slightly, like a crowd leaning to watch something happen. The air felt heavier now, charged with static that made the hair on Elias’s arms stand up.

"Shift detected," Elara whispered, her voice strained. "The path ahead is unstable. We need to find another way."

Kaelen stepped forward again, his eyes narrowing. "Where?"

"The dry channels," Elara said, pointing toward a gap in the foliage where the ground looked firmer, less saturated with moisture. "But they’re narrow and steep."

"Then we go there," Elias said. He adjusted his grip on the iron pot, feeling the heat seep into his palms. "Torin, cover the rear again. I’ll take point with Elara. Maren, stay close to Kaelen. Don’t let him fall behind."

Maren nodded, her expression grim but resolute. She wrapped a hand around Kaelen’s arm, her touch firm and steady. "You’ve got my back," she said, her voice low. "But if you start fading, I’ll carry you."

Kaelen didn’t respond, just kept walking, his steps slower now, the tremor in his legs more pronounced. The forest seemed to react to his presence, the branches rustling and the leaves shifting as if trying to block their path or guide them elsewhere. The air tasted sweeter now, cloying with the scent of rotting fruit and damp earth.

Elias followed close behind Elara, scanning for signs of movement in the shadows. Torin kept his sword drawn, ready to cut down any threat that might try to interrupt their progress. Maren stayed close to Kaelen, her eyes darting between him and the trees, watching for any sign that he was losing control.

As they moved deeper into the forest, the path grew narrower, the roots twisting and turning in ways that defied logic. The ground felt spongy beneath their boots, as if the earth itself was trying to swallow them whole. The hum of the barrier behind them faded into the distance, replaced by the low, rhythmic thrumming of the forest—a sound that seemed to grow louder with every step they took.

"They’re trying to pull us back," Kaelen said, his voice barely above a whisper. "They know we’re here."
