Kaelen leaned forward against the headboard of his cot, his breath hitching in a dry rasp that did little to clear the thick air inside the watchfort. The room smelled of old wood, drying herbs, and rusted iron, but beneath it lay a heavier scent that clung to the back of his throat—the metallic taste of copper and rot. Outside, the barrier pulsed with a rhythm that felt less like a steady beat and more like a slow, heavy thrumming against the inside of his skull. The violet hue had deepened from the bruised white he remembered before the drain, signaling saturation rather than strength.

He looked at Maren first. Her hands, which had been the anchor for his stability just moments ago, now trembled visibly as she pulled them away from his chest. She was gripping a small cloth bundle to her lips, trying to stifle the sound, but the tremor betrayed her.

"You pushed your reserves too hard," Kaelen said, keeping his voice low and professional. It was an observation of mechanics, not an expression of concern. "Your skin is cold enough to burn frost into stone."

"I can feel it," she replied, though she didn't pull the cloth away from her mouth. "But I needed you to stay warm while I worked the channel. If I hadn't..." She broke off, looking down at her own hands. The barrier outside was not just a wall; it was a trap that required maintenance, and if Elias was right about the seal's instability, they were sitting on a powder keg inside a containment field that was eating itself alive.

"And how long before it shatters?" Torin asked.

"Long enough for us to finish our business here," Elias said, not turning around. "But only if we stop arguing tactics and start assessing the flow. Kaelen, tell me what you feel."

It was not a spell of summoning or shielding this time; it was a direct application of the force that fueled the Blight, filtered through his own physiology to act as a pump.

His skin, usually pale and translucent, seemed to press inward against the pressure of the energy surging through him. Jagged scars on his forearms throbbed where fissures had previously leaked green mist, but now they held firm, sealing against the internal tide.

"The seal needs to hold," Elias insisted, walking over to stand beside Maren and Kaelen. "But if we push too hard, we overload the system again. We need a controlled release."

"Tell me how much more I can take before I collapse," Kaelen said. His voice was flat, devoid of fear or desperation. It was a statement of fact, stripped of emotion and delivered with the precision of a soldier reporting on ammunition levels. "I am already depleted. My reserves are zero."

"I know you're depleted," Maren said softly, leaning closer to him until her breath tickled his ear. She didn't look at the map; she looked only at him. We don't wait for dawn. We move while the barrier is stable."

Elias adjusted his grip on the map, his eyes darting between the symbols and the pulsing light of the barrier outside. "Five minutes," he calculated aloud. "Maybe ten if Kaelen can maintain this output without collapsing. Torin, you're right. If we don't move, the pressure will build until it shatters the seal anyway. We have to ride it out while we're still in control."

"I can do it for now," Kaelen said. He felt the drain of his own reserves, a hollow ache in his chest that reminded him how little was left of him. His skin was pale and nearly translucent, the dark veins mapping his arms clearly visible as he pushed through the pain. But the warmth held. The barrier outside continued its slow thrumming, digesting the Blight that flowed through it, growing stronger with every passing second even as the pressure threatened to burst it.

"Then get dressed," Torin ordered. "We're moving in five minutes."
