Kaelen shivered. The cold that had seeped into his bones during his sleep returned in a wave, sharp and immediate. He wrapped his arms around himself, trying to conserve the heat he no longer possessed in abundance. His skin felt tight, stretched over muscles that refused to fully contract without a source of fuel.

He looked at Maren. Her hands were shaking visibly now that she had stopped channeling magic to him. The steady warmth she had poured into him was gone, replaced by the hollow echo of his own depletion. She stood there with her cloak pulled tight, but the air around them felt thinner than before, as if the room itself had lost its density.

“Maren,” Kaelen said, his voice raspy from disuse. “Let me take this.”

She hesitated, looking down at his chest where the dark veins were already beginning to pulse faintly beneath his pale skin. They moved like slow rivers reclaiming their course.

“I can’t,” she replied quickly, though her tone lacked conviction. “My magic is drained. If I try to warm you again now, I’ll collapse before dawn.”

“Then I’ll do it myself,” Kaelen said. He didn’t explain what he meant by that. It wasn’t a request for healing; it was an offer of control. He closed his eyes and focused on the brass gauntlet resting on his wrist. The runes were dormant, cold and heavy as always, but he knew they were there, waiting for a command he couldn’t quite articulate yet.

He reached out with his mind, not pulling at the Blight like before, but reaching toward the barrier outside. He felt its presence through the floorboards, the walls, the very air pressing against the glass of the slit windows. It was a vast thing, humming with a rhythm that had changed since he woke. It wasn’t just containing anymore; it was struggling. The seal at the Gate of Silence was leaking faster than expected, and the pressure differential outside was increasing. The barrier wasn’t just failing—it was groaning under strain, its violet hue deepening to bruised purple as if it were trying to hold something in that didn’t belong inside.

“Elias,” Kaelen said without opening his eyes. “The map.”

Elias stepped forward from the hearth, where he had been reviewing a bronze plate map etched with lines showing the temple, six smaller shrines, and channels beneath the city. He held the map steady on the heavy oak table, though he didn’t speak. His shoulder pain kept him rigid, one hand braced against the table edge while the other pointed to a faint line marking the flow of Blight moving westward through the underground network.

“The resonance is shifting,” Elias said quietly. “The seal isn’t holding back what’s coming from below. The velocity is increasing.”

“I know,” Kaelen replied. He opened his eyes and looked past Maren, at Elias, Torin, Elara. They were all watching him, waiting for something he hadn’t fully decided yet.

Elara sat on the floor with her notebook closed, her small frame hunched forward slightly as if bracing herself against a draft. Her sharp eyes scanned the room, noting the faint greenish mist still clinging to Kaelen’s wrists despite Maren’s best efforts. She didn’t look away immediately when he glanced at her.

“You used your magic to wake,” Torin said from the doorway. His sword remained sheathed, but his posture was coiled, ready for anything. “That’s not a good idea if you’re going to push yourself again.”

“I didn’t use magic to wake,” Kaelen corrected gently. “I woke because my body stopped working properly. I’m just… letting it function again now.”

He took a breath, deep and slow, feeling the air fill his lungs with difficulty. His skin was translucent, veins visible beneath the surface like ink stains on parchment. But he could feel something else too—a pull, not from outside, but from within himself. From the gauntlet. From the barrier. From everything that had been tied to him since the beginning.

He stood up slowly, leaning against the headboard of his cot for support. His legs trembled slightly at first, then steadied as he focused on keeping them straight. He walked toward Maren, who hadn’t moved from her spot beside the bed. She watched him with concern etched into every line of her face.

“Can you help me?” he asked. “Warm me up before I freeze.”

She nodded once, stepping closer. Her hands hovered near his shoulders, but she didn’t touch him yet.

“I can try,” she said softly. “But it won’t last long.”

“That’s fine,” Kaelen replied. “Just keep the room warm for now.”

He turned back to Elias and Torin. “Tell me about the barrier.”

Elias stepped forward, placing the map on the table before turning to face him. “It’s stable enough right now, but the resonance is changing. The seal at the Gate of Silence is failing faster than we calculated. If we don’t redirect the flow soon, the surge will shatter the seal and flood this room.”

“And if I channel my magic into it?” Kaelen asked.

Elias studied him for a moment. “You could reinforce the barrier’s structure temporarily, but you’ll lose more of your reserves in the process. Your skin might crack again.”

“Then let me try,” Kaelen said. He closed his eyes once more and reached inward, not pulling at anything, but listening. The Blight was still there, moving westward through the channels, heading toward the forest. But it wasn’t attacking him anymore. Not really. It was part of him now, a force he carried rather than something that hunted him.

He focused on the barrier outside, feeling its rhythm through the walls. It was a slow, heavy thrumming, like a heartbeat that had grown too large for its body. He could feel the pressure building behind it, the seal weakening under the strain of the moving tide.

“I’ll do it,” he said. “But I need everyone to stay calm. No sudden movements.”

Torin nodded, stepping back into the shadows of the doorway. Elara remained seated on the floor, her notebook still closed. Elias moved to stand beside Maren, who had finally placed her hands on Kaelen’s chest again.

He took another breath, deeper this time, and let his magic flow outward from him. It didn’t feel like a surge anymore; it felt like a current, steady and controlled. He pushed warmth into the room, not just to himself but to Maren and Elias, who were already showing signs of physical exhaustion. The air grew warmer, the lanterns brightened, and the cold that had seeped into their bones began to recede.

Kaelen could feel his own body resisting the effort, his veins darkening as they drew energy from his core. His skin felt tight again, stretching over muscles that refused to fully contract without a source of fuel. But he kept pushing, channeling the warmth outward until the room felt almost normal again.

The barrier outside responded instantly. The violet hue deepened slightly, then stabilized, pulsing with a rhythm that felt less like a steady beat and more like a slow, heavy thrumming. The groaning sound faded, replaced by a low hum that seemed to vibrate through the floorboards and into their bones.

“Good,” Kaelen said, opening his eyes. “It’s holding now.”

Maren pulled her hands away from his chest, her face pale but smiling slightly. “You did it,” she said. “But you’re going to regret it later.”

“I’ll deal with that when I’m ready,” he replied. He leaned against the headboard again, catching his breath. His skin was still translucent, veins visible beneath the surface like ink stains on parchment, but the dark glow had faded significantly. The fissures on his forearms were jagged scars now, no longer leaking mist.

Elias picked up the map again, studying it with renewed focus. “The flow is stabilizing,” he said. “But we still need to decide what to do next.”

Torin stepped back into the room, his sword still sheathed. “We wait for dawn,” he said. “Then we move west.”

“Agreed,” Kaelen said. “But first, someone needs to check on Maren.”

Maren shook her head slightly, though a small smile played at the corners of her mouth. “I’m fine. Just tired.”

Elara stood up from the floor, closing her notebook with a soft click. “We all are,” she said. “But that’s okay for now. We’ll rest here until dawn.”

Kaelen nodded slowly, feeling the warmth spread through his body again. It wasn’t a full recovery, but it was enough to keep them alive until morning. He looked at each of them, seeing the exhaustion in their faces, the fear in their eyes, the determination that kept them moving forward despite everything.

He took another breath, deeper this time, and let his magic flow outward from him once more. It didn’t feel like a burden anymore; it felt like part of him now, a force he could control rather than something that hunted him.

The group remained united in their decision to wait for dawn, the barrier stable enough to hold them until then. Kaelen was recovering from the physical exertion of waking and the initial surge of magic he just used; Maren was catching her breath after stabilizing him. The room was quiet except for the low hum of the barrier outside and the occasional rustle of Torin’s movements as he patrolled the perimeter.
