The rain fell harder now, drumming a frantic rhythm against Elias's breastplate that sounded like a thousand tiny hammers striking an anvil of cold steel. His sword was heavy in his hand, the weight amplified by the adrenaline flooding his system and the tremor from the creature's iron grip minutes ago. It had felt like holding a knife through wet cardboard until he had finally snapped it.

The first Blight-Touched didn't die. As Elias stared, horrified, the flesh around the wound where his blade had bitten in retracted with a sickening squelch. The corrupted tissue knit itself together, sealing the hole and regenerating a fresh layer of green, pulsating muscle that seemed to stretch beyond its original boundaries. Then, the creature's head snapped upward, its jaw unhinging slightly wider than before, revealing rows of jagged, needle-like teeth that gleamed in the dim light.

"Resilient," Elias muttered, the word tasting like ash in his mouth. He parried a lashing claw that had swept low, sending shards of stone chipping into the air. The creature's next strike connected with his shoulder plate, driving him back until his boots hit the wet moss hard enough to bruise. Pain bloomed white-hot across his pauldron, but he didn't falter. He couldn't afford it.

The second and third creatures were already upon him, their movements a blur of distorted limbs that defied human anatomy. One extended three arms at once, its hands clutching Elias's throat while the other two slashed for his legs. Elias gasped, choking as his neck met cold, wet fingers. His vision blurred, the edges of the world darkening into shadowy static—the telltale sign of the Blight beginning to seep past his defenses.

He slammed his free hand against the creature's chest again, this time aiming for a knot of black energy swirling in its center. The impact threw the monster backward, but instead of a groan of pain, there was only a wet splash as it hit the water-filled fountain basin. From inside the black water, something else emerged—a writhing mass of viscera and bone that seemed to have no face, only endless maws opening and closing in unison.

Elias realized then that he wasn't fighting three monsters anymore; he was fighting a single hive mind that had shed its form to strike him multiple times at once. The realization hit him with the same force as the creature's claws, cold and paralyzing.

He tried to summon his sword again, but his muscles betrayed him. His arm felt heavy, as if pulled by an invisible weight. A tendril of black slime lashed out from the water, wrapping around his ankle. He could feel it tightening, constricting bone against bone. *No,* he thought, gritting his teeth until they ached. *Not yet.*

Behind him, the heavy wooden doors of the temple hall creaked open. A shadow moved through the rain-slicked courtyard, blocking out the moonlight. It was Kaelen, but not the young man Elias had known moments ago. The boy's face was pale and drawn, his eyes wide with a terror that had somehow curdled into something else—something hard and calculating. He held a staff topped with a glowing crystal, its light struggling against the encroaching fog.

"Elias," Kaelen said, his voice low and steady. "Don't you dare die on my watch."

The creature in the water surged forward, ignoring Elias entirely as it now moved toward Kaelen. The Blight-Touched didn't see people; they saw energy sources. And Kaelen's mana was a beacon.

Elias roared, using every ounce of remaining strength to kick free from the slime binding his ankle. He twisted in mid-air, bringing his sword down hard on the creature's exposed neck. The blade struck true this time, severing a long tendril that retracted into the water with a hiss. But the main mass didn't fall; it dissolved into a cloud of green smoke and reformulated itself into a towering, faceless monstrosity that rose above the fountain, its hands reaching for Kaelen like grasping talons.

"Kaelen!" Elias screamed, throwing himself between the monster's outstretched claw and the boy. The blade bit deep into his armor, tearing through steel and flesh alike, but Elias held firm, his body becoming a shield of iron and will. "Your turn!" he gasped, blood dripping from his lip onto the cobblestones.

Kaelen nodded once, a sharp, decisive motion. He raised the staff, not with the hesitation of earlier, but with the focus of someone who had finally accepted that time was running out. The crystal at its tip flared white-hot, casting long, dancing shadows across the courtyard.

"I didn't save you," Kaelen whispered, his voice echoing strangely in the damp air. "I just made sure I'd have to do it again."

With a sound like tearing silk, the staff erupted into a sphere of concentrated light that slammed into the ground between Elias and the Blight-Touched. The shockwave knocked Elias sideways, sending him skidding across the wet stones, but it also seemed to disrupt something fundamental in the air itself. The fog around them began to swirl violently, pulling inward as if drawn by a gravitational well.

The towering monstrosity shrieked—a sound that was no longer human or animal, but the grinding of gears and the tearing of metal—and its form began to destabilize. It let out a final, guttural roar that vibrated through the soles of Elias's boots, sending shivers up his spine. Then, with a sickening crunch, it collapsed into dust, scattering across the courtyard like ash caught in a windstorm.

The other two creatures, which had been circling, froze. Their green eyes dimmed, flickering out one by one until they were just hollow sockets of blackness before falling over, their limbs stiffening in unnatural angles. The immediate threat was gone, replaced by the suffocating silence of the defeated, save for the rain and the ragged breathing of two survivors.

Elias lay on his side, his sword still embedded in his shoulder plate, soaking him in blood that smelled of ozone and decay. He watched as Kaelen stood over the smoking remains of what he had called a monster moments before. The boy's face was twisted in effort, sweat dripping from his nose into his mouth.

"You're bleeding," Elias said, his voice hoarse, trying to sit up against the dizziness that threatened to pull him under. "You used mana on yourself."

Kaelen ignored him for a moment, staring at the empty space where the creature had stood. Then he looked down at his hands, which were trembling so violently they could barely hold the staff. He dropped it. It clattered softly into the mud.

"I think," Kaelen said quietly, "I might have broken the seal on my own connection."

Before Elias could process the words, a new sound cut through the quiet—the distant, rhythmic thrumming of something massive moving through the earth beneath them, followed by the crunch of stone under heavy feet that echoed unnaturally close to where they lay. The fog began to thin slightly in the center of the courtyard, revealing not just the ruined temple, but a structure far larger and darker than anything they had seen before, rising out of the mist like a jagged tooth waiting to snap shut.

Elias pushed himself up on his elbows, wincing as pain radiated through his entire body. The adrenaline was fading, replaced by a creeping exhaustion that felt different from the fatigue of running or fighting. It felt heavier, colder.

"Where are we going now?" Elias asked, his voice barely audible. "The fog is clearing..."

Kaelen didn't answer immediately. He picked up his staff again, wiping away blood and sweat with a trembling hand. His eyes were fixed on the emerging structure, wide with a mixture of awe and dread that matched Elias's own internal state.

"We don't go anywhere," Kaelen said finally. "We wait."

"But it's still coming," Elias pointed out, glancing at the massive silhouette rising from the mist. The ground beneath them shook again, a low vibration that rattled teeth in his jaw. "And we're not exactly safe here."

Kaelen looked at him, then down at their scattered belongings—the broken vial, the severed tendrils, the torn armor plating of Elias's shoulder. He nodded slowly.

"Safe doesn  mean much when the Blight is already inside us," he said. "But we're still alive. For now."

He turned toward the temple entrance, his movements stiff and deliberate. Elias followed, limping slightly on one leg, the weight of the day's events settling around them like a second skin. The rain had stopped, leaving the courtyard slick and reflecting the pale, uncanny glow of the emerging tower in its depths. Somewhere in the distance, a new kind of noise began to rise—not the screech of monsters or the crackle of magic, but the slow, deliberate thud of footsteps that seemed to echo from inside the earth itself.

They had won the battle, but as Elias watched Kaelen step into the shadows of the temple doorway, he realized they had only just begun the war.
