Maren did not step out into the night. She held the door open just enough to let the cold air circulate through the stagnant room, then stepped back inside, her hand resting on the pommel of her mace. The gesture was subtle, but the message was clear: the physical triage was over. Now came the accounting.

She turned to the small table in the center of the gatehouse, where a few crates of supplies sat. From one of them, she retrieved a flat, heavy object wrapped in oilcloth. She unwrapped it with efficient, practiced hands, revealing the bronze plate map. The metal was cool to the touch, heavy with age, and etched with lines so fine they seemed to shimmer in the dim light of the single lantern hanging from the beam.

Elias pushed himself off the bench. The motion sent a sharp spike of pain through his side, but he ignored it, stepping forward to stand beside the table. Kaelen remained seated, his hands resting on his knees, his eyes fixed on the map with a mixture of fascination and dread.

"Look," Elias said, his voice rough. He traced the line that represented the temple, his finger stopping at the cluster of smaller dots surrounding it. "This is the network. The temple isn't an isolated structure. It’s the hub. These are the shrines, and the lines connecting them are the channels. The water runs through them. The binding rites were performed here, but the containment system extends across the entire city."

Maren leaned over the table, her brow furrowed. "You said the barrier held. That it consumed the Blight."

"It did," Elias said. "We watched it happen. When the creatures hit the light, they didn’t bounce off. They dissolved. The barrier didn’t just block them; it ate them. Every time the entity outside pushed against it, the light grew brighter, thicker. It’s not a wall, Maren. It’s a mouth."

A silence fell over the room, heavy with the implication. Torin, who had been leaning against the wall with his arms crossed, pushed off and joined them at the table. He was a broad man, his face scarred from years of border skirmishes, and he looked at the map with the skepticism of a soldier who had seen too many promises break against reality.

"Eat it," Torin repeated, the word tasting strange in his mouth. "And then?"

"And then it remains," Elias said. "But it’s not safe. We saw the entity. It’s still there. It’s waiting. It’s feeding on the corruption in the city, growing stronger. If it decides to push through, and the barrier can’t consume it fast enough..." He let the sentence hang, unnecessary to finish.

Kaelen finally spoke. His voice was quiet, stripped of its usual confidence. "It’s not just the barrier."

Everyone looked at him.

Kaelen held up his left hand. The brass gauntlet was still wrapped in the coarse cloth Maren had given him, but he unwound it slowly, revealing the metal. It was dull, lifeless, but the etchings along the knuckles were visible. "When we crossed the threshold into the temple, and when we left... it took something from us. Not blood. Not pain. It took the corruption."

Elara, who had been standing near the door, stepped forward. She was smaller than the others, her eyes sharp and observant, missing nothing. "You mean the Blight?"

"Yes," Kaelen said. "I felt it. A stripping away. Like a layer of skin being peeled off. And it didn’t just take it from the creatures. It took it from me. From the magic I carry." He looked at Elias, then back to the group. "My magic is fueled by the same energy that fuels the Blight. I’ve always known that. But I never understood the connection until now. The barrier doesn’t just consume the external corruption. It resonates with the source. With *me*."

Torin frowned. "So you’re saying the barrier is fed by the wizard?"

"No," Kaelen said quickly, shaking his head. "I’m saying it’s attracted to it. The barrier is an ancient mechanism. It was built to contain the Blight, yes. But it’s also a filter. It separates the clean energy from the corrupted. My magic... it’s a hybrid. It’s the bridge. When I was near the barrier, I felt the resonance. It’s like a tuning fork. The barrier vibrates with the same frequency as the Blight, and my magic is the only thing I’ve found that can interface with both."

Maren looked at Kaelen, her expression unreadable. "And the keeper in the forest?"

Kaelen flinched. The mention of the forest, of the entity that had watched them, of the memory that had spoken to him, sent a chill through him that had nothing to do with the cold air. "The keeper knows," he said. "It knows what I am. It knows what the Blight is trying to do. It’s not trying to destroy the world, Maren. It’s trying to reach the source. To overwrite the memory. To erase the history that the forest holds."

Elara looked at the map, her eyes tracing the lines. "So the Blight is a force of erasure," she said softly. "Not just corruption. Erasure."

"Yes," Kaelen said. "And the barrier is the only thing standing between it and the source. But it’s not enough. The entity outside is growing. The barrier is consuming, but it’s also weakening. We saw it flicker."

"We saw it strengthen," Elias corrected. "When the creatures hit it, it grew brighter."

"Because it’s feeding on them," Kaelen said. "But it’s also feeding on the magic in the air. And that magic is finite. Eventually, it will run out. Or the entity will find a way around it."

The group stood in silence, the weight of the truth settling over them. The map lay between them, a fragile sheet of metal holding the map of a dying world. The lantern flickered, casting long, dancing shadows against the stone walls.

Maren straightened up. "So we have a barrier that eats corruption, a magic that resonates with it, and an entity that wants to erase the past. And we’re here, in a watchfort, with a few days’ worth of food and a lot of bad news."

She looked at each of them in turn. Elias, with his bandaged side and weary eyes. Kaelen, with his trembling hands and haunted gaze. Torin, with his skeptical stance and hardened face. Elara, with her sharp eyes and quiet intensity.

"We need to know exactly what happened," Maren said. "Not just the broad strokes. The details. The mechanics. How the barrier works. How the magic interacts with it. How the entity moves. If we’re going to survive this, we need to understand it. And we need to understand it together."

Elias nodded. "I can show you the runes. The way they were arranged. The way the water flowed."

Kaelen nodded. "I can describe the resonance. The feeling of it. The way it pulled at me."

Torin crossed his arms. "I can tell you what the entity looked like. How it moved. What it left behind."

Elara picked up the map, her fingers hovering over the lines. "And I can map the route. The channels. The shrines. The path we need to take."

Maren watched them, her eyes hard and bright. She saw the fear in their faces, but she also saw the resolve. They were not just survivors anymore. They were witnesses. They had seen the truth, and they had survived it. And now, they had to make it mean something.

"Good," she said. "Then let’s begin."
