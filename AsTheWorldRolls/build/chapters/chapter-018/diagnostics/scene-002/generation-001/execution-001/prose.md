Elias moved with the deliberate, quiet rhythm of a man who had learned to make noise unnecessary. He gathered the scattered rations and dry meats from his crate, stacking them neatly against the wall where the hearth heat would not reach. His shoulder, wrapped in bandages that smelled of dried herbs and old iron, pulled tight as he shifted, but he did not pause. Pain was a constant companion, a reminder to move slowly, to conserve energy for when it mattered. He watched Maren through the corner of his eye. Her cloak lay half-draped over her legs, heavy wool dampened with the sweat of exertion.

He approached and set a water skin down beside her. The leather was stiff, caked with dried mud from the road, but it held enough liquid for a long draught. “You need to drink,” Elias said, his voice low, avoiding the sharp edge he might have used with a stranger. He knew she could hear him, knew he was standing there. He did not expect her to move immediately. Exhaustion made people stubborn; they stayed put until forced to act by necessity or someone else’s insistence.

Maren lifted her head slowly, her eyes red-rimmed but clear. She had been watching the barrier flicker for hours, counting its pulses against the rhythm of their breathing. The dome above them hummed faintly now, a low vibration that traveled through the floorboards and into the soles of her boots. It was not a pleasant sound; it sounded like stone grinding against stone deep underground, a reminder that something massive and hungry pressed against the outside world. She nodded once toward the water skin. “Thank you.”

Elias poured her a cup from his own metal mug, testing the warmth with the back of his hand before handing it to her. The steam rose in thin white curls, carrying the scent of woodsmoke and salted meat. He watched as she drank, seeing how her hands shook slightly—not from fear, but from the sudden release of adrenaline that had been keeping her upright. Her magic had drained her reserves faster than any wound could bleed her dry, yet she had refused to stop until Kaelen stabilized. That kind of willpower was rare, dangerous even, in a soldier who valued order over chaos.

“You’re trembling,” he said finally, setting the empty cup back on the crate. “Let me check your boots.”

Maren hesitated, then extended her foot. Elias knelt beside her, inspecting the soles for cuts or debris that might have gone unnoticed during their long march through mud and frost. His fingers were calloused from years of handling swords and shields, and his touch was firm but careful. He found nothing wrong with her feet, only the lingering chill that seeped into her bones despite Maren’s constant warmth.

“I’m fine,” she said, pulling her boot free to slide it off onto the floor. “Just tired.”

“Tired is not an excuse for standing still when you’re bleeding out internally,” Elias replied, though his tone was gentle. It was one of Maren’s own concoctions, formulated from herbs she had gathered along the way.

She held still as he applied a thin layer to her ankles and calves, massaging gently until the cold receded slightly under his touch. The salve worked quickly, warming the skin beneath and soothing the stiffness that came from prolonged immobility. Elias paused when he reached her shins, where a faint bruise marked the spot where she had fallen during their escape from the temple courtyard. He pressed lightly against it, wincing as if he felt the impact himself.

“I didn’t mean to drop you,” she murmured, closing her eyes briefly as his fingers worked over the sore muscle. “The ground was slick.”

“You’re welcome for dropping me,” Elias said dryly, though there was no bite in his words. “Would have made things easier if you’d let go sooner.”

Maren chuckled softly, the sound brittle and tired. “Next time I’ll aim lower.”

Elias finished applying the salve and stepped back, offering her a hand to help her stand. She accepted it without hesitation, standing tall despite the fatigue that weighed on her shoulders. Her cloak slipped off one shoulder as she adjusted her stance, revealing the thin line of a scar running down her side from an old battle. Elias glanced at it briefly before turning away, unwilling to dwell too long on past wounds or future ones.

“Torin’s keeping watch,” he said, gesturing toward the doorway where the large man stood with his sword drawn and hand resting near the hilt. “If anything comes in here while we’re sleeping, he’ll know about it before you do.”

Maren nodded, looking at Kaelen’s pale form once more. His brass gauntlet lay nearby, still cold and inert but no longer leaking green mist into the air. The dark veins that had marked his skin were fading slowly, retreating beneath the surface as if ashamed of their presence. That was progress, however small.

“I’ll rest now,” she said quietly, sinking back onto the cot. “But I won’t sleep deeply. Just enough to keep going.”

Elias nodded again, turning toward the hearth where he stacked another bundle of dried wood. The flames crackled softly, sending sparks up into the rafters that shimmered faintly in the dim light. Outside, the barrier continued its slow pulse, absorbing whatever corruption drifted near its edge. Inside, the group found a fragile peace together, united by shared vulnerability and quiet hope.

Elias sat cross-legged on the floor beside the hearth, pulling his knees up to his chest and resting his chin against them. He closed his eyes briefly, letting the warmth of the fire soothe his aching shoulders and stiff neck.

His thoughts drifted back to Kaelen’s condition, considering how much they had all given in trying to save him. The cost of using magic like that was never trivial; it left marks on those who wielded it, whether physical or spiritual. Maren’s depletion was visible in the way she moved now, slower than before, her limbs heavy with exhaustion. Kaelen’s decline was worse, his body failing under the strain of proximity to the Blight. They could not afford to lose either of them, yet they had no choice but to push forward anyway.

“Need anything?” Elias asked quietly, leaning against the frame.

Torin shook his head once, gripping the pommel of his sword tightly. “Just keep an eye on her.”
