The air inside the barrier dome had grown thick and heavy, tasting of copper and wet rot that seeped even past the shimmering light that shielded them. Maren sat on the bench beside Kaelen's bed, her breathing shallow and controlled, but Elias remained standing by the entrance, watching the barrier edge with a grimace that suggested he found the situation intolerable yet inevitable. Torin kept his hand on Maren’s shoulder, a silent anchor against the cold radiating from the floorboards.

Elara stood near the chest of tools, her fingers tracing the leather spine of her journal before she opened it to a fresh page. The resonance of the barrier hummed in time with their own ragged breaths—a discordant groan that vibrated through the soles of their boots and settled in the teeth. It was no longer a steady hum but a strained frequency, like a bowstring pulled too tight.

She looked at Kaelen. His skin had lost its sickly luminescence, fading to a pale, human shade where Maren’s warmth still clung, though the dark veins beneath remained visible, retreating into the flesh as if ashamed of their presence. The fissures on his forearms were dry, jagged scars that held no mist, but the coldness emanating from him was absolute. He wasn’t just unconscious; he was suspended in a state of magical sleep where his own energy had been siphoned out to keep the barrier stable and Maren alive.

Elara closed her journal with a decisive snap. She didn’t have time for diagrams or theoretical resonance charts. The Blight was moving westward, draining the seal at the Gate of Silence faster than the barrier could digest it. If they didn’t move soon, the pressure would shatter the dome before they could reach the drainage channels. But if they moved now, Kaelen’s body might not survive the journey, and Maren had exhausted her reserves completely.

She reached into her pack and pulled out a small, clear quartz stone. It was cold to the touch, colder than the ambient air should allow for, yet it held no residue of corruption. Beside it lay one of her chisels, the iron head dull from repeated tapping against hard surfaces.

“I’m not going anywhere,” Elara said, her voice quiet but firm. She stepped forward, moving past Torin without breaking stride. He didn’t stop her; his grip on Maren’s shoulder remained steady, watching instead. “Elias, I need you to check the map while I work.”

Elias nodded once, a brief acknowledgment that said more than words could convey. He moved to the table near the hearth, spreading the bronze plate flat against the rough wood. His hand hovered over the etched lines showing the temple and the six smaller shrines, but he didn’t speak yet. He knew the risk of disturbing Elara’s focus.

Elara knelt beside Kaelen’s bed. The brass gauntlet on his wrist had been cleaned of residue and lay inert, cold and heavy, with dormant runes that refused to flicker or hum. That was progress, but it wasn’t enough. His body was still translucent, the edges of his skin faintly visible where light passed through him. He looked less like a man and more like a vessel that had been emptied into a well.

She held the quartz stone against his chest, just below the collarbone where her hands could feel the cold radiating from his core. Then she tapped the chisel lightly against the stone, creating a soft, rhythmic sound that matched the resonance of the barrier. It was a low-frequency vibration, barely audible at first, but it grew in intensity as Elara focused her will on maintaining the frequency.

The effect was immediate. The violet light pulsing in the air dimmed slightly, then stabilized into a softer, more even glow. Kaelen’s chest rose and fell with a slower, steadier rhythm, though his breathing remained shallow. The coldness around him didn’t vanish—it merely receded from his surface, allowing Maren’s warmth to penetrate deeper without burning out.

Elias looked up from the map, his brow furrowed. “What are you doing?” he asked, his voice low and practical.

“Stabilizing the resonance in his body,” Elara replied, her eyes fixed on Kaelen’s face. “If we move him now, the flow will tear through him again. He needs time to re-establish a baseline connection with his own energy.”

“That won’t change where the Blight is moving,” Elias said, though he didn’t argue further. He leaned closer to the map, tracing the route westward with his finger. “We need to get going before dawn. The seal at the Gate of Silence is failing, and we can’t let it break.”

“I know that,” Elara said without turning around. Her hands were moving steadily, tapping the chisel against the quartz in a precise, repeating pattern. “I’m not delaying our departure. I’m buying us enough time to leave without taking him further apart than he already is.”

Torin shifted his weight on the bench, his boots creaking softly against the floorboards. He had been watching Elara’s work with a mixture of suspicion and respect. “You’re draining yourself,” he said finally, his voice rough from disuse. “That quartz stone—it’s not magic. It’s a tool. You’re using your own energy to maintain the frequency.”

Elara paused, her chisel hovering mid-tap. She didn’t look at him, but she nodded once. “Yes. The old ways aren’t just about chanting or drawing runes in the air. They’re about understanding the vibrations of things—the earth, the stone, the magic itself. And to maintain a stable resonance like Kaelen’s requires focus. Focus requires energy.”

Maren stirred beside Kaelen, her eyes fluttering open for a moment before she closed them again. She didn’t speak, but her presence was enough to confirm that Elara’s words were true. The warmth she had given Kaelen had left her pale and trembling, her magic reserves completely spent. If Maren had collapsed now, the group would have lost their only healer, and the plan to move west would have failed before it began.

Elara tapped the chisel again, the sound sharp and clear in the quiet room. The barrier’s resonance shifted imperceptibly, the violet hue deepening slightly before settling back into a calmer shade. Kaelen’s skin became less translucent, the dark veins fading further as if they were retreating from his body. But Elara felt the drain on herself. Her own energy was waning, her limbs growing heavy, and her focus slipping at the edges.

She knew what she had to do next. The quartz stone could only stabilize Kaelen for so long before it needed to be replaced or recharged. And if she kept tapping the chisel against it without rest, she would collapse herself. That wasn’t an option. They needed her alert and capable when they moved west.

“Elias,” she said softly, turning her head toward him. “Can you take over monitoring the barrier edge? I need to check my own reserves.”

Elias nodded, stepping away from the map and walking to the entrance where he stood watching the outside world through the narrow slit-like windows. He didn’t say anything else; his presence there was enough to confirm that she was right. The group needed her focused on Kaelen’s recovery, not stretched thin across multiple tasks.

Elara exhaled slowly, setting the chisel down on Kaelen’s chest. The quartz stone still hummed faintly, a ghost of its previous vibration. She reached for a small vial of solvent from her pack and poured a few drops onto her palms, rubbing them together to clean off any residue that might have accumulated during her work.

Then she stood up, moving back toward the chest of tools where her supplies were stored. She pulled out another quartz stone, this one smaller and more fragile-looking, and held it against Kaelen’s wrist near the gauntlet. The stone fit precisely into a groove carved into the fissure of his skin, bridging the gap between the earth’s vibration and the crystal’s structure to stabilize the barrier within his own body.

The effect was instantaneous. Kaelen’s breathing deepened slightly, though he remained unconscious. The coldness around him receded further, allowing Maren’s warmth to penetrate deeper without burning out. But Elara felt the drain on herself. Her limbs grew heavier, her vision blurring at the edges, and a faint tremor began in her hands.

She didn’t stop. She kept tapping the chisel against the quartz, maintaining the frequency until her own energy was nearly spent. Then she stopped, letting the stone settle into its natural rhythm without her input. The barrier’s resonance stabilized, the violet hue deepening slightly before settling back into a calmer shade. Kaelen’s skin became less translucent, the dark veins fading further as if they were retreating from his body.

Elias stood at the entrance, watching the outside world through the narrow slit-like windows. The wind howled against the walls of the temple, a sound that seemed to grow louder with every passing moment. But inside, the group remained still, united by a shared purpose and a fragile hope that they might survive another night together.

Torin sat on the bench beside Maren, his hand resting gently on her shoulder. She didn’t speak, but her presence was enough to confirm that Elara’s work had succeeded.
