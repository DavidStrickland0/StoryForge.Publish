The forest was silent, save for the faint rustle of leaves and the distant call of an unseen bird. Elias and Kaelen stood at the edge of the clearing, their breaths shallow, their bodies tense. The presence that had emerged from the trees had vanished, but its weight lingered in the air, like a memory that refused to fade. The forest itself seemed to hold its breath, as if it were waiting for them to move, to act, to make the next step in this journey that had begun long before they had set foot in this place.

Kaelen was the first to speak, his voice low and steady. “It’s not the Blight. It’s not the creature that rose from the mist. It’s something else. Something older.”

Elias nodded, his gaze fixed on the trees, on the shifting shadows that seemed to stretch and twist in the dim light. “And it remembers,” he said, his voice carrying a weight that was not just in the words, but in the way he said them. “It remembers what was done here. What was buried. What was left behind.”

Kaelen’s fingers brushed the brass gauntlet on his wrist, the relic that had once been a tool of an age before the Blight. He had felt its power in the temple, had seen how it had reacted to the Blight, how it had changed something within the corruption itself. It had not been enough to stop the creature, but it had opened something—had made something possible.

Elias looked at him, his expression unreadable. “You think it’s the key.”

Kaelen hesitated, as if the words themselves carried a weight he had not yet fully understood. “It’s not just the key. It’s the reason the Blight came here. It’s the reason it’s still trying to reach something it can’t destroy.”

Elias exhaled slowly, his jaw tightening. “Then we have to find it before it does.”

Kaelen nodded, his gaze fixed on the trees. “We have to find the keeper.”

Elias looked at him, his expression dark with thought. “And if we don’t? If the Blight finds it first?”

Kaelen did not answer immediately. He simply looked at the forest, at the shifting shadows, at the presence that had lingered in the air long after it had disappeared. “Then everything we’ve done—everything we’ve fought for—will be for nothing.”

Elias did not respond, but his grip on his sword tightened. The weight of the words settled in his chest, a realization that had not been fully formed, but was taking shape nonetheless. They had not come to this place to fight the Blight. They had come to find something that had been lost, something that had been waiting.

Kaelen turned to him, his expression unreadable. “We need to move. The forest is not safe. It’s not just the Blight that’s watching us. It’s the memory of what was done here.”

Elias nodded, his voice low. “Then we move west. That’s where the map leads us. That’s where the watchfort is.”

It had been more than a map. It had been a guide, a promise that they were not lost, that they had a path forward.

The forest pressed in around them, the trees towering overhead, their branches stretching like fingers, their shadows shifting with every step they took. Kaelen moved ahead, his staff casting thin beams of light through the undergrowth. He could feel it in his bones, the weight of the relic, the way it pulsed with something ancient and unspoken. It had not been enough to stop the creature in the city, but it had changed something—had opened something—within the Blight itself. He could still feel the shift, the way the air had trembled around him when he had called upon it, the way the creatures had recoiled, as if they had sensed something beyond their reach.

It was a place of waiting, of things that had been hidden, of things that had been left behind. The map had shown them the way, but it had also shown them the truth: that the city was not the end of the path, but the beginning. That the Blight did not end at the walls of the temple, nor at the edge of the city. It had roots deeper than anyone had imagined, and those roots stretched far beyond the ruins.

“It’s the forest,” he said finally. “It remembers.”

Elias frowned, stepping closer. “The forest remembers what?”

Kaelen looked up, his eyes dark with something that was not quite fear. “What was done here. What was buried. What was left behind.”

Kaelen’s hand moved to the gauntlet. “It’s not attacking.”

Elias’s grip tightened on his sword. “Then what is it doing?”

The shape did not move closer. It did not move at all. It simply stood, as if waiting, as if it had been waiting for them. And then, as if it had been waiting for them to understand, it spoke.

A voice, not of any creature, not of any human, but of something that had existed before the city, before the temple, before the Blight had taken root. It was not a voice of words, but of memory, of presence, of something that had been lost and now had found them.

Kaelen’s eyes widened. “The key?”
