## Engagement Findings

- The chapter fails to advance the plot or explore new ground in the forest setting, instead recycling the same encounter with the forest memory.
- The repetition of dialogue and scene beats reduces reader engagement by presenting information already established as fact.

- Remove the entire sequence where they encounter and speak with 'the keeper' entity; this event was completed in Chapter 7.
- Advance the narrative to show them traveling deeper into the forest after the initial encounter, exploring new aspects of the sentient environment or facing a new challenge.
- Replace the repeated dialogue about the keeper's identity with reactions to new phenomena or obstacles encountered while moving west toward the watchfort.
- Show progression in their understanding of the forest's memory rather than re-explaining what they already know from Chapter 7.
- Ensure Kaelen has a functional light source (lantern or magical fire) if he is casting light beams.
- Clarify how Elias holds the map without it being physically impossible to press against his ribs.
- Show physical signs of pain or exhaustion when Elias moves after the entity speaks.
- Avoid repeating the same sequence of events without advancing the narrative or changing circumstances.
- Limit repetitive actions like brushing the gauntlet unless they serve a specific narrative purpose.
- Ensure the entity's interaction with the characters is consistent with its described nature (e.g., if it presses against thoughts, show some physical reaction).
- Avoid looping back to previous scenes without clear time progression or new developments.
