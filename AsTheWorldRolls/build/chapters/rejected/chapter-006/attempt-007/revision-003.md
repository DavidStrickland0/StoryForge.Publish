# Chapter 6: The Road Away

The barrier flickered across the temple doors.

It had begun as a sheet of pale light. Now it was scarcely more than a translucent veil. Several of the runes feeding it had already gone dark. The ones that remained pulsed unevenly, each flare weaker than the last.

Beyond it stood a shape too large for the entrance.

Elias saw fragments whenever lightning flickered through the ruined courtyard: a leg plated in black growth, a shoulder dragging against the temple facade, a cluster of green lights that might have been eyes. Each step shook dust from the ceiling. The barrier shivered in answer.

Kaelen stared at the bending light. Blood had soaked through the cloth at his side and spread down to his belt. His hands shook around the staff, but his expression had gone still.

"How long?" Elias asked.

"I don't know."

"Guess."

The creature pressed one black-plated limb against the barrier. The impact shook grit loose beneath Elias's boots.

Green corruption spread across the pale surface in branching veins.

Elias expected the light to fail.

Instead, the barrier drank the Blight.

The green veins sank into it and vanished. The creature's limb smoked where it touched the light. Black growth peeled from its flesh in long strands and dissolved into the barrier, each strand making the pale radiance brighter. A dead rune beside the doorway flashed white and came alive. Then another awakened above it.

The creature pulled back with a grinding shriek.

The barrier stood stronger than before.

"Not long enough," Kaelen said.

Elias turned from the entrance and studied the sanctuary. Broken benches lay in uneven rows beneath a vaulted ceiling. Rainwater ran down the walls and gathered in shallow channels cut into the floor. At the far end, behind a stone altar, three narrow archways opened into darkness.

They had spent their remaining strength building a wall without understanding what it was. It had looked weak because it had been starved. The moment the Blight touched it, the ancient magic had begun consuming the corruption and using it to restore itself.

That did not make the temple safe. The barrier could feed on the Blight, but Elias had no intention of waiting to learn whether it could consume the creature faster than the creature could tear down the building around it.

"Then we leave."

Kaelen looked at him as though he had spoken in another language. "The temple is containing it."

"The temple contained it before we arrived. Something still broke the first seal."

"If we run and the barrier fails—"

"Then standing here won't make it stronger. The Blight already is."

Another blow rolled through the chamber. The barrier flashed as it stripped more corruption from the creature. A third dormant rune awakened, but a hairline fracture appeared in the stone around it and crept through a carved spiral.

Kaelen's gaze followed it. "I broke the seal in the courtyard. I felt it open through me. If that thing came because of what I did—"

"Then dying beside the door won't repair it."

Elias crossed to him and took hold of his shoulder. Kaelen flinched, but Elias did not release him.

"Listen to me. We came here looking for an answer. We found one. The Blight has roots older than this city, it can turn the dead into weapons, and whatever is outside can command those weapons. We also learned that your magic can hurt it. That's more than anyone outside the fog knows. If we die here, the knowledge dies here too."

Kaelen glanced toward the barrier. The immense silhouette leaned close. Several green lights shifted behind the radiance, arranging themselves around a central darkness.

"Leaving it feels like surrender," he said.

"It is surrender. For tonight."

The admission tasted bitter, but Elias forced himself to say the rest.

"We are outmatched, wounded, and nearly out of supplies. Courage doesn't change arithmetic. We leave, find the others, and become strong enough to choose the terms next time."

For several breaths, only the rain and the strained hum of the barrier answered him.

Then Kaelen nodded.

"There may be another way out," he said. "The water channels."

Elias looked down. Four shallow grooves crossed the floor, carrying rainwater from openings near the ceiling toward the altar. Rather than pooling there, the water vanished beneath it.

"A drain?"

"More than that. These temples were built around springs. Water used in the binding rites had to enter clean and leave without crossing the same ground. There should be a lower passage."

"Should be?"

"Ancient architecture wasn't part of my training."

"I thought ancient everything was part of your training."

Despite himself, Kaelen gave a tired, breathless laugh. "That was the plan."

They moved behind the altar. Its front had been carved with kneeling figures pouring water into an open hand. Elias pushed against the stone, but pain tore through his injured shoulder. His arm weakened at once.

Kaelen wedged the end of his staff into the seam along the floor. Together they leaned against it. Stone scraped over stone. The altar shifted half an inch, then stopped.

The creature struck again.

This time the barrier did not bend. Green vapor boiled from the creature and streamed into the runes. The light brightened until Elias could no longer look directly at it. Cracks sounded from the courtyard as plates of corruption split away from the thing outside.

Kaelen stared past Elias at the doorway. "It isn't resisting the Blight. It's feeding on it."

"Then why was it dying when we arrived?"

"Because nothing had touched it. The seal starved while it waited for something to contain."

This was not simply a fortress built to keep corruption outside. It was a trap built to draw corruption in and consume it.

Elias set both hands against the altar.

"Again."

They pushed. His shoulder screamed. Kaelen's boots slipped on the wet floor, and the wound at his side began to bleed freely. The altar shifted another inch. Beneath it, Elias heard the hollow tumble of loose pebbles.

"There's space under there," he said.

Kaelen lowered the staff and drove it deeper into the seam. "Then help me prove it."

They heaved once more. The altar tilted, balanced for a terrible instant, and toppled onto its side with a crash that echoed through the sanctuary.

Beneath it lay a square shaft and a descending stair.

Cold air breathed up from below. It smelled of mineral water and wet stone. A band of pale light crossed the stair several steps down, stretched from wall to wall like a second barrier. Thin lines connected it to the glowing runes around the temple entrance.

Kaelen crouched beside the opening. "The outer barrier gathers the Bl, this one keeps it from reaching whatever lies below."

"Can we pass through it?"

"I don't know."

"Guess."

Kaelen studied the carved figures on the overturned altar. "The priests are shown carrying water through an open hand. If they tended the lower chambers, the seal must have allowed them through."

"You first," he said.

Kaelen peered into the dark. "Why me?"

"Because you have the light."

"And here I thought you were being chivalrous."

"Move."

Kaelen touched two fingers to the crystal on his staff. A weak blue glow spread across it, far dimmer than the light he had wielded in the courtyard. He lowered himself into the opening.

At the band of pale light, he hesitated. Then he stepped through.

His body arched.

Green radiance tore out of the wound in his side. It came in threads at first, so fine Elias might have mistaken them for reflections. Then a dark knot pulled free from beneath Kaelen's ribs. The barrier absorbed it with a flash that illuminated the stairwell from top to bottom.

Kaelen collapsed onto the step below.

"Kaelen!"

He rolled onto his back, gasping. The wound remained. Blood still soaked his shirt, and exhaustion had hollowed his face, but the gray-green discoloration around the claw marks was gone. His eyes, which had carried a faint green reflection since the courtyard, were clear again.

"It took it," he whispered. "The Blight. It took all of it."

The band across the stairs shone brighter.

Elias stepped into it.

Cold passed through his armor without resistance. For an instant it felt as though hooks had caught beneath every rib. Something shifted inside him—the heaviness in his muscles, the numbness around his ankle, the shadowy static that had crept across his vision during the fight. The barrier seized it all.

Green-black strands ripped from his shoulder, his throat, and the torn leather around his boot. Pain drove him to one knee, sharp but brief. The strands vanished into the light behind him.

Then warmth returned.

His shoulder was still bruised. His palm was still cut, and his leg still ached. The barrier had not mended flesh or restored strength. It had removed something colder and deeper: the corruption that had been spreading through him unnoticed.

Elias drew a full breath. For the first time since entering the fog, the air reached the bottom of his lungs.

Above them, the outer barrier rang like a struck bell. Pale light flooded the shaft as the creature attacked again. Its roar shook the sanctuary, but the barrier only burned brighter as it stripped away more of the Blight.

Elias dragged the altar fragment back toward the shaft. It would not hide their passage completely, but it would conceal the opening.

He pulled the stone over them. Darkness closed above, leaving only the clean white light of the inner seal.

Then he descended after Kaelen.

The passage narrowed until his shoulders nearly brushed both walls. Kaelen moved ahead, one hand pressed to his wound, his staff casting just enough light to show the next few steps. Water ran around their boots and deepened as they went.

Behind them, the barrier rang again. The sound followed them into the earth, steady and strong.

The stair ended in a low chamber. A spring bubbled from a crack in the wall and fed a channel wide enough for two men to walk abreast. The water reached Elias's ankles. Runes lined the channel, most worn smooth, but a few still held a soft white glow.

Kaelen stopped beside one of them.

"These aren't containment marks," he said.

"Can you read them while walking?"

He ignored the question and wiped mud from the stone. "They're directions. This one means westward flow. This one means refuge."

"Good. West is away from the center of the city."

"The others should be west of us too."

Elias felt something unclench in his chest. Three days earlier, their company had split at the old aqueduct when the fog swallowed the southern streets. Elias and Kaelen had been cut off from the others by a surge of Blight-Touched pouring from the collapsed houses. The original plan had been simple: anyone separated would withdraw beyond the city and regroup at the Greyhaven road marker before the third sunset.

They had missed that deadline.

If Captain Mira had survived, she would have waited as long as discipline allowed and then moved the company to higher ground. Bren would argue for searching. Sera would insist that two missing men were not worth losing six more. Tomas would say nothing until everyone else had exhausted themselves, then choose the sensible path and make it sound obvious.

Elias had spent the last two nights refusing to imagine the marker empty.

"They won't still be at Greyhaven," he said.

"Mira will leave a sign."

"If she reached it."

Kaelen looked back. The crystal light sharpened the exhaustion in his face. "She reached it."

Elias wished certainty could make a thing true. Tonight, he accepted the loan.

They followed the westward channel.

The passage had been built as more than a drain. Side chambers opened at intervals, some holding collapsed shelves, clay basins, and rusted racks for ceremonial tools. In one, Kaelen found sealed jars of salt turned gray with age. In another, Elias discovered the remains of a leather satchel beneath a fallen stone.

Most of its contents had rotted, but a thin bronze plate survived. It bore a map etched in lines no wider than hairs: the temple above, six smaller shrines, and a web of channels beneath the city. Black tarnish covered the eastern half. A single route ran west beyond the outer wall and ended beside a symbol resembling a split crown.

Kaelen traced it with a thumb. "A way station. Or a second refuge."

"How far?"

"The map has no scale."

"Of course it doesn't."

Elias slid the plate inside his armor. It pressed cold against his ribs.

The ceiling shook.

Water surged around their boots. The white runes along the channel flared in sequence behind them, each growing brighter as power traveled down from the barrier above.

Kaelen watched the light race through the passage. "The barrier is carrying what it absorbs into the lower seals."

"Can the creature reach us down here?"

Kaelen shook his head. "Not through that barrier. But it can still bring the temple down on top of us."

They hurried onward.

The water rose to their shins, then their knees. The smooth floor sloped downward, forcing them deeper beneath the city before the route could climb toward the western fields. Elias's leg stiffened. Every step sent pain from his ankle through his hip. Kaelen stumbled more often, catching himself against the wall.

When he fell the third time, he did not rise.

Elias grabbed the back of his coat and pulled him above the water.

"On your feet."

"I'm trying."

"Try standing."

Kaelen pushed up, but his wounded side folded beneath him. Elias caught him. Fresh blood streamed into the channel and vanished west in twisting red ribbons.

For an instant, green light appeared in the water behind them. It was faint and scattered, dozens of small points opening beneath the surface.

Elias hooked Kaelen's arm across his shoulders and forced him forward.

"The staff," Kaelen protested.

It had fallen several paces behind them.

"Leave it."

"I need it."

"You need blood more."

Kaelen twisted free, staggered back, and snatched the staff from the current. As his hand closed around it, the points of green light beneath the water brightened.

Shapes moved in the channel.

They were small, no longer than Elias's forearm, and nearly transparent. Dozens clung to the submerged walls. They had too many legs and round mouths ringed with pale teeth. Kaelen's blood had woken them.

One released its grip and darted forward.

Elias drew his sword. In the narrow tunnel he could not swing, so he drove the point into the water. The blade struck the creature and pinned it against the floor. Its body burst into a cloud of black fluid.

Every other shape turned toward them.

"Light," Elias said.

"I have almost nothing left."

"Then almost nothing will have to do."

Kaelen braced the staff in both hands. His lips moved, but no sound came at first. The crystal sputtered. A thin blue flame crawled down its length, faded, then returned as white radiance.

The creatures recoiled.

"Go," Kaelen gasped.

They pushed through the water. The light around the staff extended barely three feet, but wherever it touched, the channel creatures flattened themselves against the stone. Beyond it, more released from the walls and followed.

Elias half carried Kaelen, guided by the current and the bronze map lodged against his chest. The passage curved twice, then divided.

The water flowed through both branches.

No rune marked the walls.

"Which way?" Elias asked.

Kaelen lifted his staff. The light had begun to turn blue again. Behind them, the creatures clustered at its edge in a rippling wall of teeth.

"I don't know."

Elias pulled out the bronze plate. The etched route forked near the western edge of the city. One branch ended at a circle beneath the wall. The other continued to the split crown.

He looked at the current. Dead leaves floated through the right passage. The left carried only black water.

"Right."

"The refuge is left."

"The right branch reaches the surface sooner."

"How can you tell?"

"Leaves."

Kaelen stared at the drifting fragments and gave a weak nod. "I knew there was a reason we kept you."

They took the right branch.

The channel narrowed and began to rise. The water fell below their knees. Elias could smell rain again, clean and cold beneath the rot. A draft touched his face.

Then Kaelen's light died.

The creatures rushed forward in the darkness.

Elias shoved Kaelen ahead and turned. He could not see well enough to strike, but he heard water breaking around the swarm. He dropped his sword, pulled the gray salt jar from his belt, and smashed it across the channel.

The first creatures hit the salt and shrieked.

The sound multiplied through the tunnel. Bodies thrashed against the stones. Elias groped for his sword, found the blade instead of the hilt, and sliced his palm before closing his fingers around the grip.

"Climb!" Kaelen called.

Ahead, faint moonlight shone through an iron grate set into the sloping ceiling. Kaelen had reached it and was pushing with both hands, but the grate did not move.

Elias backed toward him. The salt had slowed the swarm, not stopped it. Several creatures crawled over the bodies of the others, their translucent flesh blistering as they came.

He joined Kaelen beneath the grate and drove his good shoulder into it.

Rust cracked. The grate lifted an inch and fell.

"Again," Elias said.

They pushed. Behind them, the shrieking ceased.

The sudden silence was worse.

Green light filled the bend in the tunnel as hundreds of the channel creatures crowded over one another. Their translucent bodies caught and magnified the glow of their eyes until the entire passage shone.

Kaelen saw it and bared his teeth.

"Again."

They struck the grate together. One hinge tore loose.

The swarm climbed the walls behind them, flowing around the salt in a mass of legs and circular mouths.

Elias wedged his sword into the opening and pulled until the blade bent. The second hinge snapped. The grate tumbled outward, vanishing into wet grass.

He shoved Kaelen through the opening, then climbed after him as the first creature fastened onto his boot.

Teeth punched through the leather. Elias kicked, but the creature held on until Kaelen struck it with the base of his staff. It fell back into the tunnel.

Elias rolled onto the wet grass. Kaelen dragged the broken grate over the hole. Green light crowded beneath its bars, then slowly receded as the channel creatures withdrew from the open air.

For a long time, neither man moved.

They lay in a drainage ditch beneath the western city wall. Behind them, the ruined towers rose through thinning fog. From this side, the temple was invisible, but the immense shape above it was not. It stood higher than the surrounding roofs, black against the clouds, crowned by a shifting cluster of green lights.

The thing did not follow.

It did not need to. The city belonged to it.

Kaelen rolled onto his side and stared back. "We failed."

Elias sat up. His wounded palm dripped onto the grass. Blood seeped through the punctures in his boot, but there was no cold pressure spreading beneath the wound. Whatever lived in the channel was flesh and blood, not Blight.

"We survived."

"That isn't the same as succeeding."

"No. It is what makes succeeding later possible."

He removed the bronze map and held it beneath the moonlight. The web of tunnels gleamed faintly. Near the temple, an etched mark he had mistaken for decoration resembled the same cluster of lights crowning the creature.

Kaelen leaned closer. "That map was made before the city."

"Then someone knew it was there."

"And built the temple over it."

"To contain it."

Kaelen looked toward the distant silhouette. "Until I opened the seal."

"Until the seal failed," Elias said. "You were standing nearby. That doesn't make you its architect."

"You don't know what I felt."

"No. But I know what blame looks like when it starts making decisions. We don't have time for it."

Elias rose unsteadily. West of the ditch, dark fields climbed toward a line of wooded hills. The Greyhaven road lay somewhere beyond them. Farther north stood the old watchfort where Mira would take the company if the rendezvous failed.

Between here and there waited miles of open ground, patrol roads, and whatever lesser horrors had spread beyond the fog. Elias had half a sword, a damaged shoulder, and a leg that threatened to buckle whenever he put his full weight on it. Kaelen could barely stand and had exhausted whatever power had awakened inside him.

They were in no condition to challenge the Blight.

For the first time since entering the city, Elias allowed that truth to settle without fighting it.

They needed more than rest. Kaelen needed a teacher who understood the broken seal and the force moving through him. Elias needed a weapon that could wound corrupted flesh and the skill to keep fighting when strength alone failed. Their company needed numbers, supplies, and knowledge of the buried shrines shown on the map.

They needed to become people who could return without depending on a miracle.

Kaelen used his staff to pull himself upright. "Greyhaven first?"

"If Mira left a sign, we follow it. If she didn't, we go to the watchfort."

"And if the others never made it out?"

Elias looked at the road ahead rather than the city behind.

"Then we find out what happened. We don't bury them in our imagination."

Kaelen adjusted his torn coat over the wound. "After we find them?"

"We get stronger."

The answer sounded small beside the enormity rising from the fog, so Elias gave it shape.

"We take the map to someone who can read every mark on it. We learn what your magic did in that courtyard and how to make it answer when you call. We find steel that doesn't pass through those things like rotten cloth. We gather everyone willing to believe this city is the beginning of the danger, not the end of it."

He met Kaelen's eyes.

"Then we come back."

A deep call rolled from the city. It crossed the fields slowly, more vibration than sound. Green lights awakened one after another in windows, streets, and broken towers until the ruins resembled a vast constellation beneath the fog.

Kaelen watched them appear. Fear remained in his face, but it no longer held him still.

"We should move before dawn," he said.

Elias offered his uninjured arm. Kaelen took it.

Together they climbed from the ditch and crossed the first field. Each step away felt wrong. Elias expected pursuit, accusation, or the sudden weight of claws against his back. None came. The city merely watched them go.

At the crest of the hill, he stopped once and looked behind him.

The fog had swallowed the lower walls. Only the spires and the towering shape remained above it. This was not victory. It was not even escape, not while the Blight could spread and the thing beneath the temple could reach through every corrupted creature it commanded.

But now Elias knew the direction of the road.

West, to Greyhaven.

North, if necessary, to the watchfort.

Back to Mira, Bren, Sera, Tomas, and anyone else who had survived.

Then onward—to teachers, weapons, allies, and the strength they had lacked tonight.

Elias turned away from the city.

He and Kaelen disappeared over the hill before the sun rose, carrying a map of the darkness beneath the ruins and a promise neither of them spoke again.

They would return.
