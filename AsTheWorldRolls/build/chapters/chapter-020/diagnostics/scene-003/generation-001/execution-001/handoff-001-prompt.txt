Elias turned to Kaelen, who stood apart from the others, leaning against a gnarled root system that had twisted around a boulder. The young man’s face was pale, the shadows under his eyes deep enough to look bruised. His brass gauntlet sat still on his wrist, the runes etched into the metal dark and dormant.

“Kaelen,” Elias said, his voice low. “It’s your turn.”

Kaelen didn’t answer immediately. He looked at the quartz stone in Elias’s hand, then at the chisel lying on the jagged stone outcrop. He seemed to be calculating the distance between where he stood and the edge of the danger, a habit Elias had learned to recognize but not yet fully understand.

“I can do it,” Kaelen said. His voice was flat, stripped of its usual hesitant lilt. “I just need to know what to listen for.”

Elias stepped closer, holding the quartz stone out. The stone was warm, pulsing with a faint, rhythmic vibration that Elias could feel in his fingertips. “The frequency is stable now. Elara found the resonance. Your job isn’t to push it, Kaelen. It’s to hold it. You’re the bridge. If you try to push, the channel breaks. If you let go, the barrier starves. You have to stay exactly where you are.”

Kaelen took the stone. His fingers were cold, and the contrast seemed to shock him, his breath catching in a short, sharp gasp. He closed his hand around the quartz, and for a moment, his eyes went wide, the pupils dilating until they were black pools.

Then, slowly, the gauntlet on his wrist began to glow.

It wasn’t the sickly green light of the Blight, nor the harsh white of the barrier. It was a soft, amber hum, a color that reminded Elias of old firelight. The light spread from the brass, creeping up the veins of Kaelen’s forearm, tracing the pale map of his blood vessels.

Elias watched Kaelen’s face. The young man’s expression was tense, his jaw clenched, but there was no tremor. No shaking. He was standing perfectly still, as if he had become part of the rock formation behind him.

“Can you hear it?” Elias asked.

Kaelen’s eyes didn’t open. They were closed, his head tilted slightly back. “It’s... quiet,” he murmured. “It’s not screaming. It’s just... waiting.”

Torin stepped forward, his hand resting on the hilt of his sword, though his posture was relaxed. He didn’t take his eyes off Kaelen. “Is he stable?”

Elias nodded. “He’s regulating it. He’s not suppressing it, and he’s not feeding it. He’s just... listening.”

Kaelen’s hand tightened around the quartz stone. The amber light flared brighter, and the air around them began to vibrate, a low, subsonic thrum that Elias felt in his teeth rather than heard with his ears. The quartz stone in his hand began to sing, a clear, crystalline note that harmonized with the hum from the gauntlet.

Elias looked at the stone, then at Kaelen. The resonance was perfect. It was the exact frequency needed to keep the barrier fed without overwhelming it. It was the precise balance they had theorized about in the tavern, but seeing it work, seeing Kaelen hold it with such quiet, terrifying control, was a different thing entirely.

Kaelen’s eyes opened. They were no longer black; they were a clear, steady blue. He looked at Elias, and for the first time since they had entered the forest, his eyes were clear. There was no fear in them. No confusion. Just a focused, almost serene intensity.

“I’m holding it,” Kaelen said. His voice was calm, steady. “I can feel the barrier. It’s... hungry. But it’s satisfied. As long as I keep this frequency, it stays open. It stays fed.”

Elias let out a breath he didn’t realize he had been holding. “How long can you keep this up?”

Kaelen looked down at his gauntlet, where the amber light was now steady, no longer pulsing but sustained. “I don’t know. But I can do it. I can feel where the line is. I won’t cross it.”

Torin let out a long breath, his shoulders dropping. “That’s all we need to know.”

Elias looked at Maren, who was standing a few paces away, her arms crossed over her chest. She wasn’t looking at Kaelen with fear, or even with the wary assessment he had expected. She was looking at him with something like pride. It was a quiet, dangerous thing, that pride. It meant she was beginning to trust him. And trust, in this situation, was a heavy burden to carry.

Kaelen released the quartz stone, and it dropped to the stone outcrop with a sharp clink. The amber light faded from his arm, leaving behind only the faint warmth of the metal. He stood there, breathing slowly, his eyes fixed on the forest floor.

Elias picked up the quartz stone. It was still warm, but the vibration had ceased. It was inert again, waiting for the next time they would need it.

“We’re ready,” Elias said. “We have the method. We have the operator. And we have the sensor.”

He looked at the group. Elara, with her tools packed. Torin, with his hand no longer on his sword. Maren, with her arms still crossed. And Kaelen, standing in the center of the circle, his gauntlet dark and silent.

They had bridged the gap. They had found the balance. And now, they had to decide what to do with it. The dawn was still hours away, but the night was no longer empty. It was full of possibility, and full of danger, and full of the quiet, steady hum of power that had been tamed, if only for a moment.

Elias turned to the others. “We rest. We eat. And then we go.”

No one argued. No one hesitated. They simply nodded, one by one, and began the slow walk back toward the light of the tavern, leaving the ridge outcrop and the silent stone behind them in the dark.
