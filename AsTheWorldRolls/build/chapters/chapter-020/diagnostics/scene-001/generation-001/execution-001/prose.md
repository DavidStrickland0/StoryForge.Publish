The night air outside the watchfort was different from what Elias had expected. It did not smell of damp earth and rotting wood, nor did it carry the faint, metallic tang of the Blight that usually clung to the perimeter like static. Instead, it smelled of ozone and crushed stone. The barrier hummed behind them, a low vibration that felt more like a heartbeat than a sound, pulsing through their boots and up into their shins. It was a strange sensation, as if the ground itself had been tuned to a frequency that made the hair on their arms stand at attention.

Elias led the way, his lantern swinging gently in the windless air. The light from it did not flicker; it burned with a steady, yellow flame that seemed to push back the darkness rather than merely illuminate it. He paused at the threshold of the watchfort, looking out into the forest.

“See?” he said, his voice quiet but firm. “No smoke. No fire. Just the hum.”

Kaelen nodded, his eyes narrowing as he scanned the tree line. The trees were tall and ancient, their trunks thick with moss that seemed to glow faintly in the dim light. They stood still, silent, and perfectly still. There was no rustle of leaves, no chirping of insects, no sound of wind moving through the branches. It was a silence that felt unnatural, as if the forest itself had been holding its breath.

“Good,” Elias said, stepping forward. “We move now. Keep close to the edge of the trees.”

They stepped out onto the ridge that bordered the forest. The ground was firm beneath their feet, packed down by years of foot traffic and natural settling. But as they moved further into the treeline, the air grew heavier again. The ozone scent intensified, and a faint vibration seemed to ripple through the grass before them.

“Nothing’s wrong,” Elias said, though his voice betrayed a hint of tension. “Just the forest adjusting to our presence.”

“This isn’t just a forest,” she said, her voice low and thoughtful. “It’s alive. And it knows we’re here.”

Kaelen looked at her, his expression unreadable. He reached out and touched the stone, feeling its warmth spread through his fingers. The glow intensified briefly before fading again.

“It’s reacting to us,” he said. “Not hostile, but… aware.”

Elias nodded, though he didn’t say anything. He knew that Kaelen was right. The forest wasn’t just a collection of trees and soil; it was something more, something ancient and sentient. And now that they were here, it was watching them, waiting to see what they would do.

They moved forward, careful not to step too far from the edge of the ridge. The ground beneath them seemed to shift slightly with each step, as if it were trying to guide them along a specific path. Elias kept his eyes on the map he had drawn earlier, marking the route they needed to take. He knew that the slurry road was no longer an option; the forest had turned it into a treacherous mess of mud and water that would swallow anyone who dared to walk upon it. Instead, they would have to follow the natural contours of the land, finding dry patches of soil where the magic of the barrier had stabilized the ground.

As they walked, Kaelen’s legs shook slightly with each step. He tried to hide it, keeping his posture straight and his movements controlled. But Maren noticed, and she tightened her grip on his arm.

“You’re doing well,” she said softly. “Just keep moving.”

Kaelen nodded, though he didn’t look at her. He knew that she was worried about him, but he didn’t want to admit it. He didn’t need pity; he needed to prove that he could still function, even in this weakened state.

They reached a clearing where the trees grew so close together that they formed a natural tunnel of green and shadow. The air here was thick with moisture and the scent of damp earth. Elias stepped forward, his lantern casting long shadows on the ground.

“Stay close,” he said. “The path isn’t clear.”

They moved deeper into the clearing, their footsteps silent on the soft moss that covered the ground. The silence was absolute, save for the faint hum of the barrier and the occasional rustle of a leaf in the windless air. It was a strange kind of peace, one that felt more like waiting than rest.

Torin scanned the tree line with his eyes, his sword ready but not drawn. He knew that danger could come from any direction, even in the middle of a forest that seemed so still. But there was no sign of movement, no sound of footsteps or growls. The only thing that seemed to be moving was the air itself, shifting around them like a living thing.

Elias stopped and looked at the map again, his brow furrowed. He had marked a route that should have been straightforward, but now he wasn’t sure if he was reading it correctly. The forest seemed to be resisting their path, forcing them to detour around obstacles that shouldn’t have been there.

“We need to find the dry channel,” Elara said, her voice cutting through the silence. “It’s supposed to be right here.”

Elias nodded, though he didn’t look convinced. He knew that the forest wasn’t cooperating with their plans, and he wasn’t sure how long they could keep moving against its will before something went wrong.

Kaelen stepped forward, his voice low and steady. “I can feel it,” he said. “The barrier is guiding us. We just need to follow.”

Maren looked at him, her eyes filled with concern. “Are you sure?” she asked. “We don’t want to push too far ahead of the others.”

Kaelen nodded again, though he didn’t look at her this time. He knew that she was worried about him, but he also knew that they couldn’t afford to stop now. The forest was waiting for them to make a move, and if they hesitated, it might change its mind about letting them pass.
